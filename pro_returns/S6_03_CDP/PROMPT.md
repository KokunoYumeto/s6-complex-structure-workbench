## Leads (unverified; prove or refute each)

Leads 1-7 are from the survey (Claude, Anthropic) and its readers' notes `notes/G1b_notes.md`, `notes/B_notes.md`, `notes/A0_notes.md`. Lead 8 is from the review of S6_02 (Claude, Fable 5.2). - **Lead 1 (the kernel).** Let S be a reduced effective Cartier divisor in a complex manifold X, T_S = ker(Ω¹_S → j_*Ω¹_{S_reg}) the torsion of Ω¹_S, Ω̃¹_S = Ω¹_S/T_S, K_S = ker(Ω¹_X|_S → Ω̃¹_S). Then 0 → N*_{S/X} → K_S → T_S → 0 is exact, and for a line bundle A on S with H⁰(S, Ω̃¹_S ⊗ A) = 0 and H⁰(S, N* ⊗ A) = 0 one has H⁰(S, Ω¹_X|_S ⊗ A) = ker(δ_A: H⁰(T_S ⊗ A) → H¹(N* ⊗ A)). If S has normal crossings with normalization η: S̃ → S then K_S ≅ η_*η*N*, so the ambient group is H⁰(S̃, η*(N* ⊗ A)) under the two vanishings. (`workbench/cdp_program.tex` l. 52-110; course lesson §§1-3.)
- **Lead 2 (the test family).** C = {y²z = x²(x+z) + tz³} ⊂ P² × Δ has smooth total space and nodal central fibre C₀; X = C × P¹ → Δ; S = C₀ × P¹; A = pr₁*A_λ with A_λ ∈ Pic⁰(C₀) glued by λ not a root of unity. Then H⁰(S, N* ⊗ A) = 0, H⁰(S, Ω̃¹_S ⊗ A) = 0 and H⁰(S, Ω¹_X|_S ⊗ A) ≠ 0. The correction note (Lemma 4.3, Theorem 4.4) has a version in which the twist is O_C(P − Q) for two sections and extends to the total space, with ambient H⁰ = C exactly for every gluing scalar.
- **Lead 3 (CDP's own propositions).** By CDP20 Proposition 4.11 every singular irreducible reduced fibre is non-normal, so the case in which the p. 692 inference is valid (S normal, [AC26] Remark 10.4) never occurs among singular reduced fibres; and in Step 1 of the proof of CDP20 Proposition 7.3 the authors derive that all η*(L_t) are trivial, which is exactly the situation in which Lead 1 gives a non-zero ambient section.
- **Lead 4 (the index).** For a compact connected complex threefold Y with b₁(Y) = b₂(Y) = 0 and a non-zero effective divisor: χ(Y, T_Y ⊗ L) = 1 − b₃(Y)/2 for every L ∈ Pic(Y); h⁰ = h³ = 0 on a dense open subset of each component of Pic(Y); if H²(Y, T_Y ⊗ L₀) = 0 for one L₀ then b₃ ≥ 2; if b₃ = 0 then H²(Y, T_Y ⊗ L) ≠ 0 for every L. (Course lesson §11.3-11.5; `notes/G1b_notes.md`, G1b-3.)
- **Lead 5 (Euler numbers).** If the general fibre of f is a complex torus then c₃(X) = e(X) = Σ_c e((X_c)_red), the sum over the singular fibres; for X of [AC26] the only contribution is e(W) = 2. So for torus fibrations "c₃ ≤ 0" is a statement about Euler numbers of degenerate fibres. (`notes/G1b_notes.md`, G1b-13.)
- **Lead 6 (monodromy).** (K ⋊ Q)^ab = Q^ab ⊕ (K^ab)_Q; the count of CDP20 Lemma 4.2 becomes r = s − 1 + t′ with t′ the rank of the coinvariants of H₁(F;Q); the rank argument of CDP98 Lemma 3.2 is valid exactly when the invariant lattice H¹(F;Z)^{π₁} spans H¹(F, O_F) over C (`workbench/cdp_program.tex` l. 1302-1358 and l. 1592-1606, condition (5.33); [AC26] Lemma 10.7, T l. 4682).
- **Lead 7 (algebraic dimension without periods).** If X is compact with b₂(X) = 0 and f: X → C is surjective with connected fibres and general fibre a compact Kähler manifold (for instance a torus), then every effective divisor of X is vertical and M(X) = f*M(C); so a(X) = 1 for every member of every known torus-fibred family on S^6 (course lesson §8, l. 736-781).
- **Lead 8 (H² through the normalization).** Let Y be a compact complex threefold, S ⊂ Y a reduced divisor with normal crossings, η: S̃ → S its normalization. Then 0 → T_Y(−log S) → T_Y → η_*N_η → 0 is exact with N_η = K_{S̃} ⊗ η*K_Y^{−1}; so for every L ∈ Pic(Y) with H³(Y, T_Y(−log S) ⊗ L) = 0 the map H²(Y, T_Y ⊗ L) → H²(S̃, K_{S̃} ⊗ η*(K_Y^{−1} ⊗ L)) = H⁰(S̃, η*(K_Y ⊗ L^{−1}))^∨ is onto. The target is the dual of the ambient group of Lead 1 (there A = (L* ⊗ K_Y)|_S and N* is trivial on a fibre). For X, S = W: η*L is trivial for every L (Pic(X) → Pic(dP₆) is zero) and K_X is trivial near W, so the target is H²(dP₆, K_{dP₆}) = C for every L, and H³(X, T_X(−log W) ⊗ L) = H¹(B, R²f_*(T_X(−log W) ⊗ L)) vanishes whenever that direct image is a torsion sheaf; expected: H²(X, T_X ⊗ L) ≠ 0 for every L, with this surjection as the reason, and the non-vanishing of CDP's H² is carried by the normalization of the non-normal fibre.
- **Lead 9 (stability of the torus fibration).** Let g: Y → C be a proper surjective holomorphic map from a compact complex threefold onto a compact Riemann surface with connected fibres and general fibre a complex 2-torus, Ω(t) the period matrix of the fibres and κ = dΩ/dt the Kodaira-Spencer matrix. The obstruction to extending g along a first-order deformation θ ∈ H¹(Y, T_Y) is g_*θ ∈ H¹(Y, g*T_C), and 0 → H¹(C, T_C) → H¹(Y, g*T_C) → H⁰(C, T_C ⊗ R¹g_*O_Y) → 0 is exact. The image of H¹(Y, T_Y) in H⁰(C, T_C ⊗ R¹g_*O_Y) lies in the kernel of the connecting map δ: T_C ⊗ R¹g_*O_Y → R²g_*T_{Y/C}; on a smooth fibre F, δ(∂_t ⊗ α) = κ(∂_t) ∪ α ∈ H²(F, T_F) = Λ²H¹(F, O_F) ⊗ Lie(F), and this is injective in α exactly when κ has rank 2. Both sheaves are locally free of rank 2, so if κ has rank 2 at one point then δ is injective on sections, and for C = P¹ every θ has g_*θ = 0: every first-order deformation keeps the fibration. For an elliptic fibration of a surface the target of δ is zero and the statement is false (elliptic K3 surfaces have deformations without elliptic fibration), so the argument separates the two cases. For X, and for every family built as in [E26] from an elliptic surface, a section and a linearization, with Ω = [[τ, μ], [μ, β/6]]: det(dΩ/dτ) = −(dμ/dτ − (i/(2π))·(ϑ₁′/ϑ₁)(μ, τ))², with ϑ₁′ the derivative in the elliptic variable (tested numerically by finite differences at one point of the family X_c; it agrees with survey F19, a zero of order 2 at the order-three point). Expected: every sufficiently small deformation of every known torus-fibred complex structure on S^6 is again fibred in tori over P¹, by this computation together with the theory of deformations of holomorphic maps (Horikawa, *On deformations of holomorphic maps* I-III: a reference from memory, to be found, opened and checked); so no complex structure of algebraic dimension 0 on S^6 is a small deformation of a known one.
