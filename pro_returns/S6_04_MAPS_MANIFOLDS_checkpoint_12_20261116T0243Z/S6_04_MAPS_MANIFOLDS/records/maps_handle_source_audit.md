# Ordinary-handle source check

Primary source opened on 2026-11-16:
Thomas Vogel, *Existence of Engel structures*, Annals of Mathematics
169 (2009), 79–137, DOI 10.4007/annals.2009.169.79.
Publisher PDF:
https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v169-n1-p03.pdf

The web reader's `turn377view0` gives Section 4.1 on printed page 103
(PDF page 25, zero-based page 24). Definition 4.1 specifies the
attaching face of an n-dimensional round handle. Equation (16),
reader lines 973–988, explicitly cuts its circle factor into two
intervals, obtaining one ordinary handle of index l and one of index
l+1. The current proof repeats this elementary geometric argument,
including its attaching faces and normal-coordinate order, and
iterates it for a product torus.

No appeal is made to Asimov's round-handle existence theorem
(Vogel Theorem 4.3), to an Engel-structure theorem, or to a purported
classification of the manifolds in the question. The cited fact is
only the literal two-piece decomposition. All nonstandard attaching
maps in E.2.21 are supplied separately by actual toric coordinate
maps, the finite-cap formula, and the marked flat-bundle cuts.

## Relative uniqueness and actual smooth comparison

John Milnor, *Lectures on the h-Cobordism Theorem*, notes by
L. Siebenmann and J. Sondow, Princeton University Press, 1965.
Primary book text opened at
https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~hekmati/Books/Milnor2.pdf
on 2026-11-16. Web-reader references `turn392view1`, `turn393view0`,
`turn393view1`, and `turn393view2`.

Theorem 1.4 (restated and proved at printed p.25, reader lines
449 onward) is the relative uniqueness of the smooth structure
on a composition along a specified boundary diffeomorphism.
Theorem 3.13 (reader lines 548–552) identifies an elementary
cobordism with the explicit model constructed from its entire
characteristic embedding. Theorem 5.8 (printed pp.63–64,
reader lines 1195–1202) extends a compact submanifold isotopy to
an ambient isotopy. Its preceding vector-field argument supplies
the cutoff construction used for the relative tubular version.

E.2.21 now derives the handle-attachment comparison from these
three precise statements. The full normal-disk embedding is
retained, an ambient collar isotopy is used only where an actual
positive radial isotopy was specified, and the identity on the
new handle extends the comparison at each stage. This also
retains every top-handle attaching sphere map, so no assertion
that an arbitrary sphere diffeomorphism extends over a disk is
being made. These statements are used for the direct finite
framed-handle identification, not for recognition by homology.
