# Independent referee note on A.4.1–A.4.4

**Verdict.** No gap was found in the integral topology or the monodromy block
of A.4.1–A.4.4, conditional on the actual families and normalization morphisms
constructed in A.2.2. In particular, the node generization is unimodular, the
Thom and Mayer–Vietoris images have the claimed integral saturation, and the
nonzero monodromy Smith factors are exactly `1,1,1,1,1,11`. This review does
not extend its verdict to other parts of A or B. No changes were made to
`AB_push.tex`.

The check below uses the displayed local charts and integral intersection
data directly. It does not accept the rank computation in the AB program as
a substitute for the geometric identifications. The independent program
`code/e_push_A4_referee.py` uses the odd unimodular blow-up lattice of rank ten,
rather than the E8 basis used by the AB program. Its output is retained as
`.out` and `.json`.

## 1. Exceptional fibres and their node generization

Write the base normalization coordinates as `(X,z,v)` and blow up `(X,zv)`.
The two charts are `X=zvA` and `zv=XB`. In the second chart the chosen small
resolution has charts

\[
 (z,a,B):\quad X=za,\quad v=aB,
 \qquad
 (X,d,v):\quad z=Xd,\quad B=dv,
 \quad ad=1.
\]

Over the base node the fibre is a chain `C0 ∪ Cs`. In the `(z,a,B)` chart,
`C0` is `z=a=0` with coordinate `B`; it is completed by the original `A`
chart. The small exceptional curve `Cs` is `z=B=0` with coordinate `a`; it
is completed by the `d` chart. They meet once, at `z=a=B=0`.

On the branch `v=0, z=t≠0`, the generic exceptional fibre has `a=0`.
Its closure over `t=0` is `C0`, with multiplicity one. On the branch
`z=0, v=t≠0`, its closure has equation `aB=t`. Its central fibre is
`C0+Cs`, again with both multiplicities one. Consequently a cohomology class
whose degrees on `(C0,Cs)` are `(r,s)` has generic degrees `(r,r+s)` on the
two branches. This proves the displayed generization matrix

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
\]

by actual specializations of fibres. The other small-resolution choice
interchanges the branches and gives the same integral conclusion.

The stalks of `R²h_*Z` are therefore one copy of Z on the smooth part of
the nodal curve and two copies at its node; the displayed generization is an
integral isomorphism to the two branch values. Complex orientation makes the
rank-one local system on the smooth part constant. Thus this sheaf is the
normalization pushforward `η_*Z_(P1)`, not a constant sheaf on the nodal curve.
This distinction is essential: replacing it by the latter would create a
spurious odd cohomology group.

Proper base change, or the local toric retractions in the displayed charts,
identifies these stalks with fibre cohomology. Every fibre is connected and
has no positive cohomology other than H², so `R⁰h_*Z=Z` and there are no other
positive direct-image rows. The two Leray rows are in even degrees and free.
There are no possible differentials or torsion extensions. This yields

\[
 b(R^\pm)=(1,0,12,0,12,0,1).
\]

For the fundamental group, a direct general-position argument supplements
the local retractions. The nodal centre has real dimension two in the
six-manifold R, so loops and their null-homotopies avoid it. Hence `R\C` is
simply connected. Every loop in the smooth `R^±` can avoid its exceptional
divisor, whose real dimension is four. The complement is `R\C`; thus the
whole normalization is simply connected as well.

## 2. Primitive integral lattices

Let `L=H₂(S;Z)` with its unimodular intersection pairing. The data used are

\[
 F^2=Fm=Fv=0,\quad m^2=-2,\quad v^2=-6,\quad mv=-1,
 \quad OF=Om=1,\quad Ov=0,\quad O^2=-1.
\]

The plane generated by `(F,O)` has determinant −1, so it splits integrally.
Put `n=m−F`. Then `n,v` belong to its orthogonal complement, a negative E8
lattice, and their Gram matrix is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-2&-1\\-1&-6\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det=11.
\]

The span `A=<n,v>` is primitive: the square of a nontrivial saturation index
would divide 11. Therefore `<F,m,v>=ZF⊕A` is primitive in L. Unimodularity
and the dual of the free quotient show that

\[
 L\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^3,\qquad a\longmapsto(aF,av,am)
\]

is onto, with no hidden finite index. This also shows that `<F,m>`, `<F,v>`
and the indicated rank-one quotient images are primitive.

The section intersection data are consistent with the elliptic-surface
input: `O·6P=2` and `P·6P=2` give `Ov=0`, `v²=−6`, and `mv=−1` for
`v=6P−O−3F`. The translation formula sends O to `6P`, fixes F and the
seven reducible-fibre root classes, and preserves the pairing. It gives
`T*m=m−F`, as used below.

## 3. Thom maps and the complement Z

Use integral degree-two cohomology coordinates `(α,h,a)` on `R^+`, where
H restricts to zero on the first section and to `−m` on the second, and A
is the exceptional divisor. Its restriction is F on the modified first
section and zero on the second. Thus the combined restriction is exactly

\[
 (\alpha,h,a)\longmapsto(\alpha+aF,\alpha-hm).
\]

Its cokernel is `L/<F,m>`, obtained by taking the difference of the two
components. Since `<F,m>` is primitive, this map has primitive image of
rank 12. These restrictions are the Poincaré duals of the degree-four Thom
maps for the removed sections. They give `H₄(Z)=0` and
`H₃(Z)=L/<F,m>≅Z⁸`.

There is also a useful explicit description of the degree-two Thom map.
The two conductor divisor classes are

\[
 D'_0=H+m-A,\qquad D_\infty=H.
\]

Write a degree-two homology class in `R^+` as `(b,λ,η)`, dual to the stated
cohomology coordinates. Its two section intersections are

\[
 (bm+\lambda-\eta,\lambda).
\]

This map is onto `Z²`; its kernel is

\[
 H_2(Z)=\{(b,0,bm):b\in L\}\simeq L.
\]

This description supplies an integral splitting without relying merely on
the rank of the sequence. The degree-six Thom map is the primitive diagonal
`Z→Z²`, giving `H₅(Z)=Z`. The remaining asserted groups follow immediately
from the same exact sequence.

## 4. Boundary Gysin and Mayer–Vietoris maps

The first normal circle bundle `π:E→S` has Euler class `n=m−F`, a primitive
class. The integral Gysin sequence gives

\[
 H_2(E)=n^\perp,\quad H_3(E)=L/\mathbb Zn,
 \quad H_1(E)=H_4(E)=0,\quad H_5(E)=\mathbb Z.
\]

All identifications here use the complex normal orientation. The plumbing
identifies the second boundary using T on S and reverses this circle
orientation, exactly as required by `xy=u`.

For `a∈n^⊥`, put `(s,t,h)=(aF,av,am)`, so `h=s`. In `H₂(R^+)` the
normalization difference is

\[
 ((I-T)a,\ h-s,\ s).
\]

It belongs to the displayed `H₂(Z)` kernel since
`m·(I−T)a=aF`. Under `H₂(Z)≅L`, the actual degree-two Mayer–Vietoris map
is therefore

\[
 a\longmapsto(I-T)a=-s v+(t-3s)F.
\]

Surjectivity of the three intersection coordinates shows that `(s,t)` is
arbitrary on `n^⊥`. Its image is exactly the primitive span `<F,v>`, and
its kernel is `K={a:aF=av=am=0}`. This verifies the claimed saturation
geometrically, rather than deriving it from an abstract matrix of the same
rank.

For H₃, a tube from the first boundary maps to `a mod<F,m>`; one from the
second boundary maps with the opposite sign in the cokernel of the combined
restriction. The gluing reverses the normal circle and covers T. Consequently
the difference map has image `(T−I)L mod<F,m>=Zv` up to the harmless global
sign set by the boundary convention. This image is primitive, because
`<F,m,v>` is primitive. Thus the H₃ exact sequence in A.4.2 is an integral
exact sequence of free groups.

The H₅ difference is zero: the normal-circle reversal cancels the fact that
the two boundary fundamental classes sum to zero in Z. The H₀ difference
is also zero. The remaining terms now give

\[
 b(X(u))=(1,1,8,14,8,1,1),
\]

and all groups are torsion free. In particular the Euler number is 2.

## 5. The coefficient of the tube map and the factor 11

The geometric coefficient in the monodromy map is one. To see this without
assuming a Picard–Lefschetz normalization, the trace of the free oriented
circle action on E is the composite

\[
 H_2(E)\xrightarrow{\pi_*}H_2(S)
             \xrightarrow{\pi^!}H_3(E).
\]

Locally this is the product of the projected two-cycle with a circle making
one complete turn. The orientation-preserving circle action patches these
local products, proving the identity integrally. Under the Gysin descriptions
it sends `a∈n^⊥` to `a mod Zn`. A positive circuit of u in `xy=u` makes
exactly one such turn. Hence the neck variation on the quotient K is

\[
 a\longmapsto a\bmod\langle F,m,v\rangle,
\]

with the sign convention stated in A.4.2. It vanishes on the left-hand tube
subgroup. No multiple arising from a normal-bundle Euler class is present.
The same trace description is zero in other degrees, by the Gysin groups
already computed. It also makes `N²=0` immediate.

Finally split `L=<F,O>⊕E8(−1)`. Then

\[
 K=\mathbb ZF\oplus A^\perp,\qquad
 L/\langle F,m,v\rangle=\mathbb Z[O]\oplus(E_8(-1)/A).
\]

N kills the F summand and is the natural inclusion
`A^⊥→E8(−1)/A` on the other six generators. Since A is primitive in a
unimodular lattice, `E8(−1)/(A+A^⊥)` has order `|det A|=11`. It is cyclic
because 11 is prime. This proves the rank-six map has nonzero Smith factors
`1,1,1,1,1,11`, independently of any choice of E8 basis. The separate exact
program verifies the same statement in the integer lattice `I_(1,9)` and
records every basis and quotient matrix it uses.

## 6. Nearby Euler function and the non-Kähler conclusion

At a smooth point of the zero divisor, the fixed nonzero-u equation `xy=uz`
is a graph. Its sufficiently small local fibre contracts to its origin by
scaling x,y and the transverse coordinate, with z scaling quadratically.
At the old node, `xy=uzv` is a quadratic cone for fixed u, and its intersection
with a sufficiently small ball contracts by simultaneous radial scaling.
These facts justify the pointwise Euler value one at both kinds of zero,
rather than only the integrated contribution of their strata. Away from
the zero divisor the annulus has Euler number zero. Since the smooth part
of the nodal curve is `C*`, its integral contribution is zero even though
its pointwise value is one. The node contributes one.

For the semistable model the transverse equation is `xy=u` at every point
of the double locus, so its local nearby Euler value is zero everywhere
there. This yields `χ(R^±)−2χ(S)=26−24=2`. These counts agree with the
independent normalization and exceptional-fibre stratifications.

A global section of `η_*Z_(P1)` has equal values on the two branches at the
node. By the verified generization, its degree on `Cs` is zero. Pullbacks
from R also have degree zero on `Cs`, and Leray exhausts H². The small curve
is therefore null in torsion-free integral H₂. This independently proves
that `R^±` cannot be Kähler. The smooth fibres are not Kähler because `b₁=1`.
Thus the paper correctly declines to apply the classical Kähler/projective
Clemens–Schmid comparison. The stated filtration of the computed rational
monodromy representation has ranks `6,2,6`; it is explicitly not asserted to
be a polarized limiting mixed Hodge structure.

## 7. Scope of the verdict

The review verifies A.4.1, A.4.2, A.4.3 and A.4.4 using the actual A.2.2
normalization and semistable plumbing. It does not infer the existence of
that global model merely from the local rank computations. The existence
and proper gluing of A.2.2 are premises of this focused referee check. The
statement about the sign of N is read with the explicit neck and boundary
orientation conventions in A.4.2; changing them changes an overall sign,
not any group, rank, or Smith invariant.
