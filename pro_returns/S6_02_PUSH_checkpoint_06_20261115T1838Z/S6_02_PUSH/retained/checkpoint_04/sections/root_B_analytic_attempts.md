## Further attack on the unrestricted analytic filling

This record concerns the actual family after arbitrary finite parameter base change. It does not weaken the open problem to a polarized or toroidal subclass. The status remains Open.

The marked map to the t-line extends meromorphically by B.4.4. One proposed next step was to compare a graph modification with the explicitly constructed local toroidal models and conclude that every central normalization is class C. That comparison is not proved. A punctured isomorphism between proper families need not extend meromorphically: multiplication by exp(1/s) on the projective-line product gives an explicit elementary obstruction. The example invalidates the general extension principle, not the existence of the S6 filling. A special argument controlling the actual comparison is still required.

Kajiwara, Kato, and Nakayama (2008), original journal PDF, Theorem 0.1 and Section5.1, especially Theorem5.1.3, were opened at https://www.ms.u-tokyo.ac.jp/journal/pdf/jms150104.pdf . The proper-model construction does allow unpolarized log complex tori. It therefore improves on the hypothesis of a polarized Mumford theorem. It nevertheless presupposes a log torus with an admissible pairing. The missing global log extension and marked affine-torsor comparison are not supplied by that theorem. No claim that it classifies arbitrary proper analytic fillings is made.

Kamenova's original four-page author manuscript was opened at https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~kamenova/homepage_files/degen.pdf . Its Theorem3.1 assumes a Kähler total space and trivial canonical bundle. These conditions have not been established for a surface slice of an arbitrary filling here. The undated manuscript is cited as n.d.; an unverified publication date is not invented.

Schröer and Siebert (2002), original manuscript at https://www.math.uni-duesseldorf.de/~schroeer/publications_pdf/kodaira.pdf , introduction and Section3 Proposition3.1, were checked. The author bibliography supplies the published collection and pages193–222. Hopf normalizations in their construction belong to degenerations of primary Kodaira surfaces, so that construction is not a degeneration of the present regular torus fibres. It is retained as an analytic alternative to examine, without changing the nearby family.

The already-read Nishiguchi example was reopened at Example2.1. It shows why proper semistability alone does not imply class-C normalization, even for another family with Kähler nearby fibres. It does not demonstrate that a Hopf component occurs in this S6 problem. The finite-orbit fan components in A.2.4 are all class C: mixed components are complete toric threefolds, and pure-top components are bimeromorphic to the stated ruled bundle over S. The analytic unramified torsor alternative is still Kähler by B.3.8.

A further candidate is a non-Kähler cusp component with rational attaching surfaces. A naive fundamental-group argument only excludes attachments that leave a component loop alive. It must not be used when a Kato divisor in the overlap can kill that loop. The stronger degree-one comparison criterion now being checked in B.4.6 explicitly follows the maps on the overlaps and addresses that restricted construction. It does not force an unknown analytic component into a toric-Kato class.

The attempted horizontal classification also remains unproved. Non-isotrivial elliptic torsors over the genus-three cover are Kähler, but it has not been shown that every horizontal normalization of an arbitrary filling is such a ruled torsor model. Meromorphic extension of the base map alone does not give this identification. An analytic MMP or a generalized Albanese construction cannot be invoked with unstated Kähler or Hodge-degeneration hypotheses.

## Root check of the all-fan topology proof

The source of A.4.8 was read in full. The original AKMW Theorems1.6.1 and2.7.1 and their finite polyhedral proof on p552, analytic correspondence in Section5.5 p565, and barycentric construction in Section5.6 were opened at https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~karu/papers/semist.pdf . Matsuki's original erratum, Section2 p2, was opened at https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0006038 . The invalid strong-factorization algorithm is not used. The proof factors finite strict cone complexes, then realizes their monomial blow-ups analytically. Temporary pure-q blow-ups are explicitly allowed and their noncompact centres have no top cohomology contribution. This is the scope required for the claimed rank invariants. The result is accepted in the stated toroidal category, independently of the separately preserved D referee.

## Root C framing diagnostics

The proposed shortcut that the quotient torus gamma-u-w bounds a four-chain inside the cusp body was rejected after E's check: it represents the central oriented normalization three-cycle and need not bound. The relative-Chern-class approach therefore still requires a global chain and an actual comparison of stable frames. No Pontryagin number or cap integer is inferred from the local affine frame.

The exact program root_C_exterior_h3.py computes A0 and the two third-exterior-power norm matrices. Their stacked rank is3, and their common kernel is generated rationally by (0,1,1,6) in the basis (gamma-u-w, gamma-u-delta, gamma-w-delta, u-w-delta). The gamma-u-w class has nonzero norm at both finite places. These are exact lattice diagnostics; integral homology of the finite quotients is not silently identified with invariant lattices.

For a possible global-chain construction, the cusp-complement orbit space is examined as a pushout of its two oriented three-dimensional finite orbit fibres along the regular three-torus. The degree-three cover and degree-four cover give the top-dimensional relation (3,-4), so the regular three-torus has coefficient12 in its resulting rank-one cokernel. There is an additional degree-three term from the degree-two kernel, so this observation is not a complete homology or framing calculation. The coefficient12 agrees with C.3.16, but has not been assigned to the missing characteristic integer J.
