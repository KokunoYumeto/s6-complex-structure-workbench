1. Choose an adjacent divisor S_j, j≠i. Its equation pulls back under h to a nonzero effective divisor A on Z. Since H²(Y;Q)=0, c₁(O_Z(A))=0 rationally. Push A to Z₀ under σ. It cannot disappear: an effective σ-exceptional nonzero divisor has negative-definite intersection matrix, whereas A has zero intersection with each curve. Thus A₀=σ_*A is a nonzero effective rationally trivial divisor, and O_Z(A)=σ*O_Z₀(A₀); the exceptional correction is zero by the same matrix. A positive integer N kills the integral class of O_Y(S_j), so O_Y(NS_j) belongs to Pic⁰(Y).
2. Pullback from Pic⁰(Y) descends through σ because holomorphic line bundles in Pic⁰(Z) have degree zero on every exceptional P¹; the blowup formula identifies Pic⁰(Z) with Pic⁰(Z₀). This gives an actual connected complex Lie-group image G⊂Pic⁰(Z₀) containing O_Z₀(NA₀).
3. For a Hopf Z₀, pass to a primary finite étale cover. Pic⁰ is the multiplier C*, and restriction to any elliptic curve E₀ is the quotient of that multiplier parameter by the period subgroup. Its differential is nonzero. The effective nonzero divisor A₀ has a multiplier of nonunit absolute value (equivalently nonzero degree against a Gauduchon metric), so G is nonzero. A nonzero connected complex Lie subgroup of the one-dimensional Pic⁰ has full tangent space; the actual restriction G→Pic⁰(E₀) is consequently nonconstant. Finite-cover pullback and trace preserve this conclusion downstairs.
4. For a non-Kähler elliptic Z₀, take the finite cyclic base cover removing multiple fibres. Every effective divisor is vertical: a horizontal curve supplies a multisection and makes the elliptic surface projective, contradicting the odd-first-Betti case. The primary elliptic-bundle Picard description gives a restriction map to the fixed elliptic fibre Picard torus; its identity-component kernel is p*Pic⁰(B). The nonzero effective vertical divisor A₀ has positive rational degree on the base, so O(NA₀) does not belong to that identity component. A connected subgroup G contained in the kernel would lie in its identity component, contradicting this fact. Therefore restriction G→Pic⁰(E₀) is nonconstant. On multiple fibres the finite cyclic cover and trace give the same nonconstancy, with a nonzero isogeny factor.
5. The strict transform E is isomorphic to E₀. If h(E) were a point, every pulled-back line bundle from Y would restrict trivially to E, contradicting step 3 or 4. Its image is therefore a curve. The induced finite map E→C̃ to the normalization of h(E) satisfies Riemann–Hurwitz, so g(C̃)≤1. Genus zero would again make every line bundle pulled back from Y restrict trivially in Pic⁰(E), since it factors through Pic⁰(P¹)=0. Hence g(C̃)=1.
6. An adjacent Enoki component has only rational curve images in its effective numerically trivial interface, by Dloussky–Teleman Remark 2.7: the divisor is a positive multiple of the rational cycle. The common image curve would then have rational normalization. Step 5 gives elliptic normalization on the Hopf/non-Kähler elliptic side. Since both normalizations are the normalization of the same actual interface curve, this is impossible.
