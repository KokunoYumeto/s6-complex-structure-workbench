# The nonplanar three-branch critical curve with delta four

**Proved; P4R.17.** At a nonplanar three-branch point of the entire
reduced critical curve, with generic transverse type \(A_3\) on
every branch and \(\delta(C)=4\), the local defect is strictly
greater than nine. The proof classifies the small local algebras
and uses their actual conormal lattices. It makes no deformation
of the function or of its boundary tacnodes.

Use the notation of P4R.10 and P4.42:
\[
 D(C,f)=\delta(C)+
        \sum_i\left(\frac{d_i}{2}+b_i-r_i\right)+q,\qquad q\ge0.
\]
Here \(d_i\) is the order of the saturated rank-one Hessian,
\(b_i\) is the projected conormal colength, and
\(r_i=m_i-1\) for a branch of multiplicity \(m_i\).

## 1. Bounds without an ordinary-square assumption

Let \(E_i\subset K_i\) be the normalized conormal lattice, with
elementary divisors \((t^{e_1},t^{e_2})\), \(e_1\le e_2\).
For the saturated Hessian line \(L_i\), put
\[
 a_i=\operatorname{length}\bigl(L_i/(L_i\cap E_i)\bigr).
\]
The exact lattice sequence gives \(a_i+b_i=e_1+e_2\).
Every first derivative of \(f\) belongs to the radical ideal
\(P\) of the critical curve. Thus every Hessian column belongs
to \(E_i\). Write the Hessian as \(ut^{d_i}\ell_i^2\), with
\(u\) a unit and \(\ell_i\) primitive. One coordinate of
\(\ell_i\) is a unit; its column gives
\(t^{d_i}\ell_i\in E_i\), so \(d_i\ge a_i\).
Also \(b_i\ge e_1\), since \(E_i\subset t^{e_1}K_i\).
Consequently
\[
                  \frac{d_i}{2}+b_i
                    \ge e_1+\frac{e_2}{2}.           \tag{A}
\]
This remains valid when a symbolic-square term occurs in \(f\).

Since the curve is nonplanar, \(P\) has no linear equation.
Every defining gradient therefore has order at least \(m_i\)
on branch \(i\), and \(b_i\ge m_i\). P4R.15 gives zero
ambient Hessian, so \(f\) has order at least three and
\(d_i\ge m_i\). For total multiplicity \(m=\sum m_i\),
\[
                  D(C,f)\ge\delta(C)+3+\frac m2.       \tag{B}
\]
Thus \(m\ge5\) gives \(D\ge19/2>9\). Only multiplicities
three and four need inspection.

## 2. Three smooth branches: the complete excess-two classification

Put
\[
 B=\bigoplus_{i=1}^3\mathbb C\{t_i\},\quad
 F^j=\bigoplus_{i=1}^3t_i^j\mathbb C\{t_i\},\quad
 A_0=\mathbb C_{\rm diag}+F^1.
\]
The actual ring \(A\subset A_0\) has
\(\operatorname{length}(A_0/A)=2\), since \(A_0\) has
delta invariant two. Each branch projection of \(A\) is onto.

Let \(W_1\subset F^1/F^2=\mathbb C^3\) be its first-jet image.
Each coordinate projection is nonzero. If \(W_1=\mathbb C^3\),
Nakayama first gives \(\mathfrak m_AA_0=F^1\) and then,
on the finite \(A\)-module \(A_0/A\), gives \(A=A_0\).
This contradicts colength two. Thus \(\dim W_1=1\) or \(2\).

### 2a. One-dimensional first jets

Scale the branch parameters so \(W_1=\mathbb C(1,1,1)\).
The two missing first jets use the entire colength, hence
\(F^2\subset A\). The exact algebra is
\[
 A=\mathbb C_{\rm diag}+\mathbb C(t_1,t_2,t_3)+F^2.
\]
It is generated by
\[
 x=(t_1,t_2,t_3),\quad y=(t_1^2,0,0),\quad z=(0,t_2^2,0).
\]
Indeed \(y,z,x^2-y-z\) give all three quadratic branch ideals,
and multiplication by \(x\) gives every higher order. The
embedded model is exactly
\[
       P=\bigl(y(y-x^2),yz,z(z-x^2)\bigr).             \tag{C}
\]
The branches are \((t,t^2,0),(t,0,t^2),(t,0,0)\).
Its differentiated generators give \(E_i=t_i^2K_i\)
on all three branches. For example, on \((t,t^2,0)\)
use the primitive basis \(dy-2t\,dx,dz\).
Formula (A) gives
\[
                         D\ge4+3\cdot3=13.            \tag{D}
\]

### 2b. Two-dimensional first jets

The three branch evaluations are nonzero linear forms on
\(W_1\). If no two are proportional, their squares are
independent: choose the first two as \(s,t\); the third
is \(as+bt\), \(ab\ne0\), whose square supplies the
nonzero \(st\) coefficient. Thus products give the
whole second-jet space. Multiplication by one first jet
nonzero on all branches then gives every later jet.
This would leave only one missing positive-jet dimension,
contrary to colength two.

Two evaluations are therefore proportional. Scale and
label the parameters to obtain \(W_1=\{(a,a,b)\}\).
Its products give the same two-dimensional second-jet
space. The one remaining missing dimension occurs there:
if the second-jet image were full, multiplication by a
nonvanishing first jet would make every later one full.
Hence \(F^3\subset A\), and the pure second-jet image
is exactly \(\{(c,c,d)\}\).

A lift of \(W_1\) has quadratic difference
\(\lambda a+\mu b\). The analytic changes
\(t_i\mapsto t_i+c_it_i^2\), on the first two branches,
remove \(\lambda a\). Thus the exact ring has common
constants, with first and second coefficients satisfying
\[
                     a_1=a_2,\qquad c_1-c_2=\mu a_3, \tag{E}
\]
and arbitrary independent terms of order at least three.

For \(\mu\ne0\), the two elements
\[
 x=(t_1,t_2,0),\qquad y=(\mu t_1^2,0,t_3)
\]
generate this whole ring. The exact identities
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 xy/\mu&=(t_1^3,0,0),\\
 x^3-xy/\mu&=(0,t_2^3,0),\\
 y^3-\mu(xy)^2&=(0,0,t_3^3),\\
 y^2-\mu x^2y&=(0,0,t_3^2)
 \end{aligned}
\]
give the independent cubic ideals and every permitted
quadratic jet. Multiplication by \(x\) or \(y\) gives
all the higher branch series. This ring is planar,
contrary to the present hypothesis.

Thus \(\mu=0\). The ring is generated by
\((t_1,t_2,0),(0,0,t_3),(t_1^3,0,0)\).
Naming the independent transverse coordinate \(z\)
gives the exact embedded model
\[
                       P=(xz,yz,y(y-x^3)).            \tag{F}
\]
Its branches are \((t,0,0),(t,t^3,0),(0,0,t)\), and
its conormal elementary divisors are
\[
                         (1,3),\quad(1,3),\quad(1,1).
\]
On the second branch use \(dy-3t^2dx,dz\); the
gradients give \(t^3(dy-3t^2dx)\) and \(t\,dz\).
Without restricting a symbolic-square coefficient of \(f\),
(A) gives
\[
                  D\ge4+\frac52+\frac52+\frac32
                       =\frac{21}{2}>9.               \tag{G}
\]

These are actual analytic ring isomorphisms. In each
nonplanar case the three displayed generators form a
basis of the curve cotangent space. Lift them to the
ambient local ring. Their linear Jacobian is invertible,
so the inverse-function theorem gives an ambient
coordinate isomorphism to the stated model.

## 3. Multiplicity four: one cusp and two smooth branches

The singular branch has multiplicity two and delta
at least one. Joining three branches costs at least two
further delta units. If the branch delta is two, all
remaining gluing is minimal, and the local ring is
\[
 \mathbb C_{\rm diag}
 +\mathfrak m_{\rm singular}
 +t_2\mathbb C\{t_2\}+t_3\mathbb C\{t_3\}.
\]
Its embedding dimension is at least \(2+1+1=4\),
impossible in a smooth threefold. A larger branch delta
is impossible. Thus this branch is the ordinary cusp
\(\mathbb C\{t^2,t^3\}\).

Set
\[
 A_0=\mathbb C_{\rm diag}
 +\mathfrak m_{\mathbb C\{t^2,t^3\}}
 +t_2\mathbb C\{t_2\}+t_3\mathbb C\{t_3\}.
\]
Now \(A_0\) has delta three, and \(A\subset A_0\)
has colength one. The one-dimensional \(A\)-module
\(A_0/A\) is killed by \(\mathfrak m_A\), so
\(\mathfrak m_AA_0\subset A\). Its elements have
zero constant term, giving
\(\mathfrak m_AA_0=\mathfrak m_A\).
Hence \(\mathfrak m_A\) is an ideal of \(A_0\).
The local algebra \(A_0/\mathfrak m_A\) has length
two, so its maximal ideal squares to zero. Therefore
\[
                         \mathfrak m_0^2\subset A.     \tag{H}
\]

The four cotangent coordinates of \(A_0\), denoted
\(a,b,c,d\), are represented by the cusp functions
\(t^2,t^3\) and the two smooth parameters. The
three-dimensional subspace
\(W=\mathfrak m_A/\mathfrak m_0^2\) is a hyperplane
\[
                 \lambda a+\mu b+\nu c+\xi d=0.       \tag{I}
\]
Its projection to \(a,b\) is onto, since the actual
branch image is the full cusp ring, and the two smooth
projections are nonzero. Ambient embedding dimension
three gives \(\mathfrak m_A^2=\mathfrak m_0^2\).
Thus products of \(W\) must generate
\(\mathfrak m_0^2/\mathfrak m_0^3\), whose coordinates are
\[
                              a^2,\quad ab,\quad c^2,\quad d^2.
                                                               \tag{J}
\]

### 3a. A nonzero coefficient of the cusp cubic

For \(\mu\ne0\), solve (I) for \(b\). Surjectivity
of the cusp projection forces \((\nu,\xi)\ne(0,0)\).
The three minimal ambient coordinate functions have
parametrizations
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{cusp}&(t^2-\lambda't^3,-\nu't^3,-\xi't^3)\\
 \text{second branch}&(0,t,0)\\
 \text{third branch}&(0,0,t).
 \end{array}
\]
Their tangent directions are independent.

By P4R.15, \(f\in\mathfrak m^3\). Let \(G_3\)
be its homogeneous cubic part. Since \(df\) vanishes
on all three branches, \(dG_3\) vanishes on each
independent tangent axis. Every cubic monomial other
than \(xyz\) has a derivative nonzero on an axis.
Hence \(G_3=cxyz\). On the \(x\)-axis its normal
Hessian is
\[
                         \begin{pmatrix}0&c\\c&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A nonzero \(c\) gives a nonzero leading minor on
the actual branch, contradicting generic rank one.
Thus \(c=0\), and \(f\in\mathfrak m^4\).

The Hessian entries have order at least \(2m_i\)
on branch \(i\), and \(b_i\ge m_i\).
Its defect contribution is at least \(m_i+1\).
Therefore
\[
                            D\ge4+(4+3)=11>9.         \tag{K}
\]

### 3b. A zero coefficient of the cusp cubic

For \(\mu=0\), surjectivity in (J) forces
\(\lambda,\nu,\xi\) all to be nonzero. Otherwise
two of \(a,c,d\) are proportional on \(W\), so
their squares are dependent, and (J) has rank at
most three. That creates an extra minimal generator,
contrary to embedding dimension three.
Scale the branch parameters so that \(a+c+d=0\).

The pure hyperplane generators belong to \(A\)
exactly by (H). They give the parametrizations
\[
                       (t^2,t^2,t^3),\quad(t,0,0),\quad(0,t,0).
\]
Their defining ideal is
\[
                 P=(z(x-y),xy(x-y),z^2-x^2y).        \tag{L}
\]
A Hilbert--Burch matrix is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 xy&z\\
 -z&-x\\
 0&-(x-y)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its maximal minors give (L), with alternating signs.
The ideal has codimension two and is Cohen--Macaulay.
Its zero set is exactly the cusp and the two axes,
and it is generically reduced on each branch.
A nonzero nilpotent quotient supported at the origin
would violate depth one, so it is the actual reduced
intersection ideal.

On the cusp use the primitive conormal basis
\[
                     k_1=dx-dy,\qquad k_2=3t\,dx-2dz.
\]
The differentiated generators are
\(t^3k_1,t^4k_1,t^3(tk_1-k_2)\).
Thus its elementary divisors are \((3,3)\).
On each smooth axis they are \((1,2)\).
Here \(r=1\) on the cusp and zero on the axes.
By (A),
\[
                  D\ge4+\left(\frac92-1\right)+2+2
                       =\frac{23}{2}>9.               \tag{M}
\]

This exhausts multiplicity four. Together with
Sections 1--2 it proves the strict local bound
for every nonplanar three-branch curve with delta four.
Only actual coordinate isomorphisms, conormal maps,
and the rank-one Hessian were used; no boundary
deformation has been introduced.

## 4. Every nonordinary triple exceeds the budget

For a planar three-branch point, P4.48 gives defect at least
ten, including the ordinary plane triple point. At a nonplanar
point with delta at least five, (B) and \(m\ge3\) give
\[
                  D\ge5+3+\frac32=\frac{19}{2}>9.
\]
Delta four was exhausted above.

At delta three the excess \(\delta-(r-1)\) is one.
The actual analytic ring calculation in P4.40 gives precisely
an ordinary plane triple, a plane tacnode with an independent
transverse line, or a cusp with two independent lines. The
first is excluded by P4.48. The second has defect at least
eleven by P4R.16. The third has embedding dimension four
and cannot occur in the ambient smooth threefold.

Finally a three-branch point with delta two has zero excess.
Its ring equals \(\mathbb C_{\rm diag}+F^1\), generated by
the independent branch parameters. It is exactly the three
coordinate axes, with ideal \((xy,xz,yz)\).
Thus every nonordinary three-branch point is excluded from
the total defect budget nine. The only remaining local
possibility is the ordinary nonplanar triple treated by
P4.48 and the separate full-quartic conductor calculation.
