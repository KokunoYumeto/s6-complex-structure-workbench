Proof. 1. Flatness and the smoothness of the fibres over V0 imply that π is a submersion there:
the local analytic smoothness criterion applies because the base is smooth. The relative
nonsmooth locus is a closed analytic subset, by the Jacobian criterion. Its proper image is
analytic by the coherent direct-image argument in Theorem 18.3, applied to the structure
sheaf of this proper analytic locus. It misses V0, hence is discrete. We remove it and work over
the connected remaining curve V ′. Removing a discrete subset from a connected Riemann
surface does not disconnect it. Thus X ′ →V ′ is a smooth proper map.

The last term denotes elements annihilated by t. The support of the torsion subsheaf of the
coherent module R1π∗L is closed discrete, by the structure theorem for finitely generated
modules over the local discrete valuation rings of the smooth base curve. Outside that support
the canonical base-change map is an isomorphism. Also F is torsion-free: multiplication by a
nonzero base function is injective on sections of L. It is therefore locally free. Its rank is one
by choosing an interior point outside the discrete torsion support. Locally a frame gives a
relative section σ of L, nowhere identically zero on a fibre. Its divisor is independent of the
frame. Let D be its reduced support. On V0, the divisor is 2Sv and D is exactly Sv.
3. The map D →V ′ is proper, since D is closed in a proper space. There is no vertical divisor
component: a vertical divisor in the smooth total space would contain a whole irreducible
fibre, contrary to the nonzero restriction of σ. Every horizontal component has proper image
all of the connected base. Since the interior fibre is irreducible, there is exactly one horizontal
component, and its multiplicity in div σ is two. Thus div σ = 2D on this base. In the smooth
ambient space D is Cartier. It is flat over the curve because its local rings are torsion-free
over the base discrete valuation ring. Restriction of the Cartier identity to an interior fibre
gives 2Dv = 2Sv, whence Dv = Sv with its reduced structure. Flatness and the smoothness of
these fibres make D →V ′ smooth over V0. Its analytic nonsmooth locus therefore misses V0;
the proper mapping theorem once more makes its image discrete. After deleting that image,
D →V good is a smooth proper family of connected surfaces. Each deletion is closed discrete
in the then-current open curve. Their finite union is at most countable, and the final good
locus is open.
4. Ehresmann’s theorem identifies the smooth fibres along every path; in particular their first
Betti number is two. (Buchdahl, 1999, Theorem 11, p. 297) then gives a Kähler metric on
every fibre. Hence h1,0 = 1, every holomorphic one-form is closed, and the integral period
map has a rank-two lattice image. Constant h1,0, properness, and analytic base change
supply a local relative holomorphic one-form. Locally on a simply connected base disc, a
relative one-form and a basis of the free part of H1 give two holomorphic periods. Their ratio
lies in one half-plane after orienting the basis. To see holomorphicity directly, choose local
primitives of the closed relative form on simply connected fibre charts; their differences are
holomorphic functions of the base, and the finite sum around a transported cycle is its period.
The modular invariant of the ratio is independent of all choices, so these local invariants glue
to a holomorphic function on V good.
