Theorem 11.11 (All compact curves). [tag 01R0]
The following descriptions give all compact irreducible curves of Xc. In Wc and Sj they are
exactly the images described in 11.10. In a smooth fibre of algebraic dimension zero there are
none. In a smooth fibre of algebraic dimension one they are exactly the translates of its unique
elliptic kernel Kt. In an abelian fibre they are exactly the translates of elliptic subtori, together
with the irreducible zero divisors of the positive Appell–Humbert theta sections described below.
These descriptions, 11.7, and 11.9 constitute a classification including every smooth exceptional
fibre and every compact irreducible surface.
Proof. 1. All curves are vertical by 11.9, so the list of fibre types is exhaustive. The dimension-
zero and dimension-one assertions, including uniqueness, were proved in 11.7.
Let now
Ft = C2/ΠΛ be abelian. For every positive definite Hermitian form H whose imaginary part
takes integer values on ΠΛ, and every function α : ΠΛ →U(1) satisfying
α(λ + ν) = α(λ)α(ν)eπi Im H(λ,ν),
take the nonzero entire solutions θ : C2 →C of
θ(ζ + λ) = α(λ)eπH(ζ,λ)+πH(λ,λ)/2θ(ζ).
(11.2)
The cocycle identity follows by multiplying the displayed factors; its integral imaginary
cross-term has exponential one. It defines a holomorphic line bundle by quotienting C2 × C.
The invariant zero set of θ descends to a compact divisor on Ft. Retain precisely those zero
divisors that are reduced and irreducible. This is an exact parametrization, with all periods
and multiplier equations given; multiplying θ by a nonzero scalar does not change its divisor.
2. The correspondence (Schnell, 2019, Theorem 4.1, p. 1, and equation (4.3), p. 2) proves that
every holomorphic line bundle and every section on this quotient has this form. For the
cohomology matrix the exact comparison is HE = eE( · , J · ) −i eE from Definition 11.4; we
choose the sign of E making HE positive. Its imaginary part is −eE, which gives the integral
semicharacter equation displayed above. The effective line-bundle criterion (Schnell, 2019,
pp. 2–3) shows that a divisor class with positive Hermitian rank two gives exactly these
positive theta sections. A divisor of Hermitian rank one is pulled back from its elliptic quotient
and, when irreducible and reduced, is a single fibre. Thus it is a translate of an elliptic
subtorus. The null lattice is computed by the explicit integer kernel in 11.7. No other rank is
possible for an effective nonempty divisor, by positivity of its averaged current. This proves
exhaustiveness on the abelian fibre.
3. The same equations give the curves in the product covers of Sj: compute them on its two-
elliptic-curve lattice and take images under the specified finite morphism. Conversely each
curve downstairs is the image of a component of its finite inverse image. In Wc the analogous
procedure uses the homogeneous polynomial parametrization of the normalization in 11.10,
with the three boundary pairings already identified. The maps used throughout are finite or
closed embeddings, so they preserve compactness and give one-dimensional images.
