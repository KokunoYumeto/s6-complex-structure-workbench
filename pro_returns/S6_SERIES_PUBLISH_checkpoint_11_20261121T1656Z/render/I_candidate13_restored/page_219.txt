Vx = {x = z = 0},

The V branches are vertical over F, and the H branches lie over the strict transform of r = 0.
On Vx, the two maps in (22.5) are
(Q, s) 7−→(y, s) = (Qs, s),
(X, y) 7−→(y, s) = (y, Xy).
These are exactly the blow-up charts of (y, s). Thus Vx is blown up along its elliptic curve
over y = s = 0; Vy is unchanged. Similarly Hy is blown up along the corresponding elliptic
curve and Hx is unchanged. Each even vertical component is therefore blown up at its two
disjoint conductor curves, whereas each odd vertical component is unchanged. The horizontal
parities are reversed.

8. Over every base chart the glued space is a copy of the proper resolved pullback. It is Hausdorff:
two points over the same base point are separated in such a chart, and points over distinct
base points are separated in the base. Properness is local on the target, and composition with
the proper β proves properness over the original bidisc. The canonical divisor section of M is
nonzero off F and trivializes the twist there. The base blow-up and the resolutions are also
isomorphisms off the origin. This gives the specified isomorphism with the original family
there, in particular over every r ̸= 0.
9. The construction can be inserted into any larger model which is standard near q0: the preceding
trivialization supplies the overlap map outside the origin. The cocharacter u is defined on this
disc. Since the local rings of the total space are regular and r is a nonzero divisor, they are
torsion-free, hence flat, over the one-dimensional regular parameter ring. This proves flatness
over r. The new vertical threefolds rule out joint flatness over the bidisc. Properness over the
r-disc alone follows only when this local replacement is inserted into a model with compact
elliptic base.
