Proof. 1. We first give the comparison that turns the local calculation into a computation of
the sheaves. On a simply connected disc of smooth torus fibres, including a finite root cover,
retain the real marking and the holomorphic period matrix. Translation by Π(s)v, for fixed
v ∈R4, is holomorphic on the total family. After subtracting the integer cocycle of a resonance,
the line bundle has constant lattice multipliers. Multiplying the lifted real translation by
its constant factor of automorphy makes the lattice act as the identity and gives a genuine
compact real torus action on the line bundle. The same action acts on the tangent bundle by
differentiation. It commutes with the full Dolbeault differential.
2. Averaging over this compact torus projects the relative Dolbeault complex onto its invariant
part. Choose the lift so that the central trivial line has trivial character. On every nonzero
Fourier character, the relative Dolbeault symbol is exterior multiplication by a nonzero
covector; contraction by a dual covector is a contracting homotopy. After shrinking the disc,
these nonzero covectors have a uniform positive lower bound for bounded Fourier frequency
and grow linearly for unbounded frequency, because the real period matrices and their inverses
stay bounded. The homotopies therefore preserve smooth Fourier series, with all derivatives,
and are uniform on compact subsets of the disc. The tangent extension is triangular with
respect to its vertical and horizontal parts; its nilpotent off-diagonal term is treated by the
finite homological perturbation series, so every nonzero Fourier complex is still contractible.
These homotopies exhibit averaging as a quasi-isomorphism.
