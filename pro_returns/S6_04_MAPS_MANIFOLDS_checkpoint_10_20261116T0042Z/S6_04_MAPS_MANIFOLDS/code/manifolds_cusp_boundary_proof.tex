Put $C=-H+E_1$ and index the six toric curves cyclically by
$C_0=E_1$, $C_1=H-E_1-E_2$, $C_2=E_2$,
$C_3=H-E_2-E_3$, $C_4=E_3$, $C_5=H-E_1-E_3$.
The pair $0,3$ is self-glued in the $u$ direction, whereas
pairs $1,2$ and $4,5$ meet the adjacent gamma translates.
Since $C_1+C_2=C_4+C_5=H-E_1=-C$, and the total central
fibre divisor is principal, the restriction of $D_i$ to its
own normalization is $2C$, and its two neighboring
restrictions are $-C$. In the free kernel generated by $C$
the equivariant restriction coefficient is therefore exactly
$2-h-h^{-1}=-h^{-1}(h-1)^2$. Reversing the deck generator
leaves this coefficient unchanged.

For the boundary cover use the Euler-one circle bundle
$\mathrm{Nil}^3(u,w,\theta)$ over the $(u,\theta)$ torus,
with central fibre $w$, and take its product with $S^1_\delta$.
On integral one-forms the deck action is
$\delta\mapsto\delta+\theta$. On loops this means
$\theta\mapsto\theta+\delta$; it preserves
$[\theta,u]=w$ and the centrality of $\delta$.
The integral Gysin sequence gives cohomology ranks
$(1,3,4,3,1)$, with degree-two basis
$uw,w\theta,u\delta,\theta\delta$ and degree-three basis
$uw\theta,uw\delta,w\theta\delta$. The class $u\theta$
is zero because the bundle Euler number is one. Thus the
deck action is the identity in degree two, and has one
unit Jordan block of length two in degrees one and three.
Dualizing the free integral groups gives the same homology
modules: $A/(h-1)^2\oplus A/(h-1)$ in degrees one and
three, $(A/(h-1))^4$ in degree two, and $A/(h-1)$ in
degrees zero and four, for $A=\mathbb Z[h,h^{-1}]$.
The exact exterior differential and all integral matrices
are checked in \texttt{manifolds\_cusp\_boundary.py}.
