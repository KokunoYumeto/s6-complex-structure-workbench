1.1    Statement
The input explicitly lists a DAG G = (V, E), n = |V | ≥ 1 jobs, and binary integers m ≥ 3, T . A
schedule is a map τ : V → {1, . . . , T } with at most m jobs per slot and τ (u) < τ (v) on every arc.
The makespan is max τ . The canonical encoding and its bit length L are specified in Section 9.
Cited result 1 (OpenAI, [P, Theorem 1.1]). For m = 3, deadline decision and witness construction
for 1 ≤ T ≤ n, and optimum-schedule construction, take polynomial deterministic multitape time
in the explicit input length.

1.4   Proof map
Idea. Pad to full m-job slots and enumerate bounded Boolean descriptions of job sets. A state
path joins smaller accepted sets through single slots. Separators split a feasible schedule into a
hierarchy. The map of Lemma 5.5 sends surviving injection entries injectively into one boundary
slot; the restricted equalities of Lemma 6.1 keep their descriptions bounded through direction
changes.
   1. Foundations, finite search and its certificate: Lemmas 2.1–2.7, Proposition 2.8, Definition
       2.9 and Lemma 2.10.
   2. Exchanges, separators and walks: Lemmas 3.1–3.6 and Corollary 3.7.
   3. Centers, sides and child labels: Lemmas 4.1–4.5.
   4. Carried lists and the capacity injection: Lemmas 5.1–5.5.
   5. Direction changes and formula counts: Lemmas 6.1–6.5.
   6. Exterior invariants and induction: Lemmas 7.1–7.2 and Proposition 7.3.
   7. Global child identities, prefixes and the budget: Lemmas 8.1–8.4 and Theorem 8.5.
   8. Correctness and itemized multitape costs: Theorem 9.1, Proposition 9.2 and Corollary 9.3;
       compact decision input: Proposition 9.4.
Hard steps. Lemma 5.5 proves the m-entry injection bound; Lemmas 6.2 and 6.5 prevent
leaf counts from growing with depth; Lemma 7.1 propagates exterior qualifiers through the first
advance using (I3). Their independent derivations are in the current audit. The constants are

2      Foundations, descriptions, and the finite search
Conventions. All job sets are finite. Maps between job sets, integer intervals, keys, bit vectors
and formulas below are ordinary functions of sets; order-preservation is asserted and proved when
needed. All restrictions of precedence use the transitive order on the full job set, not merely the
arcs induced by a smaller set.
Lemma 2.1 (reachability and a rank order).
    Let G = (V, E) be a finite directed acyclic graph. Define x ≺ y if there is a directed path of
positive length from x to y.

    1. The relation ≺ is irreflexive and transitive.
    2. For a function τ : V → Z, respecting every arc strictly is equivalent to respecting every pair
       x ≺ y strictly.
    3. Repeatedly removing a vertex with no remaining predecessor produces a total order extending
       ≺. With the smallest identifier as a tie-breaker this order is deterministic.

Uses: definitions only.

Proof. Step 1 (reachability is a strict order). Transitivity follows by concatenating paths; a
positive-length path from a vertex to itself would be a directed cycle. [acyclicity and the path
definition]
Step 2 (arc and reachability inequalities agree). Strict inequalities along a path imply the inequality
between its endpoints, and every arc is a path. [the path definition; transitivity of strict integer
inequalities]
Step 3 (a deterministic extending order exists). For the last claim, if a finite nonempty remaining
set had no minimal element, following predecessors within it would eventually repeat a vertex and
give a cycle. Thus a minimal element always exists. If x ≺ y, vertex y cannot be removed while x
remains. The removal order therefore extends ≺. [finite acyclicity; the least-identifier rule]

    The reachability relation can be computed by a search from each vertex. For a padded set J
of N jobs, write
                                   λ : J −→ [N ] = {1, . . . , N }
for the bijection sending a job to its position in this deterministic total order. It is strictly
order-preserving: x ≺ y implies λ(x) < λ(y).
Lemma 2.2 (integer start times).
    Suppose a finite collection of unit jobs has nonnegative real start times and a machine-
assignment function µ : V → [m], with no overlap on a machine and with every precedence
completed before its successor starts. There is a feasible integer-slot schedule whose makespan is
no larger.
Uses: Lemma 2.1; definitions of a unit-job schedule and machine assignment.



                                                   7
Proof. Step 1 (the augmented graph is acyclic). On each machine, order the jobs by their original
start times and add an arc from each job to the next. Each original or added arc increases
the original start time by at least one, so the augmented graph is acyclic. [unit processing times;
feasibility of the original schedule]
Step 2 (the recurrence gives integer starts no later than the old starts). In a topological order of
it, define

         s′ (v) = max {0} ∪ {s′ (u) + 1 : u is an augmented immediate predecessor of v} .
                                                                                           


This defines a function s′ : V → Z≥0 . For the induction on position in the topological order,
a vertex with no incoming arc has s′ = 0 ≤ s; assume s′ (u) ≤ s(u) for earlier vertices. The
recurrence then gives s′ (v) ≤ s(v), since every term in the maximum is at most the original s(v).
[Lemma 2.1; induction in the augmented topological order]
Step 3 (the new schedule is feasible). Added machine-order arcs prevent overlap; original arcs
enforce precedence. Setting τ (v) = s′ (v) + 1 gives the required slot schedule, with completion time
at most the original completion time for each job. Nothing depends on m = 3. [the augmented arcs
and the recurrence]

Lemma 2.3 (padding).
    For a deadline 1 ≤ T ≤ n, reject if n > mT . Otherwise add mT − n distinct isolated jobs. Let
J be the resulting set and N = mT ≤ mn. A full schedule of W ⊆ J is a finite sequence of
m-element antichains partitioning W , with x ≺ y implying that the slot of x occurs strictly earlier
than that of y. The empty sequence is a full schedule of the empty set. The original deadline is
feasible if and only if J has a full schedule.
Uses: definitions only.

Proof. Step 1 (padding gives a full schedule). A deadline-feasible original schedule leaves exactly
mT − n positions among the m machines and T slots. Fill those positions with the distinct isolated
jobs. [the deadline schedule and isolated-job definition]
Step 2 (restriction gives the original deadline schedule). Conversely, deleting isolated padding
jobs from a full schedule leaves at most m original jobs in each slot and preserves all original
inequalities. Since N ≤ mn, for fixed m the padded instance has polynomial size. [full-schedule
definition; T ≤ n]

Lemma 2.4 (open-interval replacement).
   In a full schedule of J, let W consist of all jobs in the slots strictly between two boundary
positions a < b, where a boundary can be the virtual position before the schedule or after it.
Replacing these slots by any full schedule of W preserves a full schedule of J.
Uses: definitions only.

Proof. Step 1 (the replacement occupies the same interval). Every full schedule of W has exactly
|W |/m slots, so it fits the same positions. [the definition of a full schedule]
Step 2 (every precedence pair is respected). Relations with both endpoints in W are respected by
the replacement, and those with neither endpoint in W are unchanged. An exterior job has a slot
before the entire interval or after the entire interval. Its temporal relation with every interior job

3.1      Normalization
A priority order on U is a total order extending its precedence restriction. Let π : U → [|U |]
be the bijection giving its positions, smaller meaning better priority. Encode a full schedule of
U by sorting each slot increasingly by π and concatenating the resulting m-tuples in time order.
A normalized full schedule minimizes this finite integer sequence lexicographically among all
full schedules of U . Such a schedule exists whenever U is schedulable. Normalized schedules are
existence witnesses, not schedules that the finite search must compute.
Lemma 3.3 (no improving exchange, including suffix rearrangements).
    Let a normalized schedule of U and one of its slots r be fixed. Replace the slots strictly after r
by any full schedule of the same suffix job set, keeping a full schedule of U . A job y at r cannot
then be exchanged with a later job x satisfying π(x) < π(y) so that a full schedule of U results.
Uses: the definition of a normalized schedule (Section 3.1).

Proof. Suppose such an exchange of y with x gives a full schedule Σ′ of U . Write Σ for the
normalized schedule.
Step 1 (Σ′ and Σ agree before r). The replacement changes only slots strictly after r, and the
exchange changes only the slot r and one later slot. [hypotheses of the lemma]
Step 2 (the sorted m-tuple of slot r decreases lexicographically). At r the set of priority values
changes by replacing π(y) with the smaller value π(x), which is distinct from all values at r. For
every threshold q the number of values at most q does not decrease, so every order statistic of the
m values weakly decreases. The sum of the m values decreases strictly, so some order statistic
decreases strictly. Hence the first coordinate in which the two sorted m-tuples differ is smaller in
Σ′ . [π is a bijection; π(x) < π(y)]
Step 3 (contradiction). By Steps 1 and 2 the encoded sequence of Σ′ is lexicographically smaller
than that of Σ, against the minimality that defines a normalized schedule. [definition of normalized,
Section 3.1]
Where m enters: nowhere beyond the size of a slot. The argument holds for every m ≥ 1 and uses
no exchange involving the other m − 1 jobs of the slot.

   An exchange in all following proofs swaps two jobs between their slots. It preserves the size of
both slots. Feasibility will always be checked through predecessors and successors of those two
jobs; all other pairs retain their order.

9    Correctness and running time
Theorem 9.1 (correctness of the actual finite algorithm). For m ≥ 3, run the dynamic program
of Section 2 with m-element slots and K = Km . It accepts the padded set J exactly when J has a
full schedule, and returns a full schedule whenever it accepts. Consequently it decides the original
deadline and constructs an optimum by testing deadlines.
Uses: Lemmas 2.1, 2.2, 2.3 and 2.10; Proposition 2.8; Theorem 8.5.

Proof. Step 1 (the finite search decides the padded instance [Proposition 2.8; Lemmas 2.3 and

gaps to an accepting state path of the same finite search. Lemma 2.3 proves both directions of
padding and its removal.
Step 2 (elementary deadlines are decided without large padding [Lemmas 2.1 and 2.3]). For a
nonempty instance reject T < 1. For T ≥ n, the topological order of Lemma 2.1 schedules one

increasing order to optimize. Deadline n is feasible, and the first accepted deadline is the minimum.

original jobs in a slot to distinct machines in increasing identifier order.
