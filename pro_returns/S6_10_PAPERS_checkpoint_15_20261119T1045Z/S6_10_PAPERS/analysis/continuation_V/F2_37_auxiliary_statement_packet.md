## 1. Free finite affine lifts

For any finite-order integral matrix M in GL_n(Z), prove that it admits a free finite cyclic affine lift on R^n/Z^n exactly when ker(M-I) is nonzero. For order d>1 and a primitive vector u in ker(M-I) intersect Z^n, the specified proposed lift is g(x)=Mx+u/d^2. Establish its actual order and freeness of every nonidentity power. For M=I the specified lift is translation by a primitive half-vector. The invariant lattice itself is the actual integral kernel, with no saturation change allowed. No geometric gluing theorem is requested.

## 2. Identity deletion in the marked canonical algorithm

An ordered k=3 integral tuple is (M1,M2,M3,M0), with M0=(M1M2M3)^(-1), each Mi finite order for i>=1, and M0-I square-zero of rank3. Its height is the largest absolute entry. Its order for canonicalization is height first, then row-major matrix entries in the fixed displayed marked order; matrices are simultaneously conjugated by P in GL6(Z).

Establish that there is at most one identity among the finite Mi. For a fixed identity position, delete that slot, take the canonical representative of the remaining ordered two-finite-point tuple with its returned conjugator, and reinsert I at the original slot. Show this is a canonical representative in that sector and that the exact same conjugator works. Establish that height and lex comparison are preserved by this deletion/insertion, so it agrees with direct least-height/lex enumeration whenever that branch is used. If the reduced pair is the special two-involution branch, use its already proved F2.38 canonical convention and actual witness instead; show the reinsertion convention remains canonical. The affine order at an identity-linear site is separate supplied gluing data and is not discarded by the linear matrix operation.
