Noncompletion of the prescribed partial filling
Proposition 22.54 (Noncompletion of the fixed partial filling). [tag 0310]
The partial filling of 22.41 has no proper Hausdorff complex-analytic completion over its
missing base point. This remains true after finite coordinate roots of the two base coordinates
and allows arbitrary additional three-dimensional components of the origin fibre.
Proof. 1. Every finite coordinate-root cover is dominated by one with q = Q12m and r = Rn,
where m, n > 0; proper pullback reduces to this case. Suppose such a completion exists.
Retain and normalize its reduced dominating component, resolve it, and principalize the
inverse image of the two axes, as in the proof of Theorem 18.6. These proper modifications
preserve the given family on the complement of the axes and the smooth open parts of the
two prescribed axial models. The resulting proper map to the bidisc has connected fibres: its
finite Stein factor is generically one sheet and the bidisc is normal.
2. We identify the axial markings with the standard specialization of Theorem 18.2, namely
(l0, l1, l2) = (0, 1, −1) and a = b = 1. In the notation of Proposition 22.46, one has U = −12κ0.
The two origins in Theorem 18.2 equal the two origins there plus 6ve, where e = Πcbδ = (0, 1)t.
Translation by 6ve acts on the character W as W 7→r−6W. It shifts the twelve-chain index
by six and extends, with its inverse, across the parameter axis; along the cusp axis r ̸= 0 it is
multiplication by a holomorphic unit. Thus this is an explicit comparison of the prescribed
axial models. It changes meridian labels only by multiples of bδ and leaves their bγ coefficients
unchanged.
3. In the commuting lifts of Theorem 18.6, an axis component has orders (αi, βi) and bγ meridian
coefficient κi. The same group calculation gives
(mαi + nβi)κj = (mαj + nβj)κi
for intersecting components. Every denominator mαi+nβi is positive, including for exceptional
components over the origin. Hence the ratio κi/(mαi + nβi) is constant on the incidence
graph of components meeting the corner.
4. The prescribed parameter-axis model has an extending ordinary origin. Its positive parameter
loop has zero lattice label in that origin, so the strict parameter component has ratio zero. The
prescribed cusp-axis origin has ratio a = 1, by the exact covered origin formulas (18.4)–(18.5)
and the commuting lift Qo = T−mbγQ. Both strict components meet the corner by properness.
The compact connected corner fibre makes the incidence graph connected, exactly as in the
proof of Theorem 18.6. Its ratios would therefore be both zero and one. This contradiction
proves noncompletion for every pair of positive root degrees and for all additional corner
components.
22.16
Global elliptic monodromy constrains every central meridian
Proposition 22.55 (The global meridians have only the prescribed toric direction). [tag 0311]
Suppose g : Z →∆r is any proper complex-analytic filling of a finite parameter cover of
the six-sphere family. Retain its reduced main component, normalize and resolve it, extend its
intrinsically marked map to P1 by 18.3, resolve the graph, and resolve the support of its central
divisor. Denote the resulting smooth proper modification by eZ, and its holomorphic marked map
by h. All modifications can be chosen to be isomorphisms over r ̸= 0. The central support has
normal crossings; its multiplicities need not be one.
