At denominator n the real group has O(n2) sample points, and through denominator N it has

Verifying the sufficient inequality for these grids requires bounds sharper than these envelopes.

(b) For the Mahler system in (Adamczewski and Faverjon, 2017, Theorem 1.1 and system (1.2),
p. 56), the needed algebraic Taylor coefficients and algebraic regular argument can be tested
directly: the coefficient of z3 in g is −δb/2, and b = s2 + k is transcendental by 26.19. The
logarithm u is transcendental too: an algebraic u ̸= 0 would give an algebraic point (u, Q) on
the diagonal Lie line of Ga × E; the analytic subgroup theorem would produce an algebraic
one-dimensional subgroup projecting nontrivially to both factors, which is impossible since an
elliptic curve has no nonconstant map to Ga and Ga has no nonzero group map to E. The
duplication identity acts by the explicit elliptic multiplication map. The entropy identity in
(Einsiedler et al., 2001, Theorem 5.1, author preprint, p. 9) uses the sequence built from the
denominators of x(2nQ) with scale 4n.
(c) Finally the period-refined rank comparison (Waldschmidt, 1988a, Theorem 4.1, pp. 385–386,
and Corollary 4.3, p. 386) (Roy and Waldschmidt, 1993, main theorem, p. 363) has coefficient
α(G) = 2 dim G −2d0(G) −d1(G), where the last terms count additive and multiplicative
quotients. Both vanish for GQ. An additive homomorphism vanishes on its torus and then
on the proper quotient E; a nonzero torus character extending to GQ would kill a nonzero
integer multiple of its nontorsion extension class. Thus α(GQ) = 4.
(d) The line ℓc has period rank one. A general period is (ω, ζ(u)ω −η(ω)u + 2πij). Writing
ω = aω1 + dω2, membership in ℓc gives du/ω1 ∈Z by Legendre’s relation. Since u/ω1 is
irrational, d = j = 0. The numerical rank bound in the cited theorem is (4−1) dim ℓc = 3. The
period and the logarithm (u, cu) are independent over Q and span rank two; the algebraicity
of the latter’s exponential remains exactly the point-algebraicity question in 26.14.
