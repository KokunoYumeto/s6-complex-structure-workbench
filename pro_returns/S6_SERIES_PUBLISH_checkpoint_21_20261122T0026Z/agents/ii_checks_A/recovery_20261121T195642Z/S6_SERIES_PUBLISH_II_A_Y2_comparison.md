A reduced local hypersurface branch admits a finite Weierstrass projection onto a small two-dimensional polydisk. Off its discriminant the roots form an unramified cover. A decomposition of that cover into two components would split the roots into monodromy-invariant subsets. The corresponding elementary symmetric functions are bounded holomorphic functions off the discriminant and extend across it by the removable-singularity theorem; they produce a nontrivial factorization of the irreducible Weierstrass polynomial. Therefore the cover of an irreducible local branch is connected. It is dense in the regular part of that branch, and every regular point joins it in a small coordinate neighborhood.
