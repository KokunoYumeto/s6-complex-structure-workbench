## 1. Exact bundles, conductor and adjunction map

Let Y be the smooth compact complex threefold with H²(Y,Q)=0, S its reduced divisor, and D the reduced irreducible compact lci curve in Sing(S), as in the statement. Put ν:C→D for normalization, I=I_D/Y, N=(I/I²)^∨ and L=O_Y(S)|D. Let E=ν*N and let Δ be the effective divisor on C whose ideal O_C(−Δ) is the conductor of O_D⊂ν_*O_C. Write g=g(C) and c=deg Δ.

The lci adjunction isomorphism is ω_D≅(K_Y|D)⊗det N. Its residue map in local generators f_1,f_2 of I sends a local ambient three-form divided by f_1f_2 to the dualizing differential on D. Finite duality gives

    Hom_O_D(ν_*O_C,ω_D)=ν_*ω_C.

Since ω_D is invertible, Hom_O_D(ν_*O_C,O_D) is the conductor: a multiplication element q with q·O_C⊂O_D lies in O_D by applying it to 1, and this is precisely the conductor condition. Therefore the displayed duality isomorphism pulls back to

    ω_C ≅ ν*ω_D ⊗ O_C(−Δ),
    det E ≅ ω_C(Δ)⊗ν*(K_Y|D)⁻¹.

All ambient line bundles have zero rational first Chern class on C. In particular deg ν*L=deg ν*(K_Y|D)=0, and

    deg E = 2g−2+c.                                      (1)

The divisor Δ vanishes exactly when O_D=ν_*O_C: conductor equal to O_C contains 1, which gives the reverse inclusion. In that case D≅C is smooth.

The powers I^k have no embedded zero-dimensional associated components. Indeed I is generated by a regular sequence in the regular three-dimensional ambient ring; its associated graded algebra is Sym_(O_D)(I/I²), and the usual regular-sequence resolution of its powers gives a one-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay quotient. A germ vanishing to order k at every generic branch of D consequently lies in I^k. Thus the ordinary cusp/tacnode hypothesis puts a local defining equation F of S in I², while the ordinary-triple hypothesis puts F in I³. This passage includes an analytically multibranched local ring of the globally irreducible D.

## 2. Rank-one quadratic jet and its line-bundle degree

In the cusp and tacnode cases the quadratic class is a generically nonzero rank-one section

    q ∈ H⁰(C,Sym²E*⊗ν*L).

Let K⊂E be its saturated radical and M=E/K. Both are line bundles on the smooth C, and q factors as a nonzero holomorphic section of M⁻²⊗ν*L. Put a=deg K and b=deg M. Therefore

    a+b=2g−2+c,             b≤0.                         (2)

The extension E need not split globally. All subsequent constructions use local splittings and their invariant characters; their resulting sections glue on the dense smooth part of D and therefore define unique meromorphic sections on C.

Here is a precise local choice at a possibly singular point of D. Let A=O_D,p and B be its finite normalization, a product of one-dimensional regular local rings for its analytic branches. Choose generators f,g of I and change them by a constant invertible matrix so that the covector defining the rank-one quadratic has its first component nonzero at every branch point. This is possible because only finitely many projective directions are involved. In B the saturated covector can then be written ℓ=X+λY, with λ∈B, and

    q=α(X+λY)²,   α=q_11∈A,
    αλ=q_12∈A,   αλ²=q_22∈A.

The scalar α may vanish; no inverse of α is used in the cubic construction. Choose a generator h∈B for the conductor ideal on this semilocal normalization. Since the conductor is an ideal of B contained in A, h and hλ lie in A. Lift h,hλ,α to holomorphic ambient germs H,U,A_0 and put

    Z=Hf+Ug,      G=H²F−A_0 Z².

The leading quadratic terms agree in B, hence in A; therefore G∈I³. The class G_3 in I³/I⁴ has holomorphic A-coefficients. The radical vector in this local frame is v=(−λ,1)∈B². This supplies actual holomorphic lifts and an exact cleared equation, rather than assuming an inverse normal coordinate map at a singular point of D.

## 3. Cusp cubic and the bound 2Δ

On the smooth part of D where h is invertible, the ambient function z=Z/H is a normal coordinate whose linear term is ℓ. Subtracting the square A_0 z² leaves G/H². Its cubic on the radical is consequently

    r₃ = G_3(v,v,v)/h².

The numerator is holomorphic on the normalization. Thus its pole divisor is bounded by 2Δ. The intrinsic character is K⁻³⊗ν*L: on the smooth D it is the third differential of F restricted to the Hessian radical. The chain-rule terms from a change of coordinates contain either dF, which vanishes on D, or the Hessian applied to a radical vector, which also vanishes. This proves the coordinate invariance on that dense open set; uniqueness of meromorphic continuation glues the section across the finitely many omitted points. A change of the chosen lifts in the clearing construction changes the cubic by terms divisible by ℓ or by q, both of which vanish on v.

The ordinary cusp transversal gives r₃≠0. Therefore

    r₃ ∈ H⁰(C,K⁻³⊗ν*L⊗O_C(2Δ)),
    3a≤2c.                                               (3)

Combining (2), b≤0 and (3) gives

    3(2g−2+c)≤2c,  hence 6g−6+c≤0.

Under the target hypothesis g>0, this forces g=1 and c=0. Then a+b=0, b≤0 and 3a≤0 force a=b=0. The Hessian coefficient and the cubic are nonzero holomorphic sections of degree-zero line bundles, so they have no zeros. The curve D is the smooth elliptic C, and at each point the rank-one quadratic with nonzero radical cubic is an ordinary cusp. The holomorphic Morse lemma in the nondegenerate direction and extraction of the unit cube root in the remaining coordinate give the local transverse coordinates; their coefficients vary holomorphically along the smooth D.

## 4. Tacnode fourth invariant: exact H⁴ clearing

For an ordinary tacnode, the intrinsic radical cubic vanishes on a dense open set and hence identically. In the preceding notation G_3(v³)=0. Over B its homogeneous cubic therefore factors as

    G_3=ℓ Q₂

for a homogeneous quadratic Q₂ with B-coefficients; division by ℓ is polynomial because its X coefficient is one. The conductor property puts every coefficient of hQ₂ in A. Choose R₂∈I² whose leading quadratic is hQ₂. The cubic initial forms of H²G and ZR₂ agree: both are h²ℓQ₂. Consequently

    J=H²G−ZR₂ ∈ I⁴,
    H⁴F=A_0 H²Z²+ZR₂+J.                                (4)

This is an exact identity of ambient holomorphic germs. At a smooth point with h invertible, write z=Z/H and take the complementary normal coordinate w whose radical value is v. The relevant quadratic, mixed cubic and radical quartic terms of F are

    αz²+δzw²+εw⁴,
    δ=Q₂(v,v)/h²,       ε=J₄(v,v,v,v)/h⁴.

Terms containing additional powers of z do not change the coefficient on the radical after eliminating the nondegenerate variable. Completing the square in z gives the invariant

    r₄=4αε−δ²
      =[4αJ₄(v⁴)−Q₂(v²)²]/h⁴.                          (5)

It is a meromorphic section of

    K⁻⁴⊗M⁻²⊗(ν*L)²,

whose pole divisor is bounded by 4Δ. The character follows directly from the displayed three monomials: α has weight L M⁻², δ has weight L M⁻¹ K⁻², and ε has weight L K⁻⁴. Under z↦z+uw² the two-by-two quadratic matrix in z and w² changes by a unipotent congruence; four times its determinant is (5), so it is invariant. Linear changes preserving the radical give exactly the stated line-bundle character. These identities on the smooth part determine the meromorphic global section. The newly run universal program verifies the congruence identity and the polynomial clearing identity (4); the analytic ideal membership and pole orders are the argument above, not a claim inferred from the program.

The tacnode hypothesis gives r₄≠0, so

    4a+2b≤4c.

Using a+b=2g−2+c yields b≥4g−4. Together with b≤0 and g>0, this gives

    g=1,          b=0,          a=c.                    (6)

This alone permits c>0; it is not the final smoothness argument. The degree-zero section q of M⁻²⊗ν*L is now nowhere zero. At every singular point of D, its matrix in the pulled-back ambient generators is therefore nonzero. Choose a constant change of the two generators with q_11 nonzero at that point. Since q_11∈A has nonzero residue, α=q_11 is a unit of A; thus λ=q_12/q_11 lies in A, not just B. The preceding construction can consequently be made with h=H=1 and holomorphic ambient lifts. Its cubic factorization takes place over A because ℓ is monic and the radical cubic vanishes in the injective normalization A⊂B. Formula (5) now defines a holomorphic r₄ at that point. Repeating at every point shows r₄ has no poles anywhere on C.

Its actual holomorphic line bundle has degree −4a−2b=−4c by (6). A nonzero holomorphic section forces c=0. Thus D=C is smooth elliptic, a=b=0, and both the Hessian and r₄ are nowhere zero. Parametric completion of the square gives a one-variable fourth-order germ with nonzero fourth coefficient at every point. Taking its holomorphic unit fourth root gives the ordinary tacnode transversal everywhere. This last removal of the conductor denominators uses the unit Hessian forced by the global degree calculation; it is not an assumption that the initial intermediate jet extends holomorphically.

## 5. Ordinary triple transversal

Now F∈I³ and its leading cubic q₃ is a holomorphic section of Sym³E*⊗ν*L. Its discriminant is a nonzero holomorphic section

    Disc(q₃) ∈ H⁰(C,(det E*)⁶⊗(ν*L)⁴).

For the exact character, factor a binary cubic over a splitting field as κℓ₁ℓ₂ℓ₃. Its discriminant is κ⁴∏_{i<j}(ℓ_i∧ℓ_j)². Each bracket is a section of det E*, and three squared brackets give exponent six; scaling the cubic gives exponent four. The symmetric expression is the coefficient polynomial

    B²C²−4AC³−4B³D−27A²D²+18ABCD

for AX³+BX²Y+CXY²+DY³, so it is holomorphic before any local splitting. The prereading program verifies its equality with the three squared exterior products by exact polynomial arithmetic and verifies the general GL₂ bracket transformation.

Using (1), the degree of this nonzero holomorphic section is −6(2g−2+c)≥0. For g>0 this again forces g=1,c=0. Its degree is zero, so the discriminant never vanishes. Thus D=C is smooth elliptic and every transverse cubic has three distinct tangents. Holomorphic factorization gives three smooth branches transverse in each plane. One first takes two branches as coordinates; the third is a graph with nonzero slope, and a holomorphic coordinate change preserving the first two axes makes it the diagonal. This gives the ordinary-triple analytic type at every point, including points omitted by the initial generic hypothesis.
