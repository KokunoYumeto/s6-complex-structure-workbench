## Scope and primary classification input

Work with the smooth connected compact threefold in the algebraic-reduction setting of C.3.1, with a(Y)=1 and holomorphic connected-fibre f:Y→P¹. The original C.3.1 wording does not itself impose smoothness under the series' broad convention; this proof uses the canonical line bundle on a smooth Y and certifies this explicit smooth scope. It asserts no singular counterexample.

The Hopf classification input was read in Abasheva–Déev, arXiv:2303.10764v1, §§2.2.2, 2.2.6 and the opening of §2.3.1, pp.4–5,8, which records Kodaira's primary normal forms, the resonant diagonal elliptic case, and the finite free primary cover for a secondary Hopf surface. The classification does not enter through a cohomological restriction-surjectivity assertion. CDP98 Proposition 3.6, pp.86–87, and CDP20's reference to it were inspected as historical source context; the extension of a line bundle chosen on one fibre to effective bundles on all fibres is not used in this derivation.

## 1. Explicit anticanonical sections for an elliptic primary Hopf surface

Let S=(C²\{0})/⟨A⟩ with A(z,w)=(a z,b w), 0<|a|,|b|<1, and a^p=b^q for positive integers p,q. Such a relation has positive exponents because both eigenvalue moduli are below one. The classification says that every primary Hopf surface of algebraic dimension one has this resonant diagonal form. The other primary normal form, and a nonresonant diagonal form, have only finitely many irreducible curves and so cannot have a nonconstant meromorphic function: its moving level divisors would give infinitely many curves.

A section of K_S^{-N} is represented on the cover by f(z,w)(∂z∧∂w)^N satisfying

    f(a z,b w)=(ab)^N f(z,w).

For N≥q the two holomorphic sections with coefficient functions

    f0=z^N w^N,
    f1=z^{N+p} w^{N−q}

satisfy that exact equation. Indeed f1 has multiplier

    a^{N+p}b^{N−q}=(ab)^N(a^p/b^q)=(ab)^N.

Their quotient is z^p/w^q, which is a nonconstant meromorphic function on S. By increasing N and taking the monomials z^{N+jp}w^{N−jq}, 0≤j≤floor(N/q), one obtains a linearly growing family of sections. Thus κ(S,K_S^{-1})=1; the lower bound is established by these sections and the upper bound follows from a(S)=1. No replacement of the quotient morphism is implicit.

## 2. The finite secondary quotient

For a secondary Hopf surface F with a(F)=1 choose a finite étale Galois primary cover π:S→F. Meromorphic functions on S are algebraic over their G-invariants, since each function satisfies the orbit polynomial ∏_{g∈G}(T−g·u); hence a(S)=a(F)=1. Étaleness identifies π^*K_F^{-1} with K_S^{-1} by the determinant of the differential, and descent identifies each H⁰(F,K_F^{-N}) with H⁰(S,K_S^{-N})^G. The graded anticanonical section ring of S is integral over its finite-group invariant ring, by the same orbit polynomial applied to homogeneous sections. Therefore its growth dimension, and hence the Iitaka dimension of the descended line bundle, equals one. In particular some N has h⁰(F,K_F^{-N})≥2. This proves the exact finite-cover transport needed for every secondary elliptic Hopf surface; it does not assume that a non-invariant section descends unchanged.

## 3. A fixed power on very general fibres

Let U be the regular-value locus of f. For every positive integer N, the set

    Z_N={t∈U : h⁰(F_t,K_Y^{-N}|F_t)≥2}

is closed analytic in U by proper analytic upper semicontinuity. Adjunction and the derivative of f identify K_Y|F_t with K_{F_t}: the normal line of the smooth fibre is the pullback of T_tP¹ and is trivial on that fibre.

Suppose the very general Hopf fibre were elliptic, of algebraic dimension one. Steps 1–2 put every such parameter in some Z_N. A proper analytic subset of a second-countable Riemann surface is discrete and countable. A countable union of those proper subsets cannot contain the complement of a countable set in U. Thus one Z_N is all of U. Equivalently a single fixed power K_Y^{-N} has at least two sections on a general fibre. This addresses the fact that the resonance integers and finite-cover order may initially depend on the fibre; no uniform integer is assumed without this argument.

## 4. The actual field extension contradicts a(Y)=1

Set E=f_*K_Y^{-N}. It is coherent on the projective curve P¹ and has generic rank at least two by generic base change. Choose an integer k such that E⊗O_{P¹}(k) is generated by global sections. Projection formula gives

    H⁰(P¹,E⊗O(k))=H⁰(Y,K_Y^{-N}⊗f^*O(k)).

Choose two of these sections whose restrictions to one, hence a general, regular fibre are linearly independent. Their ratio ψ is a meromorphic function on Y that is nonconstant on that connected fibre. The field homomorphism f^*:M(P¹)→M(Y) is injective. The function ψ is transcendental over its image: a nonzero polynomial relation with meromorphic base coefficients restricts at a general parameter to a polynomial with finitely many roots, forcing the meromorphic function ψ on the connected fibre to be constant, a contradiction. Hence trdeg_C M(Y)≥2.

This contradicts a(Y)=1. Therefore the very general Hopf fibre has algebraic dimension zero, and elliptic primary or secondary Hopf surfaces cannot be the general fibre of the stated algebraic reduction.
