# Derivation before target proof: 01AR

Owner `cdp:st:A.4.2`; checker `i_provenance`. Exact packet and supplement read;
target II proof and old checks are unopened. Related Paper I exposure stays
disclosed. The relevant published passage has been opened in the primary
Cambridge PDF, CDP20 Proposition 7.3, printed p.693. It explicitly permits
all the M_t to be trivial and then infers base genus at least two in Step 1.
This is a check of that displayed implication, with its precise hypotheses.

Take an elliptic curve E=C/(Z+tau Z), Im(tau)>0, and the ruled surface
S_0=E times P1, with ruling the first projection. It is smooth projective
and minimal. Indeed every rational curve maps constantly to E: pulling back
the nonzero holomorphic form on E, or Riemann-Hurwitz, rules out a nonconstant
P1->E. Therefore every rational curve lies in a fibre, and a fibre has
self-intersection zero. There is no exceptional curve of self-intersection
-1. The base of the ruling has genus exactly one.

For t in a disc, set M_t=O_(S_0), using the trivial line on S_0 times the
parameter disc as the actual constant holomorphic family. It is numerically
trivial. The product differential splitting is
Omega^1_(S_0)=pr_E^*Omega_E^1 direct-sum pr_P1^*Omega_P1^1.
The first factor has the nowhere-zero section pr_E^*(dz). It is a nonzero
section of Omega^1_(S_0) tensor M_t for every t. If a genuinely varying
one-parameter family of sections is requested, (1+t)pr_E^*(dz) gives it on
a sufficiently small disc while the line family remains constant.

These explicit maps and sections satisfy the stated premises and have base
genus one. This supplies the asserted counterexample to that implication.
No assertion about the conclusion of the whole published Proposition 7.3
under all its global hypotheses is inferred from this example.
