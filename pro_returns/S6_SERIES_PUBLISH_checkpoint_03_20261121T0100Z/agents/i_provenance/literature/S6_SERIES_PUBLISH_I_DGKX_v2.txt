                                                                         OKA GEOMETRY OF COMPLEX SIX-SPHERES

                                                                     YUN-HENG DU, BIN GUO, YUTA KUSAKABE, AND SONG-YAN XIE


                                                    Abstract. Alpöge constructed a complex structure on 𝑆 6 through a torus fibration with exactly three special
                                                    fibres. We prove that every member of the associated admissible period family is Oka and that the family
                                                    projection over each relatively compact parameter disc is an Oka map. The Oka property survives the deletion or
                                                    simultaneous blow-up of any finite set of distinct points in any member. Blowing up a single point therefore
                                                    produces an Oka complex manifold diffeomorphic, but not biholomorphic, to P3 . The proofs combine explicit
                                                    cyclic coverings with analytic extension and the invariance of the Oka property under holomorphic coverings.

                                                                                                1. Introduction
                                               The Oka principle begins with a flexibility question: when can a continuous map from a Stein space be
                                           deformed to a holomorphic map? Informally, a complex manifold is Oka if these deformations can be made
                                           with approximation, interpolation, and parameters [10, 9]. Gromov proved, using holomorphic sprays, that
                                           elliptic manifolds are Oka [14]. For a holomorphic submersion, an Oka map is the relative version: liftings
                                           over a fixed map to the base enjoy the same flexibility, and the submersion is a Serre fibration [18].
                                               Kusakabe’s analytic-Zariski localization theorem [17] assembles the Oka property from an analytic-Zariski
                                           open cover. His Oka-map criterion extends this strategy to holomorphic submersions [18]. These tools are
                                           well suited to a fibration whose general fibre is homogeneous while its special fibres are singular or multiple.
                                               Alpöge constructed a torus-fibred complex threefold diffeomorphic to 𝑆 6 [1]. Its general fibre is a complex
                                           two-torus, and the fibration has exactly three special values on P1 : two multiple fibres with bielliptic reductions
                                           and one irreducible nonnormal cusp fibre, whose normalization is a degree-six del Pezzo surface. Any
                                           ordered triple of distinct values on P1 can be moved to 0, 1, ∞ by an automorphism; these coordinates are
                                           used throughout the paper. The orders 3 and 4 and the nature of the cusp remain essential to the construction.
                                           We use Alpöge’s construction and smooth-type theorem, including its complex structure on 𝑆 6 ; Appendix B
                                           records the particular source ingredients used here and their locations.
                                               The smooth fibres and P1 are Oka, so it is natural to ask whether the total space is Oka. The base of the
                                           smooth fibration, P1 \ {0, 1, ∞}, is hyperbolic; recovering flexibility therefore depends on the three special
                                           fillings. The proof passes to cyclic covers containing explicit two-row conic bands, obtained by retaining
                                           two adjacent toric rows in the cusp model. Their algebraic charts provide Oka open subsets. The analytic

   Deleting either finite multiple fibre leaves one multiple fibre, which is removed by a finite root base
change. A cyclic period-lattice cover then contains a two-row conic band over an elliptic surface. Removing a
section from that surface gives an explicit Danielewski model. Over a two-dimensional algebraic torus in this
model, the principal conic open is a holomorphically trivial C∗ -bundle, hence isomorphic to (C∗ ) 3 . Analytic
extension gives the Oka property of the band and the cyclic cover. Classical invariance under holomorphic
coverings [9, Proposition 2.8] gives the finite-fibre complement, and a final analytic extension gives 𝑋𝑎 .
   The family assertion requires more than fibrewise Oka. The chosen period-lattice kernel has periods
independent of 𝑎, so its covering family over a disc is a product with one fixed Oka manifold. The parametric
Oka property of the product projection passes through this covering by Kusakabe’s invariance theorem [18,
Corollary 5.6]. Localization then gives the Oka property of 𝜋Δ . The cyclic-cover construction and relative
argument appear in Section 3.
   Chen [6] independently established the Oka property for the admissible members of Alpöge’s period family.
   Analytic extension also applies to the torus-fibred complex six-spheres of Liu–Rollenske [20] and
Viaclovsky [23]. In each construction, deleting the unique multiple fibre leaves a proper fibration over C
with a holomorphic section and connected complex two-tori as its smooth general fibres. The torus-fibration
theorem [3, Theorem 7.1] makes this complement Oka. Analytic extension [2, Theorem 1.9] then gives the
Oka property of the compact total space.
   Removing or blowing up points tests the flexibility of this geometry. For a finite centre, its complete lift to
a cyclic cover meets each two-row band in two parts: finitely many full deck orbits in the common core and
finitely many toroidal points.
Theorem 1.2. For every 𝑎 ∈ A and every finite set 𝑃 ⊂ 𝑋𝑎 of distinct points, both 𝑋𝑎 \ 𝑃 and the simultaneous
blow-up Bl 𝑃 𝑋𝑎 are Oka.
   On one principal band chart, a proper monomial character makes the logarithmic lift of this complete
centre tame. Its complement is Oka. Analytic extension fills the rest of the band and the cyclic cover.
Invariance under holomorphic coverings, followed by analytic extension, gives 𝑋𝑎 \ 𝑃. The exceptional-divisor
complement in Bl 𝑃 𝑋𝑎 is precisely this punctured manifold, so one further analytic extension proves the
blow-up result. The same principal-open argument yields the band curve complements in Section 4.
   For a single point 𝑝, the Oka manifold Bl 𝑝 𝑋𝑎 is diffeomorphic to P3 . Its complex structure differs from
the standard projective one: the first Chern class of its tangent bundle has divisibility 2, whereas that of the
standard P3 has divisibility 4. The topology and Chern-class calculation are spelled out in Corollary 4.6.
   The band geometry also supplies curve complements. If any chosen subcollection of the canonical
exceptional curves is deleted from a two-row band, the result remains Oka, even together with the mixed
finite/deck point deletions used above. The complement of the complete residual-axis curve configuration is
Oka as well (Theorems 4.4 and 4.5). These are band statements.
   The Oka–1 property is the corresponding Runge approximation and Weierstrass jet-interpolation property
for maps from open Riemann surfaces [11, Definition 1.1]. For an arbitrary smooth compact curve 𝐶 ⊂ 𝑋𝑎 ,
the blow-up theorem of Forstnerič–Lárusson gives Bl𝐶 𝑋𝑎 this property (Corollary 4.7); the local (−1, −1)
conifold model has an algebraically elliptic blow-up (Proposition 4.8).

    The three punctured identifications attach the two logarithmic fillings and the toric cusp filling to the
same smooth torus family. Alpöge [1, Construction 6.1 and Theorem 6.2] shows that the resulting space is a
compact Hausdorff complex threefold 𝑋 A with a proper map to P1 and exactly the fibres described above.
The affine translations also enter its topology. In Alpöge’s notation the cusp gluing is untranslated, and the
character values are (ℓ0 , ℓ1 , ℓ2 ) = (0, 1, −1); consequently

Alpöge [1, Theorem 7.17, Corollary 7.29, and Theorem 8.1] proves that these choices make 𝑋 A simply
connected with the integral homology of 𝑆 6 , and then identifies its underlying smooth manifold with 𝑆 6 .
Transporting the complex atlas along that diffeomorphism gives the complex structure on the standard smooth
six-sphere.
   Our family uses precisely the monodromy matrices, finite twists, period normalization, and untranslated
