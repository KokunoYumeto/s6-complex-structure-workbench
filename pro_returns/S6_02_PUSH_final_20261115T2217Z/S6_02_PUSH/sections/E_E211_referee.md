15 November 2026. Read `sections/E_torus_purity.tex` in full. Verdict: **PASS** at the explicit good-Jacobian hypothesis.

Flatness puts the omitted fibre in complex codimension two in the smooth total space, so coefficientwise Hartogs extension applies to the infinitesimal action fields. Their verticality and commutativity continue by uniqueness. Completeness follows from compactness of each fibre and the ordinary differential equation extension criterion; local compactness gives uniform continuation on each finite time path. Period identities hold on the dense ordinary locus and therefore on the whole total space.

The fixed real Betti torus acts by holomorphic automorphisms: its real coefficients are constants, while the period vectors are holomorphic in the base. At an isotropy identity component, the representation on the Zariski tangent of the fibre is holomorphic in a compact complex torus, hence trivial. The base tangent quotient is fixed. Compact averaging supplies an invariant complement, so the full derivative is trivial. The explicit averaging-coordinate argument then eliminates positive-dimensional isotropy. This argument does not assume that the family of group tori is holomorphically constant.

The resulting independent commuting fields give a holomorphic local product. Equidimensionality makes the transverse map a finite germ; its Jacobian divisor cannot have image supported at one isolated base point. Thus the map is a submersion. The remaining finite isotropy acts trivially on the orbit tangent because the group is abelian, and on the base quotient because the action is vertical. The same compact linearization eliminates it. Local sections of the resulting submersion give genuine torsor charts.

No Kähler assumption, polarization, section of the original torsor, general discriminant purity theorem, or separatedness of arbitrary complex-space completions is used. The good Jacobian extension is indispensable and remains an explicit hypothesis. The proof is complete within these stated conditions.
