## E.2.13

The divisor equality g*Gamma=mD holds on the entire smooth fourfold:
an additional divisor supported over the specialization parameter would
have to be the whole central threefold, which the nonconstant central
map does not send to Gamma(0). Local factoriality makes D Cartier.
Thus g*xi=a^m u, including at a possibly initially singular central
restriction of D. The normalized root cover is locally the disjoint
union of the graphs r=zeta a u^(1/m), each a copy of the original smooth
space. It really is an etale cover, with free deck permutation even
over a=0. This is a statement about normalization, not the unreduced
fibre product.

Properness, equidimensionality and smooth source/base give flatness of
the map to the root bidisc. Connectedness on the dense punctured part
and the normal Stein factor give connected fibres. Known smooth root
charts for nonzero specialization parameter, together with E.2.12
away from the isolated section, put every possible remaining critical
point over the single bidisc origin.

The noncollapsed source coordinate is xi times a unit. In the collapsed
case it is s^n xi times a unit, and the isolated section must be the
preimage of the limiting finite marked source point. A nearby central
regular target forces e|n. Since e also divides m, both displayed
root coordinates are holomorphic on the full root bidisc. The actual
Jacobian identification is the same marking/period argument already
refereed in E.2.12. E.2.11 therefore applies, and makes the root family
smooth. The etale covering descends a smooth reduced finite fibre of
exact multiplicity m. The free affine quotient and its smooth parameter
family follow. No assertion concerning a cusp or a cusp cluster enters.

## E.2.14

The direct image of the relative tangent bundle is a coherent
torsion-free rank-one sheaf on the smooth parameter disc, hence a free
module after shrinking. The punctured normalized fields patch because
the fixed-parameter comparison maps preserve the marked delta period
and have identity fibre-linear action. On a central regular torus
bidisc, the good-Jacobian comparison extends that period as a nonzero
holomorphic Lie section. Comparing a nonzero coefficient makes the
scalar b(s) extend holomorphically. If b(0)=0, the generating field
would vanish on an open subset of the central connected threefold,
hence everywhere, and its coefficients would be divisible by s.
The quotient would be another global relative field, contradicting
primitivity of the generator. This proves the actual global delta
extension, not merely existence of some central vector field.

Completeness on compact fibres makes the period-one identity extend
to the entire family. Averaging an Ehresmann connection gives genuine
equivariant smooth transport for the resulting circle action. Its fixed
two-sphere, effectiveness, semifree weights and both normal degrees
persist. The fixed set is a complex submanifold by compact-group
averaged coordinates; genus zero makes the curve P1. Its two distinct
weight lines remain holomorphic lines of degree minus one, so the
normal bundle is precisely O(-1) plus O(-1).

At either finite-root torus, the rank-two fixed lattice spans the
complex delta line and supplies a second constant nonreal period.
Its flow is the identity on the dense regular part and therefore on
the entire central fibre. The resulting elliptic action is nontrivial.
A compact connected complex group's derivative representation at a
fixed point is holomorphic and constant; averaging holomorphic
coordinates then makes the action locally, hence globally, trivial.
This contradicts the fixed rational curve. Equivalently the field
would be everywhere nonzero and force Euler number zero. The argument
retains the explicit limitation that an ordinary torus may have only
one real delta period; E.2.15 treats that separate remaining case.
