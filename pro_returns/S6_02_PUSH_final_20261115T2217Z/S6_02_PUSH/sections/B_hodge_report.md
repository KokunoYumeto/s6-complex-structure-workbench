## B.4.3: a necessary non-Kähler central stratum

The new obstruction applies to every positive base-change exponent, not just the proposed exponent six. A proper semistable filling of the actual six-sphere family would have a simply connected central fibre D, by B.4.2. On the other hand, the independently checked root-curve direct images give h¹(X,O)=1, and upper semicontinuity gives h¹(D,O_D)≥1. Thus H¹(D;C)→H¹(D,O_D) cannot be surjective.

Graf and Kovács (2014, §1.D, p.120) state surjectivity for a proper algebraic variety with Du Bois singularities, including normal-crossings singularities. Consequently D cannot be such an algebraic variety. For a simple normal-crossings D, Chen (2026, Definition 2.3 and Theorem 2.4(4)) shows that the ddbar lemma on every closed stratum forces E1 degeneration of the ordinary Hodge–de Rham sequence on a nearby fibre. That contradicts b₁(S⁶)=0 and h¹(X,O)=1. In particular, a construction using only compact Kähler or Moishezon components cannot work in this category.

The independent lower bound h¹(O)≥1 for any complex structure on S⁶ was also proved: analytic Riemann–Roch gives χ(O)=0, while Stokes' theorem kills holomorphic top forms and Serre duality gives h³(O)=0. Hence h¹(O)=1+h²(O). The small exact program b_hodge_obstruction.py checks the weighted-section count used in the more specific family calculation; the mathematical argument is in the paper, not delegated to that program.

The primary Chen version 2 of 16 October 2026 was opened and its additional K1 hypothesis was checked at the exact point where parts (3) and (4) of Theorem 2.4 are stated. K2 and a local invariant-cycle theorem are not assumed. The original Graf–Kovács journal PDF was opened. An independent agent read both primary statements and checked the proof; its full mathematical referee record is E_B_hodge_referee.md.

This does not show that an unrestricted analytic filling cannot exist. An arbitrary three-dimensional component over the cusp has not been proved Moishezon. B.4.3 originally gave its analytic ddbar conclusion for simple normal crossings; B.4.5 below now proves the stronger normalization-component obstruction for all normal crossings by a direct argument. The algebraic obstruction also includes normal crossings with self-intersections, because the local Du Bois condition applies there.

Further primary reading tested the missing implication. Nishiguchi's 1988 preprint *Degeneration of K3 surfaces, II*, Theorem 1.4 and Example 2.1, describes analytic semistable K3 degenerations with Hopf components. Istrati, Otiman, Pontecorvo, and Ruggiero (2022, Theorem 7.1) construct proper flat toric-Kato degenerations with smooth total space. These are different families. They show why non-Kähler quotient geometry deserves examination; neither supplies a comparison map or a filling for the family here. No such application is asserted.

The next unresolved step is concrete: either construct an analytic cusp model whose non-Moishezon stratum satisfies the prescribed punctured-family gluing, or prove that every possible filling is forced into a class excluded by B.4.3. Properness alone does not establish the latter.

## B.4.4: extending the marked base map

A possible essential projective-coordinate ambiguity has now been removed. For an arbitrary proposed semistable filling, the coherent direct image of the relative bi-anticanonical line bundle is torsion-free of rank two on the disc. A frame gives a meromorphic pencil. Its proper graph has an analytic critical-value image. The three intrinsically distinguishable punctured critical values extend as degree-one branches of a finite analytic curve. Their cross-ratio normalization therefore has meromorphic coefficients, even if the values collide at the centre. Composing gives the extension of the exact marked map f to P¹.

This is a meromorphic map and a projective graph modification, with the categories and comparison on the punctured disc specified. We do not assume that the graph is smooth or semistable. The construction uses only the particular coherent direct-image and base-change statements already opened and checked in E.3; the local graph and its critical locus are explicitly constructed. A second independent reader checked the source in E_B44_referee.md.

The reduction does not compare the actual punctured family with the auxiliary genus-one-torsor family, whose Euler number is 24, and does not show that an arbitrary vertical component is Moishezon. Thus it advances the unrestricted filling question without closing B.4.1.

## B.4.5: the self-crossing case and normalization components

The simple-crossing restriction has now been removed for the component obstruction. For any compact analytic normal-crossings D, including self-crossings, ddbar on its normalization components forces H¹(D;C)→H¹(D,O_D) to be surjective. The proof uses the ordered distinct-local-branch resolution and its alternating summand. A coherent degree-one class is represented by a (0,1)-form α on the normalization and a function f on the branch-pair spaces. The ddbar lemma replaces α by a d-closed representative; the cocycle equations then make f pluriharmonic on compact branch-pair components, hence constant. The same pair is consequently a de Rham cocycle lifting the class.

The distinction between local branches and global components is essential: the construction retains two branches of a self-intersection of one irreducible component. No ddbar assumption on intersections is needed in degree one. The local analytic resolutions are justified by convergent monomial expansions, with permutation-equivariance and simplex exactness checked by b_nc_degree_one.py. The proof itself does not depend on a finite computation proving the analytic step. The independent referee is E_B43_degree_one_referee.md.

Combining with B.4.2–B.4.3 shows that every semistable centre for this family, after any positive finite base change, must have a normalization component failing ddbar. In particular no model with only Kähler or Moishezon normalized components can be the desired limit. This settles the proposed extension of the Hodge obstruction to self-crossing toroidal models; it does not force unknown analytic vertical components into the excluded class, so the unrestricted existence and least-exponent question remains open.
