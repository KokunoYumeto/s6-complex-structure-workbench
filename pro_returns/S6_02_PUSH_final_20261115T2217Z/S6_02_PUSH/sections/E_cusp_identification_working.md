# E.2.20 working proof: recovering the analytic cusp from its actual torus action

Owner: root. Full-source review required before integration. This is a proposed completion-identification theorem, conditional on the fully proved E.2.16--19 geometry and action; it is not yet counted as a proved result.

## Exact setup

Normalize the three distinct critical sections to 0,1,infinity, and write q=1/t near the cusp. Let N be the restriction of the actual smooth fourfold to a small (q,s)-bidisc. Its cusp divisor W=(q=0) is relative normal crossings, irreducible on every s fibre, with normalized pair the toric dP6 hexagon and unchanged opposite-side identifications. E.2.19 supplies an actual holomorphic H=(C*)² action on N over the bidisc. Its constant period lattice is the primitive marked vanishing lattice Zw+Zdelta. The parameter c(s) extends holomorphically to the interior. The goal is an actual biholomorphism of N with the marked Mumford cusp at parameter c(s), after shrinking the bidisc.

## 1. Topology and the cover, with the comparison made explicit

The relative NC coordinates q=x,xy,xyz provide local smooth lifts of the two real coordinate directions of s which annihilate dq and are tangent to all cusp strata. Away from W the map to (q,s) is a submersion, so such lifts exist there too. A smooth partition of unity preserves these linear conditions. On the inverse image of a closed smaller q-disc the lifts integrate along a small s-path: the q-coordinate is constant, and properness prevents escape. Their flows give a q-preserving diffeomorphism of the cusp neighbourhoods and their strata. Thus the actual N has the same fundamental group and the same regular-fibre inclusion map as a prescribed punctured member.

In the known Mumford cusp, pi1(N_s)=Z². The map from a regular torus's Z⁴ kills exactly the primitive lattice Zw+Zdelta and is onto. Root opened and checked the original AC26 PDF, Proposition4.7 and Corollary4.8 on printed p30. An independent elementary proof is as follows. Each maximal toric chart restricted to the cusp disc is {|xyz|<epsilon}, which is star-shaped and simply connected. All chart intersections contain the same connected dense open torus (C*)²×Δ* and are connected; intersections with finite chart unions have the same property. Finite van Kampen and compactness of loops prove that the entire toric cover is simply connected. Its free properly discontinuous deck group is the marked quotient Λ/(Zw+Zdelta). The regular fibre of this cover is C²/(Zw+Zdelta)=(C*)². The q-loop dies through the explicit smooth section (x,y,z)=(q,1,1). Its central components form the planar hexagon unfolding. These descriptions transfer by the q-preserving diffeomorphism just constructed.

Take the universal cover Ntilde→N. It has deck group Z² and central normalized components indexed by the planar hexagon tiling. The H action lifts to Ntilde: its fundamental loops at a central open orbit lie in one normalized dP6 component, hence are null-homotopic in N. Uniqueness of the identity lift gives the action law. Pick a holomorphic (q,s)-section p through a smooth point of one central component, and lift it. On q≠0 its H orbit map is an isomorphism H×(Δq*×Δs)→Ntilde_reg: its stabilizer is precisely the marked vanishing lattice already divided out. Thus the two H characters and q are globally defined on the open cover.

The deck transformations commute with H. On this open cover they therefore have the form h→P_i(q,s)h, where the two P_i are exactly the actual marked period multipliers. The choice of p only changes the affine origin; it does not change these multipliers. They are the same q-monomials times holomorphic units as in the reference Mumford model, because the full marked period matrix is the one at c(s).

## 2. Why the character comparison has no essential singularities

For any central component D_v, choose a holomorphic base section a_v through its dense H orbit. The H orbit map gives an open chart H×(Δq×Δs), containing that dense central orbit and the entire regular cover. Indeed it is injective on each fibre, is a local biholomorphism along the central orbit, and is an isomorphism on q≠0. Two such charts differ on q≠0 by a holomorphic translation h_vw(q,s)∈H. At this point h_vw is only known on the punctured q-disc; its meromorphic extension must be proved.

If two origins lie in the same central component, their ratio extends as an H-valued holomorphic unit over q=0. At the central parameter their points are in the same free H orbit; the holomorphic inverse-function theorem for the orbit map supplies the extension in both q and s.

For adjacent components, use a relative crossing chart q=ab and an orbit coordinate z≠0. The primitive connected stabilizer along their common open conductor is a C* subgroup of H. Compact-circle averaging linearizes it with opposite normal characters, while a complementary H direction supplies z. The full characters are unimodular, as on every punctured s fibre. Choose origins in this chart by a=q/b0,b=b0,z=z0 on one side and a=a0,b=q/a0,z=z0 on the other, with a0,b0,z0 fixed small nonzero values. The normal stabilizer translates the former to the latter by the cocharacter evaluated at a0*b0/q; tangential changes are holomorphic units. The orbit path may be kept within the crossing chart by increasing |a| while decreasing |b| with their product fixed. Thus the comparison is exactly a fixed integral power q^v times a holomorphic H-valued unit. Comparing these special origins to the chosen origins adds only holomorphic units, by the same-component argument.

Every central component is reached by a finite adjacent path. The translation from the initial origin is therefore q^v u_v(q,s), with v∈Z² and u_v a holomorphic H-valued unit. There is no essential singularity. Different paths give the same meromorphic character functions because they agree on the dense regular cover. The integers v are locally constant in s: each crossing calculation holds on the full bidisc with a nonvanishing unit.

## 3. The fan and the analytic charts

At a triple point the whole H fixes the point. Average local coordinates over its maximal compact torus. The resulting three coordinates are H eigenfunctions with the same integer characters as on a punctured s fibre. Their only primitive positive invariant relation is their product; reducedness and the NC equation make q a holomorphic unit times that product. Absorb the invariant unit, which is a holomorphic function of the product and s, into one eigen-coordinate. Thus the chart has the exact form q=xyz with diagonal H action.

The valuations of the two global characters along D_v, together with ord_Dv(q)=1, give rays (v,1). At a triple point the three incident rays are a unimodular cone. In that chart the global characters are Laurent monomials in x,y,z times holomorphic units in q,s; this follows from the origin comparisons above, or by evaluating a character on a section holding two of x,y,z fixed nonzero and solving the third from q. Conversely the toric coordinates of that cone pull back to x,y,z times holomorphic units. They therefore extend holomorphically and give a biholomorphism near the triple point. The same assertion on a double or smooth stratum follows from its crossing or open-orbit chart.

The component incidence and all the integer characters are constant over s. For any s≠0 they are the prescribed A2 triangulation. Hence the resulting fan on the central fibre is exactly that fan, not an arbitrary smooth refinement. The character maps on different charts agree on the dense open torus and therefore glue. This reconstructs the analytic toric universal-cover model.

Uniformity over the infinite cover is handled by deck equivariance, not by an infimum of infinitely many radii. Choose finitely many charts covering one compact central dP6 component and finitely many overlaps for the two deck generators. Shrink the bidisc once for these charts and comparisons. On the regular cover the reconstructed map intertwines the actual deck translations with the multipliers P_i(q,s) in the reference model. Both sides extend holomorphically in the charts just constructed, so the equality extends to their central strata. Translate the finite chart collection by the actual deck group. The reference period multipliers act on the prescribed invariant fan, and these translated charts cover the entire central unfolding. This gives one common bidisc for the quotient comparison.

The reconstructed map is a local biholomorphism at every central stratum, bijective on each labelled torus orbit there, and an isomorphism on the whole regular cover. Its inverse is supplied by the same monomial-coordinate charts. Quotienting by the identical properly discontinuous deck actions yields the required biholomorphism of the actual cusp neighbourhood with the prescribed Mumford neighbourhood over the bidisc.
