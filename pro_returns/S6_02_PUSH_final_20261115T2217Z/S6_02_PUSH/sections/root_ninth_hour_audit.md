## Exceptional curves obstruct the fixed-base repair, B.4.23

Root and limits_ab read the complete source. The marked exceptional curve meets the persistent elliptic axis at the point represented by -s^12. Its positive loop has nonzero class (12,0), and a finite cover of degree d changes this to (12d,0). An analytic divisor closure would intersect the elliptic-axis family in a proper degree-one horizontal curve. Normalization would produce an extending section, contradicting that winding. Pushing a closure down through a finite sequence of proper modifications gives the same contradiction. Thus the obstruction is not merely the failure of one coordinate formula.

The separate deformation argument assumes that the exceptional curve is disjoint from every conductor and that the original q-map is a submersion there. Its normal sequence is an extension of O^2 by O(-1). The vanishing of H^1(O(-1)) splits it, so the Douady space is smooth of dimension two and maps locally isomorphically to the two original base parameters. The curve would persist in ordinary nearby two-tori, which contain no rational curve. The conductor and fixed-base hypotheses are necessary and remain in the statement.

Its local Douady argument requires a holomorphic submersion of relative dimension two, not global properness of the family, and directly supports the deformation step used here. The published metadata are Mathematische Annalen 353(4), 1283–1314 (2012), DOI 10.1007/s00208-011-0713-9. Kodaira's 1962 metadata alone were not treated as an opened proof. ## The actual logarithmic torsor, B.4.24

Root and boundary_d read the complete proof. The multiplicative period pairing has columns (q a0,a1^(-1)) and (a1^6,q r^12 a2). Its sharp pairing is diagonal with entries q and q+12r. Every face passes admissibility; residual pairings are nondegenerate after shrinking. Root read Kajiwara, Kato and Nakayama's original Logarithmic abelian varieties, Sections 1.2–1.3, Theorem 5.2.1 and Theorem 5.3.2. These permit the associated log complex torus and its proper fan models without assuming a polarization.

The choice of extending axial origins is decisive. On the reduced q-axis model the actual section sigma is holomorphic in q and gives the origin. On the rooted rotating model the identity gives the other origin. Thus the difference of the two associated group origins is F, although the raw comparison of analytic covering coordinates is zeta-sigma+F. This does not revive the previously withdrawn claim that F alone obstructs every raw analytic comparison. Its r-loop has Y-component gamma. On the q-axis neighbourhood, the ghost map sends u and gamma to (12,0) and (0,12), so any extending log-torus section has zero r-loop Y-component. On the other neighbourhood the r-loop contracts. No sum of two extending sections can therefore split F. After finite coordinate roots the obstruction is n gamma, still nonzero.

This excludes locally trivial torsor extensions under the specified associated log-torus sheaf. A fan model is not assumed to be a group object. No implication from an arbitrary analytic cap to such a torsor has been proved.

## The one-eigenvalue Hopf boundary, B.4.25

Root and limits_ab independently read the full proof. For the diagonal Hopf family with multipliers alpha=q^m kappa(q) and beta(q), where 0<|beta(0)|<1, bivector automorphy gives alpha^(i-1) beta^(j-1)=1. Under |alpha|<|beta|<1 this leaves zw and, precisely when alpha=beta^N, the additional monomial w^(N+1). Thus the anticanonical dimension is exactly one or two. Quantitative Rouché estimates with radius (2|beta(0)|^N/|kappa(0)|)^(1/m) produce distinct resonances tending to zero for arbitrarily large N.

For a hypothetical smooth proper completion, M=pi_* K_total^(-1) is torsion-free and hence locally free on the disc. The torsion of the coherent sheaf R^1 pi_* K_total^(-1) has locally finite support. The direct-image exact sequence for multiplication by q-a therefore makes the anticanonical dimension constant on a sufficiently small punctured disc. This contradicts the accumulating resonances. Reduction, retention of the dominating component and a resolution which is an isomorphism over the smooth punctured family extend the contradiction to arbitrary proper analytic completions. The proof also covers every finite base change.

## Every origin component must be vertical, B.4.26

Root and boundary_d independently read the full proof. A finite multisection of positive degree d would have a trace in each of the two actual axial group origins. At a point of either punctured axis, the normalized finite cover is a disjoint union of cyclic root covers x=t^e, after shrinking away the non-axis branch curves. The ordinary trace extends across the ordinary branch locus. In a toric chart each character is t^k times a holomorphic unit. Its norm is (-1)^(k(e-1)) x^k times a holomorphic unit. This proves extension as a bounded log character, with integral orders and the correct period ambiguity; the first character has zero r-axis order. The two traces would differ by dF, whose r-loop has nonzero Y-component d gamma. B.4.24 forbids this.

A point of local fibre dimension two in a fourfold over the bidisc gives a finite dominating two-dimensional slice by two generic local linear sections. A finite representative over a smaller complete bidisc and its normalization would supply exactly the forbidden multisection. Thus no such point exists. Every component of the origin fibre has dimension at least two, while flatness over r bounds its dimension by three. All origin components must consequently be three-dimensional.

The map to the fixed bidisc is required to be holomorphic. An initially meromorphic q-map must first be resolved by a graph modification, retaining its resulting geometry. The theorem does not exclude vertical components, which are precisely the remaining possibility.

## Point blowups cannot repair the logarithmic torsor, B.4.27

Root and boundary_d read the full source. On a sequence of point blowups S over the origin, the exact bounded-character sheaf is M_(D_Q+E)^gp times M_(D_Q+D_R+E)^gp. The missing strict-r divisor in the first factor is essential. The two-chart calculation gives vanishing of higher O-cohomology. The exceptional classes form an integral Picard basis, since their intersection matrix is unimodular. The divisor-order sequence then gives H^1=0 for each factor and H^2 equal to one integer per exceptional curve.

For q=Q^(12m), r=R^n, and exceptional valuations (a_i,b_i), the map from H^2(Y) to H^2 of the bounded-character sheaf has blocks diag(12m a_i,12m a_i+12n b_i). Both entries are positive, so the map is injective over the integers. The quotient sequence proves H^1(S,A_S)=0. An extension of the actual nontrivial punctured torsor would therefore become trivial on restriction, a contradiction. The N=0 restriction is written as beta^(-1)(B minus {0}), avoiding identification of B minus an empty exceptional union with the punctured bidisc. Root opened and read the exact pullback statement, KKN Lemma 1.2.9, printed pages 80–81. The 124 finite marked-blowup checks supplement, and do not replace, the general proof.

## Remaining approaches and exact failures

The explicit corner resolution still lacks a global second-period action. In the linearized Tate coordinate Z=xG(H), the desired second period requires G(H)/G(bH), with b=q r^12 times a unit. Although bH is regular at the new F=0 charts, the numerator has an infinite zero pattern under continuation beyond the collar. No proper global quotient has been constructed from that expression.

Adding the persistent elliptic axis as a further conductor would remove the O-valued r-deformation direction of the exceptional rational curve, so B.4.23 would no longer apply there. Pairing the degree-one axis with an anticanonical elliptic curve in a degree-one del Pezzo surface gives a candidate with smoothing Euler number 12 over a fixed ordinary q, whereas an ordinary torus has Euler number zero. It therefore does not supply the required fixed-base nearby surface. A changed map involving exceptional base directions remains a different, unconstructed possibility.

B.4.26 now requires every missing-origin component to be vertical. A construction that uses a Kato parameter s must therefore allow the original q to vanish identically on such a component, for example through a local relation q=r^a h(s), rather than identifying s with the original q on the central divisor. Compactification over the resulting exceptional direction and the exact punctured-family comparison have not been obtained.

Searches for a maximal-monodromy classification of arbitrary analytic degenerations of two-tori again found the Kähler/canonical hypotheses in Kamenova's Theorem 3.1 and the K3-specific scope in Nishiguchi's constructions. They do not establish the required unrestricted comparison. An anticanonical-pair charge argument is being tested, but obtaining a log-canonical model from an arbitrary analytic filling is itself unproved. These failures remain part of the work record, rather than being promoted to a nonexistence theorem.

## A conditional torus classification and its substantive correction, B.4.28

Root read the entire first candidate and then the entire revised argument. Boundary_d's independent review correctly rejected the candidate's free-product formula for the central fundamental group. Although each normalized rational double curve is simply connected, a triple-point attaching relation can include a path around the whole conductor cycle inside a component. For an Inoue–Hirzebruch component that path can represent its nontrivial fundamental-group generator. The component group need not survive as a free factor. The attempted strengthening using the same formula for arbitrary rational conductors was also withdrawn. The exact rejected passage is retained with a REJECTED warning, and no Proved heading, in evidence/ab_push_log_canonical_rejected_free_product.tex. The earlier B.4.6 does not use that formula: its comparison proof explicitly includes the d2 map in the five-term spectral sequence, and remains unaffected.

The repaired B.4.28 assumes, in addition to a proper semistable degeneration of two-tori with rank(T-1)=2, that the central dualizing line is trivial. Adjunction gives reduced anticanonical conductor pairs. Their open Euler contributions are nonnegative, and the nearby torus has Euler number zero. This leaves toric rational pairs, elliptic ruled or Hopf pairs, and corner blow-ups of hyperbolic or parabolic Inoue pairs. The sum of elliptic normal degrees is zero by d-semistability, whereas every parabolic component contributes a strictly negative degree and all other surviving elliptic types contribute zero; therefore parabolic components are excluded. If every conductor is elliptic, annular twists show that the rank of T-1 is at most the cycle rank of a segment or circle, hence at most one. This excludes the elliptic case.

For a remaining hyperbolic Inoue component, Oda's explicit toric cover supplies a compact T2 mapping torus in its complement of the conductor, with hyperbolic matrix A. Its angular fibre can be chosen in a node cone, where it is exactly the local Clifford torus. Corner blow-ups change the unimodular coordinate basis, not this lattice or A. A compact-core trivialization embeds that marked mapping torus into the nearby surface. Its two fibre-circle images vanish in rational H1 because A-1 is invertible. Since the cohomology ring of a complex two-torus is the exterior algebra on H1, its angular T2 has zero rational H2 image.

The trivial central dualizing line has one global section, as does the canonical line on every nearby torus. Proper analytic base change gives a relative dualizing section whose evaluation is nonzero on the whole central fibre. In xyz=t coordinates its relative two-form is U(x,y,z) dx/x wedge dy/y with U(0) nonzero. Integration on the very same angular torus gives (2pi i)^2 sum a_(k,k,k)t^k, tending to the nonzero iterated residue. This contradicts the zero H2 image. It excludes the hyperbolic components using the nearby torus, without the rejected central fundamental-group claim. The remaining toric pairs give a closed oriented dual surface; the residue signs orient it, and coherent Euler characteristic zero gives genus one.

## Invariant forms do not determine an affine comparison, B.4.29

Root and boundary_d read the entire explicit counterexample. The fibre product of two Tate families has one conifold xy=uv. Its two small-resolution charts have t=xAv and t=uBy, giving a smooth proper semistable total threefold. Both elliptic factors degenerate, so H1 monodromy has rank two. Translation of its first factor by exp(2pi i/t) leaves all relative invariant forms and the Gauss–Manin data unchanged. It also preserves the extending absolute canonical form dt wedge dZ/Z wedge dW/W. Nevertheless the images of the zero section along t=1/j and t=1/(j+1/2) tend to distinct smooth central points, with first multiplicative coordinates 1 and -1.

A hypothetical proper meromorphic graph, pulled back along the extending zero section, would have a unique dominating proper analytic curve. Its normalization is finite of degree one over the disc, hence is the disc itself. It would extend the translated section holomorphically, contradicting the two limits. Thus the failure is genuinely meromorphic, not merely holomorphic. The choice h(t)=exp(1/t) gives an essential horizontal term in a chosen absolute one-form lift while preserving the same relative and canonical data. Missing spacing-command backslashes in the first TeX draft were corrected before integration.

This is a counterexample to control of a marked affine origin by linear invariant-form data. It does not refute extension of the difference of two sections which both extend into the same cap; a common essential translation cancels from that difference. The latter question remains an active possible route to the main unrestricted filling problem.
