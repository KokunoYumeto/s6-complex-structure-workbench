# Primary analytic sources for E.2.22--23

Date opened: 15 November 2026. These are standard analytic inputs, not claimed as new results. The generic-disc propagation and its geometric comparison are supplied in the proof rather than attributed to an unrelated general separatedness theorem.

## Demailly

Demailly, J.-P. (2012). *Complex analytic and differential geometry* (version of 21 June 2012). Author-hosted manuscript: https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~demailly/manuscripts/agbook.pdf.

The original 455-page author-hosted PDF was opened successfully. Its title page gives the 21 June 2012 version. Chapter II, Theorem 4.19, printed pp. 95--96, gives the finite local projection and branched covering description. Chapter II, Theorem 7.6, printed p. 113, says a normal point has singular locus of codimension at least two; this makes normal curves smooth. Definition 7.9 and Theorem 7.12, pp. 114--115, define and construct finite proper analytic normalization. Theorem 8.8, p. 118, is the proper analytic image theorem used for the exceptional loci. Each exact statement and its surrounding proof was opened.

The older source-files URL initially timed out; the successful manuscripts URL above is the actual opened source. An additional 25-page author-hosted manuscript, *Basic results on sheaves and analytic sets*, was opened at https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~demailly/manuscripts/sheaves.pdf; its Theorem 4.8, p. 20, gives the same local parametrization statement. The main bibliography uses the fuller 2012 manuscript to avoid redundant references.

## Independent check of the curve construction

Lebl, J. (2026). *Hermitian forms meet several complex variables: Minicourse on CR geometry using Hermitian forms* (version of 19 May 2026). Author-hosted PDF: https://www.jirka.org/scv-mini/scv-mini.pdf.

The original author-hosted 62-page PDF and the author's book page were opened. Theorem 1.4.10, printed p. 17, states local parametrization; Exercise 1.4.3, printed p. 19, explicitly asks for the consequence that any sufficiently nearby point of an irreducible analytic germ lies on an irreducible curve through the distinguished point. E.2.23 proves this consequence using a finite projection and a line. The exercise is a comparison source, not an omitted proof. The general wording near Puiseux normalization is not imported beyond an irreducible curve germ; the proof instead uses finite normalization and normal-curve smoothness from Demailly.

## Exact scope of the bad-set construction

Every removal in E.2.22 is the proper image of a closed analytic locus on the parameter domain currently under consideration. Later exceptional sets may accumulate at parameters deleted earlier. Their union is not silently asserted to be discrete on the original disc. The proof fills the finite sequence of layers in reverse order, using the already established actual one-sided completion theorem.

The relative normal-crossing test works on a reduced embedded TOTAL hypersurface. A finite immersive normalization yields smooth local sheets; independent vertical normal covectors give relative normal-crossing coordinates. No assertion that normalization commutes with arbitrary base change, and no unsupported seminormality or non-Kähler good-reduction theorem, is used.
