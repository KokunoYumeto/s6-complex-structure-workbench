# B.4.30: noncompletion of the fixed partial filling

## Result and exact scope

The particular actual partial filling of B.4.19 cannot be completed properly over its missing origin, even after finite coordinate roots and even with a three-dimensional origin fibre. This result uses both prescribed punctured axial models. It does not on its own classify or exclude an arbitrary r-only filling whose r=0 axial model changes after resolving the marked map.

## Proof mechanism

The original analytic logarithmic Riemann–Hilbert theorem IKN2005 Theorem6.3 identifies relative log-de Rham H1 with the locally free nilpotent extension without a Kähler or projectivity hypothesis. A low-degree exact sequence and the absence of t-torsion in R1fO make the closed relative log1-form lattice saturated, so it equals the known normalized Hodge subbundle. Full-rank monodromy then forces all local meridian residues to be integral cocharacters. A convergent integer-weight calculation gives monomials times holomorphic units as local exponential primitives; a finite chain of charts proves logarithmic extension of the difference of two sections both extending into the same cap.

A direct excess-one coefficient-ideal argument, following the opened original Parusiński preprint, makes the cap equidimensional by a base modification supported at the origin. It does not require a full flattening theorem. Finite local slice surfaces are normalized and therefore finite-flat over the smooth surface base. Generic semistable reduction uses a simultaneous root whose degree is the product of the multiplicity common multiple and the finite-section splitting bound, followed by compatible height-one lattice subdivisions. Norms extend a common multiple of every trace difference. Hartogs for A-sections fills isolated crossing points: H1 of the punctured bidisc with constant Y coefficients is zero, so a logarithmic character lift exists. These transition differences glue an extending positive power of the actual torsor, contradicting B.4.24 and B.4.27.
