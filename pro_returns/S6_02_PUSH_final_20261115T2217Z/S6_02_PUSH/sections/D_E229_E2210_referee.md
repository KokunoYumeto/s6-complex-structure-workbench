## E.2.9

The elementary vanishing is valid on a compact reduced irreducible analytic space: the decreasing finite-dimensional section spaces stabilize; their intersection is zero by Krull intersection at a point of the nonempty effective Cartier divisor and analytic continuation on the irreducible normalization. It does not require ampleness, a Kähler metric, or normality.

Every fibre is mS with S a reduced irreducible Cartier divisor in the smooth threefold. The finite ideal filtration has the exact vector-bundle quotients V|S tensor O_S(−jS), so the same negative-divisor vanishing applies even to nonreduced fibres. Local self-branches do not defeat the non-zero-divisor condition, as E.2.8's normalization argument verifies it branch by branch.

Flatness over the smooth curve follows from torsion-freeness over its local DVR. A Cartier fibre is Cohen–Macaulay and Gorenstein. The dualizing bundle is K_X restricted to the fibre, up to the harmless constant target tangent factor; therefore compact analytic Serre duality yields exactly the stated H2/H0 pairing for every fibre, including its nilpotents.

The bad sets for H2 are closed by upper semicontinuity and nested by injectivity of multiplication on dual sections. Their empty intersection and compactness of P1 give a common N. This is an ordinary compactness argument, not an appeal to Grauert's corrected closedness statement. Constant-zero cohomology and analytic base change give R2=0; higher direct images vanish by fibre dimension. A sufficiently positive base twist kills H1 of the coherent R1 on P1, hence global H2 and H3.

The negative horizontal twist independently kills H0. The exponential sequence identifies Pic(X) with the additive vector space H1(O_X), since H1(X,Z)=H2(X,Z)=0. More strongly, the explicit affine Čech cocycles give an actual holomorphic line-bundle family on X×C joining the two good classes, so no universal Picard-space claim is needed. The finite free cohomology complex makes each bad locus a proper closed analytic subset of C. Their finite union cannot be all of C, giving the simultaneous vanishing.

All line bundles are topologically trivial, so the Euler characteristic stays chi(B). For B=T_X, analytic HRR gives chi(T_X)=c3/2=1 because c1 and c2 vanish in cohomology. Simultaneous H0=H2=H3=0 would give chi=−h1≤0. This proves the contradiction. No unsupported holomorphic-fibration assertion of CDP is used.

## E.2.10

For negative slope, a horizontal fixed component forces Kodaira dimension minus infinity and non-Kähler general fibre, hence b1=1; E.2.8 then proves every reduced fibre irreducible. E.2.9 forbids the horizontal component. The remainder of E.2.6 uses only verticality, so its connected Cartier-divisor limit removes the vertical fixed part and forces degree one. Evaluation and local flatness then extend the relative algebraic reduction; the smooth general central fibre is diffeomorphic to T4 and hence a complex torus by surface classification. E.2.7 applies for both signs and excludes the zero and outer period ends.

For a common smooth limit, intrinsicness of the reduction identifies the pencil line L. Its non-torsion follows from a nonzero section with zeros in a positive power. Comparing the two canonical-root identities yields equal D/A and then the already proved arithmetic recovers the ordered pair. This does not identify the central manifold with the reference interior-period model and does not prove invariance along arbitrary new deformation branches; the source correctly retains those separate gaps.

No numerical experiment is necessary for this proof. The conclusion is based on the exact analytic cohomology, duality, and divisor arguments above.
