# Independent full-source referee: arbitrary proper noncompletion

Reviewer: boundary_d. Date: 15 November 2026.

Source reviewed in full: sections/B_global_noncompletion.tex, reserved B.4.33.

**Verdict: PASS.** The argument excludes any proper Hausdorff complex-analytic filling of the actual punctured six-sphere family after a finite parameter cover. It no longer assumes the unknown r-axis completion is B.4.19. The crucial added input is the zero global radial-loop class in B.4.32, and only its invariant γ evaluation is used in the final comparison.

## 1. Arbitrary analytic source and the intrinsic marked map

The smooth connected punctured family is irreducible, so its closure gives the unique reduced main component. Normalization and resolution are proper and can be chosen to preserve that smooth punctured locus. The resulting smooth fourfold has nonzero parameter function; its map to the disc is flat without a reducedness assumption on the central divisor.

B.4.4 applies in exactly this scope. Its relative bi-anticanonical bundle exists on the smooth fourfold, its proper direct image is torsion-free of rank two, and the three labelled critical values give the meromorphic target normalization. Resolving the marked graph therefore gives the actual q-map, not a freely chosen or essentially varying projective coordinate. Further resolution retains central multiplicities.

The finite parameter cover convention is valid. An arbitrary positive power N is dominated by w=R^(6N), which is the B.4.19 parameter r followed by r=R^N. A general finite disc-germ map becomes a coordinate power after absorbing its nonvanishing unit.

## 2. Excess fibres and finite traces

There are finitely many central divisor components. A nonconstant holomorphic map from any irreducible three-dimensional component to the marked curve has level sets of pure dimension two. Thus three-dimensional fibres occur only at the finitely many constant component images. The cusp disc can be shrunk to retain only its own centre among them and among the additional finite values of B.4.31.

The coefficient-ideal equidimensionalization from B.4.30 is applicable: the dominant total space is pure four-dimensional and the possible origin fibre has dimension at most three because it lies in the nonzero-parameter divisor. No projective flattening theorem is required. Finite base changes and normalizations do not increase fibre dimensions. The resulting normalized finite slice surfaces are Cohen–Macaulay and finite-flat over the smooth modified base, so their positive degrees and traces are legitimate.

The finite cover of a neighbourhood of the compact inverse image K of the origin can cover the entire modified base after shrinking the original bidisc. This follows from properness of the base modification: the image of the closed complement of that neighbourhood avoids the origin. It justifies one finite collection of trace degrees and norm multipliers. When no blow-up is required, K is a point and the same argument applies.

A trace of points in a torsor is intrinsically a section of the corresponding contracted power. It does not require selecting a global origin. The ordinary trace section is holomorphic even at ramification, because locally the symmetric sum is invariant under branch permutations. The proof uses this standard finite-flat construction only on the ordinary group locus.

## 3. Every generic divisor gives the correct bounded differences

At an exceptional base component both Q and R have positive order, so the actual monodromy is full rank two with primitive invariant lattice Zw⊕Zδ. The B.4.30 saturation and explicit monomial primitive apply.

At the strict Q-axis the monodromy is likewise full rank two, and the underlying r≠0 family is the known actual cusp. The normalized q-root may introduce toric modifications, but these preserve the period pairing and the section σ through the central toric open orbit.

At the strict R-axis the proof does not falsely replace a rank-three invariant kernel by rank two. B.4.31 proves that the actual local angular images are all integral multiples of δ. The first normalized form consequently has zero residue, and the second has integral residue. The exponentiated primitives lie exactly in O*×M_R^gp. In particular the first character is a unit across R=0, with zero radial winding, rather than a general logarithmic character.

New angular cycles after the local root and toric refinement have rational images generated by the old angular cycles; integral images in the primitive δ line are integral multiples of δ. Thus this residue constraint survives the reductions.

The finite chart-chain comparison connects arbitrary extending sections because the proper normal map to the normal surface base has trivial finite Stein factor: its generic torus fibres are connected. This remains true after retaining the normalized main component under a root. The common root/norm argument is local in the coordinate along a boundary curve and retains that coordinate holomorphically. It does not use a global nonprojective semistable-reduction theorem.

## 4. The derived torsor, rather than an assumed cap identification

After a common positive norm multiplier, all trace differences extend on the generic parts of the boundary. The character sheaf is precisely

    M_(D_Q+E)^gp × M_(D_Q+D_R+E)^gp.

The Hartogs proof retains the constant period lattice Y. On a punctured bidisc H¹(Y)=0 gives a character lift. Dividing by the fixed boundary monomials produces a holomorphic unit and its inverse; both extend. This preserves the forbidden first-character order along D_R. Uniqueness modulo one constant period preserves the cocycle identities.

The resulting torsor Q is defined on all of the modified base S and its ordinary restriction is the actual contracted power P^D by the explicit trace identifications. This construction never identifies its unknown R-axis model with the rotating model. B.4.27 gives H¹(S,A_S)=0 and hence a global section h.

This is the point at which the earlier affine-origin warning is genuinely resolved: not by claiming that an old origin extends, but by constructing a new origin and controlling only its radial loop.

## 5. The two loop evaluations are independent and correctly normalized

Near a generic strict R point, h differs from a multiplied cap trace by an A_S-section. A local character lift has first component a unit and second component R^k times a unit, so this difference changes the radial label only by δ. B.4.32 then gives ν_R(h−D o_ord)=0.

The rotating origin and the ordinary origin are not literally identical. The source correctly inserts the exact B.4.19.5 comparison G(s)+6vδ, whose radial contribution is purely δ. This transfers zero γ evaluation to h−D o, with o the rotating origin. Only after this step is the exact relation σ−o=F used.

On the strict Q-axis, σ is an actual extending section of the known cusp, including its normalized root fan. Its differences with cap traces extend by the same two-section argument, with the finite norm multipliers incorporated in D. Hence Dσ is a section of Q on B_r and h−Dσ is an actual A(B_r)-section.

The Q-ghost map sends u to (12m,0) and γ̂ to (0,12m). It is injective on the period lattice. The B.4.24 cocycle argument therefore kills the radial Y-connecting class of h−Dσ. Passing from the multiplicative period lattice to full H1 can add w and δ windings, but both are annihilated by the γ covector. Thus the asserted second zero is exactly what follows and exactly what is needed.

## 6. The contradiction and its scope

The exact ordinary identity is

    (h−D o) − (h−Dσ) = D F.

The positive R-loop corresponds to v↦v+n and the actual explicit difference is

    F(s,v+n)−F(s,v)=nγ̂−6n(n−1)δ

as an additive period label at the initial parameter. Its γ evaluation is n>0. Both terms on the left have zero γ radial evaluation, while the right has Dn>0. Every root, trace degree and norm multiplier is positive, so no auxiliary operation kills this integer.

The proof allows arbitrary additional central components and multiplicities and assumes neither Kählerness nor a log structure on the unknown cap. It derives the associated log torsor through extendible pairwise differences and uses global monodromy only for the affine loop class. Essential translations with zero loop defect remain allowed and do not affect the contradiction.

No assertion about an unknown central fibre's integral topology is inferred. If B.4.33 is accepted and integrated, the requested central-topology computation for an actual proper filling is vacuous because such a filling does not exist.
