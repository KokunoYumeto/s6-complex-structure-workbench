# Proper equidimensionalization in the excess-one bidisc situation

**Status: proved auxiliary lemma for the B.4.30 referee, 15 November 2026.** This file is an independent proof note, not an additional numbered paper result. It replaces the invocation of a general flattening theorem in the preliminary working source. The source need not become flat.

## Precise statement

Let B be a sufficiently small smooth complex bidisc with centre 0. Let f:Z→B be proper and surjective, with Z reduced and pure of dimension four and every irreducible component under consideration dominating B. Suppose every fibre away from 0 is pure of dimension two, and every local fibre dimension at 0 is at most three. Then, after shrinking B, there is a proper modification S→B, which is an isomorphism off 0 and is dominated by a finite sequence of point blow-ups of B, such that the dominating strict transform Z_S→S is proper and all its local fibre dimensions are exactly two.

The conclusion is equidimensionality, not flatness. Every point of S nevertheless has a neighbourhood carrying a positive-degree finite multisection. After normalization, each such multisection is finite and flat over the smooth surface base.

In the intended application, the central r-divisor of a putative filling bounds the local origin fibre dimension by three. The prescribed partial family away from the origin has two-dimensional fibres. Reduction, retention of the dominating component, and proper resolution do not alter the prescribed smooth ordinary locus; the lemma is applied before any subsequent source resolution that might create excess fibres.

## 1. A finite local projection and its actual image

Fix z in the compact origin fibre where the local fibre dimension is three. Choose three sufficiently general holomorphic linear functions p=(p_1,p_2,p_3) in a local embedding of Z. The germ

    (f,p):(Z,z)→(B×C³,(0,0))

is finite. Take finite representatives. Its reduced image V has pure dimension four in the smooth five-dimensional target, and therefore has a local reduced hypersurface equation F(y,x)=0. All local image components are included in this equation; none is added merely to obtain a vanishing function. In a product representative write

    F(y,x)=Σ_β a_β(y)x^β.

Let I⊂O_B be the ideal generated by all coefficient germs a_β. The Noetherian property makes this a finitely generated coherent ideal after shrinking the base. Crucially, the vanishing set of I is supported at 0. Indeed, if every coefficient vanished at some y≠0 sufficiently near 0, then F(y,·) would be identically zero on the vertical polydisc. Thus V_y would contain an open three-dimensional set. The finite, surjective map onto the actual image would then have a three-dimensional fibre over y, contradicting the assumed two-dimensional fibres of f away from 0.

Using an arbitrary equation that merely vanishes on V would not prove this support assertion: such an equation could acquire an extraneous vertical hypersurface. This is why the reduced hypersurface equation of the finite image is chosen.

## 2. Convergence after dividing out the coefficient ideal

There is no formal-convergence gap in the coefficient operation. In the convergent local ring R=O_{B×C³,(0,0)}, the function F lies in I R. To see this, complete R/IR in the ideal (x_1,x_2,x_3). Every coefficient of the image of F is zero, so its image in this completion is zero. The Krull intersection theorem, or faithful flatness of the corresponding completion, makes the original map into the completion injective. Thus F is already zero in R/IR.

Consequently, after a modification on which I is invertible with local generator a, the quotient

    F'=F/a

is a convergent holomorphic function on the pulled-back product. Its Taylor coefficients generate the unit ideal at each base point: the pulled-back coefficient ideal is exactly (a). Therefore the restriction F'_s to any vertical C³ fibre is never identically zero. Its zero set has fibre dimension at most two.

The strict transform of V is contained in {F'=0}. The base change of the original finite map remains finite; its dominating strict transform remains finite onto its image in that strict-transform hypersurface. Hence the dominating strict transform of the chosen source neighbourhood has all fibre dimensions at most two.

## 3. One modification for the compact origin fibre

The locus of points whose local fibre dimension exceeds two is closed. Properness and compactness of the origin fibre let us cover the relevant locus by finitely many finite-projection neighbourhoods as above, producing finitely many coherent ideals I_1,…,I_m. At points whose original fibre dimension is already two, the base-changed fibre cannot increase its dimension: it is a subspace of the original fibre times a fixed base point.

Blow up the product I_1⋯I_m. This modification is supported at 0. Each factor becomes invertible. Indeed, in any local domain, if a product of nonzero ideals is invertible, each factor is invertible as a fractional ideal: if IJ=(a), then I·(J/a)=R. An invertible ideal in a local ring is principal. Thus all the preceding divisions work on the same modification.

Every point of the new origin fibre maps to a point in one of the finitely many original neighbourhoods; the finite local-projection estimates apply there. Resolve and, if necessary, normalize the modified surface base, and dominate it by a finite sequence of point blow-ups of the original smooth bidisc. Such further base change cannot increase a fibre dimension, and passage to a dominating strict transform only takes a closed subspace of a fibre.

The resulting dominating source is pure of dimension four, while the base is smooth of dimension two. The analytic dimension inequality gives local fibre dimension at least two at every point. Combined with the established upper bound, this gives equality everywhere. Properness is preserved under the modifications and strict-transform closure. The entire construction is the identity away from 0.

## 4. Finite multisections without flatness of the source

At any point of an equidimensional fourfold-to-surface map, a sufficiently general local analytic slice of codimension two has an isolated fibre over the given base point. Take a finite representative of a dominating slice component. Its image has dimension two and therefore contains a neighbourhood of the base point; the finite map has positive generic degree.

Normalize this two-dimensional slice. Analytic normalization is finite, so the composite remains a finite surjective map to the smooth surface base. A normal complex surface is Cohen–Macaulay: Serre's S_2 condition is exactly depth two at its two-dimensional local rings. A finite module of dimension two over a regular local ring of dimension two, with depth two, is free by the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula. Thus the normalized finite slice is finite-flat, of constant positive degree.

These are the finite-flat covers used to take traces in B.4.30. Flatness of the full fourfold is neither asserted nor needed. Later source resolutions may introduce excess fibres, but the finite multisections have already been chosen; their proper meromorphic lifts are used on the generic parts of exceptional base curves.
