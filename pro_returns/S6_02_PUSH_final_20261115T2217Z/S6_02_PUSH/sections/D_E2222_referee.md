## Pencil and finite discriminant

The total space over the contractible disc has the homotopy type of
S6, so the exponential sequence gives the unique relative fractional
canonical line. The coherent direct image is torsion-free of rank two
and hence a bundle on the disc. The multiplication exact sequence gives
the claimed injection into each central section space. The common zero
locus of an evaluated frame is proper over the disc, so its proper
analytic image is a discrete exceptional set. Outside it the map has
pure two-dimensional fibres and is flat by the smooth-source/smooth-
target dimension criterion. The Stein factor is finite birational to
the normal target, giving connected fibres.

The relative critical image is finite over the remaining parameter
curve: every individual nonconstant proper map to P1 has finitely many
critical values, and the image is proper. Its isolated components,
branch values and singular values therefore give further discrete
analytic deletion layers. A finite etale labelling cover is legitimate;
each connected component meets the inverse image of U and proper images
of subsequent discrete sets remain discrete.

The surface-classification argument is sufficient. Ordinary fibres have
the topology of a two-torus, b1=4, and integral canonical class zero.
Even b1 makes them Kahler. Exceptional and ruled curves are incompatible
with that canonical class; a nonzero pluricanonical section has no zeros
by positivity against a Kahler form, so positive Kodaira dimension is
impossible. The remaining Kodaira-dimension-zero classification selects
the two-torus. No higher-dimensional deformation-invariance theorem for
complex tori is silently used.

## Finite root normalization

Every component of a critical Cartier divisor dominates the parameter
curve, since a vertical divisor would be an entire X_s and make its
pencil constant. Properness and the known irreducible fibre give one
total component, with the known multiplicity. Locally q=a^m u, so the
normalization of r^m=q is precisely the disjoint graphs
r=zeta a u^(1/m). These graphs are smooth copies of the actual total
space even if the reduced divisor has not yet been shown smooth. The
cyclic deck group permutes them freely. Only the root-map critical locus
remains to be removed; it is proper over the parameter curve and absent
over U. This provides an exact analytic test, not an assumed extension
of the known root model.

## Exact generic normal-crossing test

The total cusp is a reduced embedded hypersurface. Its finite
normalization is proper. Successive proper images of its singular
locus, parameter critical locus and relative derivative rank-drop locus
are discrete, since absent over U. After these removals it is a smooth
relative immersion.

For a finite map which is locally an embedding, the diagonal in its
fibre square is open as well as closed. Hence the loci of ordered,
pairwise distinct preimages in the two-, three- and four-fold fibre
products are closed analytic and proper over the parameter curve.
The relative normal-line rank tests on the first two and exclusion of
the last are therefore proper analytic tests. They ensure at most three
smooth local sheets with independent vertical conormals.

The essential final point is correct: the total divisor is an embedded
reduced hypersurface in a smooth ambient space, so its local principal
ideal is the product of the distinct local sheet equations. Independent
vertical conormals make these equations and the original parameter part
of a coordinate system. The result is exactly q=x, xy or xyz, up to a
unit. Thus reduced relative normal crossings follow without assuming
normalization/base-change compatibility or an unproved seminormality
criterion.

## Period continuation and completion

The normalized toric-pair argument, conductor equalizer, torus action
and integer characters use holomorphic identities which hold on a
nonempty open set; none intrinsically requires a whole punctured disc.
The constant marked peripheral representation preserves the invariant
lattice generated by w and delta. Its two-column determinant is tested
only at a single ordinary target point. Its zero set is an analytic
discrete subset of the parameter curve.

After deleting it, normalization at that one point extracts c(s).
Equality of the actual and reference holomorphic period-kernel maps
into a Grassmannian then propagates on the universal cover of the full
ordinary base times the parameter cover. This is stronger and safer
than assuming global independence of the two columns in advance. The
kernel equality itself proves their independence everywhere. The actual
compact tori enforce the admissible parameter domain. The already
proved toric reconstruction and additive comparison apply locally
throughout the current connected parameter domain; their discrete
affine and collar classes are transported from U.

There are only finitely many deletion layers. A layer is closed discrete
on the domain present when it is deleted, but later layers need not be
globally discrete on the original disc. Filling the last layer first
by E.2.21, then continuing in reverse order, handles exactly this issue.
At each stage a small punctured disc avoiding all earlier deleted layers
consists of known members. This proves the stated disc propagation and
does not appeal to an unjustified curve-selection principle for an
arbitrary open subset.
