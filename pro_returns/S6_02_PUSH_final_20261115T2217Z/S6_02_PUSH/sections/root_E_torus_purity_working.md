# Purity at an isolated base point with a good torus Jacobian

15 November 2026, seventh working hour. Root proof; E independently supplied a simplification of the isotropy step. Full candidate source is E_torus_purity.tex, reserved statement E.2.11. Integration awaits full-source referee.

The precise hypotheses are a proper flat map from a smooth (n+2)-fold to a bidisc, connected n-dimensional fibres, a smooth proper holomorphic torus group G over the entire bidisc, and an actual G-torsor structure outside the origin. No section of that torsor is needed. The conclusion is that the map is smooth and the torsor action extends across the origin.

The argument extends the invariant vertical vector fields coefficientwise by Hartogs across the codimension-two fibre. Compact fibres give completeness; period identities extend by holomorphic uniqueness. A fixed real torus acts by holomorphic automorphisms because each real linear combination of the holomorphic period vectors is a holomorphic Lie section. The identity component of any stabilizer is a compact complex subgroup of the central torus. Its representation on the scheme-fibre Zariski tangent is holomorphic, hence trivial; it is trivial on the quotient mapping to the base tangent. Compact averaging splits the representation and then averages holomorphic coordinates to show that the group would act trivially near the point. This contradicts freeness on the punctured base.

The independent vertical fields give local orbit/transverse coordinates by their commuting flows and the inverse function theorem. The transverse map of surfaces has isolated fibres by equidimensionality, hence is finite. Its Jacobian, if zero, gives a curve of critical values. This excludes a sole critical value at the bidisc origin. The finite-stabilizer argument is repeated after smoothness to establish a torsor, not merely a submersion.

The smooth extension of G is essential. It is being verified from the actual source period coordinates in E.2.12, not inferred merely from trivial monodromy. B.4.15 does not contradict the lemma because that model is not jointly flat. No general discriminant-purity claim is made for arbitrary proper flat maps, which can have discriminant of higher codimension.

Queries included complex tori with pure discriminant, torus fibrations and codimension two, holomorphic torus isolated critical values, and Hartogs extension of torus actions. No exact theorem matching these analytic hypotheses was found. No priority is claimed. The proof supplies its compact-group linearization by averaging and its transverse branch argument by an explicit Jacobian divisor, rather than importing a groupoid slice theorem or algebraic purity theorem.
