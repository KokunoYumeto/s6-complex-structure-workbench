# Independent root check of E.2.5 and E.2.6

Checked on 15 November 2026. The hypotheses are a proper holomorphic submersion over a disc with smooth six-sphere fibres and a fixed known multiplicity pair on the puncture. The conclusions are restricted to this one-sided specialization and to a common smooth junction; they do not settle arbitrary deformation chains.

The relative root exists on the total space, not merely fibre by fibre. Ehresmann triviality over the disc gives vanishing integral H1 and H2 of the total space. The exponential sequence therefore identifies its Picard group with the complex vector space H1(O). Multiplication by the nonzero canonical weight D is bijective. This gives the unique root of K^A, and fibrewise uniqueness identifies it with the actual coarse pencil line. The direct image is coherent and torsion-free of generic rank two. The exact parameter-multiplication sequence gives an injection of its central fibre into H0 of the central line bundle, even if H0 jumps. Thus both central sections persist independently.

The primary CDP source, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.11179, Theorem 2.1, was opened independently by root; its statement concerns a non-holomorphic meromorphic map to P1 and forces c3=0. The independently opened Lehn–Rollenske–Schinko source, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.09719, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2, states the same obstruction explicitly. Their different assertion about a holomorphic algebraic reduction is not used. Because the central six-sphere has c3=2, the ratio of the two sections is holomorphic. Two algebraically independent meromorphic functions would give a local rank-two map, whose coordinate ratio has a genuine indeterminacy; thus the algebraic dimension is one. Stein factorization, H1=0, and connected fibres give the intrinsic P1 reduction.

For positive D the restriction of the root identity to a very general smooth surface fibre makes a positive canonical power effective. A positive Kodaira dimension of that fibre would supply two independent fibrewise canonical sections. A sufficiently positive twist of their coherent pushforward to P1 supplies global sections whose ratio varies on the fibre, contradicting algebraic reduction. Therefore the fibre has Kodaira dimension zero. Integral c1=0 excludes exceptional curves; minimal surface classification makes the canonical line torsion. An effective torsion divisor is zero. This removes every horizontal component of the fixed divisor.

The remaining step uses an actual proper divisor, not a formal limiting cycle. A generic combination of the relative frame has a flat Cartier zero divisor with smooth connected general torus fibre. It has no vertical component and is generically reduced, hence is reduced and irreducible. Its normalization is proper; its Stein factor over the normal disc is finite birational and therefore the disc. Its central support is connected. The proposed central zero divisor is the disjoint union of e smooth fibres and the vertical fixed part, chosen over other target points. Connectedness forces e=1 and an empty fixed part. The evaluation map is consequently base-point-free and extends the relative fibration. Local flatness follows from the two regular base parameters, or from the local flatness criterion applied first to the disc parameter and then to the torsion-free surface map to the target curve.

The common positive-slope junction calculation uses the same intrinsic pencil line on the central manifold. It is non-torsion, since a positive power has a nonzero section with a nonempty zero set. Equality of its two fractional canonical expressions forces equality of the slopes and hence of the multiplicity pairs by E.2.3. A common positive/negative junction would have sections of opposite canonical powers; their product forces the canonical line to be torsion. The Picard group of the smooth six-sphere is torsion-free, while a trivial canonical line would contradict Stokes' theorem for its nonzero holomorphic three-form. This checks the opposite-sign exclusion.

No gap was found in these restricted claims. The proof does not establish persistence on an arbitrary outgoing branch from an unconstructed central member, and it does not remove the horizontal fixed-divisor possibility for negative canonical slope. E.2.4 must remain Open.
