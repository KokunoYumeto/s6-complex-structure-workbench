## Smooth fixed-pair limits in E.2

E.2.11 was written by root and independently reviewed by classification_e and boundary_d. It is a conditional purity statement: a proper flat torus torsor over a punctured complex bidisc, with smooth total space and a smooth torus group extending over the whole bidisc, extends as a smooth torsor. Invariant vertical vector fields extend across a codimension-two analytic set. Their period identities extend and produce the actual holomorphic torus-group action. Compact complex isotropy has trivial tangent representation on the fibre tangent space; the base quotient is also fixed. Compact averaging then makes any connected isotropy trivial. The resulting full-dimensional orbits reduce a possible singularity to a finite map between smooth surface germs, whose branch divisor cannot be supported at one point. Finite isotropy is excluded by the same linearization after smoothness. A good Jacobian is a hypothesis, not a consequence of flatness.

E.2.12 applies that lemma using the actual marked periods and the interior parameter limit. It proves that there are no additional central critical values beyond the three labelled limits. It distinguishes an ordinary constant source torus, which may be nonalgebraic, from a finite-root constant source torus, which splits up to isogeny as two elliptic curves. The cusp cannot be a constant source point because its nontrivial unipotent H1 monodromy cannot disappear. Both independent source-level referees passed.

E.2.15 excludes the remaining ordinary-source collapse. Consequently the target does not collapse: the three labelled critical values remain distinct and both finite multiple fibres are controlled by their actual smooth root charts.
