## Topology and the exact cover

The relative normal-crossing coordinates provide real lifts of the parameter directions which annihilate dq, including along every cusp stratum. A partition of unity preserves these linear requirements. Properness over a closed smaller q-disc gives complete transport along a sufficiently short parameter path, tangent to its boundary. This transports the entire cusp body, its strata and the regular-fibre inclusion map.

For the prescribed toric cover, each maximal chart restricted to the cusp disc is `{|z0 z1 z2|<epsilon}` and is star-shaped. Every chart intersection contains the same connected dense regular torus `(C*)^2 x Delta*`; its components are open and each meets that dense torus, so the intersection is connected. The intersection with a finite union of previous charts is connected for the same reason. Van Kampen proves simple connectedness of finite unions, and compactness of a loop reduces the infinite cover to this case. The known free proper period action therefore makes this the universal cover. Its regular torus inclusion kills exactly Zw+Zdelta, and the q-meridian bounds the explicit section `(z0,z1,z2)=(q,1,1)`.

This exact calculation is already recorded, with all units, in the retained course source `integral-monodromy-and-fundamental-group.md`, section 6, Lemma 6.1 and Theorem 6.2. It should be used as a checked derivation, not attributed to an independent authoritative theorem merely because it appears in that exposition. The underlying primary construction is Engel's original source, Proposition labelled `mum-cor`, with the two vanishing cycles identified in section 6 before Proposition labelled `fundamental-group`. C.3.8 independently supplies the required cusp deformation retraction.

The torus action lifts because its two fundamental loops die in the cusp body. The lifted regular action is free and transitive, since its stabilizer lattice is exactly the primitive kernel of the regular-fibre fundamental-group map. A lifted section trivializes the regular cover. Connectedness of H makes all deck transformations commute with it. Thus deck transformations are exactly the marked period multipliers; changing the affine origin does not change them.

## No essential-singularity assumption

The adjacent-component argument is sufficient and should remain explicit. At a general conductor point the stabilizer is the primitive connected toric one-parameter subgroup. Its two normal weights are +1 and -1, since these are the intrinsic primitive boundary weights on the two normalized toric surfaces. Compact-circle averaging gives these exact local linear coordinates. An invariant unit in q can be absorbed while preserving the weights, yielding q=ab.

Choose the two special origins `(q/b0,b0,z0)` and `(a0,q/a0,z0)`. The stabilizer element with parameter `a0 b0/q` sends one to the other. A path in that subgroup can keep both a and b in the fixed crossing chart: enlarge one modulus while decreasing the other, and adjust phase without changing either modulus. Thus the group element is computed by the local action itself, even though its parameter grows when q tends to zero. The origins' comparison is an integral cocharacter of q times a unit. Comparing either special origin with any chosen origin in its central component adds a holomorphic H-valued unit by the inverse-function theorem at the central free orbit. All of this holds on the full (q,s) bidisc.

Every component is reached by finitely many adjacent crossings, so its comparison with the initial origin is meromorphic with integral q-orders and nonvanishing holomorphic remaining factors. Different paths agree on the regular cover, hence give identical meromorphic characters. This proves, rather than assumes, the absence of an essential singularity.

At a triple point compact-torus averaging supplies eigen-coordinates with constant primitive characters. The invariant functions are functions of xyz and s; reducedness allows the invariant unit in q to be absorbed into a coordinate. A global character has a Laurent-monomial expression times a function of q,s. Evaluating on a section with two fixed nonzero coordinates, and using the preceding origin comparison, shows that this last function is a q-power times a holomorphic unit. The ray valuations and unimodularity then make every dual toric coordinate pull back to the corresponding actual coordinate times a unit. These are local biholomorphisms through every stratum.

## Uniformity and exact scope

There is no need to take an infimum of infinitely many chart radii. One compact central normalized dP6, together with finitely many overlaps for deck generators, has a finite chart cover. Choose one radius for that finite data. The global regular character map already exists on all H x Delta*. Its equivariance there forces the chart extensions to be equivariant through the centre. Deck translates of the finite collection cover the full central unfolding and use the identical reference period action. The maps are locally invertible and bijective on all labelled central orbits as well as on the regular locus, so they identify the actual and reference analytic cusp neighbourhoods.

No arbitrary quotient-smoothness assertion, infinite-radius inference, or inference from an abstract central surface alone is used. A global central threefold comparison still needs the finite-root and ordinary charts and their affine overlaps. Separation under arbitrary connected deformation chains additionally needs a justified propagation argument; closure under full punctured-disc specializations alone should not be called topological closedness without that argument.
