# Part 3 working derivations

## Page-cover correction (independently derived)

Let C be the smooth compactification of b^3=a^4+1, and let t=b^3. The map C to P1_t has degree 12. Over t=0 it has four points of index 3; over t=1 three points of index 4; over infinity one point of index 12. These assertions follow from the local equations and gcd(3,4)=1 at infinity. Riemann-Hurwitz gives 2g-2=-24+8+9+11=4, so g=3.

Pullback of the elliptic surface with fibres IV*, III, I1, followed by relative minimalization, has good reduction over the first seven points and one I12 fibre at the last. Thus the stated assertion that this compactified pullback has twelve I1 fibres is false. The exact comparison map is the degree12 branched base change followed by resolution and contraction of vertical (-1) curves. Its restriction away from the three branch values is the ordinary pullback. The multiplicative local model xy=q becomes xy=z^12; its minimal resolution has the cycle of twelve rational components. No unspecified perturbation of the elliptic fibration is included in this statement.

The calculation of χ(O), height and relative Pontryagin classes will be completed below. The correction of the singular-fibre configuration alone does not determine the class in Theta7.


## Correction to the scope of the preceding refutation, 2026-11-17

The prompt specifies the curve b³=a⁴+1 but does not explicitly fix its covering function to the AC26 base. The assertion above that the prompt is thereby refuted was too broad. It proves the I12 conclusion only for t=b³. Composing that map with the Möbius transformation carrying (0,1,infinity) to (0,1,t*) for a regular t* keeps the 3 and 4 branch profiles at the finite special fibres and makes the cover unramified over the source I1. The resulting minimal elliptic pullback has twelve I1 fibres. Both maps have genus-three domain. The actual open-book covering map must therefore be derived before deciding the prompt's page assertion. The earlier text is retained as an explicit record of this corrected inference.
