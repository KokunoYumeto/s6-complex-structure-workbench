Proposition 22.51 (Maximal monodromy with trivial central dualizing bundle). [tag 030X]
Let p : X →∆be a proper holomorphic map from a smooth complex threefold, smooth over ∆∗,
whose nonzero fibres are complex two-tori. Suppose that D = p−1(0) is a reduced normal-crossing
divisor and that ωD ≃OD. If its monodromy on H1(Xt, Q) satisfies
rank(T −1) = 2,
(22.64)
then every normalization component of D is a smooth rational toric surface, its conductor is its
toric boundary, and the dual complex of D is a topological two-torus. In particular all normalized
components are Kähler.
The trivialization of ωD in the hypotheses is the central section used in the residue and
nearby-period arguments below.
Proof. 1. Write Vi for the compact smooth normalization components and Ci for their reduced
conductor divisors. Adjunction gives
KVi + Ci ∼0.
(22.65)
If D is smooth, the map is a proper submersion near its compact central fibre and its
monodromy is trivial. Otherwise every Ci is nonempty, by connectedness. Adjunction on a
conductor branch says that it is either a smooth elliptic curve disjoint from the other branches,
or a rational curve with two boundary points, counted on its normalization. The rational
branches form cycles, including the nodal irreducible and two-component cases.
2. Nonnegative Euler contributions. A nonempty effective anticanonical divisor makes every
pluricanonical system empty: multiplying a nonzero pluricanonical section by the appropriate
power of the anticanonical section would give a nonzero holomorphic function with a nonempty
zero divisor. Thus the surface has Kodaira dimension −∞. Its minimal model is rational,
ruled, or of class VII. We spell out the consequences for the reduced anticanonical pairs here.
3. On a rational surface, the conductor is connected, by 0 →KV →OV →OC →0 and
H1(KV ) = 0. For a rational cycle, the charge identity and positivity are
e(V \C) = 12−C2−#{cycle components} ≥0,
e(V \C) = 0 ⇐⇒(V, C) is toric. (22.66)
Here e denotes compactly supported Euler characteristic. This includes the nodal irreducible
convention: Friedman (2016, arXiv version 2, Section 1, p. 3; Definition 1.1 and Lemma 1.2,
pp. 4–5; Lemma 2.2, p. 11; Lemma 2.7, p. 14). For a smooth elliptic anticanonical curve,
e(V \ C) = 2 + b2(V ) > 0 instead.
4. On a nonrational ruled minimal model, a conductor component dominating the base has degree
at most two and genus at most one. The base is therefore elliptic. The minimal conductor is
two disjoint elliptic sections or one unramified elliptic bisection. Its Euler contribution is zero.
The two section squares sum to zero, and the bisection has square zero, also directly from
KV + C ∼0 and K2
V = 0.
