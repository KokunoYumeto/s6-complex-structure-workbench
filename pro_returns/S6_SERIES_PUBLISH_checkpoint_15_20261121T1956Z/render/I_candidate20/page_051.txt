Remark 7.7 (Period variation and norm comparisons). [tag 01Q5]
1. On a fixed ordinary fibre, the three last periods remain fixed and span C2, whereas the first
changes by δe. A marked complex-linear identification fixing those three periods must be
the identity, so the marked period map is nonconstant. The set of integral markings of one
torus is countable. A holomorphic nonconstant map from a disc has uncountable image; hence
the unmarked fibre family is locally non-isotrivial. 7.2 transfers this to total spaces. This
is the period argument of (Viaclovsky, 2026a, Proposition 4.7, pp. 14–15). A nonconstant
classifying map gives positive dimension of its local target. It does not by itself determine the
derivative at the base point, as the explicit holomorphic map z 7→z2 has nonconstant image
and derivative zero at zero. A.3 computes the derivative here.
2. The comparison in (Yang, 2026, Lemmas 6.6–6.7 and 6.9, pp. 31–32; Remark 6.8, p. 32;
Proposition 6.15, pp. 37–38) uses an integral commuting lattice map, an affine fibre lift,
normalization of the root base change, and a norm-lattice defect.
His Proposition 6.15
obtains 6/r from the first-column scaling and its order-six norm image. Here the first-column
scaling is one and the two computed norm images impose parity, yielding two. These matrix
substitutions identify the commuting maps, finite norm equations and collar corrections with
the ones just displayed. The nonzero absolute Kodaira–Spencer conclusion in (Yang, 2026,
Theorem 6.18, p. 39) is obtained for the present family by the explicit computation and
intrinsic-pencil transfer in proposition A.3.

The standard isomorphism classes
Theorem 7.8 (Classification of the standard family). [tag 01Q6]
Fix the original cusp-regular period solution, the clockwise peripheral marking, the prescribed
periodic triangular cusp fan, the affine vectors (ε, −ε′), and the untwisted cusp collar. For every
c, c′ ∈P, the constructed compact complex threefolds satisfy
Xc ≃Xc′
⇐⇒
c′ −c ∈2Z.
Every biholomorphism is over the identity of the original P1. The proper holomorphic family of
their isomorphism classes has parameter 0 < |w| < e−πM and nonzero Kodaira–Spencer class at
every point.
Proof. For the standard twist data, the classification of (Du et al., 2026a, Theorem 1.4, p. 3),
with the exact parameter identification of remark A.1, is re-derived in theorem C.5. It gives
Xc ≃Xc′ exactly when c −c′ ∈2Z. The explicit maps on the regular, finite, and cusp charts are
those of proposition 7.5, displayed in (7.28) and satisfying (7.30).
As a complex parameter space for the isomorphism classes represented by this marked family,
the quotient P/(2Z) has the explicit coordinate
w = exp(−πic),
0 < |w| < exp(−πM).
(7.31)
Indeed |w| = exp(π Im c), and two complex numbers have the same value of this exponential
exactly when they differ by an even integer. Every point in the displayed punctured disc has
a logarithm giving a c ∈P. The exponential has nonzero derivative, so these local inverse
logarithms give the claimed quotient complex structure. The integer-period quotient seen by
the middle family instead has coordinate w2; it is covered two-to-one by (7.31). Its two lifts are
distinguished by the exact order-two summand in (A.7). The resulting parameter space classifies
the members of the marked family. Proposition 7.5 proves properness of this quotient family and
its joint holomorphic dependence. Its Kodaira–Spencer class is nonzero by Proposition A.3.
