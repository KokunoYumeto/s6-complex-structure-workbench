26.3
Ordinary nonarchimedean heights
Cited result 26.12 (Nonzero ordinary heights). [tag 001H]
Let E/Q have complex multiplication by the full ring of integers of an imaginary quadratic
field K of class number one. Assume that its functional-equation sign is −1, and let p > 3 be a
prime of good ordinary reduction, pOK = pp∗. For a nontorsion P ∈E(Q) the p-adic height and
the cyclotomic p-adic height of P over K, in the normalization of Agboola and Howard (2006,
Appendix A), are nonzero. In particular this applies to
E : y2 + y = x3 −38x + 90,
P = (0, 9),
p = 5.
Proof. Use Theorem A.1 and Lemma A.2 in Rubin’s appendix (Agboola and Howard, 2006,
Appendix A, p. 1043), with its standing CM, ordinary-prime and sign hypotheses. Theorem A.1,
attributed there to Bertrand, gives nonzero p-adic height for the nontorsion point over K. The
Galois-equivariance in (Mazur et al., 2006, Section 2.9, pp. 587–588; Remark 2.2, p. 588) makes
the anticyclotomic height of a rational point zero: complex conjugation fixes the point and acts by
−1 on that height space. Lemma A.2 says that vanishing of two of the cyclotomic, anticyclotomic
and p projections forces the third to vanish. Hence the cyclotomic height is nonzero.
For the displayed curve, propositions 24.9 and 24.11 give nontorsion and analytic rank one,
hence sign −1. The CM order has discriminant −19 and is maximal. Its reduced positive binary
forms have the single class (1, 1, 5), so the class number is one. The minimal discriminant is −193.
There are seven points over F5, so a5 = −1, proving good ordinary reduction. Equivalently, −19
is a nonzero square modulo 5. These verify every stated hypothesis.
Remark 26.13 (The compact subgroup and the CM correction). [tag IA14]
The complex holomorphic CM correction is θ of proposition 26.8; its exact relation to the real
norm includes exp(−π|u|2/(2A)) as in (26.11). The height in cited result 26.12 has the ordinary
nonarchimedean normalization specified in its cited appendix.
Bertrand (1995, Theorem 2.1, p. 147) constructs nontrivial multiplicative extensions with
nontorsion algebraic points in their maximal real compact subgroup. Its data include a Rosati-
antisymmetric endomorphism and a related nontorsion point over a real number field. For the
diagonal class here, NP = f(P) with N > 0 and f + f† = 0 forces torsion: the endomorphism
N −f is an isogeny because (N −f)†(N −f) = N2 −f2 is positive. The concrete antisymmetric
and diagonal extensions are compared by the maps in proposition 26.17. The vector-extension
statements (Bertrand, 1995, Theorem 3.1, p. 150) and (Bost and Künnemann, 2009, Theorem 3.1.2,
p. 386) use an algebraic connection, as described in remark 26.11.
