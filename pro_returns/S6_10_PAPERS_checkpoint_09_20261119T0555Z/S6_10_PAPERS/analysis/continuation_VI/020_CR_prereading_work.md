## CR.1

Choose the specified ruling blowdowns σ:Z→Z0. Picard invariance under point blowup and the canonical formula give D=σ*D0−K_{Z/Z0}, where D0=2C0+Σa_jF_j. Every reverse blowup centre has positive divisor multiplicity: a centre of multiplicity zero would create a negative coefficient that cannot disappear from its later strict transform. A smooth centre of multiplicity one creates a zero-coefficient exceptional curve; a centre of greater multiplicity adds a rational leaf, or subdivides an edge, to the reduced support. Starting with the section and disjoint ruling fibres, this proves that D_red is a genus-g section with rational trees attached, with ordinary double intersections.

For an initial intersection the coefficients are 2 and a_j, so gcd(2−1,a_j−1)=1. At a node the new coefficient is a+b−1, and the gcd of the two shifted coefficients is preserved on both new edges. At a smooth point of a coefficient-a component the new coefficient is a−1; the new shifted coefficients are consecutive integers. The same calculation applies at an intersection with a zero-coefficient exceptional curve. This proves the exact gcd statement through the chosen sequence.

The fundamental group of Z is π1(B), and the section maps onto it isomorphically. Collapsing the connected exceptional fibres to obtain T kills the section group because C is contracted. Van Kampen on conical neighbourhoods of the finitely many normal singularities therefore gives π1(T)=1 and H¹(T,R)=0. The coherent pullback H¹(T,O_T)→H¹(Z,O_Z) is injective. Restriction from the latter group to C is an isomorphism, whereas the composite from T is zero because C maps to a point. Hence H¹(T,O_T)=0.

A.5.3 says that every ambient Picard line pulls back trivially to T in this contracted-section case. In particular the pullback L_T of ω_S=(K_Y+S)|_S is trivial. Finite conductor duality identifies ω_T with the nonzero conductor ideal I_A⊂O_T. A global section of that ideal is a constant vanishing on the nonempty conductor, hence zero. Serre duality gives H²(T,O_T)=0. The Leray Euler identity now gives length R¹π_*O_Z=χ(O_T)−χ(O_Z)=1−(1−g)=g.

The reduced inverse conductor A_red is the image of D_red after contraction of the section and connected rational subtrees. A zero-coefficient exceptional curve is disjoint from D_red by adjunction, so none is an additional piece over the conductor. A_red is connected and simply connected. The conductor square is an actual topological pushout, with A_red→T a triangulable closed cofibration. Van Kampen gives π1(S)=π1(Γ_red), since T and A_red are simply connected. Every normalized component of Γ_red is rational, because its strict conductor preimages are vertical rational curves. Its fundamental group is a free group.

The inclusion of a punctured tube into the whole tube surjects on fundamental groups: loops can be perturbed away from its complex divisor. The punctured tube has group Z⁴⋊_T Z. Thus π1(S), identified with the tube group by its proper-degeneration retraction, is solvable. A free solvable group has rank at most one. It has positive rank under the present ambient Betti hypotheses: the Leray transgression H0(P¹,R¹f_*Q)→H²(P¹,Q) is onto because H²(Y,Q)=0. Multiplying a nonzero rational section by a denominator gives a nonzero integral section. D.1.8, the input to D.1.9, says that this section is nonzero on every fibre. The underlying topology of the full fibre and its reduction is identical. Hence H¹(S,Z) is nonzero, so π1(S)=π1(Γ_red)=Z.

If n is the number of rational normalized conductor-image components and v_Γ the total branch-identification count, connectedness and the rank-one free group give v_Γ=n. Thus e(Γ_red)=2n−v_Γ=n. Let Q=η*O_T/O_S. Since χ(O_S)=0 and χ(O_T)=1, χ(Q)=1. The full conductor-duality equality Q=ω_Γ⊗ω_S^{-1}|_Γ and the torsion restriction of ω_S give χ(O_Γ)=−1. The same global argument as VII.2 gives χ(O_{Γ_red})≥−1. On the other hand, χ(O_{Γ_red})=n−Σδ_x and Σδ_x≥v_Γ=n, so it is at most zero. Therefore it is −1 or zero and Σ(δ_x−r_x+1)=−χ(O_{Γ_red})≤1.

Finally, if N is the number of reverse blowups and N0 counts multiplicity-one centres, then e(Z)=4−4g+N and e(D_red)=2−2g+v+N−N0. The normalization/resolution complement is unchanged away from the conductor in this ruled section configuration; exceptional curves over the conductor are included in D_red, and any remaining zero-coefficient exceptional curves are accounted for by the specified ruling blowdowns. For the displayed formula one must identify those remaining exceptional corrections exactly. The preliminary subtraction gives 2−2g−v+N0+n; a separate nonzero count of resolution-exceptional zero-coefficient curves away from the conductor would subtract that count. The claimed CR.1 formula therefore still requires proof that this count vanishes in the stated contracted-section situation, or that its contribution is already included in the author's N0 definition. This last exact-complement issue is an explicit prereading gate; the preceding derivation does not silently discard it.

## CR.2 and CR.6: canonical defect gate

### Prereading completion of the CR.1 complement gate

Before opening CR.1's printed proof, the last complement issue above is resolved by its cited A.5.17. A singular normalization point outside the conductor is the very same normal singularity of S, since normalization is an isomorphism there. It is isolated because a normal surface has only isolated singularities. The local reduced hypersurface equation would therefore have an isolated critical point, forbidden in the actual torus family by A.5.17, including the full multiplicity and its analytic unit. Thus no resolution-exceptional fibre lies outside the conductor. Both zero- and positive-coefficient corrections there vanish. The displayed CR.1 Euler formula follows exactly, and its prereading derivation is now complete.

The length of R¹π_*O_Z is g by the calculation above. The opened van Straten §2.2.2–2.2.5 gives proper analytic duality, Grauert–Riemenschneider vanishing and the dual canonical-module defect at a normal isolated singularity. This identifies the local defect module, but it does not alone give the required global length bound g≤2, nor the equality-case assertion that the full conductor scheme is smooth and reduced at q. An actual map from the relevant conductor/residue module to that defect, with the claimed dimensions and annihilator, must be derived. The analogous g=1 conclusion that the conductor ideal is generated by one linear coordinate and one plane equation also requires that precise module comparison. These are retained gates before reading CR.2 and CR.6.

## CR.3

Given the genus-two conclusion of CR.2, the normalization has one point over q and the full conductor scheme is smooth and reduced there. To exclude the generic-node branch one needs the exact finite normalization algebra above that smooth conductor, or a local Milnor comparison proving its normalization cannot have this genus-two resolution defect. The generic node has two branches and conductor coefficient one on each, but that generic information alone does not determine the algebra at q. This local-algebra step remains a prereading gate.

## CR.4 and UN.9: the rank-zero Hessian gate

At any point where the transverse Hessian has a nonzero entry, the holomorphic splitting lemma writes the reduced equation as X²−g(Y,Z). A.5.17 then gives an actual transverse A-type product; constancy of the stated generic cusp or node fixes the exponent to three or two. This proves the claim at all such points, including the rank-one node-degeneration patterns excluded by A.5.15.

Smoothness of the reduced critical support does not prove that the transverse Hessian stays nonzero. For example, x³+y³+zxy has reduced critical axis {x=y=0}, generic transverse node, and zero Hessian at the origin. The simpler x²+zy² has that same smooth reduced critical support but a rank drop from two to one and Jacobian ideal (x,zy,y²). A.5.15 excludes the latter germ in an actual torus degeneration. The former example is only a local warning about the proposed inference; no claim that it occurs in a proper torus family is made. Excluding the rank-zero case using the actual torus-family hypothesis is the remaining step required for CR.4 and UN.9. No smoothness of the Jacobian scheme is inserted into their statements.

## CR.5

The cited CR.2 gives g=1 or2. In the g=2 case, CR.3 leaves the generic cusp and CR.2 makes the full reduced critical curve smooth at q. CR.4 then identifies the actual local reduced equation with X²+Y³. Its normalization is explicitly (u,z)↦(u³,−u²,z), hence smooth at its point above q. This contradicts contraction of the positive-genus section by the minimal normalization resolution: a minimal resolution has no exceptional fibre over a smooth normalization point. Therefore g=1, and substituting it in the exact CR.1 Euler expression gives n−v+N0. This is a complete deduction from the stated cited results; their own unresolved gates remain separate.
