# The A3 ordinary-node defect: lower bound and equality normalization

Current-run proof for independent audit, 2026-11-17.

Let the entire reduced critical curve be the ordinary planar node
C=(z,xy) in a smooth threefold, and assume generic transverse type A3 on
both branches in an actual torus degeneration. Write P=(z,xy) and let I
be the ambient ideal of the scheme-theoretic conductor image. Then
P^(2)=P^2 is contained in I, and J=P/I is a torsionfree rank-one sheaf
on each branch of C. Use exactly the Hessian quantities of P4R.10:
d_i is the zero order of the rank-one Hessian after saturating its line,
b_i the projected conormal colength on branch i, and q the remaining
normalization descent defect of J. Here R=0 and delta(C)=1.

**Claim.** The local defect

    delta + sum_i(d_i/2+b_i) + q

is at least9/2. Equality forces the normalization of S to be smooth over
the point.

## 1. The two node modules

Normalize J and divide out its branchwise vanishing orders. It is an
A=O_C-submodule of B=C{x} plus C{y} which generates each summand. Since
the conductor of A in B is m_A, multiplication by it shows m_A B is
contained in J. Thus J is determined by a subspace W of C^2 whose two
coordinate projections are onto. Either W is a line, and rescaling gives
J isomorphic to A with q=1; or W=C^2 and J isomorphic to B with q=0.
These are exact module isomorphisms. The divided branchwise orders are
the b_i already present in the formula, so q is precisely this residual
colength.

## 2. A unit square coefficient is forbidden in the free case

If q=1, the surjection P/P^2 -> J is a surjection A^2 -> A. Its kernel
is generated by a primitive element g. Choosing a complementary generator
k of P gives I=(g,k^2).

We use the ring condition for the actual conductor. It is
Hom_(O_S)(I,I)=Hom_(O_S)(I,O_S); the right-hand module is the finite
normalization algebra, and I is an ideal of that algebra. In the
complete-intersection presentation I=(g,k^2), the determinant presentation
f=a g+b k^2 and its two-by-two adjugate say exactly that a,b belong to I.
Hence the equation has the exact form

    f=A g^2+B g k^2+C k^4.

The general matrix statement used here is de Jong–van Straten1990,
Theorem1.12, printed p532 (author-hosted scan listed below); the
two-by-two computation is also immediate directly from the adjugate.

If A(0) is a unit, this germ is forbidden. When g_z(0) is nonzero,
straighten g, take k to be an equation of the nodal curve in g=0, and use
the parameter Morse lemma in g. The residual is k^4 times a unit after
an arbitrarily small change of its fourth-order coefficient. Morsifying
the nodal k and shifting its critical value off zero creates an isolated
Morse point with nonzero critical value.

When g_z(0)=0, its xy coefficient is a unit; take k=z. Complete the
square, absorbing sqrt(A) into its first factor, to get exactly

    f=G^2+z^4 U,

where G(x,y,0)=xy times a unit and G_z(0)=0. Add a small constant to U,
then set Z=z U^(1/4), an actual coordinate. The parameter Morse lemma
for G in x,y gives G=XY+a(Z), with a in (Z^2). A small change
a->a+eta Z^2 makes a''(0) nonzero. Now replace G by G-epsilon.
At X=Y=Z=0 the resulting function has critical value epsilon^2; its
XY Hessian entry is -2epsilon and its ZZ entry is
-2epsilon a''(0), so this is a holomorphic Morse point.

All these deformations keep generic A3 along the moving nodal critical
curve near each fixed boundary. The same nested boundary transport as
P4.28 and P4R.14 gives a (-2)-sphere in the nearby torus, a contradiction.
Thus A(0)=0 in the free case.

If g_z(0) is a unit, the two primitive Hessian lines approach dz,
b_1=b_2=1, and d_i>=1. If g_z(0)=0, its xy coefficient is a unit,
dg on each normalized branch has a factor t_i, the saturated lines have
b_i=0, and d_i>=3. In either case the defect is at least5, since q=1.

## 3. The nonfree case and the null cubic equations

If q=0, choose actual generators a,b of P whose images in J=B are
(1,0) and (0,1). Their coefficient matrix is invertible modulo the
maximal ideal and hence invertible locally. Then

    I/P^2=(y a,x b),
    f = x U(x)b^2+y V(y)a^2 modulo P^3.

The second identity follows because the quadratic tensor has the kernel
line b on the x-branch and a on the y-branch. Write the exact generator
relation xy=alpha a+beta b. At a general point of the x-axis, solve it
for y, taking x,a,b as coordinates. The null cubic coefficient contains
V(0)alpha(x,0)/x; its remaining contribution is holomorphic at the node.
Since the null cubic vanishes identically in generic type A3, it follows
that V(0)alpha(0)=0. The other branch gives U(0)beta(0)=0.

If both a_z(0) and b_z(0) are nonzero, alpha(0) and beta(0) are nonzero.
Thus U(0)=V(0)=0. Both saturated lines approach dz, b_i=1, and d_i>=2.
The defect is at least5.

Otherwise interchange the branches so that a_z(0) is a unit and b_z(0)=0.
Then alpha(0)=0 and beta(0) is a unit, so U(0)=0. On the x-axis db has
exactly the common factor x and its remaining dy coefficient is a unit;
therefore b_x=0 and d_x=3+ord_x U>=4. On the y-axis da is primitive
with dz coefficient a unit; b_y=1 and d_y=1+ord_y V>=1. The defect is
therefore at least1+4/2+(1/2+1)=9/2. Equality means ord_x U=1 and V(0)
is a unit.

## 4. The actual conductor and normalization in equality

Use a as an ambient coordinate z. From xy=alpha a+beta b, beta a unit,
the ideal I is

    (a^2,ya,x^2y-x alpha a).

Modulo its first two generators, replace alpha by alpha(x,0,0)=x ell(x).
The exact coordinate Y=y-ell(x)a then puts the conductor in the form

    I=(z^2,Yz,x^2Y).

Rename Y as y. A Hilbert–Burch matrix for these generators is

    [ y   0 ]
    [-z  x^2]
    [ 0  -z ].

Appending a column (a,b,c) gives determinant
a z^2+b yz+c x^2 y. The ring condition says all its two-by-two minors
belong to I. Reducing those minors modulo I gives

    a=y A,   c=z C,   b=-x^2 C+z B     modulo I.

Consequently f belongs to (yz^2)+I^2, and has the exact expression

    f=U yz^2+V z^4+W x^2y^2z+T x^4y^2.

The equality orders d_y=1,d_x=4 say precisely U(0)T(0) nonzero.
Substitute y=z^2v and divide by z^4. The resulting analytic equation has
derivative U(0) with respect to v at its finite root. The implicit-function
theorem gives an actual smooth factor g1=y-z^2v(x,z). Dividing by it,
and retaining the factors x^2 and x^4 in the displayed equation, gives

    g2=z^2 A+x^2 y z B+x^4 y C,

with A(0)C(0) nonzero. This divisibility can also be seen by subtracting
the equation at y=z^2v: every extra value of y contributes z^2, and the
remaining terms retain the stated x powers.

The parameter Morse centre in z is divisible by x^2y. Its substitution
leaves x^4y times a unit, so an actual coordinate change gives

    g2=Z^2+x^4Y.

Its finite birational normalization is

    (x,u) -> (x,Y=-u^2,Z=x^2u),

which is smooth. The other branch g1 is already smooth. Thus the full
normalization is smooth over the node in the equality case, as claimed.
