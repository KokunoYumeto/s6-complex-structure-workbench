# The actual exterior slope and the conifold Euler cube

This working proof computes the exterior slope for the actual
semifree fixed-sphere model.  It retains the tubular framing: its
integer is the Euler cube in that same framing.  Comparing a Cox
product marking with the descended rotating marking of P3K.9 is a
further explicit marking calculation, not an automatic identification.

## 1. The actual spaces and their integral cohomology

Let \(X\cong S^6\) have the actual semifree delta action with
fixed complex curve \(F\cong S^2\) and normal weights \((-1,+1)\).
Let \(Y=X/S^1\cong S^5\), remove the fixed tube, and write
\[
 E=X\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu F,
 \qquad P=Y\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu F,
 \qquad E\longrightarrow P.
\]
The last map is the actual principal delta-circle bundle.  Its
integral Euler class \(x\) evaluates one on the positive Hopf
meridian.  Alexander duality gives
\(H^*(P;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\) in degrees zero and two and
zero otherwise.  The primitive meridian identifies
\(H^2(P;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Zx\).

Fix a tube product for the physical return, and make the return the
identity on that tube by its specified collar isotopy.  Put hats on
the physical positive mapping tori.  The quotient return preserves
\(x\).  The integral Wang sequence therefore gives
\[
 H^1(\widehat P)=\mathbb Z\theta,
 \quad H^2(\widehat P)=\mathbb Zx,
 \quad H^3(\widehat P)=\mathbb Z(x\theta),
 \quad H^4(\widehat P)=0.
\]
The circle bundle \(\widehat E\to\widehat P\) has the same
actual Euler class.  Its Gysin sequence gives an isomorphism
\[
 \pi_*:H^3(\widehat E;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow
       H^2(\widehat P;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Zx.             \tag{EVar.1}
\]
Indeed the preceding term is the quotient of
\(H^3(\widehat P)\) by
\(xH^1(\widehat P)\), which is zero, and the following
\(H^4(\widehat P)\) is zero.  Thus no rational-lattice ambiguity
is present in the slope.

Let \(h\) integrate one on the complex-oriented \(F\), and
let \(s\) integrate one on the normal \(S^3\) oriented as the
boundary of the original complex normal two-plane.  The chosen
tubular framing identifies
\[
 \partial\widehat E=F\times S^3\times S^1_\theta,
 \qquad H^3(\partial\widehat E)
        =\mathbb Zs\oplus\mathbb Z(h\theta).
\]
By (EVar.1), the image of restriction is generated by
\(s+t h\theta\) for a unique integer \(t\).

## 2. The relative primitive that measures the slope

Complete \(\widehat P\) by the actual conifold cap
\(D^3_F\times S^2_{\rm mer}\times S^1_\theta\), using the
same tubular framing.  Call the closed six-manifold \(N_{\rm phys}\).
The actual line extends over this cap by the meridian line.  Choose
a closed real two-form representing its integral class \(x\) that
is exactly the meridian form on a collar of the cap and throughout
the cap.  Choose a principal connection \(\eta\) on
\(\widehat E\), normalized by positive delta fibre integral one,
with \(d\eta=\pi^*x\).  The connection may be chosen to be the
positive Hopf connection on the boundary product, since the actual
boundary line is the meridian line.  Its possible parameter gauge
extends over the cap by P3K.7.

Since \(H^4(\widehat P;\mathbb R)=0\), choose
\(\beta\in\Omega^3(\widehat P)\) with
\(d\beta=x^2\).  Then
\[
                 \alpha=-\eta\wedge\pi^*x+\pi^*\beta
                                                               \tag{EVar.2}
\]
is closed.  Fibre integration gives \(-x\), so (EVar.1) proves
that its cohomology class is the corresponding integral generator.
Changing \(\beta\) by a closed form changes it by a multiple of
\(x\theta\) in cohomology.  Its pullback is zero in
\(H^3(\widehat E)\), so the resulting generator is independent
of that choice.

The normal orientation matters.  Conjugating the negative-weight
normal coordinate converts the action into positive Hopf action
and reverses the normal real four-plane orientation.  Hence the
original complex normal \(S^3\) is the negative of the positive
Hopf \(S^3\).  The integral of \(\eta\wedge x\) over the former
is minus one.  Therefore (EVar.2) has normal period plus one.
On the cycle \(F\times\{p\}\times S^1_\theta\), its first
term vanishes because \(x\) is the meridian form.  Consequently
\[
             t=\int_{F\times S^1_\theta}\beta.          \tag{EVar.3}
\]
This is the exact relative primitive that the absolute cohomology
of the exterior by itself does not determine.

## 3. Stokes and the two return conventions

The quotient convention already fixed in P3K.7 gives
\(\Omega_Y=\Omega_F\wedge\Omega_{\nu,\mathrm{Hopf}}\).
Thus the boundary of the complement \(P\) is
\(-F\times S^2_{\rm mer}\).  The physical mapping torus is
oriented by fibre followed by positive parameter, so
\[
 \partial\widehat P
       =-F\times S^2_{\rm mer}\times S^1_\theta.
\]
The meridian form has square zero on the product cap, and therefore
its cube integrates to zero there.  Stokes on \(\widehat P\)
gives, with the displayed orientation,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 k_{\rm phys}
   &=\int_{N_{\rm phys}}x^3
     =\int_{\widehat P}d(\beta\wedge x)\\
   &=\int_{\partial\widehat P}\beta\wedge x
     =-\int_{F\times S^1_\theta}\beta=-t.
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{EVar.4}
\]
The physical positive family is \(M_{f^{-1}}\) in Jin's convention,
exactly as in P3K.7.  Hence \(k_{\rm Jin}(f)=-k_{\rm phys}\), and
the actual signed relation is
\[
                         t=k_{\rm Jin}(f).             \tag{EVar.5}
\]
It is a calculation from the actual principal bundle and the actual
conifold cap.  It does not assume that the exterior family is a
product relative to its boundary.

## 4. What is and is not fixed by this calculation

In the descended rotating tubular framing of P3K.9, the original
positive return has \(k_{\rm Jin}=-1\), so its exterior slope is
\(t=-1\).  On its double parameter cover the slope is \(-2\),
by pulling back (EVar.3).  On the one-gamma sixfold cover it is
\(-6\).  These statements use that same rotating boundary product.

A Cox product marking may differ from it.  The identity (EVar.5)
holds again in the Cox marking, with the conifold Euler cube formed
using that marking; it does not equate its value to the rotating
value before the marking map is calculated.  Changing the section
\(F\times S^1\to F\times S^3\times S^1\) by a degree-\(d\)
normal map changes the second period of the generator by \(d\),
and the same Stokes calculation changes its conifold Euler cube
by \(d\).  Thus both sides retain precisely the same framing
ambiguity.

For a flop of another, non-fixed critical curve, the delta quotient
may have finite stabilizers; the principal-bundle argument above is
not silently applied there.  The general-fan relative Pontryagin
comparison still needs its own actual transport to the chosen
curve and its Cox product marking.
