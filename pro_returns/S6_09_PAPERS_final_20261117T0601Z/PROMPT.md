## The task

A paper is the publication of its results. It is what a reader cites when they say that a result is true, so every result it reports is stated in it and proved in it, completely. A new result is never proved by pointing to a repository, a GitHub page or a working file: the paper itself is the reference. A repository holds only what a page cannot: programs, certificates, data and logs. The paper names each program, says what it computes and how to run it, and the proof in the paper says how the program's output proves the step.

The work to be published is ours, all of it AI-produced:

## The binding conditions

Each of these binds every page of every paper.

1. **Every result is in a paper with its complete proof.** The results are every row of the six runs' results tables (`returns/<run>/<run>_results.csv`; for S6_05_ARITHMETIC the rows of Part C) and every result of the working edition on the subjects above that the runs did not take over. Each goes in with its proof, in the main text or in an appendix of its paper, and the lemmas a proof uses go in with their proofs. A refuted statement goes in as a counterexample or a correction, with its proof. A result due to others goes in stated with its source; where our runs re-derived it, that proof is printed in an appendix and credited as a re-derivation. A question a run left open goes into an open-problems section, with everything that is proved about it. Nothing is summarized in place of its proof, and nothing is left out.
2. **Every proof is checked, and the record says by whom.** A proof counts as checked when someone other than the run that produced it has checked it: read it step by step with every cited statement verified, or re-run the computation it rests on. Our readers' checks are recorded in `reviews/` and `notes/`; `reviews/WHO_WROTE_WHAT.md` names every reader with model, effort and date. A recorded check counts, whoever made it. Check every other proof yourself while you write it: re-derive each step, re-run its programs from `returns/<run>/code` and the other attached code, and open every cited theorem in the attached literature and match its hypotheses. Where a recorded check exists, compare the proof you print with the checked one, step by step, so that what is printed is what was checked. A result is never called unchecked when a check of it is recorded.
3. **Checked results and results not yet established are in separate parts.** The body of each paper holds the results whose proofs passed a check. A proof that fails its check has a gap to close: repair it (the rule of the outcome section below applies). A result whose proof still has a gap after that work goes into a separate, plainly headed part at the end of its paper, with its proof as far as it goes and the gap stated exactly; it is never dropped and never printed as proved. A statement that turns out false goes into the paper corrected, with the counterexample.
4. **No proof is replaced by a pointer.** Programs and certificates are cited for what they compute, by name and with the command that runs them; the proof in the paper explains the computation and how its output proves the step. The repository (https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/s6-complex-structure-workbench) is named once in each paper, in the data statement, as the home of the programs, certificates and run records.
5. **Every statement says what its checked proof proves.** No strengthening, generalization or merging beyond the proof. A change of wording against the source that changes content is recorded in the inventory with the reason. Where two runs, or a run and a reader, disagree, the correction wins only when its argument is checked; the inventory records both sides.
6. **One notation for all three papers:** the conventions of the first run (`returns/S6_02_FAMILY/sections/conventions.tex`), with a notation sheet that maps every symbol and label of the other runs and of the working edition to it, signs and normalizations included.

## What the outcome must be

**The rule that governs everything below: push.** The work is to settle the questions, not to describe them. Give most of the time to the hardest item, the main question first. Partial results (cases proved, reductions, computations, the smallest undecided instance) are steps you write down and then build on; they never finish an item. When an approach stalls, take the next one: a different method; a reduction to a theorem you find, open and check; a larger or smarter search for a counterexample; a stronger statement that implies the item; a weaker one that it implies, which you then push up. Do not deliver while an item is undecided and an untried approach remains. A gap in a proof is an undecided step: mark it and attack it; never paper over it, and never present a proof with a gap as a proof.

**Progress check at three hours** (the user's rule of 13 November 2026). After about three hours of work, and every hour after that, check what your current work is doing. If it moves the main question or one of the open items forward, keep going. If it is filling in detail away from the main question, stop that work, write the final package with every item's state, and deliver it. In that case this check takes precedence over the sentence above about untried approaches.

For this prompt the main question is the three complete papers. Writing the results into them with their proofs, and checking those proofs, is work on the main question; extending a result beyond what the papers report, or polishing what is already finished, is not.

1. **Done** means: every row of the inventory is in its paper with its checked proof, or in the part of condition 3 with its gap stated; the three papers are complete from front matter to references; every condition above holds on every page; each PDF passes the typesetting check; the report lists every discrepancy you met and how it was settled.
2. **Not acceptable:** a result without its proof in the paper; a proof replaced by a pointer to a repository, a working file or another document of ours; a selection of the results or of the proofs; a result left out; a result called unchecked when a check of it is recorded; a statement stronger than its checked proof; a result of [AC26], [E26] or [DGKX] presented as ours; a model named without its effort; a reference cited unopened for something the papers rely on; an outline, a sample or some sections in place of the papers; a summary of what would be written.
3. **Finished** only when the three papers are done in the sense of point 1. Keep working until then: do not stop because the remaining results are many, because a part looks presentable, or because much has already been written.
