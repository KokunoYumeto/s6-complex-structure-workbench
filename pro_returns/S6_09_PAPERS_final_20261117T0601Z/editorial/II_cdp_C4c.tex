
\begin{proposition}[The finite singular-fibre test and a fixed unavoidable fibre]\label{cdp:st:C4c}
Let $f:Y\to\PP^1$ be a proper connected-fibre map from a compact complex threefold with $b_1(Y)=b_2(Y)=0$ and smooth general fibre a complex two-torus. Let $\Sigma$ be its finite set of critical values, and fix a connected component $P$ of $\Pic(Y)$. For $c\in\Sigma$ retain the entire Cartier fibre $F_c$, including its multiplicities, and put
\[
 Z_c=\{L\in P:H^0(F_c,\Omega_Y^1|_{F_c}\otimes
                  (K_Y\otimes L^{-1})|_{F_c})\ne0\}.
                                                               \tag{C.18}\label{cdp:eq:C.18}
\]
Then the following are equivalent: every $Z_c$ is a proper subset of $P$; and $H^2(Y,T_Y\otimes L)=0$ on a dense open subset of $P$. In that case the generic twist in $P$ satisfies
\[
 H^q(Y,T_Y\otimes L)=0\quad(q\ne1),\qquad
 \chi(Y,T_Y\otimes L)\le0,\qquad c_3(Y)\le0.          \tag{C.19}\label{cdp:eq:C.19}
\]
For a reduced fibre, membership in the complement of $Z_c$ is exactly the two explicitly typed kernel-and-connecting-map conditions of \ref{cdp:st:A.1.1}, with kernel $N^*\otimes\mathcal J^{-1}$ computed in A.2. For $F_c=mS$, the equivalent complete test is the finite lifting filtration of \ref{cdp:st:A.3.1}. These tests use only sheaves and maps on the finitely many singular full fibres.

When $b_3(Y)=0$, at least one fixed critical fibre $F_c$ satisfies $Z_c=P$. Its support is irreducible, by \ref{cdp:st:A.3.5}. Under the integral assumption $H^2(Y,\Z)=0$, the Picard group is connected, so this is a single fixed fibre for which the ambient group in \eqref{cdp:eq:C.18} is nonzero for every line bundle on $Y$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The universal Picard family constructed in C.1 restricts to a holomorphic family on the proper space $F_c\times P$. Its underlying sheaf is flat over $P$, because it is a line-bundle twist of the fixed locally free restriction $\Omega_Y^1|_{F_c}$. Proper semicontinuity therefore makes $Z_c$ a closed complex analytic subset of $P$.

Assume that each $Z_c$ is proper. The complement of their finite union is open dense and has full Lebesgue measure in $P\simeq\C^q$. Statement \ref{cdp:st:D.1.11} supplies a dense set of full measure of line bundles non-torsion on every full fibre. Choose $L$ in the intersection. On every smooth torus fibre, $K_Y|_F$ and the conormal are trivial. The intrinsic cotangent bundle is trivial as well, so the conormal exact sequence expresses the ambient cotangent restriction as an extension of trivial bundles. The line $L|_F$ has zero integral Chern class, since $c_1(L)$ is torsion and $H^2(F,\Z)$ is torsion-free; it is nontrivial by the chosen non-torsion condition. Its cohomology in every degree vanishes, as follows by the unitary Fourier resolution of a topologically trivial line on a torus. The ambient section group is consequently zero at every smooth fibre. At each critical fibre it is zero by the definition of the chosen complement.

Serre duality on each Gorenstein Cartier fibre, with $\omega_{F_c}=K_Y|_{F_c}$, and top-degree base change give $R^2f_*(T_Y\otimes L)=0$. Twisting by $f^*\OO(n)$ preserves every fibre restriction. For sufficiently large $n$, it kills $H^1(\PP^1,R^1f_*(T_Y\otimes L)\otimes\OO(n))$. We choose $n$ divisible by the finite order of $c_1(f^*\OO(1))$, so this twist stays in $P$. The two-column Leray sequence gives an actual line bundle in $P$ with zero $H^2$. Proper semicontinuity makes this zero locus dense open. Intersecting it with the dense open endpoint-vanishing loci of C.1 proves \eqref{cdp:eq:C.19}, using $\chi=c_3/2$.

Conversely, suppose $H^2$ vanishes on a dense open subset of $P$, and choose $L$ in that subset and in the full-measure set of \ref{cdp:st:D.1.11}. The preceding smooth-fibre argument makes $R^2f_*(T_Y\otimes L)$ a torsion sheaf. Leray gives a surjection
\[
 H^2(Y,T_Y\otimes L)\longrightarrow
 H^0(\PP^1,R^2f_*(T_Y\otimes L)).
\]
Thus that torsion sheaf is zero. Top-degree base change and fibre duality imply $L\notin Z_c$ for every $c$. In particular none of the $Z_c$ equals $P$, proving the equivalence.

When $b_3=0$, C.1 gives $H^2(Y,T_Y\otimes L)\ne0$ for every $L$. The equivalence therefore forces some $Z_c$ to be all of $P$. There are only finitely many $c$, and $P$ is an irreducible vector-space torsor; a finite union of proper closed analytic subsets cannot exhaust it. The integral hypothesis makes $P=\Pic(Y)$ by the exponential sequence. This proves the assertion about a fixed fibre. Finally the identifications in A.1--A.3 prove the claimed explicit forms of the fibre tests, without replacing a thickened fibre by its support.
\end{proof}

\ref{cdp:st:C4a} is an explicit geometric instance of \ref{cdp:st:C4c}. The two vanishing groups on the support in \ref{cdp:st:B1}--\ref{cdp:st:B2} do not give its fibre test: their ambient space is one-dimensional. The compact quotient \ref{cdp:st:A.3.2} proves that the full multiple fibre cannot be replaced by one unshifted reduced-support test. \ref{cdp:st:C4c} supplies the fixed full-fibre obstruction in arbitrary torus fibrations. \ref{cdp:st:C4e} proves that its normalization target is nonzero for every twist, with arbitrary singularities and multiplicities.
