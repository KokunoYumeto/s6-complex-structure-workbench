                                                  LOGARITHMIC TRANSFORMATIONS ON THE SIX-SPHERE

                                                                                    JEFF VIACLOVSKY

                                                  Abstract. We show that there are infinitely many choices of the multiplicities in the
                                                  logarithmic transformation step of the Alpöge–Claude construction which produce a complex
                                                  threefold diffeomorphic to the six-sphere. Moreover, distinct choices of the multiplicities
                                                  yield biholomorphically inequivalent complex structures.

                                                                                    1. Introduction
                                              In [3], Engel explained the construction of Alpöge–Claude [1], starting from a rational
                                           elliptic surface π : S → P1 with singular fibers of type IV∗ , III, I1 over the points 0, 1, ∞,
                                           respectively. For a complex number u in a sufficiently small punctured disc, Engel constructs
                                           a fibration X ◦ (u) → P1 \ {∞} with smooth complex 2-torus fibers away from 0 and 1. This
                                           can be filled in at infinity by a rank 2 Mumford hexagonal degeneration to get a fibration
                                           X(u) → P1 . The added singular fiber over infinity has 3 double curves, 2 triple points, and
                                           normalizes to a degree 6 del Pezzo surface. The space X(u) does not yet have the topology
                                           of S 6 . The next step is to perform twisted logarithmic transformations to the fibration at
                                           0 and 1, to replace X0 and X1 with smooth bielliptic surfaces having multiplicities 3 and 4,
                                           respectively. This produces a fibration X3,4 (u) → P1 . For suitable choice of the logarithmic
                                           transformation data, the total space X3,4 (u) is diffeomorphic to S 6 . The main result in this
                                           paper is the following generalization of this construction.
                                           Theorem 1.1. Let u ∈ C with |u| sufficiently small and non-zero. Given positive integers
                                           k and l with gcd(3k, 4l) = 1, one can perform twisted logarithmic transformations of order
                                           3k and 4l over the points 0 and 1, respectively, so that X3k,4l (u) is diffeomorphic to S 6 .
                                           If X3k,4l (u) is biholomorphic to X3k′ ,4l′ (u′ ), then k = k ′ and l = l′ . Moreover, the local
                                           Kuranishi dimension for every such X3k,4l (u) is at least 1.
                                             We note that X3k,4l (u) also depends upon choices of admissible translation vectors a0
                                           and a1 . We will refer to the 4-tuple (m0 , a0 , m1 , a1 ) of choices of multiplicities and translation
                                           vectors as logarithmic transformation data.

Remark 1.2. One can use X3k,4l (u) as the starting point for the combs construction of the
author in [11] to produce even more new examples of complex structures on the smooth
manifold S 6 with arbitrarily large local Kuranishi dimension. Blowing up at a point yields
a similar phenomenon on the smooth manifold underlying P3 . This is a relatively straight-
forward extension, but to keep this paper focused we will not discuss this further here. The
results from [11] are not used in this paper.
1.1. Questions. We list here some questions for future research.
    (1) How are the families for different values of k, l related in the moduli space? Do they
        belong to distinct deformation classes?
    (2) What is the local Kuranishi dimension? Does varying u, with the logarithmic trans-
        formation data fixed, give all sufficiently small deformations?
    (3) For fixed multiplicities and fixed u, do different choices of the admissible translation
        vectors produce even more biholomorphically distinct complex structures on S 6 ?
    (4) Other starting rational elliptic surfaces have been found to produce complex struc-
        tures on S 6 [4, 9, 12], corresponding to many cases on Oguiso-Shioda’s list with
        Mordell-Weil group Z [10]. Which other starting configurations admit infinitely many
        allowable multiplicities also yielding S 6 ?
1.2. Outline. This manuscript was originally intended to only be lecture notes to give an
alternative presentation of some of the difficult computations in [3]. In Section 2, we recall
all the necessary details from [3], and write down the monodromy matrices in a convenient
form, where one can see the original monodromy matrix of the rational elliptic surface
as the central 2 × 2 block; this is similar to how Wang writes the monodromy matrices
in [13]. Note the monodromy matrices are independent of the logarithmic transformation
data. Subsection 2.1 is where we introduce the logarithmic transformations. In Section 3,
we compute the fundamental group using Van Kampen’s Theorem. The fundamental group
relies on this additional logarithmic transformation data, not only on the monodromy. For
the computation of the integral homology groups in Section 4, we give a slightly different
approach than in [3]. We show that, if the total space is simply-connected, then vanishing of
the second integral homology group reduces to showing that a certain explicit 4 × 4 integral
matrix, depending only upon the logarithmic transformation data, is unimodular. Once the
calculation was written in this way, the author then realized that S 6 also arises for infinitely
many choices of the multiplicities, and the evolution of this manuscript from lecture notes
to the present form was the result of that.

  We briefly summarize the construction of Alpöge-Claude [1], as explained by [3]. Take
a rational elliptic surface π : S → P1 with singular fibers of Kodaira types IV∗ , III, I1 over

the points 0, 1, ∞, respectively. This fibration has Mordell–Weil group Z, generated by a
section P , and zero section O. Consider the line bundle M = OS (P − O), and let M ◦
denote its restriction to S \ π −1 (∞). Let 0M : S → M denote the zero section of the line
bundle, and let M ×,◦ = M ◦ \ 0M (S). Choose a reference section ϕ ∈ Hom(t∗6P M, M )(S)

fiber, as in [3, Corollary 2.2]. For u ̸= 0 with |u| sufficiently small, the map uϕ induces an
automorphism of M ×,◦ covering translation by −6P on S \ π −1 (∞). Taking the quotient by
the cyclic group generated by this automorphism gives a fibration X ◦ (u) → P1 \ {∞}, with
singular fibers over 0 and 1 and all other fibers smooth complex 2-tori.
   The fibration is filled in over ∞ by a Mumford rank 2 hexagonal degeneration, which adds
a singular fiber with 3 double curves and 2 triple points, which normalizes to a degree 6
del Pezzo surface dP6 . This produces a fibration X(u) → P1 . The fiber X0 over 0 has 7
irreducible components consisting of two primary Hopf surfaces and five ruled surfaces over
elliptic curves; there are 7 components because the IV∗ fiber consists of 7 irreducible rational
curves. The fiber X1 over 1 consists of two primary Hopf surfaces; there are 2 components
because the III fiber consists of 2 irreducible rational curves. We will see that both of these
fibers will be discarded and replaced with non-reduced fibers (with smooth reductions) in
the logarithmic transformation step.

Write f ′ : V → U for the restricted fibration. We fix a section σ : P1 → X(u) as follows.

   For the second statement, fix the logarithmic transformation data and vary u in a small disc
