The exact four-dimensional diagonal 1-motive matrix P, its polarization and its primitive five-dimensional exterior comparison are those already derived in arithmetic_routes_exact.py. The primitive exterior weights are (-3,-3,-2,-1,-1). Tensoring with the dual elliptic realization H^vee gives weights (-2,-1,0), with dimensions(4,2,4). At each extreme the corresponding pure Hodge object is an End(H) factor, with a Tate twist on the bottom.

For the actual real marking tau=(1+i/sqrt19)/2, multiplication by beta=i*sqrt19 on H has Betti matrix J_B=[[-19,-10],[38,19]], square -19I. The exact source s2=-2 gives the de Rham matrix J_D=[[beta,0],[-4beta,-beta]] in the original differential/second-kind basis, since eta(beta*omega)=-beta*eta(omega)-4beta*omega. Replacing the second-kind row by eta+2omega conjugates J_D to diag(beta,-beta). The corresponding elliptic comparison matrix is [[omega1,omega2],[q1,q2]], with q_j=eta_j+2omega_j. Its exact relations are omega2=tau*omega1, q2=bar(tau)*q1 and q1=-beta*t/omega1, t=2*pi*i.

The rational projector on the CM-centralizer part of End(H) is p_CM(A)=(A-J_B*A*J_B/19)/2. Its complement gives the two non-(0,0) Hodge types. One can therefore form a Hodge subquotient of the ten-dimensional tensor object by quotienting the bottom non-Tate part and taking the inverse image of the top CM-centralizer part. Its candidate graded dimensions are(2,2,2), with the types allowed for a1-motive. The exact quotient/subobject maps and its full comparison matrix have not yet been computed. In particular no assertion is made that the resulting de Rham/Betti object is realized by a1-motive over Qbar, or that its relevant period is covered by a linear-period theorem.

The central translation v -> v+t*s acts nontrivially from the top H part of the primitive exterior to the bottom Tate*H part. On tensoring by H^vee it appears to preserve the CM-centralizer components; hence this route may retain v rather than discarding it as the previous naive weight truncations do. This must be checked by exact full matrices. A remaining arithmetic descent problem cannot be replaced by an appeal to the complex Hodge equivalence alone.
