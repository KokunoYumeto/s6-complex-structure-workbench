Corollary 22.4 (Base-changed cusp refinements). [tag 0304] [tag 0303]
For n = 2 and n = 12, 21.5–21.6 give all finite-orbit smooth invariant plain-cusp refinements,
their proper flat families, normalization charts, finite blow-up and blow-down presentations,
multiplicities and all multiple loci. 21.16–21.18 give their complete integral topology and nearby
Euler function. Reducedness is exactly the restriction ℓ= 1 on every positive-height ray.
Proof. 1. The base maps u = w2 and u = r12 induce multiplication by 2 and 12 on the integral
parameter characteristic. Insert n = 2 and n = 12 respectively into the periodic lattice action
and fan construction of 21.5. Its converse recovers the invariant subdivision from each such
plain-cusp model, so the parametrization includes every stated output.
2. For each primitive ray v, the local parameter is a monomial whose exponent on its divisor is
its integral height ℓ(v). Thus the central divisor is reduced exactly when every such height
equals one. Smoothness, flatness, properness, the normalization charts and all intersection
maps are those proved in 21.5; 21.6 gives the finite blow-up and blow-down presentation.
Finally substitute the resulting orbit counts and maps into 21.16, 21.17, 21.18 for the claimed
topology.
22.3
Component monodromy and trace obstructions
Theorem 22.5 (Peripheral component obstruction). [tag 0305]
The finite models of 22.1 and any of the plain u = r12 cusp models of 22.2 cannot be
glued to a proper complex space over B × ∆r with the prescribed central fibres on all three base
neighbourhoods. The obstruction is the peripheral component monodromy of these prescribed
finite and plain cusp models.
Proof. 1. If a further plain cusp refinement changes the top fan, its central fibre over an ordinary
q ̸= 0 has additional components or different multiplicities and already cannot agree with
the twelve-cycle model of 22.1 on the overlap. We may therefore assume its top fan is the
standard reduced twelve-cycle. Additional mixed-height rays lie over q = 0 and do not change
the following argument over the punctured base.
2. Suppose such a gluing exists and put B◦= B \ {0, 1, ∞}. Normalize its reduced central fibre
over B◦. In each of the prescribed plumbing charts this is a proper smooth family whose fibres
are the disjoint union of twelve connected ruled surfaces. The charts and their transition
isomorphisms therefore give a locally constant twelve-element set of connected components.
Equivalently, the Stein factor of this normalization is an unramified degree-twelve cover of B◦:
the algebra of fibrewise holomorphic functions is locally O12, since each proper connected ruled
surface has only constant holomorphic functions. This description constructs the component
cover over B◦.
3. The double curves give the adjacency graph of a twelve-cycle. Thus its transition permutations
lie in the dihedral group D12, the automorphism group of that graph. We record a peripheral
permutation by its deck-identification map. Holonomy defined by lifting a clockwise path and
returning its endpoint to the initial root slice uses the inverse permutations throughout; the
relation and the following contradiction are unchanged. At 0 the monodromy is a rotation by
four steps; at 1 it is a rotation by minus three steps, by the explicit root deck maps in 22.2.
Relabelling a cycle can reverse either orientation, but changes these only to ±4 and ±3. At
the cusp the monodromy is the identity by 22.3.
