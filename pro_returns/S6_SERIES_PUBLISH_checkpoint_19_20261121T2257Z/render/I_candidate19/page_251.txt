Global elliptic monodromy constrains every central meridian
Proposition 22.55 (The global meridians have only the prescribed toric direction). [tag 0311]
Suppose g : Z →∆r is any proper complex-analytic filling of a finite parameter cover of
the six-sphere family. Retain its reduced main component, normalize and resolve it, extend its
intrinsically marked map to P1 by 18.3, resolve the graph, and resolve the support of its central
divisor. Denote the resulting smooth proper modification by eZ, and its holomorphic marked map
by h. All modifications can be chosen to be isomorphisms over r ̸= 0. The central divisor has
normal-crossings support and positive integral component multiplicities.
There is a finite subset Σ ⊂P1, containing 0, 1, ∞, with the following property. For every
q ∈B◦:= P1 \ Σ, every local angular vanishing circle of the r-central normal-crossings divisor,
viewed in the homology of the nearby ordinary two-torus, lies in
Zbδ ⊂H1(Xq,r; Z).
(22.78)
The same conclusion holds for the image of the entire local angular torus at a higher crossing
over B◦. Here bδ is the primitive cycle in the fixed period marking. The conclusion is unaffected
by any further finite cover of the degeneration parameter.
Proof. 1. The global model and the finite exceptional set. The punctured total family is connected
and smooth, hence irreducible. Its closure is the unique reduced main component dominating
the parameter disc. Retain this component, normalize it, and resolve it properly, without
changing its smooth punctured part. The resulting total space is a smooth connected fourfold,
proper over the disc. The parameter is a nonzero divisor, so its map to the disc is flat.
The torsion-free coherent bi-anticanonical pencil and labelled critical-value argument of 18.3
apply to this smooth model. Its marked graph is therefore a proper modification. Further
analytic resolution and principalization of the central ideal give the stated smooth model and
normal-crossings support; the complex-analytic scope, including locally finite resolution, is
explicit in Bierstone and Milman (1997, Theorems 1.6 and 1.10, pp. 215–216, including the
noncompact extension after Theorem 1.6). Properness over a smaller parameter disc allows
us to work over a compact central fibre throughout. The modifications have their centres over
r = 0, so neither the punctured family nor its marked map changes. Flatness over r persists
because r is a nonzero divisor in the smooth local rings; all central multiplicities are kept in
the local monomial equations.
