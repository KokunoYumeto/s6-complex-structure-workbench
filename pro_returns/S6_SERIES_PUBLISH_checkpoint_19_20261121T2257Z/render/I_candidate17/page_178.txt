This calculation concerns the stratified map of central spaces.
3. To compare this splitting with the normalization mapping cylinders, resolve ZD′ by the alter-
nating ordered-local-branch complex: its first term is the constant sheaf on the normalization,
its next term is on the normalized double-branch space, and so on. Its augmentation is an
integral quasi-isomorphism, because at a point with s branches it is the augmented simplex
complex on those branches. Use alternating integral cochains, or equivalently the orientation
local system of each unordered branch set. Proper pushforward of this resolution computes
the left side of (21.6). The same resolution for D, together with the centre complex, computes
its right side.
4. The local matrices can be read directly from the blow-up charts in 21.6. If the centre lies on
k central branches among its c defining coordinates, the exceptional fibre is Pc−1. The new
exceptional branch supplies that whole projective space; the strict transform of old branch
i supplies its coordinate hyperplane Hi. Intersections supply the corresponding coordinate
projective subspaces. In each even cohomological degree the normalization differential is
the alternating restriction matrix with entries 0, 1, −1. The cover consisting of the whole
projective space and its selected hyperplanes has the elementary simplex contraction, leaving
one generator ξa in each degree 2a, 0 ≤a < c. For a horizontal centre, every central branch
meeting its generic central stratum supplies the whole exceptional fibre; the same simplex
contraction applies to these copies. Higher boundary strata are treated by the same coordinate
restrictions. The global Gysin maps just constructed glue the surviving classes with unit
coefficient.
5. Starting from the explicitly computed reference normalization matrices in 21.17, (21.6)
therefore computes the normalization complex along every recorded blow-up or inverse
blow-up. A compact centre has C0 = C. A horizontal toric surface centre retracts to its
rational-chain C0; a horizontal curve centre has C0 a point. Their cohomology is free and
even. Consequently the central computation preserves odd cohomology and torsion, adds one
to b2 for every new boundary ray, and adds one to b6 exactly for a new central ray. It gives
the same (b1, b3, b5) = (1, 7, 0), b2 = 8 + k + m and b6 = k + m as 21.17.
