Proposition 21.6 (The normalization and modification algorithm). [tag 02ZN]
For every finite fan datum Σ of Theorem 21.5, the following algorithm describes the normalized
components and their comparison with a reference model.

its gluing maps, and all multiple loci directly. There is also a terminating finite-data algorithm
that expresses its comparison with a reference model as an explicit sequence of blow-ups and
blow-downs along specified smooth strata. At each blow-up the algorithm gives the normalized
strict transforms, the exceptional projective bundle, its multiplicity, and every new intersection.
The algorithm uses both blow-ups and blow-downs.

Proof. 1. Proposition 21.4 proves that the point-star component P3 has no proper bimeromorphic
map to R. In that example the full normalization is Blp R+ ⊔P3: the first component is
the strict transform, and the two meet along the exceptional plane. These are the ordinary
point-blow-up charts of the smooth total fourfold.
2. Retain all marking data from 21.5: the lattice, the two period matrices, the original line-
bundle units, and the common unsubdivided fan. Strictify the finite quotient cone complex
by the decreasing-dimensional barycentric stars used in 21.17. Record these operations and
do the same for the reference model. Form their common intersection complex. A regular
common subdivision can be found either by toric desingularization or by enumerating rational
subdivisions with increasing primitive-ray bounds and testing their determinants and common
faces. A regular subdivision exists, so the latter enumeration terminates.
3. On a finite strict regular complex there are finitely many possible ordinary star blow-ups:
choose a cone of dimension at least two and insert the sum of its primitive basis rays. There are
also finitely many possible inverse moves: choose a ray and test whether its star is exactly the
star created by such a subdivision of a regular cone. Both tests are exact integer calculations.
Require that every intermediate complex remain a subdivision of the marked original complex.
Record face identifications; equality of two marked complexes is a finite rational comparison,
using the period orbit representatives in (21.3) and their pure top and pure bottom analogues.
