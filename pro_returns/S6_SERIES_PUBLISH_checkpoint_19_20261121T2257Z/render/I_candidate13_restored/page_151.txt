20.1
The untwisted pinching and its local smoothings
Definition 20.1 (The elliptic surface and the pinching datum). [tag PT01] S is the smooth
minimal rational elliptic surface with Weierstrass equation
y2 = x3 −3t3(t −1)x + 2t4(t −1)2,
π : S →B is its elliptic fibration, P is the section used in the period dictionary, T = t6P : S →S,
and L = MS = OS(P −O). We use the convention that PS(O ⊕L) contains the total space of L,
with fibre coordinate a, zero section s0, and infinity section s∞. The two normal bundles are
respectively L and L−1. We write R = PS(O ⊕L).
Proposition 20.2 (The pinching and its smoothing line). [tag 01R1]
The quotient
Q = R/{s0(x) ∼s∞(Tx) : x ∈S}
has a compact, reduced complex-space structure. Its normalization is the finite holomorphic map
ν : R →Q. Its singular locus is a copy of S, and its local analytic equation along that locus
is ab = 0, with two further coordinates on S. The induced map Q →B is proper. Its sheaf of
first-order local smoothing parameters is
T 1
Q = Ext1
OQ(ΩQ, OQ) ≃L ⊗T ∗L−1 ≃π∗OB(1)
on the double locus.
Proof. 1. The two sections of R are disjoint closed submanifolds. The equivalence relation consists
of their two graphs and the diagonal; it is closed in R × R. A quotient of a compact Hausdorff
space by a closed equivalence relation is Hausdorff: for distinct equivalence classes, compactness
gives disjoint saturated neighbourhoods by taking finite subcovers of the complements of the
relation. Thus the displayed quotient is compact and Hausdorff.
2. For a coordinate neighbourhood U ⊂S and frames of L|U and L−1|T(U), the two tubular
coordinate charts are (x, a) at s0(U) and (Tx, b) at s∞(T(U)). We give their identification
the ring
{(f(x, a), g(x, b)) : f(x, 0) = g(x, 0)} = C{x1, x2, a, b}/(ab).
Changes of frame are holomorphic and invertible on each branch and have the same change
of x on their intersection. They therefore glue these hypersurface charts to the inherited
charts off the sections. This constructs the asserted complex space. The direct sum of the two
regular branch rings is the integral closure: an integral element in the total ring of fractions is
integral on each smooth branch, and conversely the two branch idempotents are integral. The
resulting map ν is proper with at most two points in each fibre, and its local ring extension is
finite. The map to B is well defined because πT = π; its properness follows from compactness
of Q.
3. The conormal presentation for the hypersurface ab = 0 gives Ext1(Ω, O) = O/(a, b). A
change of normal frames a′ = g(x)a, b′ = h(x)b changes the equation and its smoothing
parameter by gh. Consequently these local modules glue as Ns0/R ⊗T ∗Ns∞/R = L ⊗T ∗L−1.
For this particular surface, Engel’s Lemma 2.1 states Hom(T ∗L, L) = π∗OB(1) (Engel, 2026,
Lemma 2.1, pp. 3–4). Its identification applies here with T = t6P and L = O(P −O). One
can recover the degree in that lemma directly: the bundle has multidegree zero on every fibre,
since T preserves every component; hence it is π∗OB(n). Its degree on O is
n = (O −P) · 6P + (P −O) · O = (2 −2) + (0 −(−1)) = 1.
