Put U=(C^2 minus {0}) x (C^2 minus {0}). Let the additive complex Lie group
C act holomorphically by

  lambda . (z,w) = (exp(lambda) z, exp(i lambda) w).

This action is free. Fixing z forces lambda=2 pi i k for an integer k;
fixing w then forces exp(-2 pi k)=1, and hence k=0.

It is proper. For lambda=x+i y the logarithms of the two Euclidean norms
change by (x,-y). On any compact subset of U both norms are bounded above
and bounded away from zero. If u and lambda.u are in prescribed compact
subsets, x and y are bounded by these logarithmic norm differences. The
inverse image of a compact set under (lambda,u) -> (u,lambda.u) is therefore
a closed subset of a compact product, hence compact.

The free proper holomorphic action has a Hausdorff smooth complex quotient
Ccal=U/C of complex dimension three. Local holomorphic slices can also be
given directly: where one coordinate z_j is nonzero, choose a local branch
of log(z_j), apply lambda=-log(z_j), and use the remaining normalized z
coordinate and the two transformed w coordinates as three holomorphic
coordinates. The only transition ambiguity is the free discrete scalar
action exp(-2 pi k) on the w coordinates, so these slices give the same
complex quotient without any assumption of a compact quotient theorem.

Every orbit meets the smooth real slice ||z||=||w||=1 exactly once. The
unique parameter carrying (z,w) to that slice is

  lambda(z,w)=-log||z|| + i log||w||.

The formula depends smoothly on (z,w), and the preceding norm transformation
shows that the resulting normalized pair is constant on each orbit. Thus
the quotient map restricted to the slice gives a diffeomorphism

  S^3 x S^3 -> Ccal.

In particular Ccal is compact and connected. The product cellular complex
has one zero-cell, two three-cells and one six-cell, with zero differentials.
Consequently H1(Ccal,Z)=H2(Ccal,Z)=0, b3=2 and
e(Ccal)=1-2+1=0. This is an actual diffeomorphism, not an Euler-number-only
comparison with another space.

The map (z,w) -> [z] to P1 is invariant under the action. It descends to a
holomorphic map f:Ccal->P1. Its differential is surjective: the map from
U to P1 is a submersion, its differential kills the C-orbit tangent, and
the quotient differential is surjective. The fibres are connected and have
the following explicit complex quotient description.

Fix a point [z0] and a nonzero representative z0. Write z=v z0 with v in
C*. Its fibre before quotient is C* x (C^2 minus {0}), with the same action
(v,w) -> (exp(lambda)v, exp(i lambda)w). A choice of log(v) puts v=1 by
lambda=-log(v). The remaining ambiguity lambda=2 pi i k acts on w by
exp(-2 pi k). Therefore the fibre is biholomorphic to

  H=(C^2 minus {0}) / <q>,  q=exp(-2 pi),

where q acts by scalar multiplication. The map is explicitly
(v,w) -> [exp(-i log(v))w] in H. Replacing log(v) by log(v)+2 pi i k
changes its value by q^{-k}, so the class is well-defined. It is invariant
under the original C-action, and its inverse is the orbit class of (1,w).
Choosing z0 holomorphically over an affine coordinate neighbourhood of P1
gives the corresponding local product with H. Thus f is a holomorphic
submersion with compact primary Hopf fibres.

The universal cover of H is C^2 minus {0}, which deformation-retracts to
S^3 and is simply connected. Its deck group is the infinite cyclic group
generated by q. Every connected finite unramified cover of H therefore has
fundamental group a finite-index subgroup nZ of Z, hence still Z. A complex
two-torus has fundamental group Z^4. An unramified torus cover would induce
an injective map Z^4 -> Z with finite-index image; tensoring with Q would
identify a four-dimensional vector space with a one-dimensional one, which
is impossible. This proves the stated absence of a finite torus cover by
the exact covering-space map.

The supplied equality-description statement for torus fibrations requires
a smooth irreducible support at Euler equality to have a finite unramified
torus cover. The fibration just constructed has c3=e=0, b1=b2=0 and smooth
supports, while the covering calculation excludes that description for
each fibre. It therefore supplies the stated counterexample upon removing
the torus-fibre hypothesis from that equality description. The separately
supplied P4.7 numerical statement still applies: every support here is
smooth, its triple-point number is zero, and c3=e=0 with b3=2. No proof of
the general P4.7 theorem is claimed by this use of its statement.
