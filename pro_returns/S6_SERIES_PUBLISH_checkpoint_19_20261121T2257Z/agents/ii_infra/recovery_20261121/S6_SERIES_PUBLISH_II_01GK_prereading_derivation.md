## 1. Exact family and the root base

Write b=(1-mu^3)^(1/2), with b(0)=1. The supplied primary construction is

    S_mu: y^2=x^3+t*x+mu*t+1,       P_mu=(-mu,b),
    M=O(P_mu-O),

with the quotient lift covering translation by -2P_mu and multiplier
lambda. Its discriminant is 4t^3+27(mu*t+1)^2. For mu close to zero its
three roots p1,p2,p3 are distinct; its discriminant as a cubic in t is
314928(mu^3-1). The first two reduced completions have rank-one
degeneration; the third has rank two. The only multiple fibre is 4B at
infinity. These are the exact fibres of this primary family.

Let Bcal be the fourth root stack at infinity of P1. Thus Bcal=P(1,4),
O_Bcal(4) is the pullback of O_P1(1), and

    K_Bcal=O(-5),       T_Bcal=O(5).

The divisor relation f^*(infinity)=4B gives the actual lift fbar:X->Bcal.
Over the root chart s=r^4 this is the smooth torus family in the primary
good-reduction construction. The deck map is composed with the invariant
translation ell/4; it is free on the central torus. Its quotient is the
original X, so this chart is an actual base change of fbar. There is no
critical scheme at the stack point. Translation by the logarithmic
gluing section preserves relative invariant vector fields and cohomology.

For a weighted line bundle, the monomial calculation gives

    H0(O(k)) = span{x^a*y^j: a,j>=0, a+4j=k},
    H1(O(k)) = H0(O(-k-5))^*.

In particular O(-1) and O(-2) are acyclic, while H0(O(5)) and H0(O(4))
both have dimension two. Their sections over the finite t-chart have
coefficients 1,t in the frames used below.

## 2. The exact vertical frames

Let xi0 be the fibrewise Euler field of M^*. On the elliptic curve use

    omega=dx/y,
    v=y*d/dx + (3x^2+t)/2*d/dy,
    h=(y+b)/(2(x+mu)),
    eta=h*omega.

The residues of eta at P_mu and O are respectively +1 and -1. In the
canonical meromorphic frame of O(P_mu-O), the connection with connection
form eta is therefore holomorphic in the regular local frames of M.
It is flat along the elliptic curve, since its curvature is a holomorphic
two-form on that curve. The horizontal lift is

    v1 = v - h*xi0.

This connection is invariant under the semiabelian group translations.
Here is the map establishing that assertion. The group structure on M^*
uses the chosen nonzero vector over O; a group translation induces the
normalized bundle isomorphism over the corresponding elliptic translation.
The translated connection has the same holonomy, since elliptic translation
acts trivially on the fundamental group. Its difference from the original
connection is a holomorphic one-form whose periods lie in 2*pi*i*Z.
As the translation varies in the connected group M^*, this difference
varies continuously in that discrete period set and is zero at the
identity. It is therefore zero everywhere. The quotient lift used here
differs from such a translation only by a scalar in C^*, also a group
translation. Both xi0 and v1 consequently descend through that quotient.

At good reduction put t=r^(-4), x=r^(-2)*x', y=r^(-3)*y'. Then

    omega=r*omega',
    h=r^(-1)*h',
    h'=(y'+b*r^3)/(2(x'+mu*r^2)),
    eta=h'*omega',
    v1=r^(-1)*v1'.

The primary comparison changes the meromorphic line frame by a power of r
and a base unit. Their relative differentials vanish, so these identities
also hold in its section-preserving line comparison. The connection is
regular on M'=O(P'-O'), including r=0; P'(0)=(0,0) is nonzero two-torsion.
The affine deck modification preserves these relative vector fields.
The Euler field has weight zero and v1 has the displayed weight -1.
Thus the bundle of vertical invariant fields is exactly

    V=O_Bcal*xi0 direct-sum O_Bcal(-1)*v1.

This identifies its transition morphisms, rather than inferring a bundle
isomorphism from its degree. In each finite toroidal chart the vertical
fields are logarithmic torus fields. For xy=t their module is generated
by x*d/dx-y*d/dy and the field in the elliptic direction. For xyz=t it
is generated by x*d/dx-y*d/dy and x*d/dx-z*d/dz. The coordinate maps of
the Mumford construction identify these with the two invariant fields
above. Their logarithmic periods form an invertible frame; their ordinary
values may vanish on the critical curve, but their sheaf generators do
not. Hence ker(dfbar)=fbar^*V as locally free sheaves.

## 3. An explicit identification V -> R1 fbar_* O

Use the primary cohomology basis (psi,u,w,delta) and the preserved class

    xi = u wedge w + 2*psi wedge delta.

On the dual homology basis its alternating matrix is

    J = [[0,0,0,2], [0,0,1,0], [0,-1,0,0], [-2,0,0,0]].

In normalized logarithmic coordinates the period matrix is

    Omega = [[-2z,1,tau,0], [rho,0,z,1]].

Direct multiplication gives det(J)=4 and Omega*J^(-1)*Omega^t=0.
Consequently W=ker(Omega) is J-isotropic, and J identifies W with F1H1.
The following map is well-defined and is an isomorphism:

    Lie(F)=C^4/W -> H1(F,O)=H1(F,C)/F1H1,
    Omega(l) |-> class of l^t J.

Changing the lift l by W changes the covector by F1H1. Nondegeneracy of
J proves injectivity and the two dimensions give surjectivity. In
particular the C^* vector (0,1), lifted by the delta homology vector,
maps to -2[psi]. The map is holomorphic and respects all monodromies:
the primary cohomological matrices satisfy T*J*T^t=J. The companion
program verifies each of those identities using the displayed matrices.

The extension at the reduced singular fibres can be checked on the
actual toroidal Cech cover, as follows. Choose two a-periods and two
b-periods, with a1 the vanishing torus circle at a rank-one fibre; choose
a2 from the remaining elliptic factor. At a rank-two fibre both a-periods
are torus circles. Their logarithmic Lie vectors remain a frame at the
central fibre. Normalize that frame so that the a-period matrix is I.
The Cech classes dual to b1,b2 remain a frame of H1(O): a b-period is
represented by the constant gluing cocycle across its identified boundary.
For rank one the first such cocycle is the dual-graph class and the second
is the ordinary elliptic class. For rank two the two cocycles are the
two independent classes of the conductor graph. The normalization
sequences in the next section show directly that these central classes
are independent and have the full dimension two. Cohomology and base
change then make these holomorphic Cech classes a frame across the disc.

The a-period plane is J-isotropic. Indeed, for rank one the vanishing
line is im(N), the surviving a2 belongs to ker(N), and
J(Nv,w)=-J(v,Nw)=0. For rank two it is the span of im(N), and
J(Nv,Nw)=0. These identities follow by comparing coefficients of k and
k^2 in invariance under (I+N)^k; N^2=0. Passing to the saturation of the
period lattice does not alter the rational isotropic plane. Therefore
J_aa=0 and J_ab is invertible. In these Lie and Cech frames the displayed
map is the constant invertible matrix J_ab. This proves both holomorphic
extension and nonvanishing determinant at the singular fibres.

At the root chart over infinity the family is smooth, so the same map
extends by its usual holomorphic torus construction and descends by deck
invariance. We have thus proved, with its actual map,

    E=R1 fbar_* O_X is isomorphic to V=O direct-sum O(-1).

The same toroidal Cech cup product identifies R2 fbar_*O with wedge^2 E:
the two constant boundary cocycles have their alternating product as the
degree-two generator. This is nonzero on the central toroidal models;
on the root chart it is the ordinary torus cup product. Thus R2=O(-1).
This also agrees with relative duality: K_X=fbar^*O(-4), so
omega_(X/Bcal)=fbar^*O(1) and (R2)^*=O(1). The latter computation uses
the canonical-bundle calculation already proved in the primary source.

## 4. The critical scheme and its restriction maps

Let Z be the critical scheme and L=fbar^*T_Bcal. The coordinate maps
xy=t and xyz=t give, with their actual Jacobian ideals,

    0 -> fbar^*V -> T_X -> Jimage -> 0,
    0 -> Jimage -> L -> Q -> 0,
    Q=L tensor O_Z.

At a rank-one fibre Z is its double elliptic curve. At the rank-two fibre
Z is the union of its three double P1 curves, meeting at its two ordinary
triple points. The ideal (xy,xz,yz) in the triple chart is reduced; thus
no hidden infinitesimal length is discarded in this description of Q.

At rank one the normalization is a ruled surface over an elliptic curve E0,
its preimage conductor is E0 disjoint-union E0, and the image conductor
is E0. The exact conductor pushout sequence has maps on H0 and on H1
of the form (a,b) -> (a-b,a-b), because the two gluing translations act
as the identity on H1(O_E0). It follows that

    H1(O_F) -> H1(O_E0)

is surjective, and its kernel is the one-dimensional constant gluing
cocycle (the dual-graph class). Under the exact J identification of
section 3, this kernel is the vanishing logarithmic Lie direction a1.

At rank two the normalization is dP6, whose H1 and H2 of O vanish. Its
preimage conductor is the hexagon C, with (h0,h1)=(1,1). The image
conductor Z has three rational components and two triple points. Its
normalization sequence is

    0 -> O_Z -> direct-sum_3 O_P1 -> C^2_at_qA plus C^2_at_qB -> 0.

The map on constants has rank two: both pairs are the two differences
between the three component constants. Hence (h0,h1)(O_Z)=(1,2).
The conductor pushout for F has surjective H0 map. Its remaining exact
sequence, together with h1(O_F)=2 and h2(O_F)=1, gives

    H1(O_F) -> H1(O_Z) is an isomorphism,
    H1(O_C) -> H2(O_F) is an isomorphism.

The dimensions of O_F can also be read from the same toroidal Cech
complex in section 3. Thus this argument does not identify the singular
fibre with its normalization or its conductor.

More explicitly, the opposite-edge identification of the hexagon has
two vertices, three edges a,b,c and one face. The oriented boundary of
that face is a*b*c*a^(-1)*b^(-1)*c^(-1), with the edges oriented between
the two vertices. Its map on the abelian group of graph cycles is zero.
Thus H1(O_Z)->H1(O_C) is zero directly. Collapsing the connecting edge
c gives a one-vertex cell structure with boundary a*b*a^(-1)*b^(-1).
This is the actual cellular comparison with the standard torus, and its
two degree-one constant cocycles have nonzero alternating cup product
in degree two. At rank one the conductor calculation is the product
of its constant gluing-circle complex with the elliptic O-cohomology;
the graph class times the elliptic class generates H2. These descriptions
also justify the Cech cup-product assertion in section 3.

For the rank-one fibres the node of the cubic is (e_i,0), where

    t_i=p_i=-3e_i^2,
    2e_i^3+3mu*e_i^2-1=0.

Set h_i=b/[2(e_i+mu)]. Since b is nonzero, these points are disjoint
from P and O. The field v vanishes at the node, whereas xi0 remains
the translation field on the double elliptic curve. Thus

    v1 restricted to that curve = -h_i*xi0.

The vanishing logarithmic Lie line is consequently span(v1+h_i*xi0).
By the exact kernel map above, restriction on E in the basis (xi0,v1)
has functional (a,bcoef) -> a-h_i*bcoef. Its target basis is the image
of xi0 under the J map; it is nonzero because xi0 is not the vanishing
direction. This specifies the scalar normalization of the target.
At p3 use the images of xi0,v1 as the target basis of H1(O_Z); this
is a basis by the rank-two isomorphism just proved.

## 5. The finite restriction matrix and tangent cohomology

Projection formula and Leray on the tame root curve give

    h^bullet(fbar^*V)=(1,1,0,0),
    h^bullet(L)=(2,4,2,0),
    h^bullet(Q)=(3,4,0,0).

Here the terms of the first calculation are O, O(-1), O(-2); the only
nonzero contributions are H0(V)=C and H0(V tensor E)=C. For L they
are O(5), O(5) plus O(4), and O(4). Their H1 groups vanish. The Q
calculation is the two elliptic curves plus the theta conductor above.

The map H0(L)->H0(Q) evaluates an affine polynomial at p1,p2,p3;
it has rank two. There is no kernel. A section of T_Bcal tensor E
has two affine coefficients a(t)=a0+a1*t and bcoef(t)=b0+b1*t.
In the target bases fixed above, H1(L)->H1(Q) is exactly

    [[1,p1,-h1,-h1*p1],
     [1,p2,-h2,-h2*p2],
     [1,p3,0,0],
     [0,0,1,p3]].

Its determinant is

    -(h1-h2)*(p1-p3)*(p2-p3).

Moreover

    h1-h2 = b*(e2-e1)/(2*(e1+mu)*(e2+mu)).

For mu close to zero all these factors are nonzero. The e_i are distinct
at zero (they are the three roots of e^3=1/2), and remain distinct after
shrinking. An equality e_i=-mu would force mu^3=1 in the node equation,
which is excluded in this neighbourhood. The p_i are distinct by the
discriminant calculation. The determinant is therefore nonzero throughout
the stated parameter neighbourhood, independently of the small nonzero
lambda. The companion fresh program verifies the exact determinant,
residue difference, discriminants, period identity and weighted counts.

The second exact sequence of section 4 now gives

    h^bullet(Jimage)=(0,1,2,0).

In the first exact sequence H0(fbar^*V)=C maps isomorphically to H0(T_X),
H1(fbar^*V)=C injects into H1(T_X), and H2(fbar^*V)=H3(fbar^*V)=0.
Consequently the complete result is

    (h0,h1,h2,h3)(X,T_X)=(1,2,2,0).

## 6. Smooth reduced Kuranishi germ and the two parameters

The primary family proposition provides two intrinsic holomorphic
invariants. The anticanonical pencil is the fibration itself, so its
four exceptional values, with their multiplicities, are intrinsic.
The cross-ratio of its three finite values and infinity depends on mu
and is independent of lambda. At mu=0, if a_i=p_i(0), implicit
differentiation gives p_i'(0)=-9/(2a_i). These three velocities cannot
come from an infinitesimal affine change A*a_i+B: otherwise the quadratic
A*z^2+B*z+9/2 would vanish at the three distinct a_i. Thus the
cross-ratio derivative in mu is nonzero, and remains nonzero nearby.

At a rank-one fibre the double curve is

    E_(mu,lambda)=C^*/<c(mu)*lambda>,

where c is a holomorphic unit. Its elliptic j invariant has expansion
j(q)=q^(-1)+744+O(q). Its lambda derivative is nonzero for sufficiently
small nonzero lambda. After shrinking the mu disc, a common sufficiently
small punctured lambda disc has this property. The two invariant
differentials are triangular with nonzero diagonal in (mu,lambda).
Thus the Kodaira--Spencer map of the displayed family is injective;
section 5 says its target has dimension two, so it is an isomorphism.

The Kuranishi germ is the analytic zero germ of its obstruction map in
H1(T_X)=C^2. The classifying map from this smooth parameter bidisc has
invertible derivative into that ambient C^2. The holomorphic inverse
function theorem gives an open neighbourhood in its image. Every
obstruction function vanishes on that neighbourhood and hence is the
zero germ. Its entire analytic ideal is zero. This proves smoothness
and reducedness, not only smoothness of the reduced underlying germ.
The classifying map is locally an isomorphism, proving miniversality.

Every sufficiently small deformation is consequently a member of this
same family. These are smooth compact threefolds diffeomorphic to S6,
with their constructed proper connected-fibre fibration over P1 and
smooth general two-torus fibre. The E1 input applies to the actual
fibration and gives the explicit field isomorphism

    f^*: M(P1) -> M(X).

Hence each such deformation has algebraic dimension one. No passage
to the original two-multiple-fibre family has been used.

## 7. Computation and exposure accounting

initial determinant assertion had the opposite row-order sign; the
actual determinant was printed and the assertion corrected to the exact
negative sign displayed here. A second assertion used structural SymPy
equality for two differently factored equal polynomials; it was replaced

assertion. Neither correction changes the nonvanishing argument.

The program is an algebraic certificate for the maps already derived;
it does not certify the analytic extension by itself. The complete
