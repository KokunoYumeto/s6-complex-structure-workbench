# Independent derivation: night:P4.6 (tag 02XY)

Target proof unopened. Input is its exact statement: a compact complex threefold X with b2(X)=0 and a reduced compact normal-crossings divisor F, allowing global self-intersection of distinct local branches. Let T be its finite number of ordinary triple points. I derive the assertion using the normalization of branches, so every map remains defined in the allowed self-intersection case.

Let ν:S→F⊂X be the disjoint union of the normalizations of all global components. Each S_i is a smooth compact complex surface and ν is a local immersion, because F has local coordinate branches. Let D⊂S be the conductor divisor, namely the preimage of the double locus, counted once per other local branch. It is a normal-crossings curve on S. It has exactly3T ordinary nodes: at a triple point each of the three lifted surfaces contains two crossing conductor branches. Let j:N→D be its normalization. The curve N parametrizes ordered pairs of different local branches along the double locus. Interchanging the two branches gives a free involution on N, and its quotient C is the normalization of the double locus viewed as unordered pairs. At every triple point C has three marked points, one for each branch pair; N has six. These statements remain true when global components or conductor components recur, since their normalization separates local branches.

Write n=c1(N_ν) for the normal line bundle of ν:S→X. The pullback of the Cartier line O_X(F) is N_ν⊗O_S(D): locally the equation for F is the product of the selected normal coordinate and the other branch equations. Taking c1 and using H2(X;Q)=0 gives n+[D]=0. The tangent-normal exact sequence
0→TS→ν*TX→N_ν→0
then gives c1(TS)=−n=[D] and c2(TS)=ν*c2(X)−c1(TS)n=ν*c2(X)+[D]². Integrating and summing over S, the first term is <c2(X),ν_*[S]>=<c2(X),[F]>=0 because the cohomology class of the Cartier divisor F vanishes rationally. Thus e(S)=D², with the square summed over the components.

For any compact smooth surface and any Cartier curve D, the exact sequence 0→O_S(-D)→O_S→O_D→0 and Riemann–Roch give χ(O_D)=−(D²+D K_S)/2. Here c1(K_S)=−[D], so χ(O_D)=0. The normalization sequence 0→O_D→j_*O_N→⊕_{nodes}C→0 has one-dimensional cokernel at every node, including self-nodes. Therefore χ(O_N)=3T. For a disjoint union of compact Riemann surfaces, e(N)=2χ(O_N)=6T. The free degree-two cover N→C gives e(C)=3T. In particular e(C) is even, so3T and hence T are even.

It remains to identify e(S) with e(C). Along each normalized double curve, the two local normal lines of its branches in X constitute the normal rank-two bundle of the curve, possibly exchanged on travelling around C. Pulling back to the ordered double cover N labels them. The tangent-normal sequence on C says that their total first Chern degree equals −e(C), because c1(TX) restricts trivially. On the ordered cover, summing the degree of the selected normal line once for every ordering is exactly the degree of that rank-two normal bundle on C. It is also the sum of deg(N_ν|D_branch)=n·D=−D² over S. Hence −D²=−e(C), so e(S)=D²=e(C).

Finally use the finite normalization map to count Euler characteristics stratum by stratum. Away from the double locus it has one sheet; on its ordinary double stratum it has two; at triple points it has three. Therefore e(S)=e(F_smooth)+2e(double_open)+3T. The normalized double curve has e(C)=e(double_open)+3T. Subtracting gives e(F)=e(F_smooth)+e(double_open)+T=e(S)−e(C)+T=T. This proves both conclusions using the reduced branch data only. If this support occurs with any positive multiplicities in a fibre, neither ν,D,C nor these topological spaces change, so the same computation applies to that same reduced support.
