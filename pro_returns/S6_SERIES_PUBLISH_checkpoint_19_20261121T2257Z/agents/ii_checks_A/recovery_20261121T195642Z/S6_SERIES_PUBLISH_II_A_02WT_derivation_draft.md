The standing ambient space has b1=b2=0. The P4.12 input gives torsion ω_S, and the P4.13 input gives e(S_reg)≥0. For a smooth rational compact surface, the required standard facts are π1(T)=1, H1(O_T)=H2(O_T)=0, Pic(T) torsion-free, and e(T)=2+b2(T)≥3. The positivity proof below does not require a classification of anticanonical divisors.

## 1. The full conductor is an anticanonical genus-one scheme

Let D be the conductor divisor on T, with its actual scheme multiplicities, and let C=Sing(S)_red. Since T is smooth, every point of S outside its nonnormal locus is smooth: normalization is an isomorphism on a normal neighbourhood. Thus ν identifies T minus D_red with S_reg, and the image of D_red is exactly C. The conductor formula is

ω_T(D)=ν*ω_S.

The right side is torsion. Pic(T) has no torsion, so it is trivial and D is an effective anticanonical divisor. D is nonempty because a normal support in this setting is smooth by P4.12, whereas S is singular. In particular O_T(−D)=ω_T and ω_D=O_D by adjunction.

Apply cohomology to 0→ω_T→O_T→O_D→0. Rationality and Serre duality give H0(ω_T)=H1(ω_T)=0 and H2(ω_T)=C, while H0(O_T)=C and H1(O_T)=H2(O_T)=0. Consequently

H0(O_D)=C and H1(O_D)=C.

Set R=D_red. The kernel of O_D→O_R is a coherent sheaf supported on a compact curve, with H2=0. Hence H1(O_D)→H1(O_R) is surjective, so h1(O_R)≤1. Also D, R and C are connected: a disconnected support would give a nonconstant idempotent in H0(O_D)=C, and C is the continuous image of R.

## 2. Equality of h1 forces the conductor to be reduced

Suppose h1(O_R)=1. This is a case distinction on the given D, not an additional assumption in the target. Write D=R+A, A effective. Adjunction gives

ω_R=O_R(K_T+R)=O_R(−A).

There is a canonical injective sheaf map ω_R→ω_D=O_D. Locally write D=(F=0), R=(r=0) and F=a r. Under residue frames the map is multiplication by a. Its image is the annihilator in O_D of the ideal generated by r, since in the regular local surface ring `(ar):r=(a)`.

Serre duality on R gives h0(ω_R)=h1(O_R)=1. Its nonzero global section maps injectively into H0(ω_D)=H0(O_D)=C, hence to a nonzero constant. That constant is a unit in O_D and is annihilated by r, so r=0 in O_D. Thus (r)⊂(F). Since F=a r and the local surface ring is a domain, a is a unit. This holds at every point, proving A=0 and D=R.

This exact adjunction morphism is the reason a reduced genus-one conductor can be used in the equality case; no replacement of a nonreduced divisor by its support is made without that proof.

## 3. The plane-curve conductor test for a generic double point

For a generic point of C, take a transverse plane curve A and its normalization B=⊕ C{t_i}. Let c be the conductor, with branch exponents c_i. The Gorenstein plane-curve duality gives

length(A/c)=length(B/A)=δ,
and therefore Σc_i=length(B/c)=2δ.

The exact module map can be obtained by applying Hom_A(−,A) to 0→A→B→B/A→0. Hom_A(B,A)=c, the finite-length Ext1 module is the local dual of B/A, and Ext1_A(B,A)=0 because B is maximal Cohen–Macaulay over the one-dimensional Gorenstein ring. Thus A/c is that dual finite-length module. This is the same conductor-duality construction already checked in the clean A.5.14 recovery batch; its local plane-curve form is written here to fix the exact length map.

When D is reduced, all c_i=1, so the number q of transverse branches satisfies q=2δ. Independently δ≥q−1: the independent residue constants on the q normalization branches modulo the common residue constant already give q−1 dimensions in B/A. Hence q≤2. The nonnormal locus has δ>0; q=1 would give 1=2δ, impossible. Thus q=2 and δ=1. The two branch delta-invariants are nonnegative and their intersection multiplicity is at least one; their sum is δ. Equality gives two smooth transverse branches. Thus the generic transverse type is the ordinary double point, with conductor degree q=2 over C.

P4.20 now applies to the same normalization morphism ν:T→S and proves normal crossings everywhere. Its use here only needs the generic ordinary-double condition outside finitely many points on the compact curve C. The failure of the generic condition is an analytic subset of C of dimension zero and is finite.

## 4. Fundamental groups of the conductor pushout

The compact normalization map realizes S as the topological pushout of R⊂T and its finite map R→C. One can triangulate the compact analytic curves and the analytic pair (T,R), and make the finite curve map cellular after a subdivision. The inclusion of R into T is then a cofibration. The ordinary pushout has the same fundamental group as the mapping-cylinder pushout. Since T and R are connected and π1(T)=1, van Kampen gives

π1(S)=π1(C)/normal-closure(image π1(R)).

For any reduced connected compact curve E, let k be the number of irreducible components, let their normalizations have genera g_i, and at a singular point p let b_p be the number of local branches. The normalization quotient is obtained by identifying finite sets of marked points. Its fundamental group is the free product of the normalization surface groups and a free group of rank

r_E=Σ_p(b_p−1)−k+1.

Its Euler number is Σ_i(2−2g_i)−Σ_p(b_p−1). The coherent normalization sequence gives

h1(O_E)=Σ_i g_i+Σ_p δ_p−k+1,

with δ_p≥b_p−1. In particular r_E+Σg_i≤h1(O_E). These are exact maps from the normalization quotient and its finite-length sheaf quotient, respectively; their roles are kept separate.

Every component of C dominated by a rational normalization component has rational normalization, by Riemann–Hurwitz. Thus in the all-rational-conductor cases π1(C)=F_r and e(C)=2c−r, where c≥1 is the number of components of C.

## 5. The needed presentation-deficiency bound

A finitely presented virtually abelian group G has presentation deficiency at most one. Here is a proof in the needed form. Choose a torsion-free abelian finite-index subgroup A≅Z^n, of index d. An r-generator, s-relator presentation of G yields a Reidemeister–Schreier presentation of A with 1+d(r−1) generators and ds relators.

For any presentation complex K of Z^n, H1(K;Q)=Q^n, and the map H2(K;Q)→H2(Z^n;Q) is surjective: attach cells of dimensions at least three to K to obtain a classifying space, which can only quotient H2. The exterior square of H1 of the n-torus gives dim H2(Z^n;Q)=n(n−1)/2. Therefore

generators−relators = n−b2(K) ≤ n−n(n−1)/2 ≤1.

Applying this to the Schreier presentation gives d(r−s−1)+1≤1, hence r−s≤1. This also covers finite virtually abelian groups by n=0. The input P4R.1 supplies precisely such a finite-index subgroup for π1(S), so this bound applies to the actual pushout group above.

## 6. Euler positivity in every case

The exact additive normalization identity is

e(S)=e(T)−e(R)+e(C)=e(S_reg)+e(C),

and P4.13 gives e(S_reg)≥0.

### Case h1(O_R)=0

The curve formulas in Section 4 force all normalization components of R to be rational and r_R=0. Thus π1(R)=1. The pushout gives π1(S)=F_r, so its virtually abelian presentation has r≤1. Every component of C is rationally normalized, and e(C)=2c−r≥1. Consequently e(S)≥e(C)≥1.

### Case h1(O_R)=1 with a positive-genus component

Section 2 gives D=R. The curve normalization formula allows only one positive-genus component, with normalization genus one. Call that component E. Adjunction on the smooth surface gives

2p_a(E)−2=(K_T+E)·E=−(D−E)·E.

The left side is nonnegative and the right side nonpositive. Therefore p_a(E)=1 and E is smooth, and it has zero intersection with every other component. Since D is connected, D=E. Section 3 gives a generically ordinary-double S, hence normal crossings by P4.20. The smooth conductor E has no nodes, so no ordinary triple point of S occurs: such a point would give intersecting conductor branches on a normalization sheet. Thus C is smooth and E→C is a finite unramified double covering. Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus(C)=1. Hence e(R)=e(C)=0 and e(S)=e(T)≥3, which is strictly positive.

### Case all normalization components of R are rational and r_R=0

The fundamental group of R is again trivial, irrespective of the possible unibranch contribution to δ. The same free-group argument as the first case gives e(C)≥1, and therefore e(S)>0.

### Case all normalization components of R are rational and r_R=1

The coherent genus bound gives h1(O_R)=1, so Section 2 again gives D=R. Equality δ_p=b_p−1 at every singular point, together with planarity, makes the singularities ordinary nodes: for b≥3 branches the pairwise intersection contribution is at least b(b−1)/2>b−1; for two branches equality δ=1 forces smooth transverse branches; for one branch δ=0 forces smoothness. Each component normalization is P1.

Adjunction gives ω_D=O_D. Pull it back to the normalization of each rational component. Its degree is −2 plus the number of node preimages on that component, counting both preimages of a self-node. Therefore every component has exactly two node preimages. Since D is connected, its dual graph is a cycle, including the one-component nodal curve and the two-component, two-node cycle. If k is its number of irreducible components, e(R)=k and π1(R)=Z.

The pushout group π1(S)=F_r/normal-closure(w) has a presentation with r generators and at most one relator, so Section 5 gives r≤2. Therefore e(C)=2c−r≥0. A positive e(C) gives strict positivity immediately. The only zero case is c=1 and r=2.

In that zero case the generic conductor map D→C has degree two by Section 3. Every irreducible component of D maps surjectively and with positive degree onto the irreducible C: the normalization map is finite, so a curve cannot map to a point. Thus k≤2. Since e(T)≥3,

e(S)=e(T)−k+0≥1.

This completes the positivity argument for the target. The degree-two conductor map is the precise reason the apparent Euler-zero graph case still has positive total Euler number.
