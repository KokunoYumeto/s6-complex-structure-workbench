## 1. The local normalization multigerm

Fix one exceptional point p of S. Choose an ambient coordinate ball B about p that contains no other exceptional point. The finite normalization has finitely many points q₁,…,q_r above p. Because the normalization is smooth, its germ there is the disjoint union of r copies of (C²,0), mapping finitely and generically one-to-one onto the r local irreducible branches of S in (C³,0). Away from p the entire image is smooth or an ordinary transverse double crossing. Its normalization map is consequently an immersion there, and each branch monogerm has only stable smooth or double images away from the origin.

The Mather–Gaffney criterion in the opened source, p.5, applies separately to each monogerm: its instability is isolated. Each is therefore finitely determined and has a stable deformation. Interactions among the finitely many branches can be made transverse by independently varying small translations and linear terms. This preserves their already stable behaviour on a compact annulus about the boundary. In particular, a cross-cap produced on one branch can be chosen away from all other branch images: the other images have complex codimension one, and an independent small translation avoids their finite set of relevant point incidences.

The ramification ideal on each source branch has finite colength, because it is the unit ideal away from its origin. A differential of rank less than two at the origin makes the ideal proper and its quotient a nonzero finite-dimensional C-vector space. Lemma 2.3 then gives a positive cross-cap count in a stable deformation of that monogerm. Its cross-caps persist under the small generic adjustments of the other branches just described.

## 2. Exact transport of compact cycles through an image deformation

Here is the particular nearby-fibre comparison used throughout the argument. The image deformation is a finite collection of deformations of the normalization branch maps, close on a fixed compact representative. On a sufficiently small sphere about p, the original image consists only of smooth and ordinary-double strata, transverse to the sphere. These are stable map germs. Their deformations stay smoothly locally equivalent on that compact boundary annulus, including their double-curve parametrizations.

A local defining equation of the total image family can be chosen holomorphically in the parameter. For an immersed branch this is its explicit hypersurface equation; take their product. For a parametrized branch, the finite image in the product of the parameter disc and C³ has pure codimension one, so its reduced height-one ideal in the regular local ring is principal. At a smooth point of the original branch on the boundary annulus the map is generically one-to-one and remains so; restriction to the central parameter has multiplicity one there. Thus the central restriction is the same reduced branch equation, up to a unit. Multiplying by a holomorphic extension of that unit makes the central equation the prescribed reduced defining function. Denote the resulting family by g_s.

There is a uniform small nonzero-value tube in which all regular level sets of g_s are transverse to the boundary sphere. To see the uniformity at values approaching zero, use the stable boundary coordinates. There the equations have the form a_s u or a_s uv, with a_s nonvanishing and varying smoothly in the parameter; the zero strata and their intersections remain transverse to the sphere. In the u case the tangent spaces of nearby levels approach the tangent space of the zero branch. In the uv case, a limiting tangent space contains the tangent space of the double stratum, or is tangent to one of the smooth strata. The derivative of the boundary defining function is nonzero on a suitable tangent vector to each such stratum. Consequently it stays nonzero on nearby level tangent spaces. A finite cover of the compact boundary annulus gives a common parameter and value bound. This is the explicit normal-crossing boundary instance of the stratified-Ehresmann argument invoked in the primary source, §5.3, p.33.

Over nonzero values, the interior critical set projects properly to the parameter/value base: boundary transversality excludes a critical point escaping through the boundary. The critical-value locus is a proper complex analytic subset of that base. For fixed parameter it consists of finitely many values, because a holomorphic function is constant on every connected component of its critical set. The complement in a sufficiently small parameter/value product is path connected; complex analytic subsets of positive complex codimension cannot separate this connected complex manifold locally. A path can also be constructed by successively taking generic complex line segments and making small detours around their isolated intersections with the analytic subset.

Along a compact path in this complement, the family of closed-ball level sets is a proper submersion with boundary. Choose a horizontal vector field tangent to the boundary, using a partition of unity on the compact inverse image of the path, and integrate it. This gives an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism between its endpoint fibres. It transports any compact cycle and its compact transverse perturbation. The two intersections are therefore identical, with their complex orientations.

In particular, take a singular point of an image at a small deformation parameter, or a nonzero critical value of its defining equation. A sufficiently small local regular fibre sits inside a regular global level in B. A regular value can be chosen as close as needed to the local critical value while avoiding all other global critical values. The preceding path transport embeds its compact cycles, with a whole compact neighbourhood, into a regular fibre of the original reduced defining function. This establishes the exact map used below; no assertion about injectivity of the entire local homology group is needed.

The common multiplicity causes no change to this map. Write the actual base function near p as a g^m and choose a holomorphic m-th root h of the unit a. Begin the preceding construction with the reduced defining function k=h g. A local fibre k=δ is an open part of the actual fibre f=δ^m. It is a smooth compact complex two-torus for sufficiently small regular nonzero δ. Whenever a weighted homogeneous local model G occurs in a deformed image with a unit factor b, choose a holomorphic D-th root of b and use the exact map Phi_j=b^(w_j/D)z_j. It satisfies G∘Phi=bG and is a local biholomorphism. The weights and degrees needed below are all positive and are given explicitly. Thus the compact-cycle transport reaches the actual torus fibre, with the same square.

## 3. Cross-caps force immersion

A cross-cap has normalization (u,v)↦(uv,u,v²) in target coordinates (x,y,z). The image equation is x²−zy²=0. Its differential minors are −u, 2v², 2v, so its ramification ideal is exactly (u,v), of length one. This is precisely the local cross-cap counted in Lemma 2.3, under the explicit target coordinate permutation from the source's example (u,v²,uv).

The nearby fibre of this image contains the compact sphere of square −4 derived in P4.16 for n=2. A stable deformation with a cross-cap would transport this sphere by Section 2 into the original nearby torus fibre. Every map from S² into a complex two-torus lifts through the universal cover C² and contracts; its homology class and square in that torus are zero. Compact-cycle transport preserves the square −4, a contradiction. Hence every branch ramification ideal is the unit ideal. Every local normalization branch is an immersion at its origin as well as outside it.

By the holomorphic rank theorem, each branch is now embedded near its normalization point as a smooth hypersurface. The remaining local problem is a finite collection of smooth hypersurface branches that is ordinary double away from p.

## 4. Three explicit non-normal-crossing models

Each model below has a positive weighted homogeneous central equation. Each admits a product deformation with a nondegenerate critical point at a nonzero value. By Section 2 its nearby fibre therefore contains an ordinary quadratic vanishing sphere with square −2.

For clarity, the quadratic sphere is concrete. After a holomorphic Morse coordinate change, the local equation is X²+Y²+Z²=ε. The real locus for positive ε is a sphere. It is Lagrangian. Multiplication by i identifies its tangent and normal bundles, reversing their induced two-dimensional normal orientation: the ordering (t₁,t₂,it₁,it₂) differs by one transposition from the complex ordering (t₁,it₁,t₂,it₂). Its normal Euler number is therefore minus the Euler number of S², namely −2. The complex linear map (X,Y,Z)↦(X,Y+iZ,−Y+iZ) identifies this quadratic equation with X²−UV=ε, the n=1 model of P4.16.

The holomorphic Morse coordinate change here is constructive: after a linear change choose a nonzero quadratic diagonal coefficient, solve the corresponding first-derivative equation by the implicit-function theorem, translate that variable by the solution, and write the remaining dependence as a unit times its square. Take the analytic square root of the unit. The Schur-complement Hessian in the remaining variables is nonsingular. Repeating in two and then one variable gives the displayed sum of three squares. Each transformation is a local biholomorphism and therefore preserves the sphere's normal Euler number.

### Two branches

Set G₂=z(z−x²−y²), with weights (1,1,2), degree 4. Its deformation is

G₂,s=z(z−x²−y²+s).

At q_s=(0,0,−s/2), the gradient vanishes, the value is −s²/4, the Hessian is diag(s,s,2), and its determinant is 2s². For every small s≠0 this is a nondegenerate critical point at a nonzero value. Thus G₂ supplies a sphere of square −2 in its nearby fibre.

### Three branches

Set G₃=xy(x+y+z²), with weights (2,2,1), degree 6. Its deformation is

G₃,s=xy(x+y+z²−s).

At q_s=(s/3,s/3,0), the gradient vanishes and the value is −s³/27. Its Hessian is

[[2s/3,s/3,0],[s/3,2s/3,0],[0,0,2s²/9]],

with determinant 2s⁴/27. This again gives a nondegenerate critical point at a nonzero value and a sphere of square −2.

### Four branches

Set G₄=xyz(x+y+z), with weights (1,1,1), degree 4. Its deformation is

G₄,s=xyz(x+y+z−s).

At q_s=(s/4,s/4,s/4), the gradient vanishes and the value is −s⁴/256. The Hessian is s²/16 times the matrix with diagonal entries 2 and off-diagonal entries 1. Its determinant is s⁶/1024. It too supplies the compact quadratic sphere.

## 5. Every remaining unstable arrangement deforms to one of those models

First consider two branches tangent at p. Put the first branch at z=0. The second is a graph z=h(x,y), with h(0)=0 and dh(0)=0. The critical point of h is isolated: a positive-dimensional component of dh=0 through the origin would have h constant zero, so the two branches would be tangent along a curve away from p, contrary to the ordinary-double hypothesis. Thus the holomorphic gradient map (h_x,h_y) is finite near the origin and has nonsingular fibres over arbitrarily small generic values.

Choose a point q near 0 where the Hessian of h is nonsingular. Subtract dh(q)·(x,y) and the constant h(q)−dh(q)·q. The deformed h has a nondegenerate critical point of value zero at q; these perturbation coefficients tend to zero as q tends to 0. The two-variable holomorphic Morse coordinate construction gives h=x²+y² after centring. The image of the two deformed smooth branches is therefore G₂=0, up to a unit and a biholomorphism. Move any other finitely many branches slightly so that their images avoid q. Their defining factors are then units near q. Section 2 transports the sphere from this local model into the original nearby fibre, which is impossible in the torus. Every pair of branches at p must consequently be transverse.

Now suppose exactly three branches meet at p and are pairwise transverse. Choose their first two equations as x=0 and y=0. Write the third as h(x,y,z)=0. Pairwise transversality says h_x(0) and h_y(0) are nonzero. The function h(0,0,z) has an isolated zero; an identically zero restriction would give a curve of triple points away from p. A simple zero makes the three normal covectors independent and gives an ordinary triple crossing. Otherwise the restriction has order at least two.

For that latter case, choose a nearby q on the z-axis where the second derivative of the restriction is nonzero. Subtract a small linear term in z and a small constant to make q a double zero of this restriction. The normal x and y derivatives stay nonzero. Solve h_z=0 for z=z₀(x,y) and complete the square as above, preserving x and y. The third equation becomes c(x,y)+Z², where c(0,0)=0 and c_x(0),c_y(0) are nonzero. Write

c(x,y)=x a(x,y)+y b(y),

with a(0),b(0) nonzero; this follows from c(x,y)−c(0,y) being divisible by x and c(0,y) by y. The map (x,y)↦(X,Y)=(x a(x,y),y b(y)) is biholomorphic, preserves the two coordinate branch divisors, and sends c to X+Y. Their product equation becomes a unit times XY(X+Y+Z²), exactly G₃. The sphere obstruction again gives a contradiction. Thus every triple of branches is transverse.

Finally, suppose at least four branches meet. Perturb the linear parts of four of their smooth equations by arbitrarily small amounts so that every three normal covectors are independent, while retaining their common point. Move any remaining branches off that point. Use the first three perturbed equations as coordinates x,y,z. The fourth can be written

x a(x,y,z)+y b(y,z)+z c(z),

where a(0),b(0),c(0) are all nonzero, by successive subtraction along coordinate subspaces. The coordinate map (x,y,z)↦(x a,y b,z c) is biholomorphic and preserves the first three coordinate planes. The four-branch product becomes a unit times XYZ(X+Y+Z), exactly G₄. Its sphere obstruction is the same contradiction. Hence at most three branches meet.

These perturbations are deformations of the immersed normalization branches themselves. They are small on the fixed boundary annulus, where the original image was stable. They therefore fall within the explicit transport construction of Section 2. Each contradiction uses an actual compact sphere with a fixed nonzero intersection, not a signature comparison or an assertion about an unspecified vanishing cycle.

## 6. Conclusion and the receiving global assertions

There is one smooth branch, two transverse smooth branches, or three transverse smooth branches at every point. The respective coordinate equations are x=0, xy=0 and xyz=0, up to a unit. This proves that S has normal crossings everywhere, with no compact Betti-number assumption.

In the standing compact setting, the ambient space is a smooth compact complex threefold with b₁=b₂=0 and a proper connected-fibre map to a smooth compact curve, whose general fibre is a two-torus. For this S, P4.6 applies to its reduced divisor, and the irreducible-support conclusions of P4.8 apply with the same normalization and conductor maps. The global sum in P4.7 applies to the fibration subclass in which every reduced support satisfies the just-proved local hypotheses or already has normal crossings. Its original all-supports hypothesis is not dropped. This is the exact receiving-use scope of the target's final sentence.
