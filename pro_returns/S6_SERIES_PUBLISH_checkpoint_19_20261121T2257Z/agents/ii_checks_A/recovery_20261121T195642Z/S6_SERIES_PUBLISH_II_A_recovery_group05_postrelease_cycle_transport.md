## WN: all six proof steps

The printed first step covers F_n by U={y≠0} and N_+,N_−. The graph domains |zy^n|<delta contract by (y,z)↦(s y,s z), 0≤s≤1; their intersections with U have coordinates (y,zy^n) in C* times a disc. The inclusion takes the positive y-loop to the positive y-loop. Van Kampen kills that loop, and Mayer–Vietoris gives the kernel of (a,b)↦a+b. This agrees with the two-chart and matching-sphere construction derived before release. The preproof proof represents its primitive generator by an embedded sphere, so the printed Hurewicz conclusion follows for exactly that class.

The second printed step constructs the compactification. The n successive blowups at each root have coordinates x²−1=y^j a_j and z=a_j/y^(n−j). Earlier exceptional curves carry poles of z, while the last one supplies the affine line y=0,x=±1. The two infinity divisors, strict transform of y=0, and earlier exceptional curves form the displayed boundary. On y≠0 this complement is the original affine graph; on the final exceptional chart the coordinate z is regular and gives its entire affine line. Thus these charts establish the actual open isomorphism F_n=T_n minus B.

In the third step, the boundary graph has root x=infinity, its two children y=infinity and the strict transform of y=0, and the two exceptional chains attached to the latter. Each vertex has a unique parent path to the root. It is a tree of rational curves, so H1(B)=0. Under compact-support duality and excision, H2(F_n)→H2(T_n) corresponds to H²(T_n,B)→H²(T_n). The pair sequence is injective because H1(B)=0, and its image is the kernel of restriction degree on each boundary component. Restriction degree is intersection. The H_x,H_y conditions remove both ruling coefficients; the chain differences force one constant coefficient on each chain; the strict y=0 transform makes the sum of those constants zero. The primitive integral kernel is therefore generated by v=sum E_+,j−sum E_−,j, and v²=−2n.

The sphere Sigma_n constructed independently maps into this same compactification by its inclusion in F_n. Near an endpoint it meets the last exceptional curve transversely: the local coordinates are (y,z), that exceptional curve is y=0, and the sphere is the graph z=−conjugate(y)^n with the stated x coordinate. The two oriented local intersections are opposite and each has magnitude one. The constant-chain kernel computation therefore sends [Sigma_n] to v or −v. Fixing its orientation at the first endpoint selects v. This is an integral transport of the two compact cycles.

The fourth printed step uses weights (n+2,2,4), degree 2n+4; the preproof derivation uses (n+1,2,2), degree 2n+2. Let epsilon=t^(n+1) and delta=t^(n+2) for positive small t. Their fibre values agree, and the scaling maps differ on that fibre by the exact automorphism
(x,y,z)↦(x,r y,r^(−n)z), r=t^(−2).
The positive-r path is an isotopy preserving x²−zy^n. Both carry the same compact class and a compact transverse representative into the local fibre.

In the fifth step, use the same local analytic m-th root h of the defining unit in both coordinate constructions. Both maps satisfy g composed with Phi=h g. Their composition Phi_printed composed with Phi_derived^-1 therefore preserves g exactly. Each derivative at the origin is diagonal with nonzero entries, so these are local biholomorphisms and give the same local-to-actual-nearby-fibre embedding after that coordinate transport.

The sixth step is the same compact-support intersection comparison and covering contraction as the preproof derivation. Every spherical map in the smooth torus lifts to C² and contracts. Its class is zero, while the exact open embedding preserves the intersection −2n. Thus all multiplicities are excluded. The statement introduces no b2 condition.

### Additional n=2 compactification in the historical program

## WO: both printed items

The first printed item replaces the two roots by the a distinct roots of x^a=1. Its Mayer–Vietoris kernel is sum:Z^a→Z. The preproof U_i cover gives the same group through successive short exact sequences. The matching sphere Sigma_i has boundary the once-around y-loop between roots i and i+1, so it maps to e_i−e_(i+1), a primitive root-lattice basis. The printed blowup map sends this to
v_i=sum_j E_i,j−sum_j E_(i+1),j.
The endpoint intersection calculation just made proves this map integrally. Both bases have adjacent pairing +n; choosing the first orientation fixes all remaining signs. This proves the full isometry, not only agreement of rank and determinant.

For an integer inverse to the boundary coordinate calculation, put q_i=c_i,n and d_i,j=c_i,j−c_i,j+1. Its inverse is
c_i,j=q_i+sum_(k=j)^(n−1) d_i,k.
The boundary kernel is d=0, sum q_i=0. Its coordinates in the root basis are k_i=sum_(h=1)^i q_h, inverse q_i=k_i−k_(i−1), with k_0=k_a=0. These inverse homomorphisms prove primitivity for every a,n. The signed incidence minors and Cauchy–Binet calculation in the preproof program compute determinant (−1)^(a−1) a n^(a−1) by a different algorithm from the old symbolic rank, determinant and extracted-minor routines. The same weighted local map and torus embedding settle the remaining exclusions in printed item 1.

The second printed item gives a general normalization map, which has now been checked completely. The map C²_(u,v)→S is
x=u^n v, y=u^a, z=v^a.
It is finite because both source coordinates satisfy monic equations. On y≠0 it has a choices of u and the uniquely determined v=x/u^n, so its generic degree is a. The faithful action (u,v)↦(zeta u,zeta^(−n)v) is transitive on those generic fibres. The quotient is consequently finite and birational over S. Its invariant ring is normal: an element of its fraction field integral over the invariant ring is integral over the original normal ring, belongs to that ring, and remains invariant. Hence the quotient is the normalization.

For (a,n)=(2,3), the invariant coordinates U=u²,V=uv,W=v² satisfy UW=V², and (x,y,z)=(UV,U,W). The target equation pulls back to U²(V²−UW)=0. This is the printed A1 example. The preproof example (a,n)=(4,2) comes from the same quotient: first divide by the order-two subgroup (u,v)↦(−u,v), and then the remaining involution acts by simultaneous negation on (u²,v). Its invariants Y=u⁴,X=u²v,W=v² satisfy X²=YW. The exact map to the independent A1 coordinates is (u_new,v_new,w_new)=(W,X,Y), and the target map becomes (v_new,w_new,u_new²), as derived before release.

For n=a, the action fixes v and the quotient is the smooth plane with coordinates (y=u^a,v). The formulas x=yv,z=v^a give the stated conductor curve map v↦v^a. This proves the printed cyclic-ramification clause through the same finite map.

## WP: all five steps and the two self-intersection values

Put d=gcd(b,c), b=dB, c=dC, so gcd(B,C)=1. Introduce
Q_A={x^a−(u_0v_0)^d=1}
over A={x^a−w^d=1}, w=u_0v_0.
The maps are
p_0:F→Q_A, (x,y,z)↦(x,y^B,z^C),
rho:Q_A→Q, (x,u_0,v_0)↦(x,u_0^d,v_0^d),
where Q={x^a−UV=1}.
They are proper finite holomorphic maps of degrees BC and d². Their composite is the preproof map p of degree bc.

For printed step 1, A is smooth and w has a simple zero at each x^a=1. Choose a disc D_l around a lifted arc and a coordinate xi with
w=u(xi)(xi−alpha)(xi−beta).
Let X=(2xi−alpha−beta)/(beta−alpha), h_0=u(xi)(beta−alpha)²/4 and y'=y/h_0^(1/B). Then the equation is exactly (y')^B z^C=X²−1. These are the specified analytic coordinate maps removing the unit and moving the endpoints.

For printed step 2, choose Bp+Cq=1. The two formulas
y=h^p t^C, z=h^q t^(−B), t=y^q z^(−p), h=X²−1,
are inverse homomorphisms on the two-dimensional multiplicative torus. The meridian matrix [[B,C],[q,−p]] has inverse [[p,C],[q,−B]]. Each graph neighbourhood contracts by simultaneous positive real scaling; its punctured part has the indicated two y,z meridians. Their unimodular images kill both local base meridian and the t-loop. Attaching both neighbourhoods therefore gives pi1(W_l)=0. All maps in this van Kampen calculation are the displayed coordinate inclusions.

For printed step 3, let K be the original matching sphere in Q over the chosen x-arc. Its inverse image under rho consists of d matching spheres K_l, one over each lifted arc in A. They share both endpoints and are otherwise disjoint. Their maps to K have positive degree d. Each K_l is a smooth Lagrangian sphere and has square −2.

Every cross-pairing can be computed explicitly. Near an endpoint Q_A has coordinates (u_0,v_0), and
K_l: v_0=c_l conjugate(u_0)+higher-order terms,
where the c_l differ by the d roots of unity. The oriented real graph basis for c_l=a+ib is
(1,0,a,b), (0,1,b,−a)
in the ambient complex orientation. For c_k=c+id, the determinant of the two graph bases is
−((c−a)²+(d−b)²)=−|c_k−c_l|².
Thus each endpoint contributes −1. The sphere orientations are pulled back with positive covering degree; the radial coordinate increases at one endpoint and decreases at the other, so both sphere orientations reverse together at the second endpoint. Its sign is again −1. Consequently K_l.K_k=−2 for every l≠k, and the self-pairings are also −2.

Let alpha_l be the compact-support dual of K_l, and let gamma_l=PD(p_0^*alpha_l). Cup naturality and degree BC give every pairing
gamma_l.gamma_k=BC(K_l.K_k)=−2BC,
including equal indices. The exact bump-graph cap comparison in the frozen WP derivation identifies the pullback dual with the oriented inverse-image cycle at both branch points, so this calculation includes their singular parametrizations.

These gamma_l are exactly the local classes used in the printed proof. On D_l also set U'=u_0/h_0. Then U'v_0=X²−1, and p_0 factors into
(y',z)↦(y',Z=z^C)↦(U'=(y')^B,Z).
The second map pulls the ordinary matching sphere back to the primitive WN class of square −2B. Pullback by the first, C-power map gives the printed class of square −2BC. The disc coordinate can be chosen as a round disc containing the endpoints, and its straight interval supports the matching sphere; equivalently the same primitive Mayer–Vietoris boundary loop proves preservation under the inclusion into the full affine WN fibre. The displayed coprime cover has pi1=0, so Hurewicz makes the same transfer class spherical.

The cap comparison gives the exact Gysin identity
rho^*PD(K)=sum_l alpha_l.
Pulling back by p_0 and applying duality gives beta=sum_l gamma_l, where beta is the preproof sphere-sum class. Therefore
beta²=sum_(l,k)(−2BC)=−2d²BC=−2bc.
This also equals degree(rho) times degree(p_0) times K². Thus the two printed and independently derived numbers agree through the exact cycle relation and every cross-pairing. A single gamma_l is not identified with beta.

Printed step 4 uses the same positive weights (b+c,a,a) and the same explicit unit-removal map as the preproof derivation. Either the local gamma_l or their spherical sum beta embeds with its compact intersection into the actual torus fibre and yields the same obstruction.

For printed step 5, its coordinates (u,v) equal the preproof coordinates (t,y). The normalization map is exactly
(x,y,z)=(u³v²,v,u²).
The image ring contains all even u powers and odd monomials u^i v^j with i≥3,j≥2. The normalization is a module over C{v,u²} generated by 1,u. A conductor element therefore satisfies that both it and its u multiple lie in the image. For even i this requires i≥2,j≥2; for odd i the original membership requires i≥3,j≥2. The full conductor is u²v²C{u,v}, including convergent series by even/odd decomposition. Its divisor has multiplicity two on each axis.

Swapping source coordinates swaps the Jacobian columns and multiplies every maximal minor by −1. The independent list (−3y²t²,4yt⁴,2t) therefore becomes the printed list (3u²v²,−4u⁴v,−2u). The ideals correspond to (t) and (u) by this exact swap. This proves the divisorial rank drop and its relation to P4.15's finite rank-drop hypothesis.
