3. For the algebraic-dimension assertion, an effective divisor on a torus has a semipositive
Hermitian form: averaging its integration current under translations gives that constant form.
A nonzero semipositive form has rank one or two. Rank two gives the abelian case. Rank one
gives the elliptic quotient just constructed. In the absence of a definite form, there cannot
be two different such kernel subtori. Indeed addition from their product onto Ft would be a
surjective holomorphic homomorphism with finite kernel, and the product polarization would
descend after multiplication by an integer, giving a definite form. Thus there is a single kernel
Kt in this case. Every effective divisor is a sum of its translates: its intersection number with
Kt is zero, whereas a curve mapping nonconstantly to the elliptic quotient has strictly positive
intersection with a general quotient fibre. Every meromorphic function therefore factors
through this quotient. One can check the last statement on a fibre avoiding its polar divisor,
where it is a holomorphic function on a compact elliptic curve and hence constant; analytic
continuation then gives the factorization. The quotient supplies nonconstant meromorphic
functions, so the algebraic dimension is exactly one. If neither locus occurs, a nonconstant
meromorphic function would have a polar divisor of one of the two forbidden ranks. This
proves dimension zero. Finally monodromy acts by integral determinant-one changes of basis,
preserving (11.1) and its Pfaffian. Thus the stated unions descend as the stated subsets of B◦.
