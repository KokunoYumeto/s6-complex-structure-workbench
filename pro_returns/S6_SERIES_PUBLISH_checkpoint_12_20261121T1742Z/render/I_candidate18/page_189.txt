5. The standard interpretation of the Weil–Châtelet group represents ξ by a smooth projective
genus-one curve over L; taking its relatively minimal regular model over C gives V . Local
triviality identifies each henselian model with that of EL. Over C the corresponding étale
identifications yield holomorphic identifications over small base discs. Equivalently, V is
obtained by gluing copies of E over these discs by translations by local sections. These
translations extend over the regular I12 model. Thus V is locally isomorphic to E , has smooth
total surface, no multiple fibres, and the same single I12 fibre. Properness and flatness hold
locally over the base and hence globally. The local translation gluing specifies V as in (Xiao,
1991, Theorem 3.4, pp. 14–15; Section 4, p. 21).
6. The G-invariance of ξ gives an affine isomorphism of its generic fibre covering σ. It extends to
the relatively minimal regular surface: in this particular situation the extension can be checked
on a common resolution. If an exceptional (−1)-curve for one projection is not contracted
by the other, its image is a vertical rational curve and has self-intersection at least −1, since
blowing down only increases self-intersection. On either locally trivial model all vertical
rational curves are components of the I12 fibre, of square −2. Thus every such (−1)-curve is
contracted by both projections and can be removed from the common resolution. Repeating
this proves that the map is an isomorphism. The same argument gives the local comparison
with the Jacobian model used above. Transporting the base by σ changes none of these
facts. Write A0 for this lift. Its twelfth power is translation by a section N ∈E(L)G = ZP.
