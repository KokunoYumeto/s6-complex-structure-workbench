Theorem 21.2 (The two minimal smooth fillings). [tag 02ZK]
The two minimal subdivisions of 21.1 give proper flat holomorphic families X +, X −→∆u
with smooth total spaces. Their joint maps to B × ∆u are flat. Over ∆∗
u they are isomorphic as
families to the specified untwisted family X(u). Each central fibre Q± is reduced, irreducible, and
has normal crossings, with smooth double locus S and no triple points.
Its normalization R± is obtained by blowing up R along S∞in, respectively, one of its two
sections and then taking the small resolution at the unique ordinary double point of that blow-up
prescribed by the fixed bottom diagonal. The two proper transforms of the sections are still copies
of S, glued by T. Their normal-bundle product is trivial; in particular T 1
Q± ≃OS.
Proof. 1. Apply either fan subdivision over the cusp neighbourhood in Theorem 20.6, and use
the identity modification elsewhere. Toric modifications are proper, and a subdivision is an
isomorphism off the strata it subdivides. Its restriction over q ̸= 0 is the identity, so this gluing
has no additional transition choice. The period actions preserve the subdivisions, and the
covering description in 20.6 descends the proper modifications to the quotient. All maximal
cones are regular. Every local ring of the resulting fourfold is therefore regular. The coordinate
u is the product of those affine coordinates whose rays have hu = 1; there are at most two
such rays in a maximal cone. This proves reduced normal crossings and the absence of triple
points for the u-central fibre. The regular-sequence argument, or the saturated face images in
21.1, proves joint flatness. Properness follows from properness of the toric modification and of
X ♭.
2. At a node of S∞, write the original normalization as coordinates (X, z, v), with section X = 0
and centre (X, zv). Blowing up that ideal has charts
X = zvA;
zv = XB.
The first is smooth, and the second has one conifold at its origin. A small resolution of the
latter is, for one of the bottom-diagonal conventions,
X = za,
v = aB;
z = Xd,
B = dv.
Interchanging z, v gives the other diagonal convention. These are holomorphic charts of a
proper small modification: they are the two usual charts of the blow-up of the appropriate
rank-one ideal on the conifold. The strict transform of X = 0 is A = 0 in the first chart and
never passes through the conifold. It is therefore unchanged as a surface. The other section is
disjoint from the centre and is unchanged as well. Over a smooth point of S∞the exceptional
fibre is one P1; over its node it is a chain of two P1’s, one being the exceptional curve of the
small resolution. This identifies the normalization morphism, including its order of operations.
3. For example the corresponding total fourfold modification of xy = uzv can be written by first
taking x = ua, ay = zv and then resolving the conifold in (a, y, z, v), together with the other
chart u = xb, y = bzv. Its three smooth affine charts map to (x, y, z, v, u; q) by
(u, z, λ, y) 7−→(uzλ, y, z, λy, u; zλy),
(u, a, ν, v) 7−→(ua, νv, aν, v, u; aνv),
(x, b, z, v) 7−→(x, bzv, z, v, xb; zv).
The first two are the conifold-resolution charts a = zλ, v = λy and z = aν, y = νv.
Substitution gives xy = uzv and q = zv in every chart. Exchanging the chosen zero and
infinity branches gives the other model.
