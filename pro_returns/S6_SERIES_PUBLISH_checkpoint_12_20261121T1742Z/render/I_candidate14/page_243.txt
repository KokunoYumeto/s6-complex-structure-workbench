8. A two-dimensional fibre germ would supply one. Suppose an origin point of Z has local
fibre dimension two. Finite base change and normalization are finite maps and preserve that
local fibre dimension at points lying above it. At a resulting point of Z′, choose two generic
local linear sections in an analytic embedding. Their intersection with the fibre is isolated.
Intersecting with Z′ gives a two-dimensional analytic germ whose map to B is finite. This
is the local finite-projection criterion from Weierstrass preparation; see also Demailly (2012,
Chapter II, Proposition 8.2 and its proof, pp. 116–117). Choose a component through the
point. Its finite image has dimension two and is therefore the whole base germ. A finite
representative of this germ, after shrinking the bidisc, is finite and surjective over the entire
smaller bidisc. Its normalization is still finite and maps to Z′, giving exactly the forbidden
multisection of positive degree. Properness of this finite representative prevents loss of sheets
at its boundary.
9. Every fibre component of a dominant map from a pure fourfold to a smooth surface has
dimension at least two. If a two-dimensional irreducible component existed, a generic point on
it would have local fibre dimension two, which we have excluded. Flatness over r makes r = 0
a Cartier divisor of pure dimension three, so the origin fibre has no component of dimension
greater than three. All its irreducible components consequently have dimension three, as
asserted.
