The commuting-meridian obstruction
Theorem 18.6 (Nonexistence of proper fillings). [tag 0313]
[tag 030D]
[tag 031A] [tag 02RH]
For every admissible (3, 4) twist, the punctured family has no proper Hausdorff complex-
analytic filling at w = 0 after any finite parameter cover. This includes every sphere member
and permits arbitrary singularities, multiplicities and extra central components.
Proof. 1. A proper filling pulls back properly to a further cover w = R6n. By 18.3 we resolve
its dominating component and marked-map graph. Near infinity take the finite base change
q = Q12m, normalize the dominating component, and resolve it. Principalize the inverse image
of the two axes using (Bierstone and Milman, 1997, Theorem 1.10, p. 216, with the analytic
category in (0.1)(2), p. 208). That theorem turns a coherent ideal on a smooth analytic space
into a locally monomial ideal after proper smooth-centre blowups. The axis ideal is coherent
and the relevant inverse image is compact over a smaller closed bidisc, so its hypotheses hold.
The smooth torus family over Q∗× R∗is unchanged.
2. The proper map to the bidisc has connected fibres. Its Stein finite factor is generically one
sheet because its general fibre is a connected torus. A finite birational map to the normal bidisc
is an isomorphism: its finite local algebra is integral over, and contained in the fraction field
of, the integrally closed local ring of that bidisc. In particular the corner fibre is connected.
3. In the axis complement, the original logarithmic deck lifts have
AQ = I −12mE32 + 12mE41,
AR = I + 12nE41,
[Q, R] = −12mnbbδ.
The affine relation is the difference H(s + 12m) −H(s) = 12mb through n parameter turns.
The lifts

Ro = R
commute. They give a specific semidirect product Λ ⋊Z2; central δ corrections do not affect
the following coefficients.
4. For every irreducible axis component Di, put
αi = ordDi(Q),
βi = ordDi(R),
κi = the bγ coefficient of its lattice meridian.
Its positive meridian is (λi, αi, βi), with nonnegative orders and positive sum. Conjugation
changes its lattice vector by a vector killed by the first-coordinate covector; thus κi is
independent of its basing path in the complement. Meeting components have commuting
meridians, represented by coordinate circles in a smooth NC chart. The bδ coordinate of their
commutator gives
(mαi + nβi)κj = (mαj + nβj)κi.
(18.8)
Both nilpotents have square zero and their products vanish, so there are no higher terms. The
E32 contribution lies in the bw coordinate; E41 gives exactly (18.8). The ratio κi/(mαi + nβi)
is therefore constant along every intersection edge. Its denominator is positive for every axis
component, including vertical exceptional components.
