5. A minimal class-VII surface with b2 = 0 and a curve is a Hopf surface. Its elliptic curves have
square zero, and a reduced anticanonical divisor has at most two elliptic components. For this
last assertion the exact sequence 0 →KV →OV →OC →0 gives h0(OC) ≤1 + h1(KV ) = 2.
The Euler contribution is zero. This also covers secondary Hopf surfaces.
6. For a minimal class-VII surface with b2 > 0, the reduced anticanonical divisor is a numer-
ically anticanonical divisor whose coefficients are all one. Dloussky (2006, arXiv version 3,
Corollary 2.25, pp. 16–17; Lemma 2.26, p. 17; Proposition 2.27, pp. 17–19) imply that it is
parabolic Inoue or Inoue–Hirzebruch. Half-Inoue has only a nontrivially twisted anticanonical
divisor and cannot satisfy (22.65). The remaining possibilities are hyperbolic Inoue with two
disjoint rational cycles, or parabolic Inoue with one rational cycle and a disjoint elliptic curve.
If n = b2 > 0, there are n rational cycle components and e(V ) = n, so the contribution is zero.
In the parabolic case the elliptic curve has square −n. The curve configurations, anticanonical
identities and underlying (S1 × S3)#nP2 topology are recorded in Fujiki and Pontecorvo
(2010, Sections 3.2–3.3, pp. 20–22).
7. These assertions extend to nonminimal pairs. An exceptional curve outside C meets it once,
transversely at a smooth point, since −KV ·E = 1; its contraction reverses an interior blow-up.
An exceptional curve in C meets the rest at two points and its contraction reverses a corner
blow-up. The reduced anticanonical condition is preserved. Interior blow-up increases e(V \C)
by one, whereas corner blow-up leaves it unchanged. Thus every contribution is nonnegative.
Equality permits only toric rational pairs, minimal elliptic ruled or Hopf pairs, and corner
blow-ups of hyperbolic or parabolic Inoue pairs. For these Inoue pairs the cycle count and
b2 increase together, and the fundamental group stays Z. Corner blow-ups do not touch the
parabolic elliptic curve, whose square remains strictly negative.

9. Elliptic normal-degree balance. An elliptic double curve contains no triple point. The local
model p = xy gives opposite degrees to its two normal lines. Sum these degrees over all elliptic
conductor branches. The total is zero. An elliptic ruled or Hopf component contributes zero;
a parabolic Inoue component contributes the strictly negative degree of its sole elliptic branch.
No rational component with elliptic conductor remains. Hence there are no parabolic Inoue
components.
