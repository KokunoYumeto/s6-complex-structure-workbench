Proposition 23.7 (Connected cyclic covers and the cusp comparison). [tag 01S7]
For an admissible (3, 4) member with n = |12l0 −4l1 −3l2| > 1, let π : Y →X be the universal
cover and g = fπ. It is a finite étale holomorphic map of degree n. Every fibre of g is connected;
its finite reductions are bielliptic, its multiplicities are 3, 4, and its cusp Wn normalizes to a
disjoint union of n copies of dP6. For EX of Definition 12.3 and the identical quotient EY on Y ,
pullback induces isomorphisms
Rqf∗OX ≃Rqg∗OY ,
Rqf∗EX ≃Rqg∗EY .
Proof. 1. We first derive the fundamental-group presentation from the fillings. The smooth
family over the punctured base has zero section, so its fundamental group is Λ ⋊⟨x, y⟩, where
x, y are the analytic peripheral generators fixed by the root-deck convention and act by
A1, A2. On a finite filling the lifted affine generator has mjth power translation by vj: its mj
translations add to vj because Ajvj = vj. Thus filling the normal disc imposes x3 = tv1 and
y4 = tv2, with the signs fixed by the clockwise convention on the first page.
2. The toric covering space of the cusp tube is simply connected. Indeed its dense torus has
cocharacter lattice Z3; a loop may be perturbed into that torus, and the normal discs of the
three rays (0, 1), (e1, 1), (e2, 1) kill a basis of this lattice. This argument applies also to the
infinite fan, since a compact loop and a homotopy meet only finitely many of its locally finite
charts. The same conclusion holds for the small tube: if a loop in |tc| < ϵ bounds a disc in
the toric space, the torus action (x1, x2, tc) 7→(x1, x2, atc) for a sufficiently small positive a
moves that compact disc into the tube, and the scaling homotopy of its boundary stays in
the tube. The cusp quotient therefore has fundamental group Λ/Λtor, and its toric meridian
bounds the normal disc at fixed x1, x2. A translated collar makes this meridian tµy−1x−1,
where γ(µ) = l0. Van Kampen consequently kills Λtor and imposes xy = tµ.
