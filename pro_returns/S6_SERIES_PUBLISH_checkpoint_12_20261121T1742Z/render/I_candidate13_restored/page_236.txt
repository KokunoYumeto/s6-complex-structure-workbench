3. If an additional finite proper modification admitted a proper analytic closure, its proper image
would give such a Z, by the proper mapping theorem; the image has dimension two and
the same generic exceptional curves. The horizontal exceptional locus of a relative proper
holomorphic blowdown likewise gives Z. For a meromorphic contraction with proper analytic
graph, resolve the graph and apply the same argument to its horizontal exceptional locus and
its proper image in K∗. This is a direct marked-intersection proof of a familiar nonproperness
phenomenon for exceptional curves in GSS surface deformations; see Dloussky and Teleman
(2012, arXiv version 2, Section 1.2, PDF pp. 3–5) for the primary context.
4. We next prove the deformation assertion. Write C = Cs0 and S = K ∩{Q = Q0}. Near this
curve, S is a smooth surface and C is its exceptional curve, so
NC/S ≃OP1(−1).
The base coordinate Q is submersive on K near C. Because C misses every other central
branch, the reduced function r is a local defining function for K near C and its normal
derivative is nowhere zero. Consequently the differential of (Q, r) gives the exact normal
sequence
0 −→OP1(−1) −→NC/Y
(dQ,dr)
−−−−−−→O⊕2
P1 −→0.
(22.36)
Its extension class vanishes because H1(P1, O(−1)) = 0. Hence
NC/Y ≃O(−1) ⊕O⊕2,
H1(C, NC/Y) = 0,
h0(C, NC/Y) = 2.
(22.37)
Moreover (22.36) induces an isomorphism on global sections, since both H0(O(−1)) and
H1(O(−1)) vanish.
5. Kodaira’s embedded-deformation theorem therefore gives a smooth two-dimensional local
parameter space T of compact embedded deformations of C, with tangent space H0(C, NC/Y).
In precisely this relative surface setting one may apply Dloussky and Teleman (2012, arXiv
version 2, Proposition 2.3 and its proof, PDF pp. 6–7): after shrinking the neighbourhood of
C, (Q, r) is a holomorphic submersion of relative dimension two, and C is a compact simply
exceptional divisor of its fibre. That proposition does not require the submersion to be proper.
The general completeness theorem is due to Kodaira; we use the exact statements in Merkulov
(1995, Section 1, p. 71, and Section 3.1, p. 74). Shrink this family so its curves remain in the
chosen neighbourhood. Their genus remains zero, so all are isomorphic to P1. The functions
Q, r are constant on every compact connected curve and thus define a holomorphic map
T −→C2.
Its derivative at [C] is precisely the isomorphism on sections induced by (22.36).
The
holomorphic inverse function theorem shows that the map covers a neighbourhood of (Q0, 0).
In particular the nearby fixed pairs with r ̸= 0 contain rational curves. A holomorphic map
from P1 to a complex torus lifts to its vector-space universal cover and is constant. Thus
those fibres cannot be the prescribed ordinary two-tori.
6. The conductor condition is essential. For example, if a further central component meets K along
the persistent elliptic axis, then its intersection number with C is one and NK/Y|C ≃O(−1).
At the conductor intersection dr vanishes on the normal fibre of C, so (dQ, dr) is not the bundle
epimorphism of (22.36). This computes precisely where that normal-sequence argument uses
the conductor-disjoint hypothesis.
