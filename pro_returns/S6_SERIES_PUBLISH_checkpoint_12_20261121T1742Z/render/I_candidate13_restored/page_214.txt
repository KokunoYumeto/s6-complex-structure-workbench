The same obstruction holds for a finite collection of such marked modifications if the period

More generally, if a compact
smooth surface contains a preserved marked elliptic curve E of nonzero normal degree d, every
holomorphic automorphism restricting to translation by b on E must satisfy d b = 0 in E. The
latter assertion also excludes a finite cycle of such preserved marked cap axes when the cycle
composite is an infinite-order translation. These assertions apply to the extension of the second
cusp period to the finite Hopf modifications in 22.27, for generic nonzero parameters, after every
finite base change. The period action in these assertions is a holomorphic action on the full finite
modifications with the specified marked core.
Proof. 1. Let S ⊂H be the image of the exceptional curves of p. It is finite. Repeated infinitely
near blow-ups change the curves over a point but do not make S infinite. If eh were a lift, the

maps to the separated complex surface H. The image under eh of a curve contracted by p is
therefore again contracted by p. Applying the same argument to eh−1 proves that the total
exceptional locus is preserved. Thus h(S) = S.
2. The automorphism h preserves E, where it is translation by [b]. Consequently the nonempty
finite set S ∩E would be invariant under that translation. The orbit of any one of its points
is finite, so for some k > 0,

This says exactly that [b] is torsion, a contradiction. In particular, no choice of multiplicities
or infinitely near centres changes the obstruction: only their nonempty finite support on E is
needed.
