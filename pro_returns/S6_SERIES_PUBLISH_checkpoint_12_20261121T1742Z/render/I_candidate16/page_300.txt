For vector extensions, Bost and Künnemann (2009, Theorem 3.1.2, p. 386) proves that
an algebraic point of the universal vector extension of an abelian variety which lies in its
maximal compact subgroup at one complex embedding is torsion. Its input is an algebraic
line bundle with an algebraic connection having unitary monodromy. The compact-character
construction in proposition 26.3 specifies a point in a multiplicative extension. An application of
the vector-extension theorem therefore requires construction of the stated algebraic connection.
Let fE be the Lattès map defined by x(2Q) = fE(x(Q)). Its canonical height is bhfE(x(P)) =
2bhO(P). For rational parameters proposition 24.2 makes algebraicity of N(P) equivalent to
algebraicity of exp(bhfE(x(P))). Nguyen (2023, Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 1.6, arXiv v1, p. 3)
settle the corresponding multiplicative-height problem for nonintegrable polynomials using
Böttcher coordinates in the basin of infinity. An elliptic duplication Lattès map has no exceptional
point: the inverse images of any point contain the images of all successive division points of an
elliptic point, an infinite set. A polynomial has the exceptional point ∞, so no conjugate of fE is
polynomial. The Julia set is the whole sphere and the required basin of infinity is absent.
The arithmetic algebraicity criterion of Xie (2024, Theorem 1.3, p. 3, and Theorem 2.3, p. 12,
arXiv v1) requires sufficiently rapid height-normalized approximation by points in a fixed number
field and an analytic germ formally defined over that field. This hypothesis fails for the compact
leaf in 26.14: its tangent has the proved transcendental slope c = ζ(u) −η1u/ω1. A smooth
formal germ defined over Q has an algebraic tangent; algebraic translations, coordinate changes
and isogenies preserve this obstruction. Assuming N(P) algebraic supplies an algebraic point
on the leaf and its integer multiples, but not an algebraic formal germ. Attempting to force
convergence by successive division also loses the fixed-number-field hypothesis: if [2]nQn = Q
with all Qn ∈E(K) for one number field, then bhO(Qn) = 4−nbhO(Q) > 0. The Qn are distinct
with bounded ample height, contrary to Northcott finiteness.
