5. There is also an equality of sheaves
π∗F = ΘY .
To check it at p, a holomorphic derivation of the cone integrates locally to a holomorphic flow:
embed the cone in a polydisc, lift its coefficient functions, and use the ordinary holomorphic
vector-field flow, which preserves the defining ideal. The flow preserves the unique singular
point, hence its maximal ideal. Functoriality of the blow-up of that ideal lifts the flow to
the resolution, preserving the exceptional curve. Its derivative is therefore a section of F.
Conversely a section of F differentiates pullbacks of holomorphic functions on Y and descends
to a derivation, because π∗OS = OY . Both operations agree off E and are inverse there;
uniqueness extends the equality over E.

7. The degree-two part of the Leray sequence is
H1(S, F) −→H0(Y, R1π∗F) −→H2(Y, π∗F) −→H2(S, F).
The first map is surjective and the last group is zero. Using π∗F = ΘY proves H2(Y, ΘY ) = 0.
8. The local-to-global lifting criterion follows from the following explicit obstruction calculation.
For a compact complex space with isolated singularities, H2(Θ) = 0 makes the restriction from
global to local deformations formally smooth. Here is the obstruction argument for the present
single singularity. Take a finite Stein cover with one small neighbourhood of p and all other
members smooth. For a small extension of Artinian bases, prescribe the lifted deformation
on the singular member and take smooth local lifts on the other members. Their pairwise
identifications lift on the smooth Stein intersections. The failure of the cocycle identity on
triple intersections is a Cech two-cocycle in ΘY tensored with the square-zero kernel. Its class
vanishes by the proved H2-vanishing; correcting the identifications by a one-cochain glues the
desired lift. This proves the relative infinitesimal lifting property.
