## Exact auxiliary selection

For each of the nine rank-one curves, the receiving coefficient and derivative results give a weight-two cuspidal newform of trivial character, of conductor level N_i, with functional-equation sign ε=−1. Apply BFH Theorem (ii) with

\[
S=\{2\}\cup\{p:p\mid N_i\}.
\]

The source supplies a fundamental discriminant D with εD>0, every prime in S split in K=Q(√D), and L(E_i^D,1)≠0. Thus D<0, every conductor prime splits, and 2 splits. This uses exactly the source's existence assertion; no infinitude assertion is required.

Here is the elementary local step that excludes the two exceptional discriminants. Splitting at 2 first implies that D is odd. For an odd fundamental discriminant, the integers of K are generated by (1+√D)/2, with polynomial

\[
X^2-X+(1-D)/4.
\]

Its splitting into distinct linear factors over F_2 requires its constant term to vanish in F_2. Hence D≡1 mod8. In particular D cannot be −3 or −4. The only imaginary quadratic fields with a unit other than ±1 are Q(i) and Q(√−3): a root of unity in a quadratic field has cyclotomic degree at most two, so its order is 1,2,3,4 or6. Their discriminants are −4 and −3. Consequently this selected K has O_K^×={±1}, exactly as assumed in Gross's setup. This is stronger than merely restating the required exclusion.

## Exact object and normalization comparisons

The character twist is the same object on both sides of the cited application. On a rational short Weierstrass model E_i:y²=x³+Ax+B, its D-twist can be written D y²=x³+Ax+B. Over K, the map (x,y)↦(x,√D y) is an isomorphism to E_i, and its conjugate differs by negation. Thus it is the quadratic-character twist in BFH and in

\[
L(E_i/K,s)=L(E_i,s)L(E_i^D,s).
\]

For the point, use the same rational modular parametrization φ:X_0(N_i)→E_i, with φ(∞)=O, and a Heegner point x defined over the Hilbert class field H of K. The trace P_K=Tr_{H/K}(φ(x)) defined on GZ preprint p.126 is Gross's y_K=Tr_{K_1/K}(φ(x_1)) on p.235, under H=K_1 and the same choice of ideal above N_i. Changing that ideal changes the trace by sign and torsion as stated by Gross, so the property of nonzero height is preserved. The units in the GZ formula have u_K=|O_K^×/±1|=1 for the chosen K. A rational scaling of the invariant differential rescales the period factor by a nonzero scalar and leaves nonvanishing of the height unchanged.

Since L(E_i,1)=0 and L′(E_i,1)>0, the product identity gives L′(E_i/K,1)≠0. GZ Theorem V.2.1 therefore gives nonzero canonical height for this trace. A canonical height is nonnegative and vanishes exactly on torsion, so the trace has infinite order and all hypotheses of Gross Theorem1.3 hold.
