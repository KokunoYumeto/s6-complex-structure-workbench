\subsection{The Hopf gluing associated with the first cyclic subgroup}
\begin{statement}{P3F.4}{Proved}
The Hopf lift of every $a$-power extends over $D^4\times S^3$; its specified sphere gluing is standard.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
Every Hopf lift of \(a^n\), for \(n\in\mathbb Z\), gives the standard oriented 7-sphere by the following gluing construction. This specifies the morphisms required to pass from these mapping classes to homotopy spheres.

Use unit quaternions \(S^3\subset\mathbb H\) and \(S^2\subset\operatorname{Im}\mathbb H\), with Hopf map \(h(u)=uiu^{-1}\). The principal circle acts on the right on (u). On

\[
 p:S^3\times S^3\longrightarrow S^2\times S^3,
 \qquad p(u,y)=(h(u),y),
\]

fix its Chern class to be the chosen positive generator; reversing the orientation of the Hopf fibre changes the sign and does not change the extension argument. Lift \(\alpha:S^3\to SO(3)\) to \(\widetilde\alpha:S^3\to SU(2)\), using the double cover given by quaternionic conjugation. The lift exists because \(S^3\) is simply connected. The map

\[
 A_n:S^3\times S^3\to S^3\times S^3,
 \qquad (u,y)\longmapsto(\widetilde\alpha(y)^n u,y)
\]

is a smooth principal-circle-bundle automorphism covering \(a^n\). The equality \(h(vu)=v h(u)v^{-1}\) verifies this statement, and left quaternionic multiplication commutes with the right Hopf action. Crucially it is the boundary restriction of the explicit diffeomorphism

\[
 E_n:D^4\times S^3\longrightarrow D^4\times S^3,
 \qquad (q,y)\longmapsto(\widetilde\alpha(y)^n q,y). \tag{P3F.4.1}
\]

Its inverse uses the inverse unit quaternion, and it preserves orientation.

Fix the orientations in the decomposition

\[
 S^7=\partial(D^4\times D^4)
     =(D^4\times S^3)\cup_{\mathrm{id}}(S^3\times D^4)
\]

by the outward-normal-first convention and rounding the corner. Gluing the first piece to the second by \(A_n\) gives a manifold carried to this standard boundary by \(E_n\) on the first piece and the identity on the second. This is an explicit orientation-preserving diffeomorphism.

Boundary-isotopic gluing maps give diffeomorphic glued manifolds: extend the isotopy over a collar. \subsection{The full affine-cap freeness criterion and the sphere sector}

\] The four classes \(u_2,v_2,p,q\) form an integral basis of \(H^2(E;\mathbb Z)\). The division by three in the last class must be retained; replacing \(q\) by \(\eta h\) would lose the torsion in \(P3F.34.1\).

Here is a proof using the already constructed cap \((m,n,d)=(3,1,0)\). Denote its projection by \(\pi_0\). The normal circle is \(3\alpha-\ell_0\), and the integral class \(-\psi\) evaluates to one on it. Its Euler class therefore vanishes by the integral Gysin sequence, and this circle bundle is topologically trivial. The exact first-cohomology pullback from this cap has basis \[
                      h,\qquad 3\psi+\eta.
\] P3F.29 proves that the cap's integral two-cohomology restricts to the full fibre invariant lattice with basis \(u_2,v_2\). Fibre integration, or direct contraction by \(3\alpha-\ell_0\), identifies its actual boundary pullback as \[
                    u_2+q,\qquad v_2.
\] Indeed these invariant forms are horizontal; the invariant forms on the finite torus cover compute all rational cohomology by averaging. The kernel of restriction is spanned rationally by \(p,q\), and horizontality forces the displayed correction uniquely. The product splitting with the class \(-\psi\) then supplies the other two integral classes \[
 (-\psi)h=-u_2,\qquad
 (-\psi)(3\psi+\eta)=p.
\] Thus \(v_2,u_2+q,u_2,p\) is an integral basis, equivalently \(u_2,v_2,p,q\).

\subsubsection{3. Determine the exterior restriction without guessing a splitting}\label{determine-the-exterior-restriction-without-guessing-a-splitting}

Fill \(V\) by the \((3,1,0)\) cap. The resulting manifold is the proved sphere of P3F.26. Its integral Mayer--Vietoris sequence shows that \(H^2(V)\cong\mathbb Z^2\) and that restriction identifies it with a direct summand of \(H^2(E)\). Its complement is the cap pullback just computed. The homology sequence similarly gives \[
 H_1(V)=\mathbb Z,\qquad H_2(V)=\mathbb Z^2.
\] The generator of \(H_1(V)\) is gamma, as in the exact van Kampen calculation of P3F.26.

Let two basis vectors of the exterior restriction have coordinates in the basis \(u_2,v_2,p,q\), and project them modulo \(v_2\) to vectors \(U_1,U_2\in\mathbb Z^3\) in the ordered basis \(u_2,p,q\). Write \[
                       W=U_1\times U_2.
\] No component of this integral vector is assumed in advance. For the circle \(P3F.34.3\), integration of the invariant forms gives the exact one-classes \[
 \begin{aligned}
 (\pi_\beta)_*u_2&=3d\psi-nh,\\
 (\pi_\beta)_*v_2&=0,\\
 (\pi_\beta)_*p&=m\psi+n\eta,\\
 (\pi_\beta)_*q&=kh+d\eta.
 \end{aligned}                                      \tag{P3F.34.5}
\] The circle has period one, so these are integral fibre-integration maps, not unnormalized contractions.

For every \(k>0\) and \(d\in\mathbb Z\), the cap with \(n=1\) gives the already proved sphere of P3F.29. The pair sequence for attaching its disc bundle shows that its map \[
 H_1(B_{3k,1,d})\longrightarrow H_2(V)
\] is an integral isomorphism. Its dual is precisely (P3F.34.5) restricted to \(H^2(V)\). Taking cross products of its two images gives determinant \[
                 \pm\bigl(W_1 k-W_2d+W_3\bigr).
\] It has absolute value one for every positive \(k\) and every integer \(d\). Therefore \[
                   W_1=W_2=0,\qquad W_3=\pm1.
\] This proves integrally that the exterior image projects isomorphically onto \(\mathbb Z u_2\oplus\mathbb Z p\) and has no \(q\)-component. Its possible \(v_2\)-terms vanish under \(P3F.34.5\), so they do not affect any map being computed. This argument uses actual proved sphere fillings to determine the exact restriction lattice; it does not assume a boundary normalization.

\subsubsection{4. The two Smith invariants and every homology group}\label{the-two-smith-invariants-and-every-homology-group}

After an integral change of basis in \(H^2(V)\), the dual transfer map has the two columns \[
       v_1=(3d,-n,0),\qquad v_2=(m,0,n)
                 \quad\text{in }L_{m,n,d}.            \tag{P3F.34.6}
\] Their cross product is \[
              v_1\times v_2=n(-n,-3d,m).
\] The primitive normal of \(L_{m,n,d}\) is \((-n,-3d,m)/g\), so their index in this rank-two lattice is \(g|n|\). The gcd of all their entries is \(g\). Because \(L_{m,n,d}\) is primitive in \(\mathbb Z^3\), divisibility of a vector in it is the same as divisibility in the ambient lattice. Thus the first Smith invariant is \(g\), and the second is \(|n|\). Freeness implies \(g\mid n\): all primes other than three are excluded by \(\gcd(k,n,d)=1\), and a nontrivial factor three occurs exactly in the already classified \(3\mid n\) case. In fact \(g\in\{1,3\}\).

Use the pair sequence of \(X=V\cup N(B)\), with the Thom identification \(H_j(N(B),E)=H_{j-2}(B)\). Its degree-two portion is \[
 H_1(B)\longrightarrow H_2(V)\longrightarrow H_2(X)
 \longrightarrow H_0(B)\xrightarrow{\ n\ }H_1(V).
\] For \(n\ne0\), the last arrow is injective. Torsion in \(H_1(B)\) maps to zero in the free group \(H_2(V)\). The transpose of the free transfer has the Smith invariants just computed, and therefore \[
                   H_2(X)=\mathbb Z/g\oplus\mathbb Z/|n|.
\] P3F.33 already gives \(H_1(X)=\mathbb Z/|n|\). Euler additivity gives \(\chi(X)=2\), because \(V\) and \(E\) are fixed, every flat cap has Euler zero, and the reference filling is \(S^6\). The rational groups in degrees one and two vanish. Poincare duality and Euler characteristic then force every middle rational group to vanish. Integral duality and the universal coefficient theorem give \[
 H_3(X)\cong\operatorname{Ext}(H_2(X),\mathbb Z),\quad
 H_4(X)\cong\operatorname{Ext}(H_1(X),\mathbb Z),\quad
 H_5(X)=0.
\] This proves all groups in \(P3F.34.1\).

For \(n=0\), the two vectors in (P3F.34.6) generate \(3\mathbb Z\psi\) in the lattice \(\mathbb Z\psi\oplus\mathbb Z(kh+d\eta)\). The pair sequence therefore has cokernel \(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/3\), followed by a free \(\mathbb Z\) kernel of the now-zero last arrow. It gives \(H_2=\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z/3\). Duality and Euler characteristic give \(P3F.34.2\).

The executable check builds a primitive integral basis of \(P3F.34.4\), expresses \(P3F.34.6\) in that basis, and computes its Smith form for all sampled free triples. The arbitrary-integer proof is the cross-product and divisibility argument above.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Every one-vertex cusp fan and the prime fixed-set obstruction}
\begin{statement}{P3F.35}{Proved}
Every one-vertex unimodular cusp fan is an integral-basis triangulation, and explicit periodic diagonal flops connect it to the vertical-edge fan while preserving sphere topology. For every prime, its cusp fixed set has total mod-prime Betti number two; Smith--Floyd therefore forces every separate cap in a sphere member to have free delta action, without a semifree hypothesis at the cusp.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
The full argument has passed independent audit.

For the whole-zero \(I_1,e=1\) cusp, let the allowed triangulation have vertex set \(\mathbb Z^2\), period group \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z^2\), and unimodular triangles. Every such triangulation is the periodic triangulation \[
 T(v,w)=\{(0,v,v+w),(0,w,v+w)\}+\mathbb Z^2                 \tag{P3F.35.1}
\] for an integral basis \((v,w)\). It is connected to the standard vertical-edge triangulation by a finite sequence of the explicitly described periodic diagonal flops. Each step preserves the integral homology and fundamental group of the compact threefold, and therefore preserves its status as the standard smooth six-sphere.

For every prime \(p\), the \(\mu_p\)-fixed subset inside this cusp has total \(\mathbb F_p\)-Betti number exactly two, whether or not the physical delta action is semifree. Consequently every separate finite or logarithmic cap in a sphere member has free delta action. This last assertion supplies the cap-freeness input to P3K.15 without assuming semifreeness at the chosen cusp. The present statement does not yet assign a return class to every fan.

\textbf{Proof: the exact form of the triangulation.}

The quotient torus has area one. Every triangle has area one half, so there are exactly two triangle orbits. Translation fixes no finite edge or triangle. Counting incidences gives three edge orbits. Choose one triangle \((0,a,b)\); unimodularity says \(\det(a,b)=\pm1\). Its three unoriented edge classes are \(a,b,b-a\), which are distinct and exhaust all edge classes. After the integral linear change \((a,b)\mapsto(e_1,e_2)\), the only possible third vertices of a triangle incident to \([e_1,e_2]\), using those edge classes, are \(0\) and \(e_1+e_2\). The opposite-side condition selects the latter. Likewise, across \([0,e_1]\) it selects \(e_1-e_2\), and across \([0,e_2]\) it selects \(e_2-e_1\). These are exactly the adjacent triangles of the periodic unit-square triangulation. Propagating across edges determines the entire plane triangulation. Taking \(v=a\), \(w=b-a\) gives (P3F.35.1). This is an equality of the actual periodic fans after the stated integral coordinate map, not a replacement by an unmarked abstract triangulation.

The three edge classes of \(T(v,w)\) are \(\{\pm v,\pm w,\pm(v+w)\}\). In the parallelogram \((0,v,w,v+w)\), replacing the diagonal \(v+w\) by \(v-w\) gives \(T(v-w,w)\). Conversely replacing the edge class \(v\) by \(v+2w\) gives \(T(v+w,w)\). Thus adding or subtracting one column of a basis from the other is exactly one periodic diagonal flip. Interchanging columns does not change the triangulation. Reversing one column changes the sole diagonal class \(v+w\) to \(v-w\), again one flip.

For completeness, an explicit terminating reduction is available. Write the two first coordinates as \((x,y)\). Repeatedly replace the first column by the first column minus \(\lfloor x/y\rfloor\) times the second, and interchange columns. Each integer multiple is executed as its finite sequence of unit column moves. Euclid's algorithm reaches \((\pm1,0)\), because the determinant is \(\pm1\). Reverse the first column if needed. The second column is now \((0,\pm1)\); reverse it if needed and subtract its appropriate multiple from the first column. The result is \((e_1,e_2)\). Every intermediate basis and every flip have been specified, and the standard second edge is the physical vertical delta direction.

\textbf{Proof: the actual local flop and its topology.}

For one selected edge orbit, each triangle contains exactly one edge from that orbit. Distinct corresponding toric curves therefore share no three-cone, hence no torus fixed point and no intersection. Their lattice translates are disjoint. No nonzero lattice translation fixes a finite edge, so in the quotient the center is one embedded rational curve. The four height-one rays of either adjacent parallelogram satisfy the actual integral relation \(r_0+r_2=r_1+r_3\). Their determinant-one adjacent cones give the normal bundle \(\mathcal O(-1)\oplus\mathcal O(-1)\). Blowing up its zero section gives exceptional divisor \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\), with normal line \(\mathcal O(-1,-1)\). The two ruling contractions are the two fan subdivisions just described. All maps commute with lattice translation and agree with the identity off the compact center; they therefore descend to the actual compact models.

The integral blowup decomposition adds precisely one free summand in degrees two and four. Primitivity can also be read directly: the exceptional divisor has degree minus one on either contracted ruling. Thus the common blowup gives \[
 H^j(\widetilde X;\mathbb Z)
   =H^j(X_-;\mathbb Z)\oplus H^{j-2}(\mathbb P^1;\mathbb Z)
   =H^j(X_+;\mathbb Z)\oplus H^{j-2}(\mathbb P^1;\mathbb Z).
\] There is no torsion quotient hidden in the additional summands. Removing a real codimension-four center preserves fundamental groups by general position; its normal sphere bundle and disk bundle are simply connected, so van Kampen also proves invariance under the blowup and either contraction. Starting with a simply connected integral homology six-sphere therefore gives the same groups after every step. The smooth sphere identification is the same Smale and \(\Theta_6=0\) argument used in P3F.26.

\textbf{Proof: the exact prime fixed sets.}

Let \(\delta\in\mathbb Z^2\) be the primitive physical circle cocharacter, and write \(\delta=a v+b w\). On the three open edge strata the finite isotropy orders are \[
 |\det(\delta,v)|=|b|,\qquad
 |\det(\delta,w)|=|a|,\qquad
 |\det(\delta,v+w)|=|a-b|.                              \tag{P3F.35.2}
\] Here order zero denotes the full circle stabilizer. The formula comes from the exact quotient lattice \(\mathbb Z^2/\mathbb Z e\) for an edge direction \(e\): the delta cocharacter has image \(\det(e,\delta)\) in that rank-one lattice. Since \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), any prime divides at most one of the three numbers in \(P3F.35.2\), including zero.

In a determinant-one triangle chart with coordinates \((z_0,z_1,z_2)\), the circle has integral weights \((c_0,c_1,c_2)\) with \(c_0+c_1+c_2=0\). Their greatest common divisor is one: the triangle rays are an integral basis of the three-dimensional toric lattice and delta is primitive. Thus modulo a prime no two weights can vanish; if two did, the third would also vanish, contradicting primitivity. The \(\mu_p\)-fixed locus in this chart is therefore either the isolated origin or one coordinate axis. Those axes are exactly the edge strata selected by \(P3F.35.2\). If a prime divides one edge order, the two local axes glue through that edge to its whole rational curve; both triangle fixed points lie on it and there are no further fixed points. If it divides none, the fixed set consists of the two triangle points. Open toric surface strata have no finite stabilizer, because their two-dimensional torus lattice is the original \(\mathbb Z^2\) and delta is primitive.

On every regular fibre the delta action is primitive translation, so no \(\mu_p\)-fixed component can leave the central cusp. Consequently the compact cusp fixed set is exactly \(\mathbb P^1\) or exactly two points. Both have total \(\mathbb F_p\)-Betti number two. A nonfree delta action in a separate cap has a point fixed by some prime subgroup. Its nonempty compact fixed subset is disjoint from the cusp fixed set and contributes at least one to the total Betti number. Floyd's Theorem 4.4, applied to the order-\(p\) diffeomorphism of the compact six-sphere, bounds that total by \(\sum_i b_i(S^6;\mathbb F_p)=2\), a contradiction. This proves cap freeness for every allowed fan.

The primary theorem was checked in E. E. Floyd, \emph{On periodic maps and the Euler characteristics of associated spaces}, Trans. AMS 72 \(1952\), Theorem 4.4, p.146; the coefficient convention is stated on p.139. The exact elementary fan reduction is reproduced in \path{code/part3/arbitrary_fan.py}.
\end{proof}
