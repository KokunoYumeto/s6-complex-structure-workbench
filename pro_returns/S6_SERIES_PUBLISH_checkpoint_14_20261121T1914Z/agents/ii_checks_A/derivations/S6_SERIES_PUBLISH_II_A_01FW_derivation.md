# Independent derivation: cdp:st:D.1.11 (tag01FW)

The target proof has not been opened. I read the exact packet, its E4 supplement, and my already frozen derivations for D.1.7–9. This argument treats the rank-one case explicitly; counting separate fibrewise exceptional sets alone would not justify a simultaneous conclusion.

Put V=H^1(Y,O_Y) and E=R^1f_*O_Y. The integral exponential sequence and b_1=0 identify Pic^0(Y) with the complex vector space V. Coherent Leray on P^1 identifies V with H^0(P^1,E). E4 makes E locally free. The beta constructed in D.1.9 evaluates nontrivially in every full fibre's cohomology. Hence the associated section beta of E has no zero: its value0 in E⊗C(c) would map to beta|F_c=0. Thus the evaluation homomorphism V⊗O→E has rank at least1 everywhere.

Suppose its generic rank is k≥2. Remove the finite critical-value set and the finite rank-drop set; on the remaining curve take a countable cover by simply connected discs with a trivialization of the integral local system Lambda=R^1f_*Z. A torsion restriction exp(v)|F_c has n ev_c(v)=iota(lambda_c) for a positive integer n and an integral flat section lambda on such a disc. There are countably many pairs (n,lambda) on each disc. For a fixed pair the incidence set in V times the disc is defined by holomorphic equations; at each base point its affine fibre is empty or has complex dimension dim V−k. Its total complex dimension is therefore at most dim V−1. A complex analytic set admits a countable stratification by smooth complex manifolds; on compact coordinate pieces the projection to V is Lipschitz, so its image has Lebesgue measure0 in the real2dim(V)-dimensional space. Exhaust these pieces by countably many compact sets. Their projected images are compact, closed, measure0 and have empty interior. The exceptional set over this open curve is a countable union of such closed sets.

At each of the finitely many removed points, restriction is a nonzero complex-linear map V→H^1(F_c,O_{F_c}), since beta has nonzero restriction. The inverse image of iota(H^1(F_c;Z))/n is a countable union of empty sets or proper affine complex subspaces. These are closed and have measure0. The total exceptional set is consequently F_sigma, meagre and null. Its complement is a dense G_delta of full measure, by the Baire theorem and the measure-zero calculation.

Now suppose the generic evaluation rank is1. Since beta is a nowhere-zero section, it defines a line subbundle O beta⊂E. Every global section has zero image in E/O beta on a dense open set, hence everywhere. It is a holomorphic scalar multiple of beta; the scalar is a global holomorphic function on P^1 and therefore constant. Thus V=C beta and Pic^0(Y)=C.

Let alpha be the integral flat section whose coefficient image is beta, as constructed in D.1.9. Work first on the smooth locus C°. For each locally flat lambda in Lambda, the condition iota(lambda) wedge beta=0 defines an analytic subset of a disc. The sets where this is not an identity are discrete. There are countably many discs and lattice sections, so all exceptional scalar parameters obtained at those discrete points form a countable set: at a fixed point beta≠0 makes the scalar in n z beta=iota(lambda) unique.

The sections for which the wedge vanishes identically form a saturated integral sublocal system W⊂Lambda. Analytic continuation preserves the identity, and divisibility of a section preserves it, proving both claims. It contains alpha. The real coefficient map is injective by D.1.7, and its image on W lies in the real2-dimensional plane C beta. Therefore rank W is1 or2. On C°, divide alpha by the greatest common divisor of its coordinates to obtain a primitive global section alpha0, so beta=d iota(alpha0) for a positive integer d.

In rank1, W=Z alpha0 and the scalar parameters from W are rational multiples of1/d; they form a countable set. In rank2 choose a local integral basis (alpha0,delta). Write iota(delta)=h iota(alpha0). The scalar h is holomorphic and has nonzero imaginary part, because a real h would contradict real injectivity and independence of the two real classes. A monodromy loop fixes alpha0 and sends delta to epsilon delta+m alpha0, where epsilon=±1 and m is integral. On the connected universal cover the sign of Im h stays constant; hence epsilon=1 for every loop. Choose delta so Im h>0. Then q=exp(2pi i h) is a well-defined bounded holomorphic function on C°, with 0<|q|<1. Bounded removable singularities extend q over every omitted point to P^1. It is constant there. Since q never vanishes on C°, differentiating gives h'=0 on every chart. All the scalar periods m+n h are therefore constant, and all torsion scalar parameters (m+n h)/(N d) are a countable set.

The finitely many nonsmooth fibres each add only a countable set, because beta has nonzero restriction and H^1(F_c;Z) is a finitely generated group. This proves the countability assertion in rank1. It also gives the required dense G_delta and full-measure conclusion in this case, since a countable union of points is F_sigma, meagre and null in C.

For another Picard component, choose a finite-order representative T by the explicit m-th-root adjustment in D.1.9. The coordinate map v↦T tensor exp(v) identifies that component with V. A fibre restriction of this bundle is torsion precisely when exp(v) has torsion restriction, since T has finite order. Thus all conclusions transfer in the claimed vector-space coordinates.

Finally two-torus fibres make rank E=2. Under a splitting E=O(a)⊕O(b) with a negative degree, global evaluation has rank at most1 by homogeneous-polynomial evaluation. Beta ensures rank at least1, so the established rank-one case applies. This proves the stated quantifier for the published general-line-bundle assertion and all of the target's clauses. No finite or numerical computation is used.
