Remark 22.20 (Elliptic-bundle and Hopf degeneration inputs). [tag PT10] The Cartier-divisor
construction used in Proposition 22.19 is the construction of Fujimoto (1990, construction, p. 85;
Definition 1.1, p. 86). His Remark 1.4 attributes non-Kähler torus degenerations to Ueno. His
Proposition 1.14, pp. 92–93, extends principal elliptic bundles across subsets of codimension at
least three; Example 1.15, pp. 93–94, gives a principal-bundle extension failure in codimension two.
Hopf components occur in the semistable degenerations of Nishiguchi (1988, Theorem 1.4, preprint
p. 7, and Example 2.1, p. 13). The diagonal Hopf quotients, elliptic-conductor identifications and
normal lines in Schröer and Siebert (2002, Propositions 3.1–3.4, preprint pp. 6–9) provide related
explicit models. Proposition 22.19 proves its own proper family using the stated Cartier-divisor
cocycle.
Remark 22.21 (Normal degrees along the exceptional ruling). [tag PT11] For the divisor D =
E ×P1 in Hi ×P1, the normal line is pulled back from E and has degree zero on every ruling fibre.
For the exceptional divisor obtained by blowing up a smooth elliptic curve in a threefold, the
tautological normal restricts to OP1(−1) on that fibre. Restricting the normal-crossing identity
ND/A ⊗ND/B ⊗OD(T) ≃OD
to the ruling therefore gives −1 + 0 + deg(T|P1) = 0. In particular an empty triple divisor has
the wrong degree, whereas the section T = E × {p} contributes exactly +1. Proposition 22.27
computes the full line bundles, including their elliptic factors.
Proposition 22.22 (Generic simplicity and the distinguished orbit). [tag PT12] Fix an ordinary
base point at which µ /∈Qτ + Q. Such base points exist. Outside a countable set of admissible
values of the parameter β, the corresponding two-dimensional complex torus is simple. For every
admissible β at this base point, the distinguished line Ce, with e = Πbδ = (0, 1)t, meets the period
lattice in Ze. The induced orbit map is the injective holomorphic group map
ι : C∗−→C2/ΠΛ,
ι(e2πiz) = [ze].
Here [z] 7→e2πiz identifies C/Z with the source.
Proof. On an ordinary uniformizing chart the equations µ = rτ + s, for (r, s) ∈Q2, define
countably many analytic loci. If they covered the chart, Baire’s theorem would force one equation
on an open set and hence identically. The marked elliptic section would then be torsion, contrary
to the construction. Thus the required base point exists.
For integral vectors v = (a, b, c, d) and v′ = (A, B, C, D),
Πv = (6aµ + bτ + c, aβ + bµ + d).
The determinant det(Πv, Πv′) is affine in β, with coefficient
(Ab −aB)τ + (Ac −aC).
If the coefficient vanishes and a ̸= 0, the nonreal number τ gives B = Ab/a and C = Ac/a. The
remaining determinant is (6aµ + bτ + c)(D −Ad/a). Its first factor is nonzero by the rational
independence of 1, τ, µ, and its second factor vanishes only when v, v′ are rationally dependent.
The case a = 0, A ̸= 0 follows by exchanging the vectors. For a = A = 0, the determinant is
(bD −Bd)τ + (cB −Cb)µ + (cD −Cd).
Rational independence makes this zero only when all the two-by-two minors vanish, again giving
dependence. Therefore an independent integral pair has zero determinant for at most one value
of β. There are countably many such pairs.
