Its last three columns are real-linearly independent because T > 0. Its determinant is TD,
so at zero determinant the rank is exactly three. Substitution gives the displayed kernel,
including its signs. If the image subgroup is discrete, it is a lattice of rank at most three
in its real span. The homomorphism Z4 →ΠZ4 therefore has a nonzero kernel, forcing the
displayed kernel vector with first coordinate one to be rational. Conversely a rational line has
a primitive integral generator extendable to a basis of Z4. The remaining three basis images
are a real basis of the image hyperplane and hence generate a discrete lattice. Its quotient
leaves one unbounded real direction. Finally a non-discrete finitely generated subgroup of this
real hyperplane cannot be closed: a closed subgroup is a product of a real vector group and a
discrete group, and a nonzero real vector group is uncountable. A quotient topological group
is Hausdorff exactly when its identity subgroup is closed.
