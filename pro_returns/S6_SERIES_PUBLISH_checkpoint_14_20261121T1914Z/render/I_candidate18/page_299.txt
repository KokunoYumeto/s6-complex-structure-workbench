For the second, use the algebraic universal vector extension with analytic periods (ω, η(ω)).
On E\{O} an algebraic fibre coordinate is t = w−ζ(z): the zeta addition formula makes its group
cocycle rational in the Weierstrass coordinates. Near O, use tO = t −℘′(z)/(2℘(z)) = w + O(z3).
The overlap change is rational, so (z, w) are the tangent coordinates of an algebraic chart at
the identity. If b = ζ(u) −s2u were algebraic, the logarithm (u, ζ(u) −b) would exponentiate
to the algebraic point with fibre coordinate −b and would lie on the algebraic line w = s2z,
since proposition 26.8 gives s2 ∈Q. The analytic subgroup theorem (Huber and Wüstholz,
2022, Theorem 6.1, arXiv v8, p. 43) would then give a one-dimensional algebraic subgroup
projecting nontrivially to E. Such a subgroup is an elliptic curve mapping isogenously to E:
a one-dimensional affine algebraic group has no nonconstant group morphism to an elliptic
curve. Its inclusion would split the vector extension after that isogeny. The extension class in
H1(E, OE) is nonzero and stays nonzero under isogeny, because trace divided by the degree is
a left inverse to pullback. Equivalently, its period vectors cannot lie on one complex line, by
Legendre’s relation. This contradiction proves the second assertion.
