Proposition 22.36 (The dual complex under local insertions). [tag 030K]
The local operation 22.34 attaches a triangulated cylinder to the original twelve-cycle in the
dual complex and admits an elementary collapse back to that cycle. In a compact normal-crossings
model, performing it at a regular place consequently preserves the homotopy type of the global
dual complex.
The same conclusion holds for finite sequences of these insertions, twists by a connected holo-
morphic group action preserving the prescribed normal-crossings model, and toroidal subdivisions
of its central cone complex. In particular, starting with a model whose dual complex has a nonzero
first integral cohomology class, these operations cannot produce a semistable S6 centre. They also
cannot change the component rotations in 22.5.
Proof. 1. Write Hi, i ∈Z/12, for the original component vertices and Vi for the new vertical
ones. At a node between an even component i and its odd neighbour j, the two charts (22.5)
have triple strata
{Vi, Vj, Hj},
{Vi, Hi, Hj}.
(22.7)
The square with horizontal edges HiHj and ViVj is thus divided by the diagonal ViHj. Over all
twelve nodes, (22.7) is exactly a triangulation of C12 × [0, 1], attached along the old horizontal
cycle C12 × {0}.
2. There is a literal elementary collapse relative to the old cycle. First remove each triangle
containing a top edge ViVj, paired with that free edge. Next remove each remaining triangle,
paired with its now free diagonal ViHj. Finally remove each vertical edge HiVi, paired with
its free vertex Vi. What remains is precisely the original twelve-cycle. These are 36 elementary
collapses. Equivalently, the retraction sends Vi to Hi and is the identity on all old vertices
and edges.
3. This description is valid inside a larger dual complex K. The only new strata lie over the
base origin, so the new complex is the mapping-cylinder attachment
K ∪f:C12→K (C12 × [0, 1]).
It retracts onto K, regardless of possible global identifications among the local horizontal
branches. For self-crossings the ordered local-branch dual complex records every local branch
and its global identifications.
4. A connected group acts trivially on the finite sets of component labels and connected compo-
nents of every stratum: its permutation is locally constant and is the identity at the identity
element. Cartier twists by such an action therefore keep this same incidence complex. Toroidal
subdivisions subdivide the corresponding central polyhedral complex and hence preserve its
underlying homotopy type. These arguments apply successively, proving the assertion for
finite compositions. Around each regular insertion point the relative component monodromy
is still the identity; no peripheral rotation is added to 22.5.
