## What is to be settled

Two side lanes of the S6 workbench are arithmetic and have no mathematical map to the complex threefold X (the survey says so plainly: F31, F32). Each leaves a well-posed open problem (I, II). The third problem (III) is about X itself: it comes from the return of the run S6_02_FAMILY.

**I. The count.** The workbench counts the integer points

R(X) = #{(a, q, n) ∈ Z³ : a² − 3q² − 18n² = −2, a > 0, q ≥ 0, n ≥ 0, a + 4n > q, 4a + 17n ≤ X}

(the level −1 of the form 4(xy + xz + yz) − x² − y² − z² in a fundamental region, after an integral substitution; `workbench/koide_counting_repair.tex`, `workbench/koide_exact_parametrization.tex`). It proves R(X) = C₀X + O(X^{1−η}) for an unspecified η > 0 with C₀ = (9/(2π²))·log(2+√3)·log(3/(2√2)) = 0.0353620478…, by Benoist-Oh and ideals of Z[√3]; it computes R(10⁸) = 3,536,002 exactly (`workbench/koide_exact_census.tex`); and it records as open (`workbench/koide_counting_repair.tex` l. 1133-1153; ledger S6-OPEN-KOIDE-SQRT-DISCREPANCY) whether R(X) − C₀X = O_ε(X^{1/2+ε}). A reader of the survey recounted R(X) independently to 10⁷ and found a reformulation that the workbench missed (`notes/D2_notes.md`, D2-8, D2-9): the height is a geodesic-to-geodesic distance, and R(X) is a count of common perpendiculars with foot in a fixed arc. That reformulation is a sketch, checked by program to z = 30,000 and by no person.

**II. The lattice.** For the Niemeier lattice N with root system A₅⁴D₄ the workbench constructs three Leech lattices Λ₀, Λ₁, Λ₂, neighbours of N along the Weyl vector, cycled by an element r of order 3 of the Weyl group, with common pairwise intersection J of determinant 81 and index 9 in each, and a fourth even unimodular overlattice of J with root system A₂¹² (`workbench/es_niemeier_residue_fixed_neighbor.tex`, `esrf:thm:common-J` l. 313-346, `esrf:thm:discriminant-correspondence` l. 363-529, `esrf:thm:fourth-extension` l. 546-782; `workbench/es_niemeier_leech.tex`; `workbench/es_niemeier_shared.tex`). A reader recomputed everything exactly and added that J has exactly eight even unimodular overlattices, six Leech and two A₂¹² (`notes/D1_notes.md`, D1-1, D1-13, with scripts). Whether J and this configuration are known is not established; the workbench's cubic "value groups" depend on a chosen isometry N ⊗ R → O³ and are not invariants (D1-5).

**III. The periods at special points.** The complex structures X_c on S^6 of [AC26] and [E26] are built from the rational elliptic surface E_t: y² = x³ − 3t³(t−1)x + 2t⁴(t−1)² over the t-line (j = 1728t; fibres IV*, III, I₁ over t = 0, 1, ∞) and the generator P = (0, −√2·t²(t−1)) of its Mordell-Weil group Z. On the uniformizing half-plane of the triangle group Δ(3,4,∞), with τ the period ratio (j(τ) = 1728t), let μ be the elliptic logarithm of P: ℘_τ(μ) = 0 (μ is the zero of the Weierstrass function studied by Eichler and Zagier), on the branch with μ → 1/2 − i·log(5+2√6)/(2π) at the cusp. Put

N(v, τ) = e^{−π(Im v)²/Im τ}·|ϑ₁(v, τ)/η(τ)|,  β^part = (6i/π)·Log(ϑ₁(μ, τ)/(2√3·η(τ))),  D_part = Im β^part − 6(Im μ)²/Im τ = (6/π)·log(N(μ, τ)/(2√3)).

The threefold X_c has period matrix [[6μ, τ, 1, 0], [β^part + c, μ, 0, 1]] and exists exactly when Im c < −max D_part; its two multiple fibres (over t = 0 and t = 1, at the points z₁, z₂ with τ = ρ = e^{πi/3}, μ = (2−ρ)/3 and τ = i, μ = (1−i)/2) are multiples of bielliptic surfaces S₁, S₂ with Albanese curves C/(Z + κ₁Z) and C/(Z + (κ₂/2)Z), κ₁ = (β^part + c)(z₁) + 2μ(z₁), κ₂ = (β^part + c)(z₂) + 3μ(z₂). The run S6_02_FAMILY proved max D_part = −(3/π)·log 6, attained only at t = 1, with a computer-assisted proof (results R1-R5 below). Its review found, numerically, that the values of N at the points where P is torsion are algebraic with striking closed forms (N¹² = 64, 27, 10, 17 + 10√3, 10√3 − 17), and closed forms for the Albanese curves; none of this is proved (Leads 7-12). A further reading (13 November 2026) adds three questions about N: its value at the rational points of the base through the canonical height of P; a proof of R4 that does not use the interval certificate; and whether N is algebraic at the CM points of the base, where P is not torsion (Leads 13-16, items C.6-C.8).

This prompt asks for theorems on all three: an explicit exponent and the neighbouring results for the count, the identification of J with its symmetries, and the arithmetic of the function N on the family.
