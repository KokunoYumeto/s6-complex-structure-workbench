1. Finite transform, independently recomputed.

For (q,6)=1, expand the Salié sum and complete the k-square. The coefficient
of k^2 is bar72*bar(a), its inverse fourfold coefficient is 18a, and the
product of the two Jacobi factors is (2/q). This gives exactly
  sum_{k mod q} T(2,bar72(k^2+d);q)e(-hk/q)
    = (2/q)G_q S(2-18h^2,bar72 d;q).
The quadratic Gauss formula used is valid for every odd q, including
non-squarefree composite q, when its quadratic coefficient is a unit.

An independent recomputation from the original three-variable sum gives
  sum_{k mod q} S_q(c1,c2,k)e(-hk/q)
    = q^2 (3/q) S(2-18h^2,bar12(c2^2-3c1^2);q).
Here the k-sum sets the original third coordinate equal to h modulo q;
the two remaining Gauss factors have product q(3/q). The extra q is
the finite Fourier orthogonality factor. For c1=1,c2=0 this agrees with
the displayed Salié completion and provides an independent sign check.

2. Change of modulus and sign.

For odd q and even M, CRT gives
  S(M,-1;2q)=S(M bar(q),-bar(q);2)
             S(M bar2,-bar2;q).
The first factor is -1. Substitution x=2y in the second gives
S(M,-bar4;q). Therefore the record's negative sign is correct.
For |h|>=1, simultaneous negation of both Kloosterman parameters gives
  S(2-18h^2,-1;2q)=S(18h^2-2,1;2q).
The resulting product of parameters is positive.

The existing exact cyclotomic program has 160 completion checks and
40 modulus-doubling checks, with stdout preserved. This audit also
verified the general algebraic proof; the finite tests are not used
as its replacement.

3. Poisson and the prefactor.

Expanding a q-periodic function in its finite Fourier series, followed
by ordinary Poisson summation for the Schwartz weight, gives the record's
factor K/q and the argument K(X-h)/q with the stated Fourier sign.
The endpoint factor K^-1 cancels K. Multiplication by the original
X^-2 q^-3 delta prefactor, X^3 physical volume and three Gauss factors
then gives
  X q^-4 (6/q)(2/q)G_q^4 = X q^-2(3/q).
There is no missing factor sqrt(q), K, or X in the completed scale.

For the exact positive dyadic radial-face coefficient, the denominator
is 1/(k+u), with 4<u<17/4. It can be retained without approximation by
writing it as K^-1 psi_{K,u}(k/K), where a fixed smooth cutoff supported
in [1,2] is divided by (v+u/K). This family and all its Fourier transforms
have uniform Schwartz seminorms for K>=1. The suggestion was sent to
the count agent to avoid implying an unproved global treatment of the
O(k^-2) remainder of a leading-term expansion.

4. Exact scope of the named classical input.

For h in [X/2,3X/2] and X>=2, set m=18h^2-2, n=1 and c=2q. The retained
face phase e(-Xu/q) is exactly
  e(2 sqrt(mn) alpha/c), alpha=-Xu/sqrt(18h^2-2).
It satisfies |alpha|<=17/8, independently of q,X,K,h and u in the fixed
triangle. Thus B=3 is a valid fixed twist bound.
The factor (3/q), with (q,6)=1, is constant on four classes modulo12;
after c=2q these become fixed classes modulo24. Browning--Kumaraswamy--
Steiner Conjecture1.1, checked in its primary author v2 p2, does include
this fixed arithmetic-progression and bounded-twist setup, uniformly in
the nonzero m,n. Steiner's published Conjecture2 is the corresponding
source already audited in the manuscript.

This is a real per-h application of the named classical conjecture,
unlike substituting it directly for the original Salié sums. It is not
a claim that the conjecture supplies cancellation across h.

5. Tails and remaining exponent.

For each h in the central range, the residual q-weight X/q times the
Schwartz transform and the fixed face amplitude has bounded supremum
and variation, with Schwartz decay in K|X-h|/X. The named partial-sum
input therefore gives O_epsilon(X^epsilon) per effective h. There are
O(1+X/K) such indices, giving X^(1+epsilon)/K for K<=sqrt(X).

The far range |h-X|>=X/2 may be treated without the bounded-twist input.
Weil's bound has gcd(m,1,c)=1 and is O_epsilon(X^(1/2+epsilon)) uniformly
in h. Summing the Schwartz tail gives
  O_A(X^(3/2+epsilon) K^(-A)).
For K>=X^delta, choosing A in terms of delta makes it arbitrarily small.
The low blocks K<X^delta are covered by the pre-completion estimate
at scale X^(1/2+epsilon)K, if its separate arithmetic assumptions are
granted. Neither treatment silently uses a fixed twist bound when h
is small.

The two termwise envelopes are exactly sqrt(X)K and X/K. Their
transition K=X^(1/4) has value X^(3/4). This is a limitation of these
two estimates, not a lower bound for the genuine sum.

6. Strict main-item assessment.

The finite transform is substantive: it absorbs the growing normal
frequency into a short quadratic sequence of ordinary Kloosterman
parameters, and the residual face phase has a bounded classical twist.
It does not prove the required X^(1/2+epsilon) error. An additional
uniform estimate for the joint h,q sum is still needed, as is treatment
of the other dual families and local factors for a complete global proof.
