1. Propagation and individual degrees.

The absolute division identity has the correct exponent n^2 and factor
delta^(n^2-1). For nR=mQ with m nonzero, the fibre of [n] has exactly n^2
geometric points in characteristic zero. Over K0=Q(delta,N(Q)), a real R
therefore costs at most n^2, and the positive n^2-th root giving N(R)
costs at most n^2. This is D n^4, with no omitted y-coordinate factor:
the degree of the entire point is controlled by the finite fibre.

For complex R, both R and its complex conjugate lie over the same real
point mQ. K0 is a real subfield at the chosen embedding, so adjoining
both points costs at most n^4. The squared absolute denominator gives
a polynomial for N(R) of degree 2n^2, hence 2D n^6. No assertion is made
that the other embeddings of the positive real norm remain metric norms.

At nonzero n-torsion, psi_n has a simple zero as a function of the
uniformizing parameter. The quotient limit is
  N(R)^(n^2) = n delta^(n^2)/|D_z psi_n(R)|.
The factor n is required and present. The invariant derivation is
algebraic, so the same two field towers apply. For n=1 there is no
nonzero torsion point in the fibre, so no exceptional case is missing.

Algebraicity through the CM hull follows from C.7.6 and ordinary
division, with finite torsion translations handled by the displayed
ratio identity. It does not require a Galois-equivariance claim for N.

2. Uniform heights.

The compact inverse image of a fixed circle |x|=A, A larger than all
branch-value moduli, excludes O and the zeros of 2y+1. Thus N and
|2y+1| have positive minima there. N has a finite maximum on compact E.
The exact division identity gives a bound exp(C n^2) for |psi_n| on
that circle. Cauchy's coefficient inequality gives the same bound for
the coefficient l1 norm, including the even-n factor 2y+1. The O(n^2)
number of coefficients adds only O(log n) to its logarithm.

For the invariant derivative, write Y=2y+1. The algebraic identities
  D_z x=Y,  D_z Y=6x^2-76,  Y^2=4x^3-152x+361
show directly that differentiation adds only polynomial factors in n
and a fixed increase in polynomial degree. Therefore its logarithmic
coefficient norm is also O(n^2).

The standard duplication-map telescoping gives
  h(x(R))=2 hhat(R)+O_E(1)
uniformly over algebraic points. Since hhat(R)=(m/n)^2 hhat(Q), the
coordinate heights are bounded for |m|<=n. Polynomial evaluation then
gives h(psi_n(R))=O(n^2), also for the invariant derivative at torsion.
After division by n^2, the stated uniform norm-height bound follows.
The inequality h(|alpha|)<=h(alpha) is valid: complex conjugation
preserves absolute height and |alpha|^2=alpha*conj(alpha).

The cited polynomial-height inequality was checked visually in the
original Gramain--Mignotte--Waldschmidt paper, Lemma1.0, printed p98.

3. Real CM hull and descent of translate relations.

After clearing a CM denominator and finite torsion, a real hull point
satisfies nR=alpha Q. Complex conjugation gives
  (alpha-conj(alpha))Q=O.
A nonzero elliptic endomorphism has finite kernel and Q is nontorsion;
therefore alpha is fixed by conjugation and is an integer. The real
locus of this negative-discriminant curve is connected, so real
logarithms are precisely (m u+k omega1)/n. The manuscript now spells
out these steps; no nonreal CM coefficient remains in a real shift.

For a common denominator q and v=u/q, the multiplier of
h_a/h_v^m under q omega2 is one, while its omega1 multiplier is one.
Thus it is an elliptic function for Lambda_q=Z omega1+Z q omega2.
The finite covering C/Lambda_q -> E has an algebraic model over Qbar
and the pulled-back algebraic invariant differential is exactly dz.
Every point in the displayed divisor is algebraic, since it lies in
a finite fibre over an algebraic division point of E.

A principal divisor over Qbar on this curve has a defining rational
function over Qbar: use base change for the one-dimensional space of
sections. The analytic function differs from that rational function
by a complex constant. Evaluating at a real division-hull logarithm
away from the finitely many zeros and poles makes that constant
algebraic, because all g-values at such real logarithms are signed
algebraic norm values. This establishes the required descent to Qbar;
a relation only over C would not suffice here, and none is substituted.

4. The cap is exactly three.

The multiplier rho=exp(-2 pi i v/omega1) has modulus one and is not a
root of unity. Otherwise v/omega1 would be rational and Q torsion.
At a fixed generic z, translates by m omega2 force a polynomial
relation for h_v to vanish at infinitely many distinct rho^m h_v(z),
so h_v is transcendental over the elliptic function field.

More explicitly, the sigma automorphy and Legendre relation give
  log|g(z+m omega2)|
    = pi Im(omega2/omega1) m^2 + O_z(m),
with a strictly positive quadratic coefficient. After clearing the
finitely many rational-function denominators, the highest coefficient
of a polynomial relation in g is a nonzero polynomial in rho^m.
An irrational rotation has a subsequence on which this coefficient
is bounded away from zero. All lower coefficients remain bounded,
while the highest power of g dominates quadratically. This proves
independence of g and h_v over the elliptic function field.

The sigma addition formula has the correct delta^2 factor:
  wp(z)=wp(v)-delta^2 g(z+v)g(z-v)/(g(z)^2 g(v)^2).
Together with h_v=delta g(z+v)/(g(z)g(v)), it shows the three displayed
translates generate a field containing wp,g,h_v. The upper bound
already proved is three, so the cap is attained over Qbar as stated.

For complex shifts, the squared-norm formula follows because real
invariants imply conjugate sigma values and Re eta(a)=b Re a. Thus
the cross term in the metric is exactly b(a+conj(a))z. The sum
a+conj(a) lies in Q u+Q omega1, including the rhombic real period
relation omega2+conj(omega2)=omega1. The same finite-cover descent
works by evaluating the squared norm at real division points. The
cap is also attained among these squared functions, because adjoining
the square roots of the three real squared translates is only a
finite algebraic extension and does not change transcendence degree.

5. The n^5 common envelope.

The analytic subgroup is
  Phi(z)=exp_{G_V}(z,(zeta(v)-b v)z).
In the meromorphic algebraic section its second coordinate is h_v.
Phi(u) is algebraic under the hypothesis, and Phi(omega1) is the
identity. Hence n Phi((m u+k omega1)/n)=[m]Phi(u). The map [n] on the
semiabelian surface has degree n^3: its kernel has n^2 points from E[n]
and n points from G_m[n]. All multiples of Phi(u) are defined over one
fixed number field, independently of m. The whole point therefore
has degree O(n^3), and adjoining g(z)=+/-N(exp_E z) costs at most n^2.
The fixed isogeny and finitely many normalization constants add fixed
degree factors. This gives O(n^5) for every fixed finite translate
family, without multiplying the individual n^4 envelopes.

One wording issue was sent to the author and corrected immediately:
the whole complex analytic subgroup is not compact. Only Phi(R) is
a compact real circle with period omega1. This does not alter the
division-degree argument. The current source makes the distinction.

The patched height argument for fixed complex shifts uses their
rational expressions on the fixed isogeny, times g^2 h_v^m and fixed
algebraic constants. Height functoriality on a fixed isogeny preserves
the bounded coordinate-height condition. Exceptional removable poles
give finitely many fixed values and do not affect the uniform bound.

6. The scope of the interpolation conclusion.

Original printed pp101--102 of GMW Theorem2.1 were read visually.
The displayed decisive condition is reproduced correctly, including
the exterior d and the k factors max(d psi_j,theta_j). On fixed discs
with fixed height/growth envelopes it has exponent d^(k+1) against
mu^(k-1). The stated comparisons N^12 versus N^3 and N^20 versus N^6
are precisely what the chosen envelopes produce. In addition, the
source's hypothesis mu*phi/d -> infinity is not certified by these
same envelopes. No theorem is being applied with failed hypotheses.

The text repeatedly distinguishes this failure of supplied upper
envelopes from a lower bound on actual field degrees. That distinction
is essential and is retained. The O(N^3) grid-size bound is sufficient
for this limited negative test; no unjustified exact asymptotic for
the number of distinct fractions is needed.

Herblot Theorem6.1 was read in the primary text, Section6.1, pp31--32:
the formal germ must be a smooth arithmetic subscheme over the residue
number field. Gasbarri Theorem4.16 was read in the primary text p25:
it requires the corresponding LG-germ hypothesis in its field of
definition. The slope obstruction is valid at every real nontorsion
division point: wp' and g are nonzero algebraic, while zeta(z)-b z is
transcendental by the general period-line argument of C.7.9. A formal
germ over Qbar would have an algebraic tangent in the local x-coordinate,
contradicting the displayed dg/dx. Thus these hypotheses are genuinely
absent in the proposed application, without claiming that all possible
arithmetic interpolation reorganizations are impossible.

Final audit conclusion: the statement proves its propagation, degree,
height and function-field assertions; its limited route-test conclusion
is logically correct. It does not decide the unresolved norm value.
