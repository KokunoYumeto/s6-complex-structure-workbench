# Root mathematical review of C.7.12

Reviewed on 15 November 2026. Verdict: PASS for the stated conditional propagation, degree and height bounds, function-field assertions and limited tests of the cited criteria. This is not an algebraicity or transcendence decision for the metric norm.

The exact section reviewed after both clarifications has SHA256 `1bbf6a1452f28174b935ae57a178a5bb3d02d32c76c7d086f89dc06841e5472f`. The separate full independent audit is `B_C712_independent_audit.txt`.

The division identity has exponent n squared and discriminant factor delta to n squared minus one. At a nonzero n-torsion point the limiting identity contains the required factor n from the derivative of multiplication. For a real division point the field tower has degrees at most n squared for the elliptic point and n squared for its positive norm root. In the complex case adjoining both conjugate points and using the squared norm gives the stated 2D n to the sixth envelope. None of these steps treats a metric norm as equivariant under every embedding.

The coefficient-height argument is valid uniformly in n: choose a fixed circle in the x-plane outside the branch values, use the positive minimum of the norm on its compact inverse image and the finite maximum on E, and apply Cauchy's coefficient bound. For even n the nonvanishing factor 2y+1 can be divided out on that circle. The fixed invariant derivation changes logarithmic coefficient bounds by O(n squared). Canonical-height telescoping bounds the coordinates of nR=mQ for bounded m/n. Dividing the height identity by n squared then gives a constant bound for the hypothetical algebraic norm heights.

Two clarifications were requested and are present. First, a real point in the CM division hull is in the ordinary division hull: conjugation gives (alpha minus conjugate alpha)Q=O, so nontorsion of Q forces alpha to be a rational integer after clearing the fixed denominator and torsion. Second, the entire complex analytic subgroup used for the n to the fifth common field envelope is not compact. Its real restriction is a circle, and the argument uses its real period and the algebraic degree n cubed of the semiabelian division map.

The descent of each translate relation is over the algebraic numbers, not merely over the complex numbers. Sigma automorphy makes the ratio elliptic on a fixed isogeny. Its algebraic divisor gives a rational function over the algebraic numbers up to a scalar; evaluation at a real division point away from its divisor makes that scalar algebraic under the norm hypothesis. The irrational unit multiplier proves transcendence of the third-kind coordinate over the elliptic field. Quadratic growth of the normalized sigma function along the second period, together with a subsequence on which the leading coefficient stays nonzero, proves the second required transcendence. The addition identity recovers the elliptic coordinate from three translates. Hence the cap is exactly three.

For complex shifts the holomorphic extensions of the squared norms have a multiplier determined by the real sum of the shift and its conjugate. The same descent applies. The height proof for these shifts uses the resulting fixed rational expression in elliptic coordinates, the normalized sigma function and the third-kind coordinate. It does not incorrectly identify a nonreal CM multiple of Q with an integer multiple. Removable exceptions form a fixed finite set. Adjoining finitely many square roots changes the degree envelope only by a constant.

The original Gramain–Mignotte–Waldschmidt theorem was checked in the scanned primary source on printed pages 101–102. Its exterior degree factor and the degree factor in each maximum are retained. On fixed discs, the proved envelopes lead to the comparisons recorded in the manuscript. Failure of these upper envelopes to certify a sufficient inequality is not a lower bound on the actual degrees and is not a logical impossibility theorem for the method. The extra monotonicity and limit hypotheses have not been silently discarded.

Herblot Theorem 6.1 and Gasbarri Theorem 4.16 require the arithmetic field of definition of the formal germ. At a real nontorsion division point, the theta derivative in the algebraic x-coordinate has the transcendental compact slope already proved in C.7.9, multiplied by nonzero algebraic factors under the hypothesis. This specifically prevents the proposed germ from satisfying that hypothesis.

Its holomorphic CM sigma assertion, the Reyssat three-number statement and the algebraic-nome hypothesis in the functional-equation theorem are distinct. None states transcendence of the metric norm.
