# Further routes checked for the unresolved metric norm

Date: 15 November 2026. These are limited source comparisons, not impossibility theorems for all reorganizations of the methods.

## Mahler functional equations

Adamczewski, B., & Faverjon, C. (2017). Méthode de Mahler: Relations linéaires, transcendance et applications aux nombres automatiques. *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society, 115*, 55–90. https://doi.org/10.1112/plms.12038 . Author-public primary text opened at https://faverjon.perso.math.cnrs.fr/AdamczewskiFaverjon_mahler1.pdf .

Theorem 1.1, attributed there to Nishioka, concerns a linear q-Mahler system with algebraic rational-function coefficients and functions with algebraic Taylor coefficients, evaluated at an algebraic regular argument. The current compact theta normalization involves the transcendental coefficient eta1/omega1, and its algebraic elliptic point is represented by a transcendental elliptic logarithm. The duplication map in the algebraic x-coordinate is a Lattès map, not the power map in that theorem. No representation meeting the stated hypotheses has been obtained. The theorem therefore has not been applied to the unresolved norm. This does not assert that no alternative Mahler-type representation exists.

## Entropy interpretations of canonical height

Einsiedler, M., Everest, G., & Ward, T. (2001). Entropy and the canonical height. *Journal of Number Theory, 91*(2), 256–273. https://doi.org/10.1006/jnth.2001.2682 . Author-public primary text opened at https://people.math.ethz.ch/~einsiedl/height.pdf . Journal metadata independently verified on the publisher's article page.

Theorem 5.1 represents the canonical height by a sequence entropy built from denominators of x(2^n Q), with the scale 4^n. Theorems 5.2, 6.1 and 6.3 provide the adelic/local and elliptic Jensen interpretations. This sequence construction does not identify the height with the entropy of a single finite-dimensional algebraic automorphism, so it does not supply an algebraic Mahler measure whose logarithm is the required height. The interpretation does not decide the arithmetic nature of its exponential. No stronger negative statement is inferred.
