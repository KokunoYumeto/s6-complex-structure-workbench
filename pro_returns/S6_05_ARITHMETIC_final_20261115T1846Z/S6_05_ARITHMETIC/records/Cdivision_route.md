# Division-hull route: exact scope and source audit

The root requested a genuinely new test of the hypothesis that the distinguished E361 theta norm is algebraic. The completed draft is `sections/C_division_values.tex`, Statement C.7.12. Its conclusion is a propagation theorem and a quantitative criterion test, not a decision of the original norm question.

## What was established

1. Multiplication alone proves torsion-translation ratios and division-hull propagation. No additional harmonic normalization is required.
2. For `nR=mQ`, `|m|≤n`, under norm algebraicity the absolute logarithmic heights of `N(R)` are uniformly bounded. The joint field degree is at most `D n^4` for real points and `2D n^6` for complex points, with `D=[Q(delta,N(Q)):Q]`. The torsion case is handled by the invariant derivative of the division polynomial.
3. The needed division-polynomial coefficient height bound `O(n²)` was proved from the exact norm identity on a fixed x-circle and Cauchy's estimate. This avoids importing an unspecified coefficient estimate. The canonical-height comparison follows by telescoping the elementary fixed duplication-map height estimate.
4. The potential escape using many translates was checked explicitly. Sigma quotient automorphy makes every finite real translate family algebraic over `Qbar(E)(g,h_(u/q))`. The quotient is first seen to be elliptic on a fixed isogenous curve; evaluation at an algebraic real division value proves its scalar is algebraic. Hence the cap is over Qbar, not merely over C. Three translates attain the cap, by sigma addition and quasiperiodic independence.
5. Complex norm translates restrict to `N(a)^2/delta² · g(z)^2 h_a(z)h_bar(a)(z)` on the real axis; the character depends only on `a+bar(a)`, so they have the same cap. A fixed finite translate family has joint degree `O(n^5)` using n-division in the fixed semiabelian extension and one further n²-th root. Heights remain bounded.
6. GMW's actual sufficient inequality was tested with these envelopes. At fixed analytic radius, it reduces to `d^(k+1) << mu^(k−1)`. The available real grids have at most `O(N³)` points through denominator N. The two-function certificate uses `d=O(N⁴)` and would compare `N¹²` with `N³`; three independent translates use `d=O(N⁵)` and would compare `N²⁰` with `N⁶`. These upper-bound envelopes therefore do not verify the theorem. This does **not** prove the actual degrees violate the criterion, and no unproved Kummer-theoretic lower bound is being used.
7. The formal-germ criteria cannot be applied to the real nontorsion norm graph, whose tangent is proved transcendental by C.7.9. This is a checked missing hypothesis, distinct from the quantitative GMW test.

## Primary sources actually checked

- Gramain, Mignotte, and Waldschmidt (1986), *Acta Arithmetica* **47**(2), 97–121, DOI `10.4064/aa-47-2-97-121`. Public original scan: `https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa47/aa4721.pdf`. Printed pages 98–103 were rendered and visually read. Lemma 1.0 gives the polynomial height estimate. Theorem 2.1 and all its hypotheses are on pp.101–102; the decisive inequality is condition (5). Its conclusion is algebraic dependence over Q, and it does not require differential equations. The volume 67 in Murty's later bibliography is a typo; the original title page/publisher metadata give volume47.
- Herblot (2012), *Algebraic points on meromorphic curves*, author preprint dated18April2012, arXiv1204.6336. Full author PDF: `https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~mathilde.herblot/publi/SL.pdf`. Definition2.5 and Theorem6.1, printed pp.31–32, were read. Its reciprocal-degree inequality requires formal subschemes over their residue number fields and arithmetic jet estimates; algebraic point values alone do not supply this.
- Gasbarri (2010), *Analytic subvarieties with many rational points*, *Mathematische Annalen* **346**(1),199–243, DOI `10.1007/s00208-009-0394-9`. Full primary preprint: `https://preprints.ihes.fr/storage/newscheiderlang.pdf`. Definition3.7 and Theorem4.16 were read, including the LG-germ over its field of definition.

## Remaining concrete modifications

The current comparison leaves open sharper actual joint-degree bounds, a different sampling regime, or a criterion using additional arithmetic structure. Merely adding translates cannot give arbitrarily large k: beyond three the algebraic dependence conclusion is already known. Replacing an integer real division by a nonreal CM endomorphism cannot preserve a real nontorsion point, since conjugation would give `(alpha−bar(alpha))R=0`. Thus that apparent degree-saving modification does not apply to the one-variable real restriction used here. A genuinely several-variable criterion with stronger arithmetic control would be a separate route, not a consequence of the envelopes proved in C.7.12.
