# P3F.29. All logarithmic orders in the coprime OS49 sector

**Proved.** Keep the actual component-supported construction of
P3F.26. Let \(m=3k\), with \(k>0\), and choose the logarithmic
vector
\[
 \ell=n(1,-1,0,0)^t+d(0,0,0,1)^t,
 \qquad \gcd(n,m)=1.                                  \tag{P3F.29.1}
\]
The root-chart action of order \(m\), with the same order-three
linear part, is free. The resulting smooth compact complex
threefold has
\[
 \pi_1(X)=\mathbb Z/|n|,
 \qquad H_i(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/|n|
       \quad(1\leq i\leq4),
 \qquad H_5(X;\mathbb Z)=0,
 \qquad H_0=H_6=\mathbb Z.                            \tag{P3F.29.2}
\]
Consequently every \(n=1\) or \(n=-1\) member is the standard
smooth six-sphere, for every \(k\) and every delta twist \(d\).
This statement determines topology in its coprime sector.
P3F.31--32 construct and compute the actual positive-fan page and
return; P3K.17 proves equality with the negative-fan return, so
both sphere sectors have index zero. P3F.34 supplies the additional
torsion factor in the more general noncoprime free-cap sector.

**Proof: the actual finite cap.** Write \(A=\widehat T_A\) for
the homology matrix in P3F.26. It fixes \(\ell\), and \(A^3=I\).
On the \(m\)-th-root chart use the affine action
\[
 \alpha(z)=Az+\ell/m.
\]
Its \(m\)-th power is translation by the integral vector \(\ell\).
The invariant integral covector \(\psi\) evaluates to \(n\) on
\(\ell\). Thus the nonidentity powers have nonintegral gamma
displacements \(jn/m\), for \(0<j<m\). They have no fixed
points. The punctured gluing is the logarithmic translation already
displayed in P3F.26, now with \(t=s^m\). Its inverse and branch
independence are the same explicit formulas. This constructs the
specified smooth cap and its attachment to the unchanged ordinary,
type \(II\), and component cusp pieces.

The invariant first-cohomology lattice upstairs has basis
\[
 \psi,\qquad h=-w+3\delta.
\]
The integer map \(\Lambda\to\mathbb Z^2\) given by these two
covectors is onto and has kernel \((A-I)\Lambda\): the matrix
\(A-I\) has primitive image of rank two. After adjoining the
affine generator, the abelianized cap group therefore has the
presentation
\[
 \mathbb Z^3/\langle(n,3d,-m)\rangle.
\]
It is free of rank two because \(\gcd(n,m)=1\). Poincare duality
and the universal coefficient theorem show that the cap surface's
integral cohomology is torsion-free, with unimodular degree-two
intersection form.

Its integral first-cohomology pullback is exactly
\[
 L_1=\{a\psi+b h:na+3db\equiv0\pmod m\}.
\]
If \(\bar n n\equiv1\pmod m\), a basis is
\(m\psi,\ h-3d\bar n\psi\). In particular the global
invariant \(\psi\) has specialization index \(m\).

**Proof: all specialization lattices.** Keep the degree-two
invariants of P3F.26,
\[
 u_2=\psi h=-\psi w+3\psi\delta,
 \qquad v_2=(\psi+u)w=\psi w+uw,
 \qquad\omega=u_2+v_2.
\]
The subgroup generated by \(\alpha^3\) is the pure translation
subgroup of order \(k\); it enlarges the original torus lattice
by \(\ell/k\). An integral alternating form descends to that
torus precisely when contraction by \(\ell\) is divisible by
\(k\). Here the contractions are exactly
\[
 \iota_\ell u_2=nh-3d\psi,
 \qquad \iota_\ell v_2=0.
\]
Since \(n\) is invertible modulo \(k\), the invariant
degree-two forms on the intermediate torus have basis
\(ku_2,v_2\). Their intersection matrix, computed using that
torus's fundamental class, is \(3U\): the original pairing
\(u_2v_2=3\) is multiplied by \(k\) in the form and divided
by \(k\) by the covering degree.

The remaining covering has degree three. Its downstairs
intersection lattice is unimodular, so its degree-two pullback
has determinant nine. The full invariant lattice \(3U\)
already has determinant of absolute value nine. The index formula
for a sublattice therefore forces this pullback to be the whole
invariant lattice. In the original torus the precise degree-two
specialization lattice is consequently
\[
 L_2=\mathbb Z(ku_2)\oplus\mathbb Z v_2.
\]
Thus \(\omega\) has index \(k\), and its image generates
the local specialization cokernel \(\mathbb Z/k\).

For degree three the full invariant lattice has basis
\[
 \phi=\psi uw,
 \chi=(\psi+u)w\delta.
\]
The nonzero pairings with \(\psi,h\), in the original positive
volume \(\nu=\psi uw\delta\), are
\(\psi\chi=\nu\) and \(h\phi=-3\nu\).
Perfect pairing downstairs and the covering degree \(m\)
therefore identify its pullback lattice exactly as
\[
 L_3=\{a\phi+b\chi:a+d\bar n b\equiv0\pmod k\}.
\]
Indeed pairing with \(m\psi\) imposes no condition, while
pairing with \(h-3d\bar n\psi\) requires
\(m\mid3(a+d\bar n b)\). The class \(\phi\) has index
\(k\) and generates the cokernel. In degree four the covering
degree gives index \(m\). We have proved the four global
specialization indices
\[
                        (m,k,k,m).                  \tag{P3F.29.3}
\]
The global invariant reaches each full local cokernel. The
skyscraper exact sequence therefore creates no extra first
cohomology of the direct-image sheaves. Their parabolic complexes
and their free coinvariant groups are exactly the executed ones
in P3F.26; the local system and the nonfinite fillings have not
changed.

**Proof: fundamental group and every differential.** The two
nonfinite fillings give the same primitive relation lattice as
P3F.26, with quotient generated by gamma. The remaining base
meridian dies by the base relation. Its \(m\)-th power supplies
the relation \(\ell=0\), which becomes \(n\gamma=0\), since
the delta generator already dies in the nonfinite quotient. These
are all the boundary generators, so van Kampen gives the asserted
fundamental group.

The inversion and coarse linear-cap comparison of P3F.26 applies
without change. The actual translation prism now gives
\[
 d_2(a)=\frac{H_B}{m}[\iota_\ell a].
\]
In the four coinvariant targets the contractions of
\(\psi,\omega,\phi,\nu\) are respectively
\[
 n,\quad3n[\delta],\quad3n\eta,\quad n[uw\delta].
\]
Every term involving \(d\) maps to zero. Multiplication by the
four source indices \((m,k,k,m)\), followed by division by
\(m=3k\), gives four differentials equal to multiplication
by \(n\). The integral targets and sources are free, so the
rational prism calculation determines these integral maps
uniquely. There are no other differentials. It follows that
\(H^2,H^3,H^4,H^5\) are \(\mathbb Z/|n|\), while
\(H^1=0\) and \(H^0=H^6=\mathbb Z\). The universal
coefficient theorem and Poincare duality give (P3F.29.2), without
extension ambiguity. For \(|n|=1\), simple connectivity and
these integral groups imply a homotopy sphere, and
\(\Theta_6=0\) gives the standard smooth sphere.

The symbolic contractions, pairings, invariant bases and

`code/part3/os49_all_orders.py`; its finite sample checks are
additional checks, not a replacement for the arbitrary-integer
lattice argument above. **End of proof.**
