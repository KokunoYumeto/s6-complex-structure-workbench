# P3F.21. A weighted OS47 sphere family and its nonzero page expression

**Proved.** There is a proper holomorphic family of complex structures
on the standard six-sphere over a punctured parameter disc, obtained
from the rational elliptic configuration \(I_7+III+I_1+I_1\) by taking
\[
 M=\mathcal O_S(7G-O),\qquad Q=2G,
\]
where \(G\) generates its Mordell--Weil group, choosing the
linearization zero at one of the nodal fibres, and making the specified
order-four logarithmic transformation at \(III\). An explicit parameter
is \(r\), with scalar linearization parameter \(\lambda=r^4\).

The actual regular-binding page of its circle quotient has four
\(I_7\) fibres and eight \(I_1\) fibres. Its elliptic completion has
\[
 \chi(\mathcal O)=3,\quad \sigma=-24,\quad
 h(7G)=14,\quad (7G)\cdot O=4.
\]
The circle-line square on the page is \(-14\), and its relative
Pontryagin pairing is \(-72\). Thus the **page expression** is
\[
 \frac{p_{\rm rel}-12s_\delta}{24}
   =\frac{-72+168}{24}=4.
\]
The actual signed comparison is proved in P3F.25 by the real trace
P3K.6--7: the return invariant is the negative of this displayed
page expression, so \(A=-4\), with oriented class \(24\) in
\(\Theta_7\). The original construction and page calculation
are retained below as the inputs to that completed comparison. Its line section is \(7G\); replacing it by the primitive
section \(G\) changes the line and the calculation.

## 1. An explicit rational elliptic surface and primitive section

Put
\[
 f=1+8t+40t^2+48t^3,\qquad
 g=1+12t+84t^2+280t^3+504t^4,
\]
and take the minimal regular model of
\[
 y^2=x^3-3f(t)x+2g(t).
\]
The exact identity
\[
 g^2-f^3=-1728t^7(64t^2+13t+2)
\]
gives \(I_7\) at zero, simple nodal fibres at
\(t_\pm=(-13\pm7i\sqrt7)/128\), and \(III\) at infinity.
At infinity the minimal Weierstrass vanishing orders of
\((a_4,a_6,\Delta)\) are \((1,2,3)\). At zero \(a_4,a_6\) are units
and the discriminant has order seven. The other two discriminant roots
are distinct and have unit \(a_4\). These checks give exactly the stated
Kodaira types and no additional singular fibres.

The point
\[
 G=(1+4t,12\sqrt3\,t^2)
\]
satisfies the equation. Its polynomial coordinates show that it is
disjoint from the zero section. At zero, write
\(\sqrt f=1+4t+12t^2+O(t^3)\). In the two local branches of the node,
the point has orders two and five: one of
\(y\pm\sqrt3(x-\sqrt f)\) has leading term
\(24\sqrt3t^2\), and the product of suitable analytic branch
coordinates has order seven. Therefore \(G\) meets component two,
up to reversing the cyclic numbering, of \(I_7\). At infinity its
transformed coordinates tend to the singular point, so it meets the
nonidentity component of \(III\).

Shioda's intersection formula now gives
\[
 h(G)=2-\frac{2(7-2)}7-\frac12=\frac1{14}.
\]
The root rank is \(6+1=7\), so Shioda--Tate gives Mordell--Weil rank
one. There is no torsion: the possible local contributions, multiplied
by fourteen, are \(0,12,20,24\) from \(I_7\) and \(0,7\) from
\(III\). Their sums never equal twenty-eight, and their maximum
thirty-one is below fifty-six. Thus none can equal
\(28+28R\cdot O\), as a nonzero torsion section would require.
The unimodularity of
the rational elliptic surface's Néron--Severi lattice then gives
primitive height \(1/14\). Thus \(G\) is a generator.

For later use, exact addition gives
\[
 x(7G)=-\frac{140t^3-609t^2-420t+92}{(t+10)^2}.
\]
Its numerator is nonzero at \(t=-10\). The height formula gives
\((7G)\cdot O=1\): its \(I_7\) contribution is zero and its \(III\)
contribution is \(1/2\). The displayed pole therefore locates the whole
intersection at the ordinary fibre \(t=-10\), with intersection
multiplicity one. It is not at a reducible fibre.

## 2. The linearization and every local filling

Write \(R=7G\) and \(Q=2G\). Translation acts trivially on the Picard
group of degree zero of a smooth elliptic fibre, so
\[
 L=\operatorname{Hom}(t_Q^*M,M)
\]
is vertical. At \(I_7\), \(R\) is in the identity component, hence
\(M\) has degree zero on every component; translating by \(Q\)
preserves this multidegree. At \(III\), \(Q\) is in the identity
component and preserves every component. Thus \(L\) has zero degree
on every fibre component. The kernel of the component intersection
matrix is generated by the full fibre, so
\(L=\pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(d)\).

Intersecting its divisor
\(R-O-(R-Q)+(-Q)\) with \(O\) gives
\[
 d=1+1-1+0=1.
\]
Here \((5G)\cdot O=1\) and \((2G)\cdot O=0\), obtained directly from
the same height formula and the component classes \(2n\pmod7\),
\(n\pmod2\). Consequently \(L=\mathcal O(F)\).

Choose a section of \(L\) whose sole zero is the full fibre at
\(t_c=t_-\). It gives a lift of translation by \(-Q\) on \(M^\times\)
away from that fibre. Multiply the lift by \(\lambda\), with
\(0<|\lambda|\) uniformly small. This defines the smooth two-torus
family on the regular base. The contraction/proper-discontinuity
argument uses an invariant Hermitian norm: over a compact base set the
lift is bounded, and its only degeneration is a zero, so scaling
makes the norm strictly decrease. A compact annulus in the norm
direction is a fundamental domain.

At the zero fibre \(I_1\), the two torus periods have valuation vectors
\((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\). The unit-square triangulation along a diagonal
has unimodular cones. The affine toric charts are
\(q=z_0z_1z_2\), and the two periods identify the opposite sides of
one hexagonal component. This gives a smooth total space and a proper
one-component rank-two Mumford filling. Its reduced fibre normalizes
to \(dP_6\), has three double curves and two triple points, and has
Euler number two.

At the other \(I_1\), the exponent vectors are \((1,0)\) and \((0,0)\),
with the zero-exponent period contracting by a scalar multiple of
\(\lambda\). Its quotient first makes the second factor an elliptic
curve. The rays \((j,1)\), \(j\in\mathbb Z\), give the standard smooth
rank-one filling with equation \(xy=q\); one period shifts \(j\) by
one. It has one component, an elliptic ruled normalization, and Euler
number zero.

The \(I_7\) filling must retain both periods. The section \(R\) is in
the identity component, whereas \(Q\) meets component four. In a Tate
coordinate and a frame of \(M\) which has degree zero on all components,
the two periods have the form
\[
 h:(z,v)\longmapsto(q^7u_1z,u_2v),\qquad
 g:(z,v)\longmapsto(q^{-4}u_3z,\lambda u_4v),
\]
with holomorphic nonzero units \(u_i\). The integral change of period
generators
\[
 H=h^{-1}g^{-2},\qquad K=h^4g^7
\]
has determinant one. Their exponent vectors are \((1,0)\) and
\((0,0)\), respectively. The second multiplier of \(K\) is
\(\lambda^7\) times a unit, so it is uniformly contracting. The same
ray fan therefore gives a **one-component primitive rank-one**
filling. This explicit change of generators is essential; replacing
the source \(I_7\) by seven independent components would give the
wrong local attachment. Its charts, properness, Hausdorff property and
Euler number are exactly those just described for the primitive
rank-one filling. There is also a direct identification retaining the
source elliptic map: at this fibre the chosen linearization is
invertible on the whole original elliptic neighbourhood, so the
filling is the quotient of its actual smooth total line complement
\(M^\times\) by the contracting lift of \(-Q\). Locally at a
source node its charts are \(xy=q\) times \(\mathbb C^*\), and
translation permutes the seven components and their nodes cyclically.
The displayed unimodular change of period generators identifies this
quotient with the primitive toric model. Thus no elliptic component
map has been discarded.

At \(III\), take the fourth-root base change. The relatively minimal
elliptic surface has good reduction; the section \(R\) specializes to
the nonzero two-torsion point and \(Q\) to zero. The pulled-back
degree-zero line and its nonvanishing linearization therefore give a
smooth family of two-tori across the root origin. The order-four
linear deck action is the homology matrix specified below. Its affine
translation is \(\ell/4\), where
\[
 \ell=(1,-1,4,0)^t.
\]
The invariant covector \(\psi\) evaluates to one on \(\ell\). Hence
the \(j\)-th power of the action changes the invariant real circle
coordinate by \(j/4\); it has no fixed point for \(1\leq j\leq3\).
The quotient is smooth and has a reduced bielliptic central surface
of multiplicity four.

Its punctured identification is the explicit logarithmic translation
\[
 (x,s)\longmapsto
 \left(x+\frac{\log s}{2\pi i}\ell(s),s\right),\qquad q=s^4.
\]
Changing \(\log s\) by \(2\pi i\) adds the integral period \(\ell\).
Under the root deck action it changes by \(\ell/4\), proving descent
and giving the inverse by the negative translation. These are exactly
the charts used in the global gluing, with no extra affine clutch.

Use nested discs and annular overlaps to glue these four local models
to the regular torus family. All changes of charts are the displayed
holomorphic translations or monomial toric identifications. A finite
cover by a compact regular-base set and four smaller closed discs
proves compactness. The compact fundamental domains in the toric
models prove Hausdorffness. The unimodular charts and the free finite
action prove smoothness.

## 3. Actual integral monodromy, not an abstract substitute tuple

The elliptic monodromy can be marked as
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}-5&26\\-1&5\end{pmatrix},\quad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}1&7\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 C=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 D=\begin{pmatrix}-2&9\\-1&4\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad ABCD=I.
\]
Here \(A^2=-I\); \(B\) is the seventh power of the positive
transvection along \((1,0)\); \(C,D\) are positive transvections along
\((0,1),(3,1)\). The positive base meridians are chosen so that this is
the ordered cohomological product. Equivalently, the corresponding
based path product is read in the reverse order. The choice is used
consistently in every complex below.

We verify that this is a marking of the elliptic surface, rather than
assuming it from the fibre labels. In
\(PSL_2(\mathbb Z)=\langle S,R\mid S^2=R^3=1\rangle\), put \(T=SR\).
The subgroup generated by
\[
 T^5ST^{-5},\quad T^7,\quad STS^{-1},\quad T^3STS^{-1}T^{-3}
\]
has the following exact coset permutations:
\[
 S=(12)(34)(57)(6)(89),\qquad
 R=(123)(456)(789).
\]
The product corresponding to \(T\) has cycles
\((1,3,5,8,7,6,4),(2),(9)\). The complete nine-row coset table and
its exact verification are in `os47_topology.json`. It gives a
degree-nine genus-zero modular cover, with ramification
\(3^3\) over zero, \(2^4 1\) over one, and cusp widths \(7,1,1\).
Miranda's Proposition 3.4 constructs its rational elliptic surface
with fibres \(III,I_7,I_1,I_1\); all its numerical conditions hold,
and the displayed permutations supply its required map. The local
positive lifts give the matrices above. Fixing these four local fibre
types removes quadratic-twist ambiguity: an odd valuation of a twist
changes the local type, while a rational function on \(\mathbb P^1\)
with every valuation even is a square over \(\mathbb C\).

For completeness this surface is the explicit Weierstrass model of
Section 1. Place \(I_7\) at zero and \(III\) at infinity. After scaling,
write \(f=1+at+bt^2+ct^3\); the degree-four polynomial \(g\) is the
Taylor polynomial of \(f^{3/2}\). The coefficients of degrees five
and six must vanish. Solving these exact two equations gives either
\(b=a^2/4,c=0\), for which the discriminant vanishes identically, or
\(b=5a^2/8,c=3a^3/32\). The nondegenerate case has \(a\ne0\), and
rescaling \(t\) makes \(a=8\), giving precisely Section 1. Thus the
modular construction and the displayed algebraic source are related
by an actual Weierstrass change of coordinates.

We next determine the section extension in this marking. Let \(k_i\)
be the integral Abel--Jacobi cocycle of \(G\). Its class at \(I_7\)
is two modulo seven; reverse \(G\) if necessary. A change of its
integral Abel--Jacobi lift first sets \(k_B=(2,0)^t\), then sets
\(k_D=0\). Indeed the first changes by \((7x_2,0)^t\), while the
coefficient of \(k_D\) along \((3,1)^t\) changes by
\(-x_1+3x_2\). These two choices can therefore be made successively
with integral \(x_1,x_2\). Write \(k_C=(0,j)^t\). The sphere relation
then forces
\[
 k_A=-A(k_B+Bk_C)=(9j+10,2j+2)^t.
\]

In the cohomology basis \((\psi,u,w,\delta)\), a section cocycle
\(k=(k_1,k_2)^t\), elliptic matrix
\(E=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
line weight seven and translation weight two give the integral lift
\[
 T(E,k,z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&2(ck_1-ak_2)&2(dk_1-bk_2)&z\\
 0&a&b&7k_1\\0&c&d&7k_2\\0&0&0&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
This formula follows by changing the Abel--Jacobi representative in
the two period columns. It also preserves
\(\omega=7u\wedge w+2\psi\wedge\delta\), as direct multiplication
checks. At \(B\) the rank-one condition gives \(z_B=8\); at \(C\) it
gives \(z_C=14j^2\). At \(A\), the fourth-power identity gives
\(z_A=7(5j^2+8j+4)\). At the scalar-zero \(I_1\), \(k_D=0\) and its
period multiplier \(\lambda q\) gives \(z_D=-1\). These signs use
the positive circle in the line as the dual homology class
\(\widehat\delta\).

The only possibly nonzero entry of the product minus the identity is
now the upper-right entry, equal to
\[
 7(j+1)(7j+5).
\]
Its only integral zero is \(j=-1\). This determines the actual
geometric lift uniquely in the normalized marking, without selecting
it on the basis of a desired homology answer. The four matrices are
\[
 T_A=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2&10&7\\0&-5&26&7\\0&-1&5&0\\0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 T_B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&4&8\\0&1&7&14\\0&0&1&0\\0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
 T_C=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&0&14\\0&1&0&0\\0&-1&1&-7\\0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 T_D=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&-1\\0&-2&9&0\\0&-1&4&0\\0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their homology transports are \(\widehat T_i=(T_i^{-1})^t\).
The vector \(\ell=(1,-1,4,0)^t\) is fixed by \(\widehat T_A\).

## 4. Fundamental group and every integral cohomology differential

The regular family has a section. One can choose it from a meromorphic
frame of \(M|_O\simeq\mathcal O(2)\) whose divisor is twice the selected
scalar-zero fibre. At the two rank-one models its valuation is zero.
At the rank-two model its valuation is an integral period vector and
is moved to zero by a deck period, so it extends. The good-reduction
comparison at \(III\) is chosen with this section as punctured origin
before the displayed logarithmic translation. This is the allowed
translation identification in the finite-cap construction.

The three Mumford fillings kill their section meridians and their
primitive vanishing lattices. These lattices are exactly the images
of \(\widehat T_i-I\), \(i=B,C,D\): the two rank-one matrices have
primitive rank-one image and the rank-two matrix has Smith factors
\((1,1)\). The primitive fans in Section 2 give the corresponding
collapsing circles, with no index omitted. The sum of these three
images has Smith factors \((1,1,1)\) and quotient generated by
\(\widehat\psi\). All boundary maps are onto their filled fundamental
groups: in a primitive rank-one chart the pinched circle is the only
new kernel; in the unimodular rank-two chart the two angular circles
are the only new kernels. The finite quotient has generators from the
torus and its deck meridian.

The base sphere relation kills the remaining finite meridian. Its
fourth-power relation then kills \(\ell\), whose image in the
remaining fibre group is \(\widehat\psi\). Hence \(\pi_1(X)=1\).
The exact integer matrices consisting of these twelve vanishing-image
columns and then the additional column \(\ell\) are saved in the
program output. Their Smith forms are respectively \((1,1,1)\) and
\((1,1,1,1)\); this also checks the integral abelian calculation.

We give the full cohomology calculation. In degree \(q\), put
\(L_q=\bigwedge^q\mathbb Z^4\), \(E_i=\bigwedge^qT_i\), using the
lexicographic exterior basis. The parabolic cellular complex is
\[
 L_q\xrightarrow{d^0}\bigoplus_i(E_i-I)L_q
       \xrightarrow{d^1}L_q,
\]
\[
 d^0x=((E_i-I)x)_i,\qquad
 d^1(y_A,y_B,y_C,y_D)=y_A+E_Ay_B+E_AE_By_C+E_AE_BE_Cy_D.
\]
Cutting the four-punctured sphere along its marked meridians and
attaching each disc with its invariant stalk gives this complex
integrally. Local image bases are computed by integer Hermite normal
form. The program saves every entry of \(d^0,d^1\), checks their
product is zero, and computes their Smith forms.

The ranks of the four local images in degrees one, two and three are
\((2,1,1,2)\), \((4,2,2,2)\), and \((2,1,1,2)\). In those degrees
both differentials have ranks three, five and three, respectively.
The stacked ambient matrix \(((E_i-I)^t)_i^t\) has only unit nonzero
Smith factors. Therefore the middle cohomology is both torsion-free
and rationally zero, hence zero. The last cokernels are free of rank
one. The invariant generators in degrees zero through four are
\[
 1,\quad\psi,\quad\omega=7uw+2\psi\delta,\quad
 \phi=\psi uw,\quad\nu=\psi uw\delta.
\]
The coinvariant generators are
\[
 1,\quad[\delta],\quad\eta,\quad[uw\delta],\quad[\nu],
 \qquad[\psi\delta]=7\eta,\quad[uw]=2\eta.
\]
All omitted exterior basis classes map to zero in these quotients.

The primitive rank-one collapse is the cellular map
\(E\times T^2\to E\times(S^2\vee S^1)\), killing one primitive
one-cell, carrying the other one-cell with degree one and carrying
the two-cell with degree one. It therefore identifies integral
specialization with the full invariant lattice in every degree.
The rank-two local fan is the unimodular one in E26, Lemma 7.4; its
normalization-and-double-curve cellular calculation applies verbatim
because both period vectors form an integral basis. Its hypotheses
are exactly the checked valuation vectors \((1,0),(0,1)\), the same
triangulation and unit changes of coordinates. Its top specialization
has degree one off the double curves. Thus all three Mumford
specializations are integral isomorphisms onto the invariant lattice.

Here is a separate integral calculation at the finite cap. Let
\(B_0\) denote its reduced bielliptic surface and \(p:T^4\to B_0\)
the degree-four covering. Abelianizing its deck presentation gives
the five-generator relation matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\widehat T_A-I&-\ell\\0&4\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose Smith factors are \((1,1,1)\). Thus \(H_1(B_0)=\mathbb Z^2\).
Poincaré duality and the universal coefficient theorem imply that its
cohomology is torsion-free. Its Euler number is zero, giving
\(b_2(B_0)=2\). Its canonical bundle is torsion (the fourth power of
the fibre volume form descends); since \(H^2(B_0)\) is torsion-free,
\(c_1(K_{B_0})=0\). Its unimodular intersection lattice is therefore
even.

The invariant first-cohomology basis is
\[
 \psi,\qquad h=-28u-7w+2\delta.
\]
Because \(\psi(\ell)=1\), \(h(\ell)=0\), its pullback image is
\(\mathbb Z(4\psi)\oplus\mathbb Zh\). In degree two, the full
invariant lattice has basis
\[
 h_2=\psi h,\qquad v_2=uw+4\psi u+\psi w,
\]
intersection matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&2\\2&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
and \(\omega=h_2+7v_2\). Since the covering has degree four, the
pullback of the downstairs unimodular lattice has determinant
\(4^2=16\), so its index in this invariant lattice is two. An
index-two sublattice whose divided-by-four intersection is even
cannot contain a vector with both coordinates odd: that vector has
square \(4ab\), with \(a,b\) odd, and would have odd square
downstairs. Hence \(\omega\) does not descend and \(2\omega\) does.

An invariant third-cohomology basis is
\(\phi,\,4\psi u\delta+\psi w\delta+uw\delta\). Its pairing with
\((\psi,h)\) has matrix
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-2&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
The first-degree pullback index is four, so perfect-pairing comparison
gives third-degree index two. The class \(\phi\) cannot descend,
because its pairing with the descended class \(h\) is \(-2\), which
is not divisible by four; hence the global generator is \(2\phi\).
In top degree the index is four. Thus the four global specialization
indices are precisely \((4,2,2,4)\). Each global invariant generator
surjects onto the corresponding local cokernel. There are no extra
first-cohomology terms from these skyscraper cokernels.

The resulting Leray page has only columns zero and two. Its column
zero in degrees zero through four is generated by
\[
 1,\quad4\psi,\quad2\omega,\quad2\phi,\quad4\nu;
\]
column two has the coinvariant generators listed above, multiplied
by the positive base class \(H\). The only clutch is the order-four
logarithmic translation. We justify the absence of an intrinsic
differential as follows. Replace that single affine cap temporarily
by the coarse quotient of the same root torus family under its
linear order-four action. This is a specified compact topological
filling; smoothness of this auxiliary quotient is not needed.
Its rational fibre cohomology is the invariant lattice tensored
with \(\mathbb Q\). Indeed an equivariant barycentric subdivision
identifies the quotient cochains with invariant cochains, and
averaging is exact over \(\mathbb Q\). Inversion about the
global origin commutes with the linear cap and extends over the
centrally symmetric toric fans. It acts by \((-1)^q\) in
degree \(q\), so naturality forces its rational \(d_2\) to
vanish. On the boundary, the linear and affine mapping tori are
identified by the explicit translation path
\(z\mapsto z+t\ell/4\). Four lifted copies of its prism give
the integral translation prism for \(\ell\); rational transfer
therefore divides that contraction by four. This computes the
rational differential of the actual affine cap. Since the source
and target groups already computed for the actual family are free,
that rational computation determines the integral differential on
the stated specialization lattice uniquely.

A cellular translation prism through a
cycle \(z\) induces contraction \(\iota_z\) on exterior cochains.
Going once around the base changes the root logarithm by one quarter
of \(\ell\); consequently
\[
 d_2(a)=\frac H4[\iota_\ell a].
\]
This formula is integral on the stated specialization lattice. The
exact contractions are
\[
 [\iota_\ell\psi]=1,\quad
 [\iota_\ell\omega]=2[\delta],\quad
 [\iota_\ell\phi]=2\eta,\quad
 [\iota_\ell\nu]=[uw\delta].
\]
Thus all four differentials are multiplication by one. There are no
higher differentials because the base has dimension two. The sole
surviving groups are \(H^0(X)=H^6(X)=\mathbb Z\), with no extension
ambiguities. Poincaré duality gives the same integral sphere homology.
Together with simple connectivity, Hurewicz and Whitehead identify
\(X\) as a homotopy six-sphere. The group \(\Theta _6\) is zero
(Kervaire--Milnor, the table on printed p.505), so it is diffeomorphic
to the standard \(S^6\).

## 5. The punctured holomorphic family and its closing map

The local constructions depend holomorphically on \(\lambda\) over
the universal logarithm half-plane, with a common bound on its
absolute value. Put \(c_{47}=(\log\lambda)/(2\pi i)\), so
\(c_{47}\mapsto c_{47}+4\)
adds \(4\delta\) to the period \(\gamma\) and to \(\ell\). At the
finite cap \(q=1/t=s^4\), the logarithmic gluing translation changes
by \((\log q)/(2\pi i)\delta\).

The ordinary-fibre multiplication by
\[
 h(t)=t-t_c
\]
is the closing map from \(c_{47}+4\) back to \(c_{47}\).
Equivalently the positive deck from \(c_{47}\) to \(c_{47}+4\)
multiplies by \(h^{-1}\). The direction follows directly from the
displayed logarithmic gluing: its change is
\(\log(q)/(2\pi i)\delta\), so the positive ordinary deck has that
same logarithmic coefficient at the finite cap. There \(h^{-1}\)
equals \(q\) times the inverse of the unit \(1-t_cq\). Thus it
cancels the change when passing back to the finite-chart coordinate; the
remaining unit acts holomorphically on the filled torus quotient. At
the chosen scalar-zero cusp, it equals the cusp parameter \(q_c\).
Its period generator has source translation \(-Q\) and line multiplier
\(\lambda q_c\) times a unit. Modulo that period, multiplication by
\(q_c\) is source translation by \(+Q\) and multiplication by
\(\lambda^{-1}\) times a unit. These extend on the resolved cusp,
since \(Q\) has an invertible Tate representative at this \(I_1\)
fibre. At the other two fillings \(h\) is a unit and extends directly.
The inverse has the reverse descriptions, so these are genuine
biholomorphisms. They agree on every annular overlap by the explicit
logarithmic equation.

Therefore the family closes holomorphically under
\(c_{47}\mapsto c_{47}+4\).
Its quotient parameter is \(r=e^{2\pi ic_{47}/4}\), with \(\lambda=r^4\).
The action is properly discontinuous on the parameter half-plane.
The same compact local fundamental domains prove that the quotient
family is proper over every closed annulus in the punctured disc.
All its local total-space charts are smooth over \(r\), and the finite
actions are free, so this is a proper holomorphic submersion with the
sphere fibres just proved. Its positive parameter loop is the
positive \(r\)-loop. This explicit closing map fixes which family
monodromy is being investigated.

## 6. The actual page and its section line

The line circle is free on both primitive rank-one fillings, including
all the nodes of the original \(I_7\). Here is a verification on the
actual line complement, not just its open torus. Choose a Hermitian
norm on \(M\) over a compact elliptic neighbourhood where the
linearization is invertible. For sufficiently small \(|\lambda|\),
the map \(g=\lambda\widetilde t_{-Q}\) has norm ratio strictly below
one everywhere. If \(e^{i\theta}v=g^n(v)\), the equality of norms
forces \(n=0\), also for negative \(n\) by applying \(g^{-n}\).
Then \(e^{i\theta}=1\). This proves freeness through every source
node and every component permutation in the contracting quotient.
The order-four cap has no extra stabilizers, because its
