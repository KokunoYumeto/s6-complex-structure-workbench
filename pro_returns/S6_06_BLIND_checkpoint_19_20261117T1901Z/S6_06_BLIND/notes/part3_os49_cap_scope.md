# P3F.33. The full affine-cap freeness criterion and the sphere sector

**Proved.** Keep the explicit marked OS49 root charts, put
\(m=3k\), \(k>0\), and use
\[
 \alpha(z)=Az+\ell/m,\qquad
 \ell=n(1,-1,0,0)^t+d\delta,\qquad n,d\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Here \(A\) is exactly the integral order-three matrix of P3F.26.
This cap action is free exactly for
\[
 \gcd(k,n,d)=1,\qquad
 3\nmid n\ \text{or}\ 3\mid k.                 \tag{P3F.33.1}
\]
For every cap in this explicit free sector, the completed threefold
has fundamental group \(\mathbb Z/|n|\), with the convention
\(\mathbb Z/0=\mathbb Z\). Its sphere members are consequently
exactly \(n=\pm1\). Their topology is already proved in P3F.29;
all their positive-diagonal returns are computed in P3F.32.
This does not assert the still-uncomputed higher integral
homology for the remaining non-coprime free caps.

## 1. All stabilizers on the actual root chart

A nonidentity base rotation has no fixed point away from the
central root fibre, so only that fibre needs to be checked.
For \(j=3r\), \(A^j=I\), and
\(\alpha^j\) is translation by \(r\ell/k\).
It has a fixed point precisely when
\[
 k\mid rn,\qquad k\mid rd.
\]
No \(1\leq r<k\) satisfies both conditions precisely when
\(\gcd(k,n,d)=1\).

For \(3\nmid j\), the images of \(A^j-I\) and \(A-I\)
are the same. For \(j=2\) the exact identity is
\(A^2-I=-A^2(A-I)\). The primitive quotient lattice
\(\Lambda/(A-I)\Lambda\) has the two coordinates
\[
 \psi,\qquad h=-w+3\delta
\]
of P3F.29. Their values on \(\ell\) are \(n\) and \(3d\).
Thus the affine fixed-point equation is soluble exactly when
\[
 m\mid jn,\qquad m\mid3jd.                     \tag{P3F.33.2}
\]
This is an equivalence: when the two coordinates are integral,
surjectivity of \(\Lambda\to\mathbb Z^2\) supplies a lattice
vector with those coordinates; subtract it and solve in the
real image of \(A^j-I\). The resulting point lies in the same
underlying complex torus and is an actual fixed point.

Put \(g=\gcd(m,n,3d)\). The integers satisfying (P3F.33.2)
are the multiples of \(m/g\). There is no such integer
\(j<m\) with \(3\nmid j\) precisely when \(3\mid m/g\).
Together with \(\gcd(k,n,d)=1\), this is equivalent to
(P3F.33.1). Indeed every prime other than three dividing
\(g\) would divide \(k,n,d\); if \(3\mid n\), freeness
requires \(3\mid k\), and the first gcd condition then forces
\(3\nmid d\). This checks every group element, without a
bounded search.

In particular the non-coprime free sector is nonempty:
\(m=9,n=3,d=1\) satisfies (P3F.33.1). The gamma numerator
alone does not detect its free action; the delta displacement
is essential.

## 2. The full fundamental-group presentation

The cap universal cover has deck generators \(\Lambda\) and
the lifted root rotation \(\alpha\), with exact relations
\[
 [\Lambda,\Lambda]=1,\qquad
 \alpha\lambda\alpha^{-1}=A\lambda,\qquad
 \alpha^m=\ell.
\]
This presentation is valid throughout the free sector just
classified. Attach it using the same punctured torus charts
as in P3F.26. The unchanged type-II and cusp fillings impose
the already computed primitive relation lattice, whose
quotient is generated by gamma: \(u,w,\delta\) vanish.
Their base meridians vanish as well, and the base relation
then kills the remaining meridian \(\alpha\).
Its power relation becomes exactly \(n\gamma=0\).
All its conjugation relations become trivial because the
gamma covector is \(A\)-invariant. Thus the displayed
presentation reduces to
\[
 \langle\gamma\mid n\gamma=0\rangle.
\]
This proves the fundamental group for every free cap, including
the non-coprime ones. For \(|n|\ne1\) it excludes a sphere.
For \(n=\pm1\) the cap satisfies the coprime hypotheses of
P3F.29, which prove simple connectivity and the full integral
homology of a six-sphere. The actual common blowup transfers
that topology to the positive fan, as proved in P3F.31.
There are therefore no additional sphere members among the
non-coprime free affine caps.
