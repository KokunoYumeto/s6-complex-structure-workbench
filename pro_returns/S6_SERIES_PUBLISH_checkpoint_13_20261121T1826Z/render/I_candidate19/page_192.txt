Theorem 22.14 (Fundamental-group obstruction). [tag 030O]
Let f : Y →∆be a proper holomorphic map from a smooth complex manifold, with smooth
connected fibres over ∆∗and reduced normal-crossings central fibre D. There is a continuous
specialization map from a nearby fibre Yt to D which induces a surjection
π1(Yt) −→π1(D).
Hence the central fibre of any such semistable filling of S6 is simply connected. In particular it
cannot be a simple pinching of a connected normalization along one pair of disjoint nonempty
conductor sections, whether its elliptic normalization base is split or is a genus-one torsor.
Proof. 1. The surjectivity assertion also follows from (Nakayama and Ogus, 2010, Theorem 5.1,
p. 2226). We use the explicit neighbourhood retraction needed in theorem 21.16 and proposi-
tion 22.19. Put W = f−1(∆ϵ) and W ∗= W \D. The negative gradient of |f|2, for a Hermitian
metric, retracts W onto D. In a product chart f = z1 · · · zk the arithmetic– geometric mean
inequality gives
∥∇|f|2∥≥c |f|2−1/k.
A finite cover of compact D gives a common exponent α < 1 in ∥∇ρ∥≥cρα, where ρ = |f|2;
for a fourfold one may take α = 7/8.
The remaining trajectory length is bounded by
c−1ρ1−α/(1 −α). Hence trajectories converge uniformly to D, and parametrization by the
level of ρ gives a continuous strong deformation retraction fixing D. General position moves
every loop of W off the real-codimension-two normal-crossings strata, so π1(W ∗) →π1(W) is
onto. The smooth proper fibration over the punctured disc gives
π1(Yt) −→π1(W ∗) −→Z −→0.
A small transverse meridian at a smooth point of a reduced component maps to the generator
of Z and bounds a disc in W. Thus the image of π1(Yt) alone generates π1(W) = π1(D).
Restricting the retraction W →D to Yt gives the asserted continuous specialization and its
surjection on fundamental groups.
2. For the last assertion, let R be the connected normalization and D0, D∞its two disjoint
conductor sections. Choose a continuous function h : R →[0, 1] equal to zero on D0 and one
on D∞. The function exp(2πih) descends to the pinching. A path from a point of D0 to its
paired point in D∞becomes a loop whose image has winding number one. Consequently the
pinching has a surjection of its fundamental group onto Z. This contradicts the preceding
necessary condition when Yt ≃S6.
