Proof. 1. The formula for D in proposition 3.2, theorem 6.6 gives the equation, and the theta
transformation law shows that it is independent of the modular and period representatives.
Compactness away from the cusp follows from Dpart →−∞. The analytic and regular-level
assertions follow in each root chart from the real-analytic implicit function theorem. On the
complement, the real period matrix is invertible. On compact subsets of that complement its
inverse is bounded, which proves uniform discreteness and proper discontinuity of the lattice
action. The quotient is therefore a complex manifold with compact torus fibres. The finite
deck action is free for the same invariant-coordinate reason as proposition 7.2, proposition 7.4:
a nontrivial power translates that circle by a noninteger fraction, regardless of the sign of D.
Near the cusp D < 0, so its existing holomorphic Mumford completion and its overlaps remain
available. At a zero with irrational kernel line, the fibre quotient is already non-Hausdorff.
At a zero with rational kernel choose a primitive integral kernel vector. Its holomorphic
period vanishes at that root point but is not identically zero nearby. Its first nonzero Taylor
coefficient, multiplied by arbitrarily large integer multiples and approached along suitable root
directions, yields nonseparated central points. Thus no neighbourhood of a zero in the naive
quotient is a Hausdorff complex manifold. This justifies the asserted maximal open base locus.
