Lemma 16.5 (The cusp neighbourhood and its circle classes). [tag IP07]
For the cusp neighbourhood N0 and the vanishing lattice K = Z⟨w, d⟩,
π1(N0) = Λ = Λ/K.
(16.5)
The punctured-cusp map to this group kills K and the base meridian. Both long-period covectors
extend in integral H1(N0; Z). The literal character on an ordinary fibre has no extension to all
of X ≃S6.
Proof. The toric covering has rays (v, 1) for all v ∈Z2. Meridians about its toric divisors kill the
whole cocharacter lattice: (0, 1) is a ray and (ei, 1) −(0, 1) = (ei, 0). Hence the toric cover is
simply connected. Its free period deck group is Λ = Λ/K, proving the displayed isomorphism.
The base meridian bounds the disc given by a point of the dense orbit on a central component.
Both long-period covectors kill these relations and therefore extend in integral H1. An extension
of θγ to all of X ≃S6 would give a nonzero homomorphism H1(F) →Z factoring through
H1(X) = 0, which is impossible.
Proposition 16.6 (The filled three-dimensional target). [tag 02PC]
For the original data the quotient over the complement of the cusp disc is the exterior of the
(3, 4) torus knot, with the specified Seifert structure. For general admissible (l0, l1, l2) the filled
quotient is the lens space determined by the following meridian slopes in one oriented integral
torus basis (ρ1, h):
µ1 = (3, −l1),
µ2 = (−4, 4l0 −l2),
det(µ1, µ2) = p = 12l0 −4l1 −3l2.
(16.6)
In the lens-space convention that the second meridian is −q0µ1 + pλ1, where det(µ1, λ1) = 1, the
lens parameter satisfies 3q0 ≡4 (mod p). In particular the target depends only on the signed
integer p, and is a three-sphere exactly when |p| = 1.
Proof. 1. Let P be the three-holed sphere, with oriented boundary meridians ρ0 + ρ1 + ρ2 = 0
and positive fibre h. The two finite meridian relations are 3ρ1 −l1h = 0 and 4ρ2 −l2h = 0;
the cusp solid-torus meridian is ρ0 + l0h. Eliminating ρ0 gives ρ2 = l0h −ρ1 on the annulus
joining the finite solid tori, which gives exactly (16.6). Since both slopes are primitive, this is
a genus-one Heegaard splitting. Choose a, b ∈Z with al1 + 3b = 1 and put λ1 = (a, b). Direct
multiplication gives
µ2 = −q0µ1 + pλ1,
q0 = a(4l0 −l2) + 4b,
3q0 −ap = 4.
(16.7)
This proves the claimed lens parameter in a convention fixed by the displayed oriented
meridians. The fundamental group of this gluing is Z/|p|. Admissibility implies gcd(p, 6) = 1,
so the modular inverse of 3 used above exists.
2. For (l0, l1, l2) = (0, 1, −1) the two exceptional-fibre data are (3, 1), (4, −1). They are realized
by the circle action
s · (z1, z2) = (e4isz1, e3isz2)
on S3 ⊂C2.
Its invariant map is [z3

2]; the local circle translation residues are 4−1 = 1 (mod 3) and
3−1 = −1 (mod 4). Removing a regular orbit gives the same two Seifert solid tori and their
product annulus as above. A regular orbit is the (3, 4) torus knot under the coordinate
exchange (z1, z2) 7→(z2, z1) when the opposite ordering is used. The displayed orbit map and
meridian slopes identify the manifold with its peripheral structure.
