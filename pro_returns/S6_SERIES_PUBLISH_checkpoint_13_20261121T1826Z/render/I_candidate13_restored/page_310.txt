5. To prove k transcendental, recall the real period formula η1 = (s2 + k)ω1. If k were algebraic,
the nonzero period vector (ω1, η1) of the algebraic universal vector extension of E would lie on
the algebraic line of slope s2 + k. Dividing this period by a sufficiently large integer supplies a
nonzero algebraic torsion exponential. The analytic subgroup theorem (Huber and Wüstholz,
2022, Theorem 6.1, arXiv v8, p. 43) would give a one-dimensional algebraic subgroup with
that Lie line. Its projection to E is nonzero, so it splits the vector extension after an isogeny.
The nonsplitting argument in cited result 26.9 excludes this: the coordinates here are exactly
(z, w) with periods (ω, η(ω)) and algebraic charts t = w −ζ(z), tO = t −℘′(z)/(2℘(z)).

7. Differential and growth criteria. The differential criterion of Waldschmidt (2009, The-
orems 3.6–3.7, author proof, pp. 21–22) requires a finite algebraically independent family
whose partial derivatives belong to the polynomial ring generated by that family over one
number field. Algebraic values of the full family then give algebraic values of all iterated
partial derivatives. The calculation above gives the exact value-field obstruction: the field
containing H(pα) and its second directional derivative contains k as soon as the nonzero
value is inverted. In particular its normalized second jet is transcendental. The issue persists
although the first cancelling direction is algebraic; the direction also varies with α.
