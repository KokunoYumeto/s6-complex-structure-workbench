5. Use the unit-square cusp triangulation, with twelve consecutive bottom cells in the y1 direction
and the same bridge choice in every unit prism. Its translates have bottom cell indices (i, j)
and top interval [j, j + 1]. Thus H increases the bottom second index and the top index by
one, while G1 shifts the first index by twelve. The fan is invariant under both, and therefore
under G2. For each bottom cell its three maximal cones are the determinant-one cones of
21.1. The usual compact-core construction of 20.6, now on the I12 elliptic root model and
with twelve plumbing intervals before the G2 quotient, gives a proper model on the root
cover. Its unfolded charts are precisely this fan. The compact core and collars therefore prove
properness and proper discontinuity.
6. The affine deck extends to this model because its unit multiplication preserves every toric
chart and its shear preserves the fan. It is free. On the r-central divisor a point lies on at
most two adjacent component labels in Z/12; no nonidentity translation can preserve that set.
This covers their intersection over the common base origin as well. On the q-central divisor
with r ̸= 0, the bottom triangle has second component indices contained in {j, j + 1}, again
not preserved by a nonzero translation modulo twelve. Off both axes the root-base action is
free. Its finite quotient is therefore smooth, proper over (q, r), flat over r, and has a reduced
normal-crossings r-central divisor.
