Proposition 19.8 (The finite boundary quotient and its leaves). [tag 032G]
At the boundary, the construction over B \ {1} is a complex manifold, since proposition 3.2,
theorem 6.6 makes every remaining finite period map invertible and the cusp completion is
unchanged. The order-four deck action descends continuously to T H. On its rational central
circle it is translation by −a/4; on the irrational central point it is trivial. Its finite quotient is
Hausdorff with central fibre a circle or a point, respectively. Neither quotient is a complex space
over the base compatible with the given holomorphic family off the centre. The linear foliation
by C(2, −1)t on the original central torus cover has compact elliptic leaves. For rational a the
leaves are closed; for irrational a the closure of a leaf is the set with its fixed value of Im ξ.
Proof. 1. The deck transformation is a biholomorphism of the original quotient, so the universal
property extends it uniquely to a homeomorphism of the Hausdorff reflection. The action on
the fixed product coordinate at the centre is ξ 7→ξ −κ2/4 = ξ −a/4. A finite group quotient
of a Hausdorff space is Hausdorff: finite orbits are separated by intersecting finitely many
translated open neighbourhoods. A finite rotation quotient of a circle is a circle.
2. On a closed small base disc every noncentral torus point has a representative in the image of
[0, 1]4 under its period matrix. These representatives belong to a single compact subset of
C2. At the centre their images meet every class of the circle or the point. The image of that
compact parameterized parallelepiped is therefore the full inverse image of the closed disc in
the reflection. The reflected map is proper; the same holds after the finite quotient. If it were
holomorphic on a compatible complex space, the central fibre would be analytic. A circle
cannot be a complex analytic space: a positive-dimensional analytic germ has real dimension
at least two, and a compact zero-dimensional analytic space is finite. A central point is also
impossible here: upper semicontinuity of fibre dimension, or the local dimension inequality at
a point in the closure of the three-dimensional smooth locus, forces a fibre dimension of at
least two for a holomorphic map to a curve. Thus this Hausdorff reflection cannot be glued as
a complex-space filling to the remaining constructed manifold.
