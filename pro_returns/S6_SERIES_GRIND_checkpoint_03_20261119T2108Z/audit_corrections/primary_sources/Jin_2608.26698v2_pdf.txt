   Suppose M is of dimension ≥ 5. There are several points of view to understand the role of
MCG(M ) in the topology of M . The determination of the mapping class group MCG(M ) is
the same as the isotopy classification of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms of M , which
is a natural subsequent step to the characterization of the oriented diffeomorphism type of
M . Moreover, MCG(M ) = π0 Diff + (M ) is the group of path components of Diff + (M ) and
thus contains homotopic information of Diff + (M ), the latter is a major object of interest in
geometric topology, and is accessible by methods and techniques from various branches of
mathematics (see for example [Kup19, WW01, BM20]). Despite of its significance, a complete
description of MCG(M ) can be hard to achieve. When M is simply connected, the seminal
work of Sullivan [Sul77] implies that MCG(M ) is finitely presentable, so one may have a better
chance to compute MCG(M ) under this assumption.
   In this paper we study the mapping class groups of simply connected closed 5-manifolds
that are spin and having no 2- and 3-torsion elements in the second homology group. For
these manifolds we could compute the Torelli groups explicitly and provide a complete set of
concretely constructed generators for these groups. Our main tool is Kreck’s modified surgery
theory [Kre99, Kre85]. We summarize our main results in 1.1 and sketch our methods in 1.2.
1.1. The results. The primary result of this paper is an explicit computation of Torelli groups
of simply connected closed spin 5-manifolds with no 2- and 3-torsion elements in the second
homology group. The simplest 5-manifold of such type is S 5 . By h-cobordism theorem and
Cerf’s pseudoisotopy theorem [Cer71, Theorem 1], for n ≥ 5 MCG(S n ) is isomorphic to the
Kervaire–Milnor group Θn+1 of smooth homotopy spheres, so MCG(S 5 ) = Θ6 is trivial. Thus
we focus on the case where the second homology of the 5-manifold is nontrivial, since otherwise
this manifold is a smooth homotopy sphere which is diffeomorphic to S 5 by the fact that Θ5
is trivial [KM63].

Remark 1.4. At this point we compare our computation to relevant results in the literature.
4                                            HUIZE JIN


     (1) Using techniques of pseudoisotopy Sato computed MCG(S p × S q ) for certain p and q
         [Sat69]. In particular his results imply that there is a splitting exact sequence
                         1 → I(S 2 × S 3 ) → MCG(S 2 × S 3 ) → Z/2 → 0,
         and I(S 2 × S 3 ) is isomorphic to F C33 , which is Haefliger’s group of isotopy classes of
         framed 3-knots in S 6 and is isomorphic to Z2 by [Hae66, Theorem 5.17]. Our result
         extends Sato’s computation of MCG(S 2 × S 3 ) in the sense that we could determine
         the group structure and provide explicit constructions for generators of I(S 2 × S 3 ).
     (2) Diffeomorphisms of S p × S q have also been considered by Turner [Tur69] and Levine
         [Lev69]. For p < q < 2p − 3, Turner proved that I(S p × S q ) is isomorphic to a
         certain semi-direct product (πq (SO(p + 1)) × Θp+q+1 ) ⋊ πp (SO) [Tur69, Theorem 3.10].
         Independently, for q < 2p − 1 and q ≥ 3, Levine [Lev69, Proposition 5.2] proved that a
         diffeomorphism of S p × S q (not required to be orientation preserving) is pseudoisotopic
         to a composition of diffeomorphisms of the following three types:
           (i) (x, y) 7→ (β(y) · x, y), where β : S q → O(p + 1) is a smooth map;
          (ii) (x, y) 7→ (x, β ′ (x) · y), where β ′ : S p → O(q + 1) is a smooth map;
         (iii) a diffeomorphism supported in an embedded (p + q)-disc.
         Note that S p × S p+1 is included in Turner’s result for p ≥ 5 and in Levine’s result for
         p ≥ 3. Our result shows that I(S 2 × S 3 ) is not a semi-direct product of such type, and
         one of its generators, tS1 #S1 ,α , cannot be written as such a composition. In this sense
         S 2 × S 3 differs from S p × S p+1 with p ≥ 3.
     (3) For #g (S p × S p ) where p ≥ 3, there is an exact sequence
                    1 → I(#g (S p × S p )) → MCG(#g (S p × S p )) → Gg → 1,
         where Gg is a certain group encoding the action of diffeomorphisms on homology
         [Kre79, Theorem 2]. For p odd this extension problem was studied by Krannich.
         His result [Kra20, Corollary C], which extends earlier results of Crowley [Cro11] and
         Galatius–Randal-Williams [GRW16], states that this exact sequence does not split for
         g ≥ 2, and splits for g = 1 and p ̸= 3, 7. For g = 1, when p = 3 it does not split
         by [Kry03], and the case p = 7 is open. Our result indicates that the behaviors of
         MCG(#g (S p × S p+1 )) and MCG(#g (S p × S p )) are quite different.

1.2. The methods. We sketch the method of using modified surgery to study the Torelli
group of high-dimensional manifolds. For simplicity we restrict ourselves to the case of simply
connected closed spin 5-manifolds. Let M be such a manifold. The starting point is to consider
the mapping torus
                               Mf := M × I / (x, 0) ∼ (f (x), 1)
of an orientation preserving diffeomorphism f of M . This is a closed orientable 6-manifold
whose diffeomorphism type is determined by the isotopy class of f .
   We view Mf as (M × I) ∪(idM ∪f ) (M × I), namely the result of gluing two copies of M × I
via idM on two copies of M × {0} and via f on two copies of M × {1}. Let p : B → BSO be
the fibration appearing in the normal 2-type of M . When f lies in the relative Torelli group
I(M, D), where D ⊂ M is an embedded 5-disc, we could construct a normal B-structure
ν : Mf → B such that the induced normal B-structures on the two copies of M × I are normal
2-smoothings. This construction defines a map
                                   ρ : I(M, D) → Ω6 (B, p)
                                            [f ] 7→ [Mf , ν].
The two versions of Torelli groups, I(M ) and I(M, D), are connected by an exact sequence
(1)                             Z/2 → I(M, D) → I(M ) → 1
due to Wall [Wal63a, page 265] (see Lemma 2.2).
   Kreck–Su made the following key observation (compare [KS25, page 514]). Suppose (Mf , ν)
bounds in Ω6 (B, p), then by modified surgery theory, there exists an h-cobordism W from
M × I to itself, such that the two copies of M × {0} are h-cobordant via M × I and the two
copies of M × {1} are h-cobordant via the mapping cylinder of f . By h-cobordism theorem,
there is a diffeomorphism W → M × I × I extending idM ×I : M × I → M × I × {0}. The
restriction of this diffeomorphism,
                      ∂W \ (M × I) → ∂(M × I × I) \ (M × I × {0}),
yields a pseudoisotopy between idM and f . Cerf’s pseudoisotopy theorem then implies that
f is isotopic to idM . All these diffeomrphisms and isotopies could be made to fix the disc D
pointwise. In our case we could show that ρ is a homomorphism, thus it is injective.
   Kreck–Su’s observation suggests investigating the bordism group Ω6 (B, p). We compute
this group and determine the relevant bordism invariants using Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral
sequence. When H2 (M ) has no 2-torsion elements this bordism group has no 2-torsion either,
so Wall’s exact sequence (1) implies that I(M, D) = I(M ). For M = Mg , we calculate
the bordism invariants for the mapping tori of certain Dehn twists using explicit geometric
methods, where the Hopf invariant plays a fundamental role. This leads to the determination
of I(Mg ) and its generators.
   Based on the determination of I(Mg ) we could compute I(M ). Using modified surgery
we show that MCG(♮g (S 2 × D3 ), ∂), the relative mapping class group of the g-fold bound-
ary connected sum of S 2 × D3 , is isomorphic to I(Mg ). Barden’s result [Bar65, Theorem
2.3] implies that M is a twisted double of ♮g (S 2 × D3 ) for certain g, so there is a homomor-
phism MCG(♮g (S 2 × D3 ), ∂) → I(M ) induced by extending a diffeomorphism by identity.
The computation of I(M ) and the determination of its generators result from analyzing this
homomorphism using bordism groups and spectral sequences.
   The major advantage of this modified surgery approach is that it enables us to define
isotopy invariants for diffeomorphisms in the Torelli group, in terms of certain differential
topological invariants (in our case, bordism invariants) of their mapping tori. (For a similar
but more complicated result for certain 6-manifolds, making use of the Kreck–Stolz invariants
for certain 7-manifolds, see [KS25].) These invariants, or more generally, considering the
                   MAPPING CLASS GROUPS OF CERTAIN SPIN 5-MANIFOLDS                           7


bordism properties of mapping tori, converts the isotopy classification of diffeomorphisms into
bordism theoretic constructions and differential topological calculations.
Remark 1.12. The modified surgery approach described in this subsection applies to non-spin
5-manifolds after mild modification (compare [KS25, Section 10]). Moreover one may use this
approach to study the mapping class groups of closed (4q + 1)-dimensional manifolds (see the
statement of [Kre99, Theorem 5]).
1.3. Structure of the paper. In Section 2 we define the map ρ. Section 3 is devoted to
the determination of relevant bordism groups and their bordism invariants. In Section 4
we compute I(Mg ). In Section 5 we prove Theorem 1.1, completing our computation of
Torelli groups. In Section 6 we show that MCG(Mg ) is a semi-direct product by a bordism
