## 1. Integral and coherent classes with their actual map

Delete the finite critical-value set and write U for the regular base.
Ehresmann identifies the differentiable smooth fibres with a primary
Kodaira surface. Borcea pp.373–374 explains that every complex structure
on this underlying Kodaira manifold is again of this type, so all fibres
over U have the required surface model.

The integral Leray edge sequence, H1(Y,Z)=0 and finite H2(Y,Z), gives

    d2 : H0(P1,R1 f_* Z) → H2(P1,Z)=Z

injective with image d0 Z for an integer d0>0. Use the precise D1.9
construction: choose γ with d2γ=d0; take its image under the coefficient
map Z→O and its inverse under coherent Leray to obtain β in H1(Y,O).
For every scheme-theoretic fibre Yc the actual naturality square gives

    βc = ιc(γc).

The positive meridian argument in D1.9/D1.8 gives γc≠0. The real coefficient
map is injective by D1.7, so βc≠0 on every full fibre. This is the cited
construction itself; I do not identify it with an arbitrary exponential
lift of f*O(k).

Put E=R1 f_* O and G=R2 f_* O. D1.12 gives E locally free of rank two,
G a line bundle, all-fibre base change, and the cup isomorphism

    det E → G.

Thus the actual global section β of E is nowhere zero, and cup with β
gives an exact sequence

    0 → O·β → E → G → 0.                         (A)

This is a fibrewise linear-algebra assertion transported by the actual
cup-product map; no unidentified quotient is used.

## 2. The two intrinsic multiplicative parameters

For a primary Kodaira surface p:F→B, Schröer–Siebert §1 gives an elliptic
base B, an elliptic principal fibre, and a line bundle A on B of degree
m>0 with the explicit presentation

    F = (A minus its zero section)/<v↦α v>,  0<|α|<1.

It also gives Pic0(F)/p*Pic0(B)≅C*, with the kernel of restriction to an
elliptic fibre generated by the intrinsic contraction parameter α.
The quotient can be constructed directly from the exponential sequence:
the rank-two lattice H1(B,Z) maps into H1(F,Z), and the quotient lattice
is Z. Its primitive generator, mapped into the one-dimensional quotient
of coherent cohomology, is the period 1; x modulo Z is sent to exp(2πix).
Choose its sign so that the class of p*A is represented by α. The choice
is fixed by |α|<1. It makes the construction compatible on overlaps.

Here is the line-bundle identification in this normalization. On A\0
the tautological section of p*A transforms by α under v↦α v. Descent
therefore identifies p*A with the character-α flat bundle for this
cyclic covering. Any line bundle having the same class in the displayed
Picard quotient differs from p*A by p*N, N∈Pic0(B), and hence is the
pullback of a degree-m line bundle on B. This records the actual descent
map and the degree; no equality between a flat representative and an
unidentified positive line bundle is assumed.

In a holomorphic family these intrinsic Picard quotients and their
rank-one kernel generator vary holomorphically; this is also the
holomorphic moduli coordinate of the explicit §1 presentation and
Borcea's complete family. Thus α is a holomorphic map U→Δ*. It is bounded,
extends across the finitely many missing points, and is constant on P1.
Its constant value stays nonzero because it is nonzero on U.

I will also use Borcea's marked period coordinate, with its own explicit
map rather than silently identifying it with α. With Borcea's m and
period coordinates, put

    τ = p14/p24,  s = p13/p14.

On one component of the marked period domain these lie in the upper
half-plane. Formula (17) changes s by −2k/m, so

    q_Bor = exp(π i m s)

is its quotient coordinate in Δ*. It too is globally holomorphic and
bounded, hence constant. No equality α=q_Bor is needed or asserted.

## 3. A positive-degree line bundle gives the required contradiction

The locally constant integer c which is the image of γ in
H1(F,Z)/p*H1(B,Z) controls the actual Picard-quotient morphism:

    q_F(exp(2πi t βc)) = exp(2πi c t).

This follows from the coefficient/exponential square and the primitive
period-1 normalization just specified. If c≠0, choose a fixed complex t
with exp(2πi c t)=α. The global line bundle

    L = exp(2πi t β) on Y

then restricts on every regular F to p*AF with deg(AF)=m>0, by the
tautological descent calculation above. A fixed sufficiently high power
L^n restricts to a line bundle whose sections give a nonconstant map of
the elliptic base B; choose nm≥3.

The sheaf f_*L^n is coherent on P1 and has generic rank nm. After twisting
by O(k) for sufficiently large k, its global sections generate at a
general base point. The associated sections of L^n⊗f*O(k) restrict to
the full space H0(F,p*AF^n)=H0(B,AF^n), the equality following from
p_*O_F=O_B. Their ratios include a meromorphic function nonconstant on F.
This contradicts M(Y)=f*M(P1), the defining function-field identity of
the algebraic reduction. This argument constructs sections of an actual
line bundle on Y; it does not presume a compact relative elliptic
quotient surface.

It remains to treat c=0.

## 4. Zero central component forces the marked H2 period to be constant

Now γ is a nonzero integral vector in the elliptic-base rank-two
local system. Monodromy fixes this vector. In an oriented integral basis
its SL2(Z) action is a subgroup of a parabolic stabilizer, so a marked
elliptic period τ transforms by integer translations. Consequently
exp(2πiτ) is a single-valued bounded map U→Δ*. It extends to P1 and is
constant and nonzero. A holomorphic lift τ is then locally constant;
equivariance of that constant lift forces all the translation integers
to vanish. The elliptic-base monodromy matrix is M=I.

Independently q_Bor is constant, so the marked lift s is locally constant.
The translations −2k/m likewise vanish. In Borcea (17), M=I and k=0
therefore give the identity on H2(F,Z) modulo torsion. The shear entries
in (15) do not occur in (17); they are not assumed zero on H1.

In a global flat H2 marking over U the holomorphic two-form line is now
the constant line generated by

    v = τs[θ13] + s[θ23] + τ[θ14] + [θ24],

where the θi are Borcea's invariant real one-forms. The cup square with
its conjugate is strictly positive. This gives an actual constant
positive cohomology class v, not only an unmarked isomorphism class.

## 5. Direct extension of that period line across every full fibre

Here is the local calculation, including the multiple-fibre exponent.
Let Δ be a disc around a missing base point, coordinate t. Smooth Y is
flat over Δ, and the full fibres are Cartier Cohen–Macaulay surfaces
with dualizing sheaf ω_{Y/Δ}|Yc. Analytic Serre duality on these full
fibres, h2(O_Yc)=1, and Grauert base change give a line bundle

    H = f_*ω_{Y/Δ} = G^∨.

The equality is the perfect relative Serre pairing; it is compatible
with restriction to every full fibre.

Take a generator Ω of H on Δ. Multiplication by dt identifies it with
a holomorphic top form Ω3 on f^{-1}(Δ). On Δ*, its fibre form Ωt has
period class a(t)v, for a holomorphic scalar a. Its squared fibre
integral is

    N(t) = ∫Yt Ωt∧overline(Ωt)
         = |a(t)|² Q(v,overline v),       Q(v,overline v)>0.

The measures and the fibre-integration identity are fixed explicitly.
For a two-form η on a complex surface use the positive four-form
η∧bar(η); in coordinates this is 4|h|² times Euclidean four-volume
for η=h dz2∧dz3. On the disc use i dt∧dbar(t), twice Euclidean area,
and on the total space use i Ω3∧bar(Ω3), which is 8|h|² times Euclidean
six-volume for Ω3=h dz1∧dz2∧dz3. On the submersion locus Ω3=dt∧Ωt
in relative notation, and moving dbar(t) past Ωt has sign +1.
Consequently Fubini gives the exact identity

    ∫Δε N(t) i dt∧dbar(t)
       = ∫f^{-1}(Δε) i Ω3∧bar(Ω3).

The latter integral is finite: f is proper, and Ω3 is holomorphic on
the inverse image of a slightly larger disc. The critical set has
measure zero. Thus a is an L2 holomorphic function on Δ*, and its Laurent
series has no negative terms; it extends holomorphically to Δ.

There is also a strictly positive lower bound near 0. Write the full
central divisor as Σ mi Di and let ai=ord_Di(Ω3). Some ai<mi: otherwise
Ω3/t would be holomorphic along every prime divisor and hence everywhere
on the smooth, normal total space, contradicting that Ω generates H.
At a general smooth point of such a Di, away from the other components,
absorb a holomorphic unit into a local coordinate and write

    t=z1^mi,
    Ω3=z1^ai u(z) dz1∧dz2∧dz3,    u nonzero.

On one local sheet of a nearby fibre, the actual relative form is

    Ωt=(1/mi) z1^(ai-mi+1) u(z) dz2∧dz3.

Choose a fixed closed bidisc of radius r in z2,z3 and a sufficiently
small z1-disc on which |u|≥u0>0. For each nonzero t choose any one of
the mi roots z1 of t, and integrate only over that sheet and that fixed
bidisc. With the surface measure specified above this gives

    N(t) ≥ C |t|^[2(ai-mi+1)/mi],
    C = 4 u0² π² r⁴ / mi² > 0.

Since ai≤mi−1, the exponent is at most zero. Shrinking the coordinate
disc to |t|≤1 gives the uniform bound N(t)≥C>0. Consequently a(0)≠0. The
calculation retains the mi−1 factor from division by dt; dropping it
would give the wrong relative-canonical exponent.

It follows that the period map H→O·v is an isomorphism across 0.
This applies at every missing point and glues because v is the same
global flat class. Thus H≅O_{P1} and G≅O_{P1}. This proof uses neither
an unproved global quotient extension nor a monodromy-invariant-cycle
surjectivity assertion.

## 6. The second coherent section has the required constant quotient

Sequence (A) is now an extension of O by O. Since H1(P1,O)=0, it splits.
Choose β' with β∪β'=g, where g is the global generator of G dual, by
the actual Serre pairing, to the just-extended period form with class v.
The quotient component of β' is nonzero on U. Its constancy needs a map
calculation; it is not inferred solely from the abstract splitting.

Use the invariant forms in a marked model:

    dθ1=dθ2=dθ3=0,       dθ4=−2 θ1∧θ2,
    A=τθ1+θ2,           Ξ=sθ3+θ4,          η=A∧Ξ.

The conjugates bar(A),bar(Ξ) represent a basis of H1(O_F).
The coefficient map on the integral free basis is

    θ1 ↦ −bar(A)/(τ−barτ),
    θ2 ↦ τ bar(A)/(τ−barτ),
    θ3 ↦ −bar(Ξ)/(s−bar s).

For γ=aθ1+bθ2 with a,b real integers and not both zero, this gives

    β_F=K bar(A),    K=(−a+bτ)/(τ−barτ)≠0.

K is constant. Write β'_F=u bar(A)+w bar(Ξ). Serre pairing with η gives

    <β_F∪β'_F,η> = K w ∫F η∧bar(η).

The integral is the fixed positive cup pairing of v with bar(v).
The left side is 1 by the choice of g. Therefore w is one fixed nonzero
complex number. The actual derivative of the Picard quotient, normalized
by its primitive integral θ3 period, sends β'_F to

    c' = −(s−bar s)w,

also a fixed nonzero complex number. Shearing θ3 by base classes changes
only u; it leaves this quotient and the displayed pairing unchanged.
The integral calculation is invariant under the marked isomorphism to
Borcea's model, since it preserves the period v and the cohomology maps.

Consequently, for the global exponential line bundle L_t=exp(2πitβ'),

    q_F(L_t|F)=exp(2πit c').

Choose the fixed t with this value α. The exact tautological descent
of §2 again identifies L_t|F as p*AF with degree m>0. The direct-image
and base-twist construction of §3 yields a meromorphic function varying
on F, contradicting a(Y)=1.

Both possibilities for the integral quotient c have now led to that
contradiction. Primary Kodaira general fibres are excluded in the
explicit smooth domain. Combining this conclusion with the separately
cited secondary-Kodaira exclusion gives the two-type statement at its
properly bound smooth scope.
