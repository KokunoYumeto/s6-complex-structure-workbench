    w1=N0*epsilon-rho0*dhat,
    w2=N1*epsilon'-(rho1+N1)*dhat,

## 3. The one critical fibre

The root lift is a submersion at both stack points. Its only critical
scheme Z is the reduced conductor of the rank-two Mumford fibre W.
In the chart xyz=t its ideal is (xy,xz,yz), with no embedded nilpotent
length. Globally Z is three rational curves meeting at two ordinary
triple points. Its normalization sequence is

    0 -> O_Z -> O_P1^3 -> C_qA^2 plus C_qB^2 -> 0.

The map on constants takes the two differences of component constants
at each vertex; its rank is two. Consequently h0(O_Z)=1,h1(O_Z)=2.
The preimage conductor in dP6 is the hexagon C, with h0=h1=1.
Its oriented boundary maps to the graph Z by the word
a*b*c*a^(-1)*b^(-1)*c^(-1). This is zero on abelian graph cycles,
so H1(O_Z)->H1(O_C) is zero. The conductor pushout sequence therefore
identifies H1(O_W) with H1(O_Z), and H2(O_W) with H1(O_C).
Collapsing one connecting edge c gives the cell with commutator boundary;
the two constant boundary cocycles have their nonzero alternating cup
product. These are the exact special-fibre maps used in section 2.

Put L=fbar^*T_Bcal=fbar^*O(d). The local Jacobian maps give

    0 -> fbar^*V -> T_X -> Jimage -> 0,
    0 -> Jimage -> L -> Q -> 0,
    Q=L tensor O_Z.

The logarithmic vertical generators in xyz=t are x*d/dx-y*d/dy and
x*d/dx-z*d/dz, which identify the kernel with fbar^*V. Since Z lies
over one ordinary base point, its factor from L is a one-dimensional
constant line and h(Q)=(1,2,0,0).

## 4. Universal weighted arithmetic

For P(3k,4l), H0(O(n)) has monomial basis X^A Y^B with
3k*A+4l*B=n, A,B>=0, and H1(O(n))=H0(O(-n-d))^*.

The lines O(-z) and O(-2z) are acyclic. Their H0 groups vanish by
negative degree. A putative monomial in H0(O(j*z-d)), j=1 or2,
would give

    3k*(A+1)+4l*(B+1)=j*k*l.

Coprimality forces A+1=l*a and B+1=k*b for positive integers a,b.
Then 3a+4b=j, impossible for j=1,2. This proves the H1 vanishing
for every admissible pair, not only bounded examples.

H0(O(d)) is generated by X*Y. Indeed A=1+l*a and B=1+k*b, and
3a+4b=0 gives a=4t,b=-3t. Nonnegative A,B force t=0.

For H0(O(d-z)), the same congruences give 3a+4b=-1, hence
a=1+4t,b=-1-3t. At t=0 the exponents are (1+l,1-k), allowed
exactly when k=1. At t>=1 the second exponent is negative; at
t<=-1 the first exponent is negative. Therefore, with epsilon=1
for k=1 and epsilon=0 otherwise,

    H0(O(d-z)) has dimension epsilon,
    its sole monomial when k=1 is X^(l+1).

H1(O(d-z)) vanishes: a monomial in O(z-2d) would force
A+2=l*a, B+2=k*b for positive a,b and 3a+4b=1.

Projection formula and Leray now give

    h(fbar^*V)=(1,1,0,0),
    h(L)=(1,1+epsilon,epsilon,0),
    h(Q)=(1,2,0,0).

The intermediate h(L) genuinely has two regimes. Keeping those regimes
is necessary for the actual calculation; the final dimensions agree.
The fresh integer program checks the monomial formulas for all 1<=k,l<=80
with gcd(3k,4l)=1 (1,965 pairs) as an implementation check. The congruence
proof above establishes the unrestricted claim.

## 7. Smooth reduced miniversality

The Kuranishi germ has tangent dimension one. Present it as the zero
germ of an analytic ideal I in C{z0}. The classifying map from the
nonzero-scale disc has nonzero derivative by section 6. Before quotienting,
the induced substitution C{z0}->C{u-u0} is an analytic isomorphism.
Every element of I vanishes under this isomorphism, so I=0. This proves
the full germ is smooth and reduced. The classifying map is locally an
isomorphism, hence the fixed-data nonzero scale family is locally miniversal.
