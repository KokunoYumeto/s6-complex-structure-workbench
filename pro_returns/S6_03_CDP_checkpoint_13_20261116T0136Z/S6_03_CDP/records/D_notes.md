## Proved conclusions and dependencies

- **C.1.1–C.1.3 / Lead 4.** Rational Betti-number vanishing b1=b2=0 suffices. The integral first cohomology vanishes, integral second cohomology is finite, and the exponential sequence identifies Pic0 with the complex vector space H1(O). Every Picard component is a torsor for this space. A positive multiple of any nonzero effective Cartier divisor lies in Pic0; arbitrary multiplicities cause no difficulty. The descending section spaces for negative multiples stabilize at zero. The explicit universal family has transitions exp(2πi Σv_k a^k_ij). Proper analytic cohomology gives closed analytic jump loci. Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch gives χ(T⊗L)=1−b3/2. Hence b3=0 forces H2(T⊗L) nonzero for every line bundle, including all components.
- **C.3.1.** The base is P1; every smooth fibre is minimal, has rationally trivial canonical class, c2=0, and χ(O)=0. Very general fibres have Kodaira dimension at most zero: rank at least two of f_*K^m would give a meromorphic map with image dimension at least two into a projective bundle. The necessary classification list is tori, bielliptic surfaces, primary/secondary Kodaira surfaces, primary/secondary Hopf surfaces, and Inoue surfaces. This is not an occurrence theorem.
- **C.3.2–C.3.3.** Explicit secondary Kodaira and secondary Hopf surfaces each have H1(homology)=Z and H2(cohomology)=0. The Kodaira example uses an order-six affine automorphism of a Heisenberg lattice and has canonical bundle of order six. The Hopf example is (C2\0)/(2^Z × binary icosahedral group). The 120-element group and its perfectness are checked in exact Q(sqrt5) arithmetic. These examples refute the intrinsic torsion assertions used in the printed CDP20 Lemma 4.3, but are not asserted to be general fibres of a threefold with the required Betti numbers.
- **C.3.4.** A component of a reducible fibre cannot have a compact Kähler resolution when b2(Y)=0. Restricting the line bundle of an adjacent component produces a nonzero effective divisor of real Chern class zero on the resolution, contradicting integration against a Kähler form.
- **C.3.5.** All types on the necessary list have Euler number zero. Thus Lead 5's Euler sum over exceptional reduced fibres holds for every surviving type. No sign of the terms follows from this identity alone.
- **C.3.6.** Both primary and secondary Hopf types actually occur. The free proper action t·(z,w1,w2)=(e^t z,e^{it}w1,e^{i sqrt2 t}w2) on (C2 minus zero)^2 has quotient diffeomorphic to S3×S3, hence b1=b2=0. Projection [z] gives a smooth bundle over P1 with primary Hopf fibre of contraction factors exp(−2π), exp(−2π sqrt2). An explicit convergent-product adjustment of the unit in a meromorphic eigenfunction proves that every invariant meromorphic function on this fibre is constant. Thus the total algebraic dimension is one. A free scalar μm quotient on the w-factor gives secondary Hopf fibres and preserves rational Betti vanishing. Both constructions have c3=0.
- **C.3.7.** Bielliptic general fibres are excluded. Kawai Proposition 2 supplies a compact relative Albanese surface and meromorphic map, holomorphic over the good base; use the finite orbit sum of its chosen local divisor to repair the asserted deck invariance. Resolve target and map over only the exceptional base values. The resolved Albanese surface has b1=0, hence is Kähler. Its Kähler class pulls back to a combination of exceptional divisor classes because b2(Y)=0; these are all vertical and restrict to zero on a general fibre. Its actual pullback by the bielliptic Albanese submersion has strictly positive integral against a fibre Kähler form, a contradiction.
- **D.1.1–D.1.2 / Lead 6.** The explicit abelianization map sends [(k,q)] to ([q],[k]) in Qab ⊕ (Kab)_Q. For a base of genus g and s removed critical values, b2(Y)=0 gives r=2g+s−1+t′−b1(Y), where t′ is the rational coinvariant rank, equivalently the invariant cohomology rank. Under H1(Y;Z)=H2(Y;Z)=0, H1(U;Z)=Z^r, so the coinvariants are torsion-free; this says nothing by itself about torsion in H1(F;Z). The compact-support sequence and Poincaré duality giving this formula are written with exact maps.
- **D.1.3 and D.1.14.** For the retained AC construction, invariants are Zδhat on homology and Zγ on cohomology, coinvariants are Z, and r=s=3. The full group is proved to equal the kernel of an explicit surjective character from the stabilizer of δhat to Z/12. The group law, lifts, three Heisenberg generators, commutator and index are checked exactly.
- **D.1.4.** The restriction map is the coherent Leray edge map followed by evaluation and coherent base change. On P1 its general rank equals the number of nonnegative summands of R1f_*O. It is not determined by the rank of the integral monodromy invariants alone. Local freeness uses E4's analytic proof.
- **D.1.10.** The original divisor-logarithm argument, corrected by the coinvariant count and E4, shows that the image of coherent restriction contains the complex span of the invariant integral lattice. This proves the invariant-span implication in Lead 6. “Exactly when” describes whether the final invariant-span step establishes surjectivity, not an equivalence between spanning and the actual restriction map being surjective: its image can contain more classes.
- **D.1.5.** Explicit bielliptic and rational-elliptic product examples refute the erroneous general steps. Their missing global hypotheses are specified. They are supplementary to the strict Wang counterexample.
- **D.1.6 and D.1.13.** Wang's rational homology six-sphere is a second counterexample satisfying the integral cohomology hypotheses. Its H2 homology is Z/2, while H2 cohomology is zero by the universal coefficient theorem. - **D.1.8.** R1f_*Z→j_*R1f_*Z|smooth is injective. - **D.1.9.** Low-degree integral Leray supplies a rank-one lattice of global sections of R1f_*Z mapping to dZ⊂H2(P1;Z). Rational b1=b2=0 yields d>0; the stronger integral H2=0 yields d=1. - **D.1.11.** The all-fibre non-torsion locus is dense Gδ and of full Lebesgue measure in every Picard component for every proper connected map Y→P1 with b1=b2=0. This holds for all smooth-fibre types, not just tori. E4 gives the splitting and constant evaluation rank k. For k≥2, analytic incidences have complex dimension at most dim Pic−1. For k=1, the nowhere-zero β forces Pic0=C. Any moving integral period lying identically in Cβ defines a rational real two-plane preserved by monodromy; its ratio τ has nonzero imaginary part and monodromy τ↦τ+n. The bounded function exp(±2πiτ) descends and extends to P1, hence is constant. The exceptional set is therefore countable. This proves the conclusion of CDP20 Proposition 5.6 with an explicit meaning of “general.” There is no counterexample to this repaired conclusion. - **D.1.12.** For a two-torus or primary-Kodaira general fibre and b2(Y)=0, cup product det R1f_*O→R2f_*O is an isomorphism. Riemann–Roch gives deg G + length torsion(G)=deg E; the generically nonzero cup map gives the opposite degree inequality, forcing no torsion and equal degrees. This proves full-fibre hq(O)=(1,2,1) and all degree-one/two base change. It repairs both conclusions of Proposition 5.1 without the printed canonical-bundle argument. For a primary Kodaira surface the invariant Dolbeault classes bar(alpha),bar(eta) have nonzero wedge by their Serre pairing with alpha wedge eta.
- **D.2.1.** Trivial monodromy forces r=s+3. Hence reducible fibres occur, and every component of a reducible fibre lacks a compact Kähler resolution. This proves nonexistence when every fibre component has a Kähler resolution.
