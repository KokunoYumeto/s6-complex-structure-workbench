\begin{abstract}
We canonically quantize the complete spatially homogeneous mechanics
of an $SU(3)$ gauge field coupled to a traceless Hermitian adjoint scalar
$\Phi$ and a complex triplet $b$, retaining all thirty-eight real
configuration coordinates. Its potential contains the original cubic
$\det\Phi-b^*\Phi b$ and quartic term in
$s=\operatorname{tr}\Phi^2+2b^*b$; no minimum-manifold constraint is imposed.
An explicit root-frequency calculation in the metric $-\operatorname{tr}$
gives a coercive quadratic-form bound despite the commuting classical
valleys. Together with a sharp cubic estimate, this proves compact
resolvent of the full coupled Hamiltonian. We derive the Gauss generators
and their domains, construct the constant-$SU(3)$ invariant Hilbert space,
and prove that its ground state is unique and its first excitation gap
is strictly positive for every fixed positive spatial volume, gauge
coupling, quartic coupling and Planck constant. No numerical or
volume-uniform gap is claimed. The mechanism of bosonic matrix
discreteness is classical; the purpose is its explicit application to
this retained coupled model. We also prove the precise classical
embedding into the torus field theory and exhibit a quartic Fourier-mode
coupling that prevents a fixed discarded-mode vacuum from defining an
automatic quantum subsector. Thus the finite-model result is not a
continuum Yang--Mills mass-gap theorem.
For the exact interacting two-Fourier-coordinate truncation, we then
construct the actual positive quartic fibre ground state $\Omega_q$.
Its isometric embedding has zero Berry connection but a nonzero
Born--Huang potential $\hbar^2\|\partial_q\Omega_q\|^2/(2\mathcal V_3)$
away from $q=0$. We retain its full off-diagonal residual and prove
an explicit finite-energy, physical-time comparison of order
$\mathcal V_3^{-1/2}$ at fixed $\hbar,\Gamma,L_1,m^2$.
The sufficient volume bound and every error constant are given;
neither the mixed quartic interaction nor the pure discarded-mode
quartic is removed. This correspondence is not a reducing subspace
or a comparison with an unconstructed continuum Hamiltonian.
\end{abstract}
