# Exact open-boundary polynomial for native magnetic spectral leakage

8 September 2026. The coefficient calculated here is the first nonzero
out-of-band spectral mass of the original native magnetic state. Every
positive physical link, every elementary face, all coplanar and hinged
shared-edge pairs, and both shared-edge spin channels are retained. The
polynomial is derived from their original coordinates and boundary counts.
The physical state is the one built from the actual interacting vacuum.

## 1. Exact object whose coefficient is counted

Fix \(L\ge2\), with vertices \(\{-L,\ldots,L\}^3\). Include every
positive nearest-neighbor link \(e=(n,i)\) and elementary face
\(p=(n;i,j)\), \(i<j\), contained in that box. Denote the sets by
\(\mathsf E_L,\mathsf P_L\), with cardinalities
\[
 N_L^{\rm edges}=3(2L)(2L+1)^2,\qquad M_L=3(2L)^2(2L+1).
\tag{1.1}
\]
Each face has its original word
\[
 W_{n;i,j}(U)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl[
 U_i(n)U_j(n+\mathbf e_i)U_i(n+\mathbf e_j)^{-1}U_j(n)^{-1}\bigr].
\tag{1.2}
\]
There is one \(SU(2)\) variable per physical link; inverse traversal
uses the inverse of that same variable. Use product Haar probability
measure, the gauge-invariant closed subspace \(\mathcal H_{\rm phys}\),
\(T_a=-i\sigma_a/2\), the left derivatives \(X_{e,a}\) generated by
\(T_a\), and the exact link Casimirs \(E_e=-\sum_aX_{e,a}^2\).
Retain
\[
\begin{gathered}
 H=\kappa K_\xi+2bM_LI,
 \quad K_\xi=H_0-\xi S,
 \quad H_0=\sum_eE_e,
 \quad S=\sum_pW_p,\\
 \kappa=\frac{2g_{\rm YM}^2}{a},\qquad
 b=\frac1{2g_{\rm YM}^2a},\qquad
 \xi=\frac b\kappa=\frac1{4g_{\rm YM}^4},\qquad a>0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1.3}
\]
The original metric \(c(A,B)=-\operatorname{tr}(AB)/2\) has
\(-\Delta_e^c=4E_e\), included in \(\kappa\). The operator and form
domains of \(H\) are \(H^2\cap\mathcal H_{\rm phys}\) and
\(H^1\cap\mathcal H_{\rm phys}\), respectively: the product Laplacian
is elliptic on the compact configuration manifold and \(S\) is bounded
smooth multiplication. Its positive unit vacuum \(\psi_\xi\) is smooth,
with \(K_\xi\psi_\xi=e(\xi)\psi_\xi\). Positivity and uniqueness
follow from the modulus inequality, elliptic maximum principle, and the
identity
\[
 \langle\psi_\xi F,(H-\mathcal E_\xi)\psi_\xi F\rangle
 =\kappa\sum_{e,a}\int\psi_\xi^2|X_{e,a}F|^2dU,
 \qquad \mathcal E_\xi=\kappa e(\xi)+2bM_L.
\tag{1.4}
\]
A second ground vector divided by the strictly positive \(\psi_\xi\)
would have every derivative zero, hence be constant. Gauge transformations
therefore fix this same actual vacuum.

The native original weights, face averages, and angle are
\[
\begin{gathered}
 w_{(n,1)}=n_2^2,\qquad w_{(n,2)}=w_{(n,3)}=0,
 \qquad a_p=\frac14\sum_{e\in\partial p}w_e,\\
 a_{n;1,2}=\frac{n_2^2+(n_2+1)^2}{4},\qquad
 a_{n;1,3}=\frac{n_2^2}{2},\qquad a_{n;2,3}=0,\\
 \Gamma=\sum_ew_eE_e,\qquad
 h_1(n)=\exp(-\theta n_2H_c),\quad h_2=h_3=I,
 \qquad H_c=i\sigma_3=-2T_3,\qquad \theta=\frac{2\pi a^2}{D},\quad D>0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1.5}
\]
Here \(a\) without a face subscript is the original spatial spacing.
All integer origins and angles in (1.5) are unchanged. The operator
\(\Gamma\) is diagonal on the joint link-spin decomposition, with
eigenvalue \(\sum_ew_ej_e(j_e+1)\); its self-adjoint domain is the
set of coefficient families square summable after multiplication by that
eigenvalue. The inequality \(0\le\Gamma\le L^2H_0\) on these
coordinates proves \(\Gamma:H^{k+2}\to H^k\). Thus all expressions
below applied to the smooth vacuum lie in their asserted domains.

Let \(\Pi\) be the orthogonal vertex gauge projection and let
\((\mathcal T_\theta f)(U)=f((h_e^{-1}U_e)_e)\). The actual centered
physical state and its angular coefficient are
\[
\begin{split}
 \chi_{\xi,\theta}
  &=\Pi\mathcal T_\theta\psi_\xi
    -\langle\psi_\xi,\mathcal T_\theta\psi_\xi\rangle\psi_\xi,\\
 v_\xi&=(\Gamma-\gamma_\xi)\psi_\xi,
 \qquad\gamma_\xi=\langle\psi_\xi,\Gamma\psi_\xi\rangle,\\
 \chi_{\xi,\theta}&=-\frac23\theta^2v_\xi+O_{H^k,L,\xi}(\theta^4).
\end{split}
\tag{1.6}
\]
The factor \(-2/3\) follows from the exact full-representation gauge
average. Each translated direction-1 link has a different canonical
source. Independent source twirls therefore act on its spin-\(j\) sector
by \(c_j(\theta n_2)=(2j+1)^{-1}\sum_{m=-j}^je^{2im\theta n_2}\).
Its second angular term is
\(-2\theta^2n_2^2j(j+1)/3\), and its odd terms vanish. The product
over links proves (1.6). Taylor's integral formula for the averaged
translation flow bounds four derivatives \(H^{k+4}\to H^k\), so this
is a vector-domain expansion of the actual vacuum. All spin sectors of
that vacuum remain in the exact average.

Write \(P\) for the free physical energy-three face projection. Its
basis \(W_p\) is orthonormal: a link of unmatched central parity kills
an unequal-face Haar inner product, and an equal-face holonomy is Haar
with fundamental character squared integral one. Thus
\(\mathcal J_L(a_p)_p=\mathbf a_L=\sum_pa_pW_p\) is the exact
isometry from face coefficients into that subspace. A nonconstant physical
spin support has minimum vertex degree two, hence a cycle of at least
four links. Below energy \(9/2\) it can only be one elementary square
with four equal spin-\(1/2\) links. This proves that the free physical
bottom is simple, its next eigenvalue is 3 on this face space, and the
next possible energy is \(9/2\).

For real \(0<\xi\le3/(128M_L)\), let
\[
\begin{gathered}
 P_\xi=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|z-3|=3/4}(z-K_\xi)^{-1}dz,
 \qquad T_\xi=PP_\xi P|_{\operatorname{ran}P},\\
 V_\xi=P_\xi P T_\xi^{-1/2}:
 \operatorname{ran}P\longrightarrow\operatorname{ran}P_\xi.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1.7}
\]
The Neumann resolvent estimate using \(\|S\|=2M_L\) gives
\(\|P_\xi-P\|\le1/7\) on the larger complex disk
\(|\xi|\le3/(64M_L)\). Thus the inverse square root is its convergent
binomial series. At real coupling \(P_\xi\) is orthogonal and
\(V_\xi^*V_\xi=I\); its range has the same finite dimension as that
of \(P\). The min--max bound \(2M_L|\xi|\le3/64\) and the free
isolation prove that \(P_\xi\) is the actual entire first excitation
band and that \(P_\xi\psi_\xi=0\) exactly. In particular
\[
 v_\xi=V_\xi(V_\xi^*v_\xi)+\rho_\xi,
 \quad \rho_\xi=(I-P_\xi)v_\xi,
 \quad \rho_\xi\perp\psi_\xi,
 \quad \rho_\xi\perp\operatorname{ran}P_\xi.
\tag{1.8}
\]
This is the exact map to the interacting band; the same vacuum is used
on both sides. The finite-dimensional range is smooth by elliptic
regularity, so the map and the products in (1.8) respect the vector
domains used here.

For adjacent faces \(p,q\) sharing their unique physical edge \(e\),
define
\[
 Y_p=W_p^2-1,\quad
 Z_{pq,0}=\int W_pW_q\,dU_e,\quad
 Z_{pq,1}=W_pW_q-Z_{pq,0},\quad s_{pq}=a_p+a_q.
\tag{1.9}
\]
Haar integration gives \(Z_{pq,0}\) as one half of the fundamental
trace on their six-edge boundary. Hence the free electric energies and
squared norms of \(Y_p,Z_{pq,0},Z_{pq,1}\) are respectively
\((8,1),(9/2,1/4),(13/2,3/4)\). The last norm is the total product
norm one minus the orthogonal Haar projection norm \(1/4\). Different
unordered pairs have different outer-boundary central parity. Otherwise
their symmetric difference would be a closed mod-two collection of at
most four elementary squares; one square would require four distinct
other squares to cover its four edges, which is impossible. This proves
the pairwise orthogonalities; repeated faces have distinct joint spins
and even parity, giving the remaining orthogonalities.

The exact transport calculation yields
\[
\begin{split}
 V_\xi^*v_\xi&=\xi\mathbf a_L+O_L(\xi^3),\\
 \rho_\xi&=\xi^2\mathbf r_L+O_{H^k,L}(\xi^3),\\
 \mathbf r_L&=\sum_p\frac{2a_p}{15}Y_p
 +\sum_{\{p,q\}\text{ adjacent}}
 \left[-\frac{2(s_{pq}+w_e)}9Z_{pq,0}
       +\frac{2(3s_{pq}+7w_e)}{273}Z_{pq,1}\right].
\end{split}
\tag{1.10}
\]
Here is a direct coefficient verification that also specifies what was
subtracted. The actual vacuum has
\[
\begin{split}
 \psi_\xi&=1+\frac\xi3S+\xi^2B^{\rm vac}+O_{H^k,L}(\xi^3),\\
 B^{\rm vac}&=-\frac{M_L}{18}+\sum_p\frac{Y_p}{24}
 +\sum_{\{p,q\}\text{ edge-disjoint}}\frac{W_pW_q}{9}
 +\sum_{\{p,q\}\text{ adjacent}}
     \left(\frac4{27}Z_{pq,0}+\frac4{39}Z_{pq,1}\right).
\end{split}
\tag{1.11}
\]
Indeed coefficient comparison gives
\(H_0B^{\rm vac}=(S^2-M_L)/3\) and the unit norm gives its Haar
mean \(-M_L/18\). Resolve the repeated and unordered pair terms in
\(S^2\) and divide by their energies \(8,6,9/2,13/2\) to get
(1.11). Analyticity in each fixed Sobolev space follows from the isolated
vacuum contour and repeated elliptic improvement in its eigen-equation.
Its scalar \(\gamma_\xi\) has second coefficient \(\sum_pa_p/3\).
The weighted electric eigenvalues of those same four channels are
\(8a_p,3s_{pq},3s_{pq}-3w_e/2,3s_{pq}+w_e/2\).

Let \(R_3=(I-P)(H_0-3)^{-1}(I-P)\), with inverse on
\(\operatorname{ran}(I-P)\). Differentiating (1.7) gives
\(V'_0=R_3SP\). Its value on \(\mathbf a_L\) has constant

\(2s_{pq}/3,2s_{pq}/7\). The second coefficient of \(v_\xi\) is

spectral dressing cancels the identical constant and edge-disjoint terms.
The repeated difference is \(a_p/3-a_p/5=2a_p/15\). The two adjacent
differences are
\((4s-2w)/9-2s/3=-2(s+w)/9\) and
\((12s+2w)/39-2s/7=2(3s+7w)/273\). This proves (1.10), including
the actual vacuum cancellation and both nonlinear spin channels.
Finite-angle edge-disjoint terms need not vanish: their second coupling
coefficient is \((\alpha_p-1)(\alpha_q-1)W_pW_q/9\), where
\(\alpha_p=\prod_{f\in\partial p,\ f=(n,1)}\cos(\theta n_2)\).
They have angular order \(\theta^4\), explaining their absence in
(1.10) while retaining them in the exact state (1.6).

Define the unchanged mass and leakage coefficient by

\begin{split}
 A_L&=\|\mathbf a_L\|^2=\sum_pa_p^2,\\
 \mathsf R_L&=\|\mathbf r_L\|^2
 =\frac4{225}A_L
  +\frac1{81}\sum_{\{p,q\}\text{ adjacent}}(s_{pq}+w_e)^2
  +\frac1{24843}\sum_{\{p,q\}\text{ adjacent}}(3s_{pq}+7w_e)^2.
\end{split}
\tag{1.12}
\]
The last denominator includes the squared norm \(3/4\):
\((3/4)\cdot4/273^2=1/24843\). Thus (1.12) is exactly the
unnormalized Hilbert norm, not a changed representation amplitude.
The symbol \(\mathsf R_L\) here denotes leakage; it is distinct from

MAGNETIC_TRANSLATION_PLAQUETTE.md is \(\mathcal A_L=4A_L\).

## 2. Exhaustive adjacent-face classification in the original box

For an original transverse coordinate \(m\in\{-L,\ldots,L\}\),
write
\[
 \Sigma_L(m)=\{+1:m<L\}\cup\{-1:m>-L\},
 \qquad k_L(m)=|\Sigma_L(m)|
       =\begin{cases}1,&|m|=L,\\2,&|m|<L.\end{cases}
\tag{2.1}
\]
The notation lists available physical sides; it creates no wrapping side.
Its exact sum is \(\sum_{m=-L}^Lk_L(m)=4L\).
For arbitrary real scalar parameters \(c,d\), introduce the polynomial
sum
\[
 H_L(c,d)=\sum_{\{p,q\}\text{ adjacent}}
                 [c(a_p+a_q)+d w_{e(p,q)}]^2.
\tag{2.2}
\]
We will evaluate it at \((c,d)=(1,1)\) and \((3,7)\), precisely
the two original sums in (1.12). The parameters do not change the link
weights, face vectors, or Hamiltonian. Put \(t=c+d\).

Each adjacent pair has a unique shared edge, since two distinct elementary
squares share at most one edge. For an edge in direction \(i\), its
incident faces have one of the two transverse directions, with the
available signs in (2.1). An unordered pair is either the two opposite
faces of one plane, or one face of each transverse plane. These are
exactly the two coplanar types and the hinged type listed below.

For a direction-1 edge with original source \((x,m,z)\),
\(-L\le x<L\), \(-L\le m,z\le L\), its incident 12-face on side
\(\sigma\in\Sigma_L(m)\) has weight
\[
 t_\sigma(m)=\frac{m^2+(m+\sigma)^2}{4}
       =\frac{m^2}{2}+\frac{\sigma m}{2}+\frac14;
\tag{2.3}
\]
its incident 13-faces each have weight \(m^2/2\). Here \(w_e=m^2\).
The three types therefore contribute
\[
\begin{split}
 H_{1,12}(c,d)&=2L(2L+1)
       \sum_{m=-L+1}^{L-1}(t m^2+c/2)^2,\\
 H_{1,13}(c,d)&=2L(2L-1)t^2\sum_{m=-L}^{L}m^4,\\
 H_{1,\rm hinged}(c,d)&=8L^2\sum_{m=-L}^L
       \sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_L(m)}
               (t m^2+c\sigma m/2+c/4)^2.
\end{split}
\tag{2.4}
\]
For the first line both 12-sides exist exactly when \(|m|<L\);
their weights add to \(m^2+1/2\), with \(2L\) values of \(x\)
and \(2L+1\) of \(z\). For the second line both 13-sides exist
exactly when \(|z|<L\); their weights add to \(m^2\), with
\(2L(2L-1)\) choices of \((x,z)\). In the hinged line their sum
is \(m^2+\sigma m/2+1/4\). The number of available 13-sides summed
over \(z\) is \(4L\); multiplying by the \(2L\) values of \(x\)
gives \(8L^2\). Thus every boundary side has its actual multiplicity.

For a direction-2 edge with source \((x,m,z)\),
\(-L\le m<L\), \(-L\le x,z\le L\), one has \(w_e=0\).
Both possible 12-faces have weight \(r_m/2\), where
\(r_m=m^2+m+1/2\), and each possible 23-face has weight zero.
The coplanar 12-pair requires \(|x|<L\) and has sum \(r_m\).
A hinged 12/23-pair has sum \(r_m/2\), with
\((\sum_xk_L(x))(\sum_zk_L(z))=16L^2\) possible sides at each
\(m\). The coplanar 23-pair contributes zero. Consequently
\[
 H_2(c,d)=c^2(2L-1)(2L+1)\sum_{m=-L}^{L-1}r_m^2
        +4L^2c^2\sum_{m=-L}^{L-1}r_m^2
        =c^2(8L^2-1)\sum_{m=-L}^{L-1}r_m^2.
\tag{2.5}
\]
The dependence on \(d\) vanishes because the original shared-edge weight
is zero, not because the faces or their interactions were removed.

For a direction-3 edge with source \((x,m,z)\),
\(-L\le z<L\), \(-L\le x,m\le L\), again \(w_e=0\).
Its possible 13-faces each have weight \(m^2/2\), and its possible
23-faces each have weight zero. A coplanar 13-pair contributes \(c^2m^4\)
with \(2L(2L-1)\) choices of \((z,x)\). A hinged 13/23-pair
contributes \(c^2m^4/4\), and its \((z,x,\text{sides})\) count is
\((2L)(4L)k_L(m)=8L^2k_L(m)\). The coplanar 23-pair contributes
zero. Thus
\[
 H_3(c,d)=2L(2L-1)c^2\sum_{m=-L}^Lm^4
       +2L^2c^2\sum_{m=-L}^Lk_L(m)m^4.
\tag{2.6}
\]

As a count independent of all weights, each edge direction has
\(2\cdot(2L)(2L-1)(2L+1)\) coplanar pairs and
\((2L)(4L)^2\) hinged pairs. Their sum is \(48L^3-4L\) per
direction. Summing the three directions gives exactly
\(144L^3-12L\) unordered adjacent pairs. This verifies that
(2.4)--(2.6) include every pair, including the zero-weight types.

## 3. Exact finite sums and complete polynomial evaluation

Define the original one-sided and symmetric sums
\[
 S_j(L)=\sum_{m=-L}^{L-1}m^j,
 \qquad F_j(L)=\sum_{m=-L}^{L}m^j,
 \qquad I_j(L)=\sum_{m=-L+1}^{L-1}m^j.
\tag{3.1}
\]
The needed exact values are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 j&0&1&2&3&4\\\hline
 S_j&2L&-L&(2L^3+L)/3&-L^3&(6L^5+10L^3-L)/15.
\end{array}
\tag{3.2}
\]
Also
\[
\begin{split}
 F_4&=(6L^5+15L^4+10L^3-L)/15,\\
 I_4&=(6L^5-15L^4+10L^3-L)/15,
 \qquad I_2=(2L^3-3L^2+L)/3,\qquad I_0=2L-1,\\
 \sum_{m=-L}^{L-1}r_m^2&=\frac{L(4L^4+1)}{10},\qquad
 \sum_{m=-L}^{L}k_L(m)m^4=2F_4-2L^4.
\end{split}
\tag{3.3}
\]
For a direct proof of (3.2), all five formulas have the correct value
at \(L=1\). The increment of the defining sum from \(L\) to \(L+1\)
adds the two original endpoint values \((-L-1)^j+L^j\). Expanding
each displayed polynomial's increment gives those same two values,
proving the formulas by induction. Adding the endpoint \(L\) and
removing the two endpoints \(\pm L\) proves the formulas for \(F_4\)
and \(I_j\). Finally
\(r_m^2=m^4+2m^3+2m^2+m+1/4\) gives its sum from (3.2);
the last formula uses precisely the two endpoint deficiencies of
\(k_L\). These evaluations retain the negative odd sums \(-L,-L^3\).

In the last sum in (2.4), the \(\sigma=+1\) range is
\(-L\le m<L\). Its \(\sigma=-1\) range is \(-L<m\le L\).
The change of summation index \(m\mapsto-m\) in the latter shows
that its value equals that of the former, including their boundary
terms. This is an exact evaluation of both original ranges. Therefore
\[
\begin{split}
 H_{1,\rm hinged}(c,d)
 =16L^2\bigl[t^2S_4+tcS_3
       +(tc/2+c^2/4)S_2+(c^2/4)S_1+(c^2/16)S_0\bigr],\\
 H_{1,12}(c,d)=2L(2L+1)
         \bigl[t^2I_4+tcI_2+(c^2/4)I_0\bigr],\qquad
 H_{1,13}(c,d)=2L(2L-1)t^2F_4.
\end{split}
\tag{3.4}
\]
Substitution of (3.2)--(3.3) yields the three complete direction
polynomials, with \(H_1=H_{1,12}+H_{1,13}+H_{1,\rm hinged}\):
\[
\begin{split}
 H_1(c,d)={}&\frac{48}{5}(c+d)^2L^7
 +(20c^2/3+16cd+12d^2)L^5
 -\frac83(c^2+cd)L^4\\
 &+(26c^2/15-6cd/5-8d^2/5)L^3
 +\frac23(c^2+cd)L^2-\frac12c^2L,\\
 H_2(c,d)={}&c^2(16L^7/5-2L^5/5+4L^3/5-L/10),\\
 H_3(c,d)={}&c^2(16L^7/5+16L^6/5+10L^5/3
                       -4L^4/3-8L^3/15+2L^2/15).
\end{split}
\tag{3.5}
\]
For example, (2.6) becomes
\(c^2[(8L^2-2L)F_4-4L^6]\) before expansion, proving its
direction-3 boundary term. Summing (3.5) gives the full pair polynomial
\[
\boxed{\begin{split}
 H_L(c,d)={}&(16c^2+96cd/5+48d^2/5)L^7+(16c^2/5)L^6\\
 &+(48c^2/5+16cd+12d^2)L^5-(4c^2+8cd/3)L^4\\
 &+(2c^2-6cd/5-8d^2/5)L^3
 +(4c^2/5+2cd/3)L^2-(3c^2/5)L.
\end{split}}
\tag{3.6}
\]
Every coefficient in (3.6) is thus obtained from the explicit edge
classification and exact finite sums, independently of any interpolation.
The two required evaluations are
\[
\boxed{\begin{split}
 H_L(1,1)&=\frac{224}{5}L^7+\frac{16}{5}L^6
 +\frac{188}{5}L^5-\frac{20}{3}L^4-\frac45L^3
 +\frac{22}{15}L^2-\frac35L,\\
 H_L(3,7)&=\frac{5088}{5}L^7+\frac{144}{5}L^6
 +\frac{5052}{5}L^5-92L^4-\frac{428}{5}L^3
 +\frac{106}{5}L^2-\frac{27}{5}L.
\end{split}}
\tag{3.7}
\]

For completeness, the unchanged face-vector mass follows from the same
coordinate counts. At each \(-L\le m<L\) there are \(2L(2L+1)\)
12-faces of weight \(r_m/2\). At each \(-L\le m\le L\) there are
\(4L^2\) 13-faces of weight \(m^2/2\). Hence
\[
\begin{split}
 A_L&=\frac14\left[
 2L(2L+1)\frac{L(4L^4+1)}{10}+4L^2F_4\right]\\
 &=\frac{L^2(2L+1)}{60}(24L^4+24L^3+8L^2-4L+3)\\
 &=\frac45L^7+\frac65L^6+\frac23L^5+\frac1{30}L^3+\frac1{20}L^2.
\end{split}
\tag{3.8}
\]
In particular \(A_L\ge4L^7/5>0\).

 \mathsf R_{8,L}=\frac4{225}A_L,\qquad

\tag{4.1}
\]
Equations (3.7)--(3.8), with these distinct factors retained, give
\[
\boxed{\begin{split}
 \mathsf R_L
 =\frac1{251535375}\bigl(&153001392L^7+15594888L^6
 +129973560L^5-21634000L^4\\
 &-3201942L^3+4992787L^2-1917900L\bigr).
\end{split}}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Thus its leading coefficient is
\[
 \lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\mathsf R_L}{L^7}

\tag{4.3}
\]
The negative coefficients at degrees 4, 3, and 1 are retained open-boundary
terms. Positivity of the full polynomial for every allowed \(L\) follows
from the original orthogonal sum (1.12), with the quantitative bound
\(\mathsf R_L\ge4A_L/225>0\).

\[
 C_{\rm leak}=\frac{12750116}{16769025}

 \qquad b_{\rm edge}=\frac{54848}{51597}.
\tag{4.4}

\tag{4.5}

There is no normalization of the original state in (4.5): it is the

To verify both the limit and the correction exactly, define the two

\[
\begin{split}
 P_5(L)&=2139072L^5-24724L^4+216340L^3
                          +95770L^2+45698L+19179,\\
 P_4(L)&=3233332L^4+1566220L^3-95770L^2
                                      +43430L+114513.
\end{split}
\tag{4.6}
\]
Multiplication of (3.8) and (4.2) gives the exact identities
\[
 C_{\rm leak}A_L-\mathsf R_L
       =\frac{4L}{10061415}P_5(L),
 \qquad
 \mathsf R_L-C_{\rm leak}A_L+\frac{b_{\rm edge}}L A_L
       =\frac{4L}{10061415}P_4(L).
\tag{4.7}
\]
Their coefficients provide an independent arithmetic check on every
term in the ratio. For \(L\ge2\), the first polynomial is positive
because its first two terms are \(L^4(2139072L-24724)>0\) and the
others are positive. The second is positive because
\(L^2(3233332L^2+1566220L-95770)>0\) and its remaining terms are
positive. Consequently
\[
 C_{\rm leak}-\frac{b_{\rm edge}}L
   <\frac{\mathsf R_L}{A_L}<C_{\rm leak}
      \quad(L\ge2),
\tag{4.8}
\]
and, using \(A_L\ge4L^7/5\) and omitting just the negative term
when bounding \(P_4\),
\[
 0<\frac{\mathsf R_L}{A_L}-C_{\rm leak}+\frac{b_{\rm edge}}L
 \le\frac{24787475}{10061415L^2}.
\tag{4.9}
\]
Indeed \(P_4(L)\le(3233332+1566220+43430+114513)L^4
=4957495L^4\) for \(L\ge1\). Finally the last quotient in
(4.7) has leading term
\((4\cdot3233332/10061415)/(4/5)\,L^{-2}
=3233332/(2012283L^2)\); division of its explicit polynomials has
next remainder \(O(L^{-3})\), since the denominator divided by
\(L^7\) tends to \(4/5\) and is bounded away from zero. This proves
(4.5), rather than assuming the rate from numerical values.

If instead the denominator is the original angular coefficient
\(\mathcal A_L=4A_L\), the same exact map gives
\[
 \frac{\mathsf R_L}{\mathcal A_L}
  =\frac{3187529}{16769025}-\frac{13712}{51597L}
        +\frac{808333}{2012283L^2}+O(L^{-3}).
\tag{4.10}
\]
This separate formula records the factor of four rather than silently
using the two coefficient names interchangeably.

## 5. Original amplitudes, exact spectral measures, and order of limits

Let \(\mathscr A_\xi=H-\mathcal E_\xi I
=\kappa(K_\xi-e(\xi)I)\). Define the actual out-of-band physical
measure of the native state by
\[
 \chi^{\rm out}_{\xi,\theta}=(I-P_\xi)\chi_{\xi,\theta},
 \qquad
 \mu^{\rm out}_{L,\xi,\theta}(J)
 =\langle\chi^{\rm out}_{\xi,\theta},
                 1_J(\mathscr A_\xi)\chi^{\rm out}_{\xi,\theta}\rangle.
\tag{5.1}
\]
The state in (5.1) is exactly orthogonal to the actual vacuum and band,
by (1.7)--(1.8). Define the fully evaluated dimensionless coefficient
measure
\[
 \Lambda_L=\frac4{225}A_L\,\delta_8
       +\frac{H_L(1,1)}{81}\,\delta_{9/2}
       +\frac{H_L(3,7)}{24843}\,\delta_{13/2}.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Its total mass is precisely \(\mathsf R_L\). The exact map from
dimensionless excitation energy to physical excitation energy is
\(u_\kappa(x)=\kappa x\), since (1.3)--(1.4) give
\(\mathscr A_\xi=\kappa(K_\xi-e(\xi)I)\). On the coefficient
measure it gives the physical atoms
\[
 8\kappa=\frac{16g_{\rm YM}^2}{a},\qquad
 \frac92\kappa=\frac{9g_{\rm YM}^2}{a},\qquad
 \frac{13}2\kappa=\frac{13g_{\rm YM}^2}{a}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Functional calculus proves this pushforward for every Borel set, not
only for the three moments: \(1_J(\kappa A)=1_{u_\kappa^{-1}(J)}(A)\)
for a self-adjoint \(A\), by applying the spectral theorem to the
composed indicator.

For every bounded continuous \(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C\), the precise
ordered coefficient statement is
\[
 \lim_{\xi\downarrow0}\frac1{\xi^4}
 \left[\lim_{\theta\to0}\frac9{4\theta^4}
       \int f(\lambda/\kappa)\,d\mu^{\rm out}_{L,\xi,\theta}(\lambda)
 \right]=\int f\,d\Lambda_L.
\tag{5.4}
\]
To prove it, (1.6) supplies the inner limit as the spectral expectation
of \(\rho_\xi\), and (1.10) gives
\(\rho_\xi/\xi^2\to\mathbf r_L\) in \(H^2\).
The bounded difference \(K_\xi-e(\xi)-H_0\) tends to zero, giving
norm-resolvent convergence by the resolvent identity. Uniform approximation
by the algebra generated by nonreal resolvents proves convergence of
\(C_0(\mathbb R)\) functional calculus. Moreover the \(H^2\) convergence
gives a bounded second spectral moment of \(\rho_\xi/\xi^2\), so
the mass outside \([-R,R]\) is bounded by a constant times \(R^{-2}\).
Approximate a bounded continuous \(f\) by its continuous compactly
supported cutoffs in the expectations; this tail bound gives the same
conclusion for that full class. The energies and orthogonal norms in
(1.9)--(1.12) yield exactly the three terms of (5.2). This proves (5.4).

The parity cochain \(z_i(n)=(-1)^{\sum_{j<i}n_j}I\) sends every
\(W_p\) to \(-W_p\), fixes all link Casimirs, and commutes with the
native translation. Its unitary \(\mathcal Z\) satisfies
\(\psi_{-\xi}=\mathcal Z\psi_\xi\),
\(P_{-\xi}=\mathcal ZP_\xi\mathcal Z\), and
\(\rho_{-\xi}=\mathcal Z\rho_\xi\). Thus the squared norm is even
analytic. In particular the fixed-box scalar remainder and the fraction
of the original state's mass are
\[
\begin{split}
 \lim_{\theta\to0}\frac9{4\theta^4}
               \|\chi^{\rm out}_{\xi,\theta}\|^2
       &=\xi^4\mathsf R_L+O_L(\xi^6),\\
 \lim_{\theta\to0}\frac9{4\theta^4}
               \|\chi_{\xi,\theta}\|^2
       &=\xi^2 A_L+O_L(\xi^4),\\
 \lim_{\theta\to0}
  \frac{\|\chi^{\rm out}_{\xi,\theta}\|^2}{\|\chi_{\xi,\theta}\|^2}
       &=\xi^2\frac{\mathsf R_L}{A_L}+O_L(\xi^4).
\end{split}
\tag{5.5}
\]
The denominators are nonzero on a sufficiently small positive coupling
interval because \(A_L>0\). All vacuum centering and dressing operations
precede this division. The fixed-box mixed Sobolev Taylor formula further
gives
\[
 \chi^{\rm out}_{\xi,\theta}
 =-\frac23\theta^2\xi^2\mathbf r_L
       +O_{H^k,L}(\theta^2|\xi|^3+\theta^4\xi^2).
\tag{5.6}
\]
For its justification the smooth vacuum is analytic in \(\xi\) in
all needed higher Sobolev spaces, and averaged translations have four
bounded angular derivatives between those spaces. The out-of-band state
and its first coupling derivative vanish at \(\xi=0\) for every
angle; it is even and vanishes at \(\theta=0\). Taylor integral
remainders therefore factor \(\xi^2\theta^2\), with the indicated
next coupling and angular terms. The leading coefficient is (1.10).

The original squared-norm prefactors are exactly
\[
\begin{split}
 \frac49\theta^4\xi^4\mathsf R_L
   &=\frac{\theta^4}{576g_{\rm YM}^{16}}\mathsf R_L
     =\frac{\pi^4a^8}{36D^4g_{\rm YM}^{16}}\mathsf R_L,\\
 \frac49\theta^4\xi^2 A_L
   &=\frac{\theta^4}{36g_{\rm YM}^{8}}A_L
     =\frac{4\pi^4a^8}{9D^4g_{\rm YM}^{8}}A_L.
\end{split}
\tag{5.7}
\]
The unsquared leading out-of-band amplitude is
\((2/3)\theta^2\xi^2\sqrt{\mathsf R_L}
=\theta^2\sqrt{\mathsf R_L}/(24g_{\rm YM}^8)
=\pi^2a^4\sqrt{\mathsf R_L}/(6D^2g_{\rm YM}^8)\).
Its physical excitation-form leading coefficient, when needed, is
\[
 \frac49\theta^4\xi^4\kappa
       \left[8\mathsf R_{8,L}
             +\frac92\mathsf R_{9/2,L}
             +\frac{13}2\mathsf R_{13/2,L}\right].
\tag{5.8}
\]
This follows from (1.10) in \(H^1\) and the channel electric energies;
it retains their different energies and does not infer an energy merely
from a small unnormalized norm.

Since (5.2) has fixed three-point support, the coefficient-measure limits
are exact weak limits and also limits in total variation:
\[
\boxed{\begin{split}
 \frac{\Lambda_L}{L^7}&\longrightarrow
  \frac{16}{1125}\delta_8+\frac{224}{405}\delta_{9/2}
                         +\frac{1696}{41405}\delta_{13/2},\\
 \frac{\Lambda_L}{A_L}&\longrightarrow
  \frac4{225}\delta_8+\frac{56}{81}\delta_{9/2}
                         +\frac{424}{8281}\delta_{13/2}.
\end{split}}
\tag{5.9}
\]
Indeed every atom's coefficient converges by (3.7)--(3.8), and the
total variation of the difference is the sum of the three absolute
coefficient differences. The second limiting measure has mass
\(C_{\rm leak}\), rather than 1, because its denominator is the
unchanged *first-band* face-vector mass \(A_L\). Composing (5.4) and
(5.9) is precisely the order \(\theta\to0\), then \(\xi\to0\),
then \(L\to\infty\). For example (5.5) proves
\[
 \lim_{L\to\infty}\left[
 \lim_{\xi\downarrow0}\frac1{\xi^2}
  \left(\lim_{\theta\to0}
     \frac{\|\chi^{\rm out}_{\xi,\theta}\|^2}{\|\chi_{\xi,\theta}\|^2}
  \right)\right]=C_{\rm leak}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

All \(O_L\) terms in (5.5)--(5.6) remain fixed-box estimates. The
proved band interval itself requires \(\xi\le3/(128M_L)\), and
the higher Sobolev and coupling coefficients have no volume-uniform
bound established here. Neither (4.5) nor (5.9)--(5.10) asserts a
fixed-positive-coupling infinite-volume leakage fraction or a joint
continuum limit. What is completely evaluated is the specified first
native leakage coefficient, its full boundary polynomial, all its
spectral channels, and the original amplitude and physical-energy maps.
