# Remaining exact maps after the 4+4+4+1 construction

## 1. Toroidal family to the quaternionic carrier

The workbench has the exact toric/filling map

\[
\overline F:N_3^C\to N_0,
\qquad
\Phi=P\oplus Q,
\qquad
\Phi M_3=M_F\Phi,
\qquad
c_F\Phi=Qc_3.
\]

The new quaternionic carriers have explicit structure groups

\[
\operatorname{Sp}(1)^3\rtimes C_3,
\qquad SU(3),
\qquad \operatorname{Sp}(3).
\]

A global structure-preserving map requires the actual toroidal overlap matrices to reduce to one of these groups and to intertwine the boundary/filling inclusions. Shared dimensions or Weyl-chamber counts do not supply this reduction.

**Strongest current map:** the marked C3 matrix

\[
P\Pi R_P=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

and the complex/quaternionic spectral-floor square. The global cocycle reduction is still open.

## 2. The 13-dimensional and full-color models

The equal-diagonal triality slice and the Lorentz 13-slice both include into \(\operatorname{Herm}_3(\mathbb H)\). Neither is a full SU(3)-invariant linear completion of the old nine-dimensional space.

**Exact obstruction:** under

\[
\operatorname{Herm}_3(\mathbb H)
=\operatorname{Herm}_3(\mathbb C)\oplus\mathbb C^3,
\]

the real SU(3) representation \(\mathbb C^3\) is irreducible. Any nonzero full-color transverse direction generates all six real transverse directions.

**Strongest surviving constructions:** 13 dimensions after an explicit symmetry reduction; 14 dimensions at fixed trace; 15 dimensions without the trace constraint.

## 3. Fermionic interpretation

The Lorentz 13-slice contains a vector \(G\), a left Weyl spinor \(z\), a dual Weyl spinor \(\chi\), and a scalar \(\lambda\). The theorem is a classical representation and cubic-invariant statement.

A quantum fermion theory requires Grassmann fields, a spin structure on spacetime, a first-order kinetic operator, canonical anticommutation relations, and anomaly checks. The present bosonic Jordan field theory does not silently provide those objects.

**Strongest current map:** the exact SL2(C) action

\[
(G,z,\chi,\lambda)\mapsto(gGg^*,gz,g^{-*}\chi,\lambda)
\]

and the cubic

\[
\lambda\det G-z^*\operatorname{adj}(G)z-\chi^*G\chi.
\]

## 4. Continuum and infinite-volume quantum limits

For each finite lattice, the compact-link Hamiltonian has a unique physical ground state and a positive discrete gap. The couplings \(a_g,a_s,\beta_p,\beta_h,m^2,\Gamma,\kappa\) have not yet been assigned a common lattice-spacing and volume scaling.

A continuum theorem must define a sequence \(\Lambda_n\), all coupling scalings, vacuum-energy subtractions, embeddings or comparison maps between the physical Hilbert spaces, and prove convergence of correlation functions or resolvents. A mass-gap theorem additionally needs a nonzero uniform lower bound after the continuum and infinite-volume limits.

**Strongest current object:** one exact nonlinear finite-volume Hamiltonian with product Haar--Lebesgue measure and the exact projector

\[
P_{\rm phys}=\int_{SU(3)^V}\mathcal U(g)\,dg.
\]

## 5. Relation to pure Yang--Mills

The dynamical unlock uses an adjoint Hermitian scalar and a fundamental complex triplet. Removing them removes the constructed 14-dimensional carrier and its cubic determinant interaction.

A map to pure Yang--Mills would have to integrate out these fields or exhibit a controlled sector whose effective Hamiltonian acts only on gauge links while preserving the relevant low-energy topology. No such renormalization or decoupling morphism has been proved.

## 6. Relation to the torus-fibred complex S6

The equal-diagonal complex rung has a compactified determinant boundary S6, but the source workbench's S6 carries specified torus fibres, monodromy, and three singular fillings.

**Exact relation currently available:** both sit in the division-algebra ladder and the complex/quaternionic cone inclusion. An identification requires a map preserving the period law and every singular filling. No such map follows from the sphere homeomorphism alone.
