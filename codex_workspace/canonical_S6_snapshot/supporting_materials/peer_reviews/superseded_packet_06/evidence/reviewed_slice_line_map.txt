   217	Then $\langle\mathcal A,\operatorname{sw}(y)\rangle=\langle\iota\mathcal A,y\rangle$ for $\mathcal A\in H^{k+1}(Z)$, where $\iota\mathcal A:=p^*p_!\mathcal A$. Moreover the fibrewise translation by $\hat\delta\in\Lambda^{\langle A_1,A_2\rangle}$ defines such a smooth free $S^1$-action on the compact manifold with boundary $X^\circ$, whose restriction to $F$ is translation, so that $\iota|_{H^*(F)}=\iota_{\hat\delta}$, contraction with $\hat\delta$. (The opposite choice of generator $[S^1]$ reverses the fibre orientation and changes $\iota$ by a global sign, immaterial below.)
   218	
   219	\noindent\textit{Proof.} First, by naturality and the above normalisation of the slant product,
   220	\[
   221	 \langle\mathcal A,a_*([S^1]\times y)\rangle
   222	 =\langle a^*\mathcal A,[S^1]\times y\rangle
   223	 =\langle[S^1]\backslash a^*\mathcal A,y\rangle.
   224	\]
   225	Next, $[S^1]\backslash a^*\mathcal A=p^*p_!\mathcal A$. Indeed, for a free action the map
   226	$S^1\times Z\to Z\times_{Z/S^1}Z$, $(\theta,z)\mapsto(\theta z,z)$, is a diffeomorphism, so $(\operatorname{pr}_2,a)$ identifies the trivial circle bundle $\operatorname{pr}_2:S^1\times Z\to Z$ with the pullback of the oriented principal circle bundle $p:Z\to Z/S^1$ along $p$, the map $a$ covering $p$; the fibre orientations correspond (both are the orbit direction, oriented by $[S^1]$). Integration over the fibre commutes with such base change, whence $(\operatorname{pr}_2)_!a^*\mathcal A=p^*p_!\mathcal A$; and for the product bundle $\operatorname{pr}_2$, integration over the fibre is, with the above normalisation of the slant product and of the fibre orientation, precisely $[S^1]\backslash(\mathord\cdot)$. This proves the first assertion.
   227	
   228	Since $\hat\delta$ is $\langle A_1,A_2\rangle$-invariant and the transition maps $R_g$ are linear, translation by $t\hat\delta$ ($t\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$) is well defined and smooth on $J$ and on $N_1,N_2$. On $J$ it is translation by $t\hat\delta$ in the fibre torus $(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb R)/\Lambda$, free because $\hat\delta$ is primitive. On $N_j$ it suffices to check freeness on $S_j=A_0^{(j)}/\langle\alpha\rangle$, $\alpha(x)=A_jx+v_j/m_j$: the image of $x$ is fixed by $t\hat\delta$ iff
   229	\[
   230	 (A_j^k-I)x+\left(\sum_{i<k}A_j^i\right)\frac{v_j}{m_j}+t\hat\delta\in\Lambda
   231	 \qquad\text{for some }0\le k<m_j.
   232	\]
   233	Applying the $A_j$-invariant form $\gamma$, which kills $\hat\delta$, gives $k\ell_j/m_j\in\mathbb Z$; as $\gcd(\ell_j,m_j)=1$ by admissibility this forces $k=0$, and then $t\hat\delta\in\Lambda$, i.e. $t=0$. The same computation applies on the nearby fibres of $N_j$, so the action is smooth and free on $X^\circ$, which is a compact manifold with boundary $M$.
   234	
   235	Finally, the inclusion $i:F\hookrightarrow X^\circ$ of a smooth fibre is $S^1$-equivariant, and both $a_*$ and the slant product are natural, so $i^*(\iota\mathcal A)=\iota_F(i^*\mathcal A)$, where $\iota_F=p_F^*p_{F!}$ is the corresponding operator for the translation action on $F$. On the torus $F=(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb R)/\Lambda$ this action is that of the subcircle $\mathbb R\hat\delta/\mathbb Z\hat\delta$; $p_F:F\to F/S^1_{\hat\delta}$ is a torus quotient, $H^*(F)=\bigwedge^*V$ is computed by translation-invariant forms, $p_F^*$ is the inclusion of $\bigwedge^*(\hat\delta^\perp)$, and integration of an invariant form over the (oriented) orbit circles is contraction with $\hat\delta$. Hence $\iota_F=p_F^*p_{F!}=\iota_{\hat\delta}$ on $H^*(F)$. For the fixed points of the finite subgroups $\mu_n\subset S^1_{\hat\delta}$ see Remark 9.25.\hfill$\square$

   308	\noindent\textbf{Lemma 7.25.} Let $\mathcal L$ be a local system of free finitely generated $\mathbb Z$-modules on $B^\circ=B\setminus\Sigma$, with monodromy group $G$ acting on the stalk $M$.
   309	\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.8em,itemsep=.55em]
   310	\item $H^2(B,j_*\mathcal L)\cong M_G$, the coinvariants.
   311	
   312	\item These isomorphisms are compatible with cup products: if $\mathcal L',\mathcal L''$ are two such local systems with stalks $M',M''$ and $\mathcal L'\otimes\mathcal L''$ has stalk $M'\otimes M''$, then under $H^0(B,j_*\mathcal L')=(M')^G$, $H^2(B,j_*\mathcal L'')\cong(M'')_G$ and $H^2(B,j_*(\mathcal L'\otimes\mathcal L''))\cong(M'\otimes M'')_G$ the cup product is $x\smile[y]=[x\otimes y]$, and symmetrically for $H^2\otimes H^0$.

   314	
   315	\noindent\textit{Proof.} (i) From
   316	$0\to j_!\mathcal L\to j_*\mathcal L\to\bigoplus_{p\in\Sigma}M^{G_p}\to0$
   317	(skyscrapers, which have no higher cohomology) one gets $H^2(B,j_!\mathcal L)\cong H^2(B,j_*\mathcal L)$, and
   318	$H^2(B,j_!\mathcal L)=H_c^2(B^\circ,\mathcal L)\cong H_0(B^\circ,\mathcal L)=M_G$ by Poincar\'e duality with local coefficients on the oriented surface $B^\circ$ [Hat02, \S3.H] (see also [Bre97, Ch. VI]).

   321	\[
   322	 (x\smile\alpha)\frown[B^\circ]
   323	 =x\frown(\alpha\frown[B^\circ]),
   324	 \qquad x\in H^0(B^\circ,\mathcal L'),\quad
   325	 \alpha\in H_c^2(B^\circ,\mathcal L''),
   326	\]
   327	the right-hand operation being the coefficient action $y\mapsto x\otimes y$ on $H_0$ (for cup/cap products with coefficient pairings and for Poincar\'e duality with local coefficients see [Hat02, \S3.H], [Bre97, Ch. VI]). Passing to $H_0(B^\circ,\mathcal L'')=(M'')_G$, the action of a $G$-invariant $x\in M'$ is $[y]\mapsto[x\otimes y]$, which is well defined precisely because $x$ is $G$-invariant.    451	\noindent\textbf{Lemma 8.2.} Let $M$ be a closed smooth simply connected $n$-manifold, $n\ge2$, with $\widetilde H_*(M;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H_*(S^n;\mathbb Z)$. Then $M$ is homotopy equivalent to $S^n$.
   452	
   453	\noindent\textit{Proof.} By the Hurewicz theorem [Hat02, Thm. 4.32], applied inductively in degrees $2,\ldots,n$, one gets $\pi_i(M)=0$ for $i\le n-1$ and an isomorphism $\pi_n(M)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_n(M;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$. Let $\varphi:S^n\to M$ represent a class mapping to a generator. By naturality of the Hurewicz map $\varphi_*$ is an isomorphism on $H_n$, hence (the remaining reduced groups vanishing) on all integral homology. Both spaces are simply connected and of the homotopy type of CW complexes: this is clear for $S^n$, and for the closed topological manifold $M$ it is [Hat02, Cor. A.12]. Hence $\varphi$ is a homotopy equivalence by the homology Whitehead theorem [Hat02, Cor. 4.33].\hfill$\square$
