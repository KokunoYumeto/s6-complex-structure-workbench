# S6_3_FAMILY: the family of complex structures on S^6 of [AC26] and [E26]. Its exact classification, its parameter space, its boundary and its limit

Development prompt, 12 November 2026. File prefix for your deliverables: `S6_3_FAMILY`.

## What is to be settled

[AC26] constructs X_c from period functions τ, μ, β = β^part + c on the upper half-plane, for a constant c with Im c sufficiently negative, and writes (Remark 6.4, T l. 1890): "Whether X_{c₀} ≅ X_{c₀′} for admissible c₀ ≠ c₀′ is left open." [E26] constructs X(u, a₀, a₁) for u in a small punctured disc and discrete data (a₀, a₁), and remarks (8.1) that the complex structure "is indeed deforming", with a sketch; its Conjecture 8.2 proposes a flat proper filling at u = 0; its Remark 8.4 records Klingler's question whether the monodromy group acts properly discontinuously on the period domain. Viaclovsky (arXiv:2610.39102, §1.1) asks: (2) "Does varying u, with the logarithmic transformation data fixed, give all sufficiently small deformations?" (3) "For fixed multiplicities and fixed u, do different choices of the admissible translation vectors produce even more biholomorphically distinct complex structures on S6?" X. Yang (arXiv:2609.29666) proves, for related families with multiplicities (4,6) and (3,6) that are not S^6, that two members are biholomorphic exactly when their parameters differ by an element of (6/r)Z.

Where the matter stands (survey F2-F5, F12, F17, F20-F22, F27-F29):

1. The workbench and the course claim the classification: for c, c′ with Im c, Im c′ < −M, X_c ≅ X_{c′} as abstract complex manifolds if and only if c′ − c ∈ 2Z; every biholomorphism commutes with f; the isomorphism classes form the punctured disc 0 < |w| < e^{−πM}, w = e^{−πic} (`workbench/period_shift_classification.tex`, `pshift:thm:classification`, `pshift:lem:centralizer`, (PS1)-(PS29); `course/periods-and-nontrivial-deformation.md` §§5-8, Lemma 5.1, Theorem 8.1, (7.14)-(7.16), (8.2)). AI-produced, 6 September and 11 October 2026; two readers of the survey re-derived the "only if" part and read the "if" part. Inputs: the anticanonical ring and f = |−2K_X| (`workbench/analytic_canonical_ring.tex`; `course/canonical-divisor-and-intrinsic-fibration.md`, Corollary 7.2); the finite fillings (`workbench/finite_filling_certificates.tex`; `course/finite-quotients-and-line-bundles.md`, `course/normal-boundaries-and-integral-maps.md`, `course/varying-finite-fillings.md`); the family and its Kodaira-Spencer class (`workbench/period_parameter_deformation.tex`).
2. The course gives the dictionary with [E26]: L_μ = O(P − O), the first period column is a lift of translation by 6P, Engel's u = λ₀e^{−2πic} = λ₀w² with λ₀ not evaluated, and the admissible range is exactly Im c < −M with M = max D_part not evaluated (`course/global-periods-and-line-bundle-quotients.md` §§5-8; `course/cusp-and-compact-threefold.md` §§9-13; `workbench/candidate_geometry.tex` l. 563-596 and, for the gluing units of the cusp fibre, l. 1029-1064).
3. Sketches by readers of the survey, checked by no one: the extension of the classification to all twist vectors, with the conclusion that for fixed u Engel's threefolds with the given multiplicities fall into exactly two classes (`notes/G1a_notes.md`, G1a-13(a), G1a-2; `notes/B_notes.md`, B-7); Aut(X_c) = C^* (G1a-13(f)); the fibres over t = 64(47 ± 27√3)/11 are abelian surfaces (`notes/F_notes.md`, F-3; `workbench/cusp_spectral_geometry.tex` l. 959-1176).
4. Open: the value of M and what happens at |w| = e^{−πM}; the limit w → 0 (Conjecture 8.2); Klingler's question.

This prompt asks you to establish the classification conclusively, extend it to all the discrete data, put numbers on the parameter space, and decide the limit and the period-domain question.

## Leads (unverified; prove or refute each)

- **Lead 1 (centralizer and norm obstruction).** The rational centralizer of A₁, A₂ in End(Λ ⊗ Q) is {aI + bE}, E = δ̂ ⊗ γ. A biholomorphism X_c → X_{c′} covers the identity of B, is affine on fibres with L Π_c = Π_{c′}(aI + bE), a = ±1, so L = aI and c′ − c = −b/a ∈ Z. Extending over the multiple fibres requires l_j ∈ Λ with N_j l_j = −(1 − a)v_j − nε_jδ̂, N_j = 1 + A_j + … + A_j^{m_j−1}; N₁Λ = 3Zv₁ + Zδ̂, N₂Λ = 4Z(1,3,−3,0) + 2Zδ̂; Λ^{A₁}/N₁Λ = Z/3, Λ^{A₂}/N₂Λ = Z/4 ⊕ Z/2; a = −1 is impossible and for a = 1 the obstruction is the class of nδ̂ at the order-four fibre, of order two.
- **Lead 2 (even shifts).** For even n there is an isomorphism X_c → X_{c+n} with linear part I and lattice map I − nE, built from r₁ = −(n/3)(û + ŵ), r₂ = (n/4)(û + ŵ), the toric map (x, t) ↦ (t^{q_n}x, t) with q_n = (−3n/2, n/2) at the cusp, and a correction from H¹(B, V) = 0 for the rank-two bundle V, 0 → O_B → V → O_B(−p₀) → 0.
- **Lead 3 (Albanese cross-check).** Alb(S₁) = C/(Z + κ₁Z) and Alb(S₂) has modulus κ₂/2, with κ_j(c) = κ_j^part + c; so the nome of Alb(S₂) is a constant times w and that of Alb(S₁) a constant times w². This would prove the "only if" for generic c without the lattice argument.
- **Lead 4 (all discrete data).** For twist vectors w_j, w′_j ∈ Λ^{A_j}, a map X(c; w) → X(c′; w′) with linear part I exists iff there are l_j with N_j l_j = w_j − (I + nE)w′_j and a compatible cusp value; this holds iff p = p′ and, writing w₂ = r₂ε′ + k₂δ̂, c′ − c ≡ k₂ − k₂′ mod 2. Consequence: every member diffeomorphic to S^6 of the family with multiplicities (3,4) is biholomorphic to exactly one X_w; in Engel's notation X_{c+1} = X(u; a₀ + δ/3, a₁ − δ/4) up to conventions, and for fixed u the threefolds X(u, a₀, a₁) with |12ψ(a₀ + a₁)| = 1 form exactly two isomorphism classes, exchanged by the monodromy of the marking around u = 0.
- **Lead 5 (μ and the cusp fibre).** e^{2πiμ(p₀)} = −(5 + 2√6)^{±1}; μ is the Eichler-Zagier zero of ℘ (formula in `notes/G1a_notes.md`, G1a-3; Duke-Imamoglu attached). The three gluing multipliers of W are e^{−2πiμ₀}, e^{12πiμ₀}, e^{−2πi(7μ₀ + b₀)} with b₀ = (β + τ)(p₀); two are units of Z[√6] and the third moves with c, so the cusp fibres of X_c and X_{c+1} are isomorphic.
- **Lead 6 (special fibres).** The fibre over t has a surjection onto its elliptic curve E_t with elliptic kernel iff 6μ ∈ Zτ + Z, i.e. 6P(t) = O(t); on y² = x³ − 3t³(t−1)x + 2t⁴(t−1)² this holds exactly at t = 64(47 ± 27√3)/11; these two fibres, and the fibres over the dense countable set where P(t) is torsion, are abelian surfaces; the number of t with 6kP = O is 3k² − 1 with multiplicity.
- **Lead 7 (automorphisms).** Aut(X_c) = C^* (connected): cocycles with l_j ∈ (1 − A_j)Λ modulo coboundaries give no extra component.

## Parts

**A. The classification theorem, established.**
A.1. The inputs. Prove: ω_X ≅ O_X(−2S₂); f_*O_X(kS₂) = O_B(⌊k/4⌋p₂); ⊕_m H⁰(X, ω_X^{−m}) = C[U, V] with degrees 1, 2; the bi-anticanonical map is f; hence every biholomorphism X_c → X_{c′} covers an automorphism of P¹ fixing 0, 1, ∞. Prove that the three special fibres are pairwise non-isomorphic as complex spaces with multiplicity. Prove the description of the neighbourhoods of the multiple fibres as free cyclic quotients of smooth torus families (the normalization of the base change), with the product covers of S₁ and S₂ of degrees 9 and 8.
A.2. Prove or refute Lead 1 completely, including the lifting of a biholomorphism of a neighbourhood of S_j to the cyclic cover and its exact commutation with the deck generator. Compute the groups Λ^{A_j}/N_jΛ by program.
A.3. Prove or refute Lead 2 completely: the local maps at p₁, p₂, the extension over every stratum of the cusp fibre, the overlap identities with the gluings of [AC26] §6, the sheaf V and H¹(B, V) = 0. Prove the cocycle identity F_{2h, c+2k} ∘ F_{2k, c} = F_{2(h+k), c} and construct the proper holomorphic family over the w-disc {0 < |w| < e^{−πM}} whose fibres are pairwise non-biholomorphic.
A.4. Prove or refute Lead 3 and give the j-invariants of Alb(S₁), Alb(S₂) as functions of w. Re-derive the Kodaira-Spencer class of ∂/∂c and its non-vanishing in H¹(X_c, T) by your own computation; compare with Viaclovsky's proof of non-isotriviality (arXiv:2610.39102, §4) and with the method of Yang (arXiv:2609.29666, the theorem on (6/r)Z), and say which statements of each follow from the other's method.
A.5. State the theorem with all hypotheses, and say exactly what it does not cover.

**B. All the discrete data, and the dictionary with Engel.**
B.1. Prove the dictionary: for fixed (τ, μ, β) the fibre C²/ΠΛ is the quotient of the C^*-bundle of O_{E_τ}(P − O), P = [μ], by a lift of translation by ±6P with fibre multiplier e^{±2πiβ} (determine the signs); the family over P¹∖{0,1,∞} is Engel's; μ is the elliptic logarithm of a generator of the Mordell-Weil group; u = λ₀e^{−2πic}. Determine λ₀ for a normalization of Engel's reference linearization that you state (for instance by the leading coefficient at the fibre over ∞), or prove that no normalization is canonical and say what is. Give the sharp form of [E26] Proposition 2.4: the exact set of u for which the Z-action gives 2-tori on every fibre.
B.2. Prove or refute Lead 4: classify up to biholomorphism all threefolds X(c; w₁, w₂, l₀) of [AC26] (all admissible twist vectors and cusp twists, every value of p = 12l₀ − 4l₁ − 3l₂, not only |p| = 1), including maps with linear part −I and maps that do not preserve the marking. Translate the result into Engel's (u, a₀, a₁) and answer Viaclovsky's Question 3 for multiplicities (3,4): how many biholomorphism classes for fixed u, and how the classes are permuted when u goes once around 0.
B.3. Do the same for Viaclovsky's X_{3k,4l}(u) as far as your method reaches: for which (k, l) and which translation vectors are two of them biholomorphic; what replaces 2Z.
B.4. Prove or refute Lead 7: determine Aut(X_c) as a group, and the group of anti-holomorphic diffeomorphisms; decide for which c, c′ the complex conjugate of X_c is biholomorphic to X_{c′}.

**C. The parameter space in numbers.**
C.1. Define β^part by a normalization you state (for instance b₀ = (β^part + τ)(p₀) = 0), and compute τ, μ, β^part numerically on the fundamental domain: verify Lead 5's closed form for μ against the original Eichler-Zagier paper or re-derive it, and obtain β from the transformation laws (β1), (β2) of [AC26] page 2 (for instance by integrating an explicit modular differential that you derive, or by a theta-function formula for the linearization). Give μ(p₀), μ(z₁), μ(z₂), β^part(z₁), β^part(z₂) to 20 digits with two independent methods.
C.2. Compute M = max of D_part = Im β^part − 6(Im μ)²/Im τ over B∖{p₀} and the point(s) where it is attained, to at least 10 digits, with a proof that the maximum is where you say (D is superharmonic; a rigorous enclosure by interval arithmetic if you can). Hence the exact radius e^{−πM} of the w-disc and, with B.1, of Engel's u-disc.
C.3. The boundary. Describe what happens as Im c → −M: which fibre degenerates, to what (a quotient C²/Π_cΛ with Π_cΛ of real rank 3), and whether the family X_c has a limit as a complex space or as a foliated or non-Hausdorff object; prove what you state.
C.4. Prove or refute Lead 5: the three gluing units of W as numbers; that the isomorphism class of the surface W_c depends only on w² (or on which function of c); which double curve is the fixed curve of Aut⁰ = C^*.

**D. Special fibres and curves.**
D.1. Prove or refute Lead 6: the exact set of t for which the fibre F_t has an elliptic quotient; the torsion locus of P; NS(F_t) for every t (it is Zη for very general t, [AC26] Corollary 9.8, T l. 3551); which fibres are abelian surfaces and which have algebraic dimension 1 or 0.
D.2. Classify all compact complex curves and all irreducible compact complex surfaces in X_c (surfaces: the fibres, by `course/non-normal-fibres-and-vanishing.md` §8; curves: in W, in S₁ and S₂, in the abelian fibres), with proofs; decide whether X_c contains a rational curve outside W and an elliptic curve outside the special fibres.

**E. The limit w → 0 ([E26] Conjecture 8.2).**
E.1. For Engel's untwisted family X(u) → Δ*: construct the candidate filling over u = 0 (the P¹-bundle P_S(O ⊕ M_S) with the zero and infinity sections glued by translation by 6P, over the complement of the fibre at ∞, and its completion over ∞), and prove or refute that the total space is a complex space, flat and proper over the u-disc, with that central fibre. Treat separately: a neighbourhood of a regular fibre; the fibres over 0 and 1; the Mumford filling over ∞ as u → 0, where two degenerations meet. If flatness fails, find the correct limit (after a base change or a modification) and prove it.
E.2. For the S^6 family Y = X(u, a₀, a₁), over the w-disc by B.2: construct the limit as a logarithmic transform of the limit of E.1, describe the central fibre over w = 0 and its fibres over t = 0 and t = 1 ("semiabelian degenerations of bielliptic surfaces"), and prove or refute flatness and properness. Compute the topology of the central fibre (Euler number, π₁, H_*) and the monodromy of the family around w = 0 on H*(S^6) (trivial) and as an element of the mapping class group π₀Diff⁺(S^6) = Θ₇ = Z/28: decide which element it is, or reduce it to an explicit computable invariant.

**F. Klingler's question ([E26] Remark 8.4).**
F.1. Let Γ ⊂ Sp(Λ, ψ) be the monodromy group generated by A₁ and A₂ (ψ the invariant alternating form of type (1,6), [E26] Lemma 7.2; the invariant class η of [AC26] Lemma 9.4), and D = Sp₄(R)/U(1,1) the period domain of complex structures on Λ ⊗ R for which ψ is of type (1,1) with Hodge form of signature (1,1). Determine Γ as an abstract group and as a subgroup of the parabolic P_Z preserving the filtration Zδ̂ ⊂ ker γ ⊂ Λ; compute its Zariski closure and its image in the Levi quotient.
F.2. Decide whether Γ acts properly discontinuously on D. If not, exhibit a point with infinite stabilizer or a non-discrete orbit, and determine the largest Γ-invariant open subset of D on which the action is properly discontinuous; decide whether it contains the image of the period map z ↦ Π_c(z) for every admissible c. Do the same for the full group P_Z.
F.3. Prove that the period map of the family is an immersion on B° (det of the derivative: `workbench/hodge_frolicher.tex`, `prop:period-derivative-determinant`, l. 218) and decide whether the induced map B° → Γ∖D (or to the quotient of the open set of F.2) is injective; describe its closure and its behaviour at the three punctures.
