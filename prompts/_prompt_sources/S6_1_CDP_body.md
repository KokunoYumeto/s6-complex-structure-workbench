# S6_1_CDP: Campana-Demailly-Peternell after S^6. Where the proofs fail, a counterexample that does not use S^6, and the strongest theorem that is true

Development prompt, 12 November 2026. File prefix for your deliverables: `S6_1_CDP`.

## What is to be settled

Campana, Demailly and Peternell state that a compact complex threefold X with H¹(X,Z) = H²(X,Z) = 0, algebraic dimension a(X) = 1 and holomorphic algebraic reduction f: X → C satisfies H^i(X, T_X ⊗ M) = 0 for i ≠ 1, χ(X, T_X ⊗ M) ≤ 0 and c₃(X) ≤ 0, and hence that a complex structure on S^6 has algebraic dimension 0 (CDP98, Compositio Math. 112, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 4.2; CDP20, the corrigendum, Compositio Math. 156, Theorem 2.2, Proposition 2.4, Corollary 2.3; both attached in their arXiv versions). The threefolds of [AC26] and [E26] are diffeomorphic to S^6, fibre holomorphically over P¹ and have c₃ = 2, so the statement is false.

Where the matter stands (survey F6, F13-F16, F18; `status/STATUS_2026-10-05.md`):

1. [AC26] §10 (T l. 4400-4860: Lemmas 10.2-10.3, Remark 10.4, Theorem 10.5, Corollary 10.6, Lemma 10.7, Proposition 10.8) locates two failures for X: the reduction at CDP20 p. 692 (arXiv v2 p. 14, opening of §7) from the ambient group H⁰(S, Ω¹_X|_S ⊗ A) to H⁰ of the torsion-free quotient Ω̃¹_S ⊗ A, and the count of H₁ in CDP20 Lemma 4.2 = CDP98 Lemma 3.2, which forgets the monodromy. Settled for X in [AC26]; [E26] omits the discussion.
2. The workbench and a correction note turn the first failure into a general statement and give a test family that does not use X (`workbench/cdp_program.tex` l. 40-353, `prop:missing-cotangent-filtration`, `prop:missing-global-section-criterion`, `prop:conductor`, `prop:nodal-counterexample`; `correction_note/N_singular_fibre_vanishing_step.tex`, Proposition 2.3, Lemma 4.3, Theorem 4.4; `course/non-normal-fibres-and-vanishing.md` §§1-6, §9). Proved only there; AI-produced; no paper states it.
3. A conditional theorem survives (`workbench/cdp_program.tex` l. 1368-1606, `thm:corrected-cdp-global`, `cor:corrected-cdp-relative`, `thm:corrected-cdp-fibrewise`; the correction note, Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 5.3; the course lesson §11.3-11.5): one vanishing H²(X, T_X ⊗ L₀) = 0 forces c₃ ≤ 0. It assumes exactly the vanishing whose proof failed. The workbench calls it "the strongest intact replacement"; nothing of the kind is proved.
4. Not examined by anyone: non-reduced fibres (CDP20 Remarks 7.2 and 7.4 pass from S = red X_c to the fibre mS in one sentence); CDP20 Proposition 7.3 itself, whose proof uses "all line bundles η*(L_t) are trivial" in one sub-case; the proof of CDP20 Theorem 2.1 (the case of a non-holomorphic reduction), of which [AC26] Remark 9.9(e) says that it may be used "only insofar as its proof is independent" of the failed steps; the corrected general forms of CDP20 Lemma 4.2, Corollary 5.2, Propositions 5.5 and 5.6; and what is true in place of Theorem 2.2 under geometric hypotheses.

This prompt asks for the mathematics: the general theorems with complete proofs, counterexamples given completely, and a corrected theory of compact complex threefolds with b₂ = 0 and a(X) = 1 carried as far as it goes. It is not a commentary on CDP.

## Leads (unverified; prove or refute each)

The Leads are from the survey (Claude, Anthropic) and its readers' notes `notes/G1b_notes.md`, `notes/B_notes.md`, `notes/A0_notes.md`. Nobody has checked them.

- **Lead 1 (the kernel).** Let S be a reduced effective Cartier divisor in a complex manifold X, T_S = ker(Ω¹_S → j_*Ω¹_{S_reg}) the torsion of Ω¹_S, Ω̃¹_S = Ω¹_S/T_S, K_S = ker(Ω¹_X|_S → Ω̃¹_S). Then 0 → N*_{S/X} → K_S → T_S → 0 is exact, and for a line bundle A on S with H⁰(S, Ω̃¹_S ⊗ A) = 0 and H⁰(S, N* ⊗ A) = 0 one has H⁰(S, Ω¹_X|_S ⊗ A) = ker(δ_A: H⁰(T_S ⊗ A) → H¹(N* ⊗ A)). If S has normal crossings with normalization η: S̃ → S then K_S ≅ η_*η*N*, so the ambient group is H⁰(S̃, η*(N* ⊗ A)) under the two vanishings. (`workbench/cdp_program.tex` l. 52-110; course lesson §§1-3.)
- **Lead 2 (the test family).** C = {y²z = x²(x+z) + tz³} ⊂ P² × Δ has smooth total space and nodal central fibre C₀; X = C × P¹ → Δ; S = C₀ × P¹; A = pr₁*A_λ with A_λ ∈ Pic⁰(C₀) glued by λ not a root of unity. Then H⁰(S, N* ⊗ A) = 0, H⁰(S, Ω̃¹_S ⊗ A) = 0 and H⁰(S, Ω¹_X|_S ⊗ A) ≠ 0. The correction note (Lemma 4.3, Theorem 4.4) has a version in which the twist is O_C(P − Q) for two sections and extends to the total space, with ambient H⁰ = C exactly for every gluing scalar.
- **Lead 3 (CDP's own propositions).** By CDP20 Proposition 4.11 every singular irreducible reduced fibre is non-normal, so the case in which the p. 692 inference is valid (S normal, [AC26] Remark 10.4) never occurs among singular reduced fibres; and in Step 1 of the proof of CDP20 Proposition 7.3 the authors derive that all η*(L_t) are trivial, which is exactly the situation in which Lead 1 gives a non-zero ambient section.
- **Lead 4 (the index).** For a compact connected complex threefold Y with b₁(Y) = b₂(Y) = 0 and a non-zero effective divisor: χ(Y, T_Y ⊗ L) = 1 − b₃(Y)/2 for every L ∈ Pic(Y); h⁰ = h³ = 0 on a dense open subset of each component of Pic(Y); if H²(Y, T_Y ⊗ L₀) = 0 for one L₀ then b₃ ≥ 2; if b₃ = 0 then H²(Y, T_Y ⊗ L) ≠ 0 for every L. (Course lesson §11.3-11.5; `notes/G1b_notes.md`, G1b-3.)
- **Lead 5 (Euler numbers).** If the general fibre of f is a complex torus then c₃(X) = e(X) = Σ_c e((X_c)_red), the sum over the singular fibres; for X of [AC26] the only contribution is e(W) = 2. So for torus fibrations "c₃ ≤ 0" is a statement about Euler numbers of degenerate fibres. (`notes/G1b_notes.md`, G1b-13.)
- **Lead 6 (monodromy).** (K ⋊ Q)^ab = Q^ab ⊕ (K^ab)_Q; the count of CDP20 Lemma 4.2 becomes r = s − 1 + t′ with t′ the rank of the coinvariants of H₁(F;Q); the rank argument of CDP98 Lemma 3.2 is valid exactly when the invariant lattice H¹(F;Z)^{π₁} spans H¹(F, O_F) over C (`workbench/cdp_program.tex` l. 1302-1358 and l. 1592-1606, condition (5.33); [AC26] Lemma 10.7, T l. 4682).
- **Lead 7 (algebraic dimension without periods).** If X is compact with b₂(X) = 0 and f: X → C is surjective with connected fibres and general fibre a compact Kähler manifold (for instance a torus), then every effective divisor of X is vertical and M(X) = f*M(C); so a(X) = 1 for every member of every known torus-fibred family on S^6 (course lesson §8, l. 736-781).

## Parts

Every Part asks for theorems with proofs or counterexamples given completely. Where a statement resists, prove the strongest case within reach, compute, and state the obstruction as an exact statement with its smallest undecided instance. Carry each Part to where it stops.

**A. The conormal step, in full generality.**
A.1. Prove or refute Lead 1 for a reduced effective Cartier divisor in a complex manifold of any dimension, with every sheaf map typed. Prove the normal-crossings description K_S ≅ η_*η*N* with the local computation at double curves and at triple points (local equations x = 0, xy = 0, xyz = 0 times a unit), and decide whether the isomorphism is canonical.
A.2. Go beyond normal crossings. Compute T_S, K_S and the criterion for the ambient vanishing for: a pinch point (x² = y²z), a cuspidal edge (x² = y³), a general non-normal surface with ordinary singularities, and a general reduced hypersurface germ in terms of its normalization and conductor. State and prove a criterion of the form "H⁰(S, Ω¹_X|_S ⊗ A) = 0 if and only if …" in the widest class you can.
A.3. Non-reduced fibres. For a fibre X_c = mS with S smooth (the multiple fibres 3S₁ and 4S₂ of X are of this kind) and more generally mS with S reduced with normal crossings, compute H⁰(X_c, Ω¹_X ⊗ B|_{X_c}) for a line bundle B on X through the filtration by the thickenings kS, and prove the exact condition for its vanishing. Then decide whether CDP20 Remarks 7.2 and 7.4 are correct as stated; if not, give the counterexample and the corrected statement.
A.4. CDP20 Proposition 7.3 ("Equation (11) holds for singular reduced fibres"): read its proof step by step and decide whether statement (11) is true in the generality claimed and whether the printed proof is valid. If a step fails, give the counterexample; if the statement is true, give a complete proof. Check your conclusion on W ⊂ X, where H⁰(W, Ω̃¹_W ⊗ A) = 0 and H⁰(W, Ω¹_X|_W ⊗ A) = C for every L ([AC26] Theorem 10.5, T l. 4549; course lesson §9): reprove both equalities.
A.5. Prove or refute Lead 3, and say exactly which hypotheses of CDP20 §7 are inconsistent with which.

**B. Counterexamples that do not use S^6.**
B.1. Prove or refute Lead 2 in both versions, completely: smoothness of the total space, the line bundle and its extension, each cohomology group, the dimension of the ambient group for every value of the gluing scalar including roots of unity.
B.2. Make it compact: construct a compact complex threefold Y with a surjective holomorphic map to a compact curve, a reduced non-normal fibre S and a line bundle L on Y, for which the two vanishings printed at CDP20 p. 692 hold on S and H²(S, (T_Y ⊗ L)|_S) ≠ 0, and compute R²g_*(T_Y ⊗ L) and H²(Y, T_Y ⊗ L). Say which hypotheses of CDP20 Theorem 2.2 your Y satisfies and which it does not.
B.3. Prove the strongest necessary conditions you can for a threefold satisfying all hypotheses of CDP20 Theorem 2.2 to have c₃ > 0: which fibres must occur (a non-normal reduced fibre with H⁰(S̃, η*(N* ⊗ A)) ≠ 0 for every L? a non-reduced fibre failing A.3?). Test them on X, on Viaclovsky's two-parameter family (arXiv:2609.33785, whose fibres are one hexagonal, two rank-one degenerations and one multiple fibre) and on the family of Liu and Rollenske (arXiv:2609.33826).

**C. The strongest theorem that is true.**
C.1. Prove or refute Lead 4 with exact hypotheses (integral or rational vanishing of H¹ and H²; what is needed of the divisor; non-reduced divisors), including the family of line bundles over Pic(Y) used for semicontinuity.
C.2. Prove or refute Lead 5 with its hypotheses (which fibres are allowed; local triviality away from finitely many points). Then decide: for a proper holomorphic map from a compact complex threefold onto a curve with general fibre a 2-torus, is e((X_c)_red) ≥ 0 for every fibre? Classify the fibres with e = 0 as far as you can (smooth reductions; rank-one degenerations; which others), and the possible values of e for Mumford-type degenerations of rank two. Deduce a theorem of the form: for X as in CDP20 Theorem 2.2 with general fibre a torus, c₃(X) ≥ 0, with equality if and only if …; or refute it.
C.3. CDP20 §4 restricts the general fibre of the reduction. Re-derive, with the corrected Lemma 4.2 of Part D, which surfaces can be the general fibre of the algebraic reduction of a threefold with b₁ = b₂ = 0 and a = 1 (tori, bielliptic, K3, Kodaira, Hopf, Inoue, …), with proofs; for each type that survives, say what Lead 5 becomes.
C.4. State and prove the strongest corrected form of CDP20 Theorem 2.2 and of Corollary 2.3 that you can reach: geometric hypotheses on the fibres (for example: every reduced fibre satisfies the criterion of A.2 and every multiple fibre that of A.3) under which a), b), c) hold; and, for X homeomorphic to S^6 with a(X) = 1 and holomorphic reduction, what the fibres of the reduction must look like (total Euler number 2; at least one fibre failing the criterion). Prove that your hypotheses cannot be weakened in the direction of each counterexample of Part B, or say what is undecided.

**D. The monodromy gap.**
D.1. Prove Lead 6 and give the corrected statements, with complete proofs, of CDP20 Lemma 4.2 (= CDP98 Lemma 3.2), Corollary 5.2 (surjectivity of H¹(X, O_X) → H¹(F, O_F)), Proposition 5.5 (R¹f_*O_X = O(b₁) ⊕ O(b₂) with b_j ≥ 0; for X it is O ⊕ O(−1), [AC26] Proposition 9.13, T l. 3858) and Proposition 5.6 (a general L is non-torsion on every fibre; true for X by [AC26] Proposition 10.8, T l. 4754, by a different argument). For each, the true general statement in terms of the monodromy representation on H¹(F;Z), and a counterexample to the printed one (X is one; find a second that does not use X, for instance among bielliptic or Kodaira fibrations).
D.2. Trivial monodromy. Under trivial monodromy on H₁(F;Q) the printed Lemma 4.2 stands. Decide whether compact complex threefolds with b₁ = b₂ = 0, a = 1, holomorphic reduction with general fibre a torus and trivial monodromy exist; prove c₃ ≤ 0 for them, or construct one with c₃ > 0, or prove there are none.

**E. The algebraic dimension of complex structures on S^6.**
E.1. Prove or refute Lead 7, and verify its hypotheses for X_c of [AC26], for Engel's X(u, a₀, a₁), for Viaclovsky's families (arXiv:2609.33785, 2610.10451, 2610.39102) and for the family of Liu and Rollenske.
E.2. CDP20 Theorem 2.1 (the case in which the algebraic reduction is not holomorphic) and the second proof of it cited there, by Lehn, Rollenske and Schinko (arXiv:1912.09719, attached): write out the proof of Theorem 2.1 as printed, list every earlier statement of CDP98 and CDP20 it uses, and decide for each whether it is affected by the failures of Parts A and D. Conclude with a theorem: either Theorem 2.1 with a complete proof, or the exact step that is not proved, with a counterexample if you can make one.
E.3. Decide, as far as it can be decided, which algebraic dimensions occur for complex structures on S^6. a = 3: a Moishezon threefold has b₂ > 0 (CDP98 Corollary 1.2): reprove it, and say exactly what replaces CDP98 Lemma 1.5 (stated to be incorrect in the corrigendum) and where that lemma was used. a = 2, and a = 1 with non-holomorphic reduction: CDP20 Corollary 2.3 excludes them through Theorem 2.1 (c₃ = 0), so the answer is the theorem you proved, or the gap you found, in E.2. a = 0: is there a complex structure on S^6 with a = 0? Prove existence, or prove that the known constructions and their small deformations all have a = 1 (for X_c: every small deformation carries the pencil |−2K|, if you can prove it; `workbench/analytic_canonical_ring.tex`), and state what is open. Conclude with a theorem listing the values of a(X) that are proved possible, proved impossible, and undecided for S^6.
