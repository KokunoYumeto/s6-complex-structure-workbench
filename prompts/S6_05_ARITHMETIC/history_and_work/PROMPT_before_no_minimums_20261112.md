## What is to be settled

Two side lanes of the S6 workbench are arithmetic and have no mathematical map to the complex threefold X (the survey says so plainly: F31, F32). Each leaves a well-posed open problem.

**I. The count.** The workbench counts the integer points

R(X) = #{(a, q, n) ∈ Z³ : a² − 3q² − 18n² = −2, a > 0, q ≥ 0, n ≥ 0, a + 4n > q, 4a + 17n ≤ X}

(the level −1 of the form 4(xy + xz + yz) − x² − y² − z² in a fundamental region, after an integral substitution; `workbench/koide_counting_repair.tex`, `workbench/koide_exact_parametrization.tex`). It proves R(X) = C₀X + O(X^{1−η}) for an unspecified η > 0 with C₀ = (9/(2π²))·log(2+√3)·log(3/(2√2)) = 0.0353620478…, by Benoist-Oh and ideals of Z[√3]; it computes R(10⁸) = 3,536,002 exactly (`workbench/koide_exact_census.tex`); and it records as open (`workbench/koide_counting_repair.tex` l. 1133-1153; ledger S6-OPEN-KOIDE-SQRT-DISCREPANCY) whether R(X) − C₀X = O_ε(X^{1/2+ε}). A reader of the survey recounted R(X) independently to 10⁷ and found a reformulation that the workbench missed (`notes/D2_notes.md`, D2-8, D2-9): the height is a geodesic-to-geodesic distance, and R(X) is a count of common perpendiculars with foot in a fixed arc. That reformulation is a sketch, checked by program to z = 30,000 and by no person.

**II. The lattice.** For the Niemeier lattice N with root system A₅⁴D₄ the workbench constructs three Leech lattices Λ₀, Λ₁, Λ₂, neighbours of N along the Weyl vector, cycled by an element r of order 3 of the Weyl group, with common pairwise intersection J of determinant 81 and index 9 in each, and a fourth even unimodular overlattice of J with root system A₂¹² (`workbench/es_niemeier_residue_fixed_neighbor.tex`, `esrf:thm:common-J` l. 313-346, `esrf:thm:discriminant-correspondence` l. 363-529, `esrf:thm:fourth-extension` l. 546-782; `workbench/es_niemeier_leech.tex`; `workbench/es_niemeier_shared.tex`). A reader recomputed everything exactly and added that J has exactly eight even unimodular overlattices, six Leech and two A₂¹² (`notes/D1_notes.md`, D1-1, D1-13, with scripts). Whether J and this configuration are known is not established; the workbench's cubic "value groups" depend on a chosen isometry N ⊗ R → O³ and are not invariants (D1-5).

This prompt asks for theorems on both: an explicit exponent and the neighbouring results for the count, and the identification of J with its symmetries.

## Leads (unverified; prove or refute each)

- **Lead 1 (coordinates).** With s = x + y + 4z, t = y − x: 2(4(xy + xz + yz) − x² − y² − z²) = s² − 3t² − 18z². In the workbench's coordinates s = 17a + 72n, t = q, z = 4a + 17n. So R(X) = #{(s, t, z) ∈ Z³ : s² − 3t² = 18z² − 2, 1 ≤ z ≤ X, t ≥ 0, t + 4z < s ≤ 17z/4}.
- **Lead 2 (ideals in a fixed arc).** Let ε₀ = 2 + √3, ρ(m) = Σ_{d|m} χ₁₂(d) the number of ideals of norm m in Z[√3], m_z = 18z² − 2 = 2(3z−1)(3z+1). Each ideal 𝔞 of norm m has a unique generator α = s + t√3 with √m ≤ α < ε₀√m; put θ(𝔞) = log(α/√m)/log ε₀ ∈ [0,1). With λ₀ = log(3/(2√2))/log(2+√3) = 0.0447178…: R(X) = Σ_{1≤z≤X} #{𝔞 : N𝔞 = 18z² − 2, 0 ≤ θ(𝔞) ≤ λ₀} + R₀(X) − 1, where R₀(X) = #{k ≥ 1 : 4a_k ≤ X} with a_k + q_k√3 = (1+√3)(2+√3)^k is O(log X) and the −1 removes the point (s,t,z) = (4,0,1). The only solutions with t ≥ 0, 0 < s ≤ 17z/4 violating s − 4z > t are (4,0,1) and (17,1,4).
- **Lead 3 (geometry).** For F = s² − 3t² − 18z² and Γ = SO(F, Z) (cocompact, arithmetic, from the quaternion algebra of discriminant 6), the integer points v with F(v) = −2 are the Γ-translates of finitely many closed geodesics of Γ∖H; z(v) = (1/3)·cosh d(g_v, g₀) with g₀ the closed geodesic dual to e_z = (0,0,1) (stabilized by (s,t) ↦ (2s+3t, s+2t)); θ is the position of the foot of the common perpendicular on g₀. So R(X) counts common perpendiculars of length at most arccosh(3X) from g₀ to the translates, with foot in an arc of proportion λ₀ of g₀; C₀ = λ₀·c with c = (9/(2π²))·log²(2+√3) the mean value of ρ(18z² − 2).
- **Lead 4 (what the literature gives).** Without the arc restriction the count is the double-coset count of Good (*Local analysis of Selberg's trace formula*, 1983, Theorem 4) and of Lekkas-Petridis (arXiv:2509.12902, Theorem 1.2; attached), with error O(X^{2/3}); so Σ_{z≤X} ρ(3z−1)ρ(3z+1) = (9/(2π²))·log²(2+√3)·X + O(X^{2/3}). Mean square: Lekkas-Petridis Theorem 1.5. Voskou (arXiv:2407.03134, attached) refines the count of geodesic segments. The bookkeeping (which lattice; the number of classes of v; orientations and Good's sign cases; the standing assumptions of Lekkas-Petridis) is not done.
- **Lead 5 (census data).** R(10⁶) = 35,381; R(10⁷) = 353,598; R(10⁸) = 3,536,002; |R(X) − C₀X| ≤ 0.172·√X for 10⁴ ≤ X ≤ 10⁸, with extreme values −812.83 at X = 32,461,294 and +721.79 at X = 77,078,404.
- **Lead 6 (the lattice J).** J^∨/J ≅ F₃⁴ with a hyperbolic quadratic form, so it has eight totally isotropic planes in two rulings of four; all eight overlattices are even unimodular: six Leech lattices and two copies of the Niemeier lattice A₂¹² (one of them the 2-neighbour of N along ρ/2); r fixes the two A₂¹² and permutes each triple of Leech lattices cyclically. The d-neighbour of N along its Weyl vector is A₂¹² for d = 2, A₁²⁴ for d = 3 and Leech for d = 6 = h.
